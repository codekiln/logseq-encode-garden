  • My Thoughts

    • Logseq/Namespaces should use hyphens instead of spaces - My/Pref/Logseq/Namespaces/Should-be/Slugified

      • My/Pref/Logseq/Namespaces/Should-be/Slugified/Discussion
        • Now that Person/codekiln/GitHub/logseq-gardener is rendering the static site in such a way that the https://<domain>/<namespace-path> pulls up a particular logseq namespace, I'm beginning to have the preference that Logseq page names have Key/Hyphen (-) instead of Key/Space ( ) in them, so as to try to converge towards the invariant that the path at the top of the Logseq page is the same path that would be appended to the end of the static site's URL to get to that page.
        • This small change has quite a few implications for my knowledge gardens and will take some time to converge on. In particular, how best to refactor older namespaces such as GitP/A whose assets are named after them and published elsewhere will take some care to get right.

        • Conversion Phases

          • My/Pref/Logseq/Namespaces/Should-be/Slugified/Phase/1-Net-New-Pages-Slugified
            • start making net new pages and namespaces follow the slugification preference, using Key/Hyphen (-) instead of Key/Space ( ).
          • My/Pref/Logseq/Namespaces/Should-be/Slugified/Phase/2-Create-Skill
            • convert certain namespaces "by hand," using individual case studies to create an AI/Agent/Skill to do the conversion reliably, including any irregularities to account for. beginning with namespaces that are pulled out into other gardens, such as GitP, Launchpad, Microfreak, Ableton/Live, etc.
          • My/Pref/Logseq/Namespaces/Should-be/Slugified/Phase/3-Scheduled-Task
            • create a scheduled task to identify a noncompliant page or namespace and change it with the skill

2026-11-05 Thu

  • My Thoughts

    • Cal Newport's Person/Cal Newport/Pod/26/10 Do Hard Things retells Jerry Seinfeld's answer to the McKinsey question: "Are they funny?" I take Newport's point to be that efficiency can undermine comedy and other work whose value comes from hard thinking, and that AI tools can pull us toward busywork without our noticing.
      • I remembered the story as coming from Acquired/Pod/26/10 Seinfeld. Acquired read it aloud, but it comes from Seinfeld's 2017 Harvard Business Review interview, and the McKinsey question came from the interviewer.
    • Person/Nufar Gaspar's advice on Person/Nathaniel Whittemore/Pod/26/11 How to Deslopify Your AI fits with it: before opening an agentic tool, stop and decide what I want to do, then open it and give it the context it needs.
    • The question I want to discuss with colleagues is when the difficulty of a task is the point — learning, judgment, practice, writing, creative work — and when it is friction we should remove. Some difficulty is waste, and removing a barrier does not always remove the learning.
