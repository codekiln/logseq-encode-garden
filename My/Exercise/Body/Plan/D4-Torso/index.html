My/Exercise/Body/Plan/D4-Torso

  • Tuesday — Day 4: Torso

    • Plan on 60–80 minutes of work sets, rests included, after the warm-up. Warm up and choose loads per My/Exercise/Body/Plan, and finish with My/Exercise/Body/Plan/Mobility-Circuit if time allows.
    • Chest, back, and abdominals, using the exercises Huberman demonstrates in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles.
    • Chest gets one compound and one isolation exercise, and back two or three compound exercises. Where Huberman shows several options for the same job, pick one of them for the session.

    • Chest: two exercises, and an optional third

      • Compound — barbell bench press: 3 work sets of 6–10 reps

        • Set the rack safeties or use a spotter. When benching alone, dumbbells are the safer choice.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Barbell Bench Press

          • Barbell Bench Press

            • Practice a horizontal barbell press with a repeatable grip, stable support, and controlled bar path.
              • Day 4 at 0:42 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Barbell, Equipment/Exercise/Weight Bench, Equipment/Exercise/Power Rack

            • Setup

              • Set the bench and rack so you can unrack without reaching excessively. Use rack safeties or a capable spotter. Lie with feet planted, shoulders supported, and hands slightly wider than shoulder width; wrap the thumbs around the bar.

            • Practice

              • Lower the bar slowly toward the chest while keeping wrists stacked over forearms, then press upward. Keep the hips on the bench and avoid bouncing the bar off the chest.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

            • Cues and Variations

              • A machine chest press is a substitute when a suitable rack or spotter is unavailable. Start with a light bar to establish a repeatable grip and bar path.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's bench press, 0:07. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles. Additional instructions: ACE's barbell chest press.
        • Or one of the alternatives Huberman shows: Exercise/Body/Strength/Dumbbell Bench Press or Exercise/Body/Strength/Incline Bench Press.

      • Isolation — dumbbell fly: 2 work sets of 8–15 reps

        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Dumbbell Fly

          • Dumbbell Fly

            • Practice an arcing chest isolation movement with dumbbells and a fixed bend in the elbows.
              • Day 4 at 4:36 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Dumbbell, Equipment/Exercise/Weight Bench

            • Setup

              • Lie on a flat or slightly inclined bench with a dumbbell in each hand, feet planted and shoulder blades pulled down and back. Hold the weights above the chest, palms facing each other, elbows slightly bent.

            • Practice

              • Keeping the elbow bend fixed, lower the dumbbells in a wide arc until the upper arms reach about chest level or a comfortable stretch. Bring them back up along the same arc, as if hugging a large barrel.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Huberman suggests two to three work sets of 6 to 15 repetitions. Add load only while the elbow angle stays fixed.

            • Cues and Variations

              • If the elbows bend more as the weights come down, the fly has turned into a press; use lighter dumbbells. Huberman demonstrates a slight incline. At the end of a set, bring the weights together over the chest and sit up before setting them down rather than straining to lower them to the floor from the bottom position. A Exercise/Body/Strength/Cable Fly keeps tension through the whole movement.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's dumbbell fly, 4:20. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles. Additional instructions: ACE's lying chest fly.
        • Or the Exercise/Body/Strength/Cable Fly or a fly machine.

      • Optional third compound — dip: 2 work sets of 6–12 reps

        • Huberman does dips on chest and back day or on shoulders and arms day. They also work the triceps.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Dip

          • Dip

            • Practice lowering and pressing the body between parallel bars to train the chest, front and side shoulders, and triceps.
              • Day 4 at 3:48 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Dip Station

            • Setup

              • Grip parallel bars, jump or step up, and support yourself with straight arms and the shoulders pulled down away from the ears.

            • Practice

              • Lean the torso forward somewhat and bend the elbows to lower the body under control until the upper arms are about parallel to the floor or to the deepest point you control without shoulder pain. Press back up to straight arms. Keep the shoulder blades set back and down so the shoulders do not roll forward at the bottom.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, and rest follow the training plan. Once 10 to 15 bodyweight repetitions come easily, add load, for example a dumbbell held between the feet or a dip belt. Huberman calls the weighted dip an upper body squat.

            • Cues and Variations

              • A more forward lean emphasizes the chest; a more upright torso shifts work toward the triceps. Use an assisted dip machine or a band for support until full bodyweight repetitions are possible. Stop the set if the front of the shoulder hurts at the bottom.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's dips, 3:15. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles.

    • Back: three compound exercises

      • Back takes in the lats, the traps, and the lower back. Huberman trains it with compound exercises only, two or three of them.

      • Compound pull — pull-up or lat pulldown: 3 work sets of 6–10 reps

        • Once you can do 10–12 pull-ups in good form without hitting failure, start adding weight.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Pull-Up

          • Pull-Up

            • Practice pulling the body toward an overhead bar through a controlled hanging range.
              • Day 4 at 6:42 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Pull-Up Bar, Equipment/Exercise/Power Rack

            • Setup

              • Use an overhand grip on a secure bar and begin with the arms extended in a comfortable hanging position.

            • Practice

              • Pull the chest toward the bar by driving the elbows down and back, then lower under control. Keep the movement free of swinging or kicking.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

            • Cues and Variations

              • Use an assisted pull-up machine or another controlled assistance method when needed for the intended range. Reduce the added load or increase assistance if the movement becomes a swing.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's pull-up, 6:13. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Lat Pulldown

          • Lat Pulldown

            • Practice pulling an overhead cable handle toward the chest with a steady supported torso.
              • Day 4 at 7:18 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Lat Pulldown Machine

            • Setup

              • Secure the thighs under the machine pad and begin with the arms extended and the handle held securely.

            • Practice

              • Pull the handle toward the upper chest, then let the arms extend slowly. Keep the torso mostly upright and pull in front of the head.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

            • Cues and Variations

              • Choose a load that permits the whole intended range without swinging backward. A close underhand grip is the variation Huberman demonstrates; a comfortable neutral or overhand grip is another option when the equipment permits it.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's pulldown, 6:56. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles.

      • Compound row — dumbbell row: 2 work sets of 6–10 reps per side

        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Dumbbell Row

          • Dumbbell Row

            • Practice a one-arm horizontal pull with a dumbbell while a bench supports the opposite hand and knee.
              • Day 4 at 8:06 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Dumbbell, Equipment/Exercise/Weight Bench

            • Setup

              • Hold a dumbbell in one hand. Rest the other hand and the knee on that same side on the bench, with the bench hand under the shoulder and the knee under the hip. Brace the core, keep the back flat, and let the weighted arm hang.

            • Practice

              • Lift the weight slightly first to feel the target muscles engage. Row the dumbbell up by driving the elbow back close to the side, stopping before the torso has to twist. Lower slowly, and set the weight down at the end of the set rather than dropping it.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

            • Cues and Variations

              • Reduce the load if the torso rotates or the back rounds to finish a repetition. Barbell and machine rows train the same pattern. Huberman uses compound pulls and rows with progressive overload for the back and skips isolation movements.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's dumbbell row, 7:43. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles. Additional instructions: ACE's single-arm row.
        • Or the barbell or machine rows Huberman names. He names the Exercise/Body/Strength/Shrug as an option for the traps.

      • Lower back — back extension: 2 work sets of 8–15 reps

        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Back Extension

          • Back Extension

            • Practice lowering and raising the torso from a hip-hinged position on a hyperextension bench to train the lower back and hip extensors.
              • Day 4 at 10:30 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Back Extension Bench
              • Day 4 at 11:24 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Reverse Hyperextension Machine

            • Setup

              • Adjust a hyperextension (45-degree or horizontal) bench so the pad sits just below the hip crease and the heels are secured under the ankle rollers. Start with the body in a straight line, hands crossed on the chest or behind the head.

            • Practice

              • Bend at the hips to lower the torso slowly toward the floor, then raise it back until the body is in a straight line again. Stop at a straight line rather than arching the lower back past it. Each repetition is slow and controlled.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Huberman suggests 8 to 25 controlled repetitions per set and does about 100 repetitions a week. Add load by holding a plate or dumbbell at the chest or by anchoring a band under the bench.

            • Cues and Variations

              • Reduce the range or remove the added load if the movement turns into a swing or the lower back pinches at the top. Without a machine, hold the edge of a high countertop or sturdy table with the torso supported and lift the legs behind you.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's back hyperextension, 9:53. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles.
        • Without a bench, Huberman lifts his legs while holding a high countertop.

    • Abdominals and core

      • Hanging leg raise: 2–3 sets

        • Progress from knee raises to the L-sit to pikes, as on the exercise page.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Hanging Leg Raise

          • Hanging Leg Raise

            • Practice raising the legs while hanging from a bar to train the abdominals and grip together.
              • Day 4 at 12:36 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Pull-Up Bar
              • Day 4 at 13:30 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Pull-Up Bar

            • Setup

              • Hang from a secure pull-up bar with an overhand grip, arms straight, and the shoulders engaged rather than slumped toward the ears. Let the body settle so it is not swinging.

            • Practice

              • Brace the abdominals and lift the legs in front of you without swinging, then lower them slowly to a still hang before the next repetition. The progression stage sets how high and how straight the legs go.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Huberman's progression has three stages:
                • 1. Knee raise: bend the knees and lift them until the thighs are level with the hips, at 90 degrees.
                • 2. L-sit: lift straight legs to horizontal and hold the L position.
                • 3. Pike: lift straight legs all the way up toward the bar.
              • His goal is three sets of five pikes. Move to the next stage once the current one is controlled for the planned repetitions.

            • Cues and Variations

              • If the body swings, pause in a still hang between repetitions or return to the previous stage. Grip often fails before the abdominals at first; that is part of the training effect Huberman describes. Straps or a captain's-chair station with forearm pads take grip out of the movement.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's hanging core progression, 11:49. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles.
