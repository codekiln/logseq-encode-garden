My/Exercise/Body/Plan/D7-Shoulders-and-Arms

  • Friday — Day 7: Shoulders and Arms

    • Plan on 60–80 minutes of work sets, rests included, after the warm-up; trim the optional forearm, neck, and calf work first when time is short. Warm up and choose loads per My/Exercise/Body/Plan, and finish with My/Exercise/Body/Plan/Mobility-Circuit.
    • Shoulders, biceps, triceps, and abdominals, using the exercises Huberman demonstrates in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms, plus the face pull and palms-facing pull-up the book asks for on this day.
    • Each muscle group gets one compound and one isolation exercise; shoulders add a rear-delt exercise. Where Huberman shows several options for the same job, pick one of them for the session.

    • Shoulders: three exercises

      • Compound — overhead press: 3 work sets of 6–10 reps

        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Overhead Press

          • Overhead Press

            • Practice pressing a load from shoulder height overhead while controlling the trunk.
              • Day 7 at 0:36 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Barbell, Equipment/Exercise/Power Rack

            • Setup

              • Begin with the bar at the front of the shoulders, or dumbbells at shoulder height. Brace your trunk and keep the ribs over the pelvis. A seated bench with back support is an alternative to standing.

            • Practice

              • Press overhead through a comfortable range, allowing the head to move just enough for the bar to pass. Lower slowly to the start. Keep the forearms underneath the load.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

            • Cues and Variations

              • Reduce the load if you need to lean backward or turn the lift into a push with the legs. A machine press or supported dumbbell press can simplify balance while you practice.

            • Sources

              • Demonstrations: barbell press, 0:11 and dumbbell press, 0:52. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms.
        • Or the Exercise/Body/Strength/Dumbbell Overhead Press Huberman shows. He also names the Exercise/Body/Strength/Machine Shoulder Press.

      • Isolation — lateral raise: 2 work sets of 8–15 reps

        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Lateral Raise

          • Lateral Raise

            • Practice raising light dumbbells out to the sides to train the side deltoids.
              • Day 7 at 2:46 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Dumbbell

            • Setup

              • Stand holding a light dumbbell in each hand at your sides; some people need as little as 5 pounds to keep the movement controlled.

            • Practice

              • Raise the dumbbells out and slightly in front of the body to about shoulder height, keeping the thumbs level with or a little higher than the pinkies. Lower slowly.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

            • Cues and Variations

              • Reduce the load if you swing the torso or shrug to lift. Experiment with the path: some people feel the side deltoids best straight out to the side, others slightly in front of the body.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's lateral raise, 2:22. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms.

      • Rear delts — face pull: 2 work sets of 12–15 reps

        • Use a cable machine or exercise band. Or the Exercise/Body/Strength/Rear Delt Fly Huberman demonstrates, for 8–15 reps.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Face Pull

          • Face Pull

            • Practice pulling a cable to face height into external rotation to train the rotator cuff, rear deltoids, and upper back.

            • Setup

              • Set the pulley at face height and attach two ropes so the hands can travel wide enough to reach 90 degrees at the elbows. Grip the ropes underhand with the thumbs pointing back.

            • Practice

              • Pull toward the face while rotating the forearms up and back, ending in a double-biceps position with the elbows at 90 degrees and the thumbs pointing behind you. Squeeze the shoulder blades together, hold, and return under control.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Jeff Cavaliere suggests two or three sets to failure, two or three times a week at the end of a workout, after one light set to rehearse the pattern. A few plates on the stack is enough load.

            • Cues and Variations

              • An overhand grip or pulling the hands straight to the face leaves the shoulder internally rotated and removes the rotator cuff work. A single rope is too narrow to reach the 90-degree angle. Open the elbows a little wider if the rear deltoids aren't working.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Jeff Cavaliere's face pull setup, 1:51. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/05/Jeff Cavaliere Shows Andrew Huberman the Face Pull.

    • Biceps: two exercises

      • Compound — palms-facing pull-up: 2 work sets of 6–10 reps

        • The book suggests palms-facing pull-ups on this day as compound work for the biceps.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Chin-Up

          • Chin-Up

            • Practice pulling the body toward an overhead bar with the palms facing you, a compound pull that works the biceps along with the lats.
            • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Pull-Up Bar, Equipment/Exercise/Power Rack

            • Setup

              • Take an underhand grip on a secure bar, palms facing you and hands about shoulder width apart, and begin from a comfortable hang with the arms extended.

            • Practice

              • Pull the chest toward the bar by driving the elbows down toward the ribs, then lower under control to the full hang. Keep the movement free of swinging or kicking.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Once the planned repetitions come with consistent control, add resistance gradually.

            • Cues and Variations

              • The palms-away grip of the Exercise/Body/Strength/Pull-Up shifts more of the work to the lats; an underhand, close-grip Exercise/Body/Strength/Lat Pulldown trains a similar pull for anyone not yet able to lift their bodyweight.

            • Sources

              • Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols suggests palms-facing pull-ups on the shoulders-and-arms day as compound work for the biceps.
              • In Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles, Huberman names chins with pull-downs and rows as the compound exercises for the lats.

      • Isolation — biceps curl: 2 work sets of 8–15 reps

        • Dumbbells, both arms together or alternating. Or the Exercise/Body/Strength/Preacher Curl Huberman shows.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Biceps Curl

          • Biceps Curl

            • Practice elbow flexion against resistance with a steady upper arm and controlled wrist.
              • Day 7 at 5:42 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Dumbbell

            • Setup

              • Stand or sit with a dumbbell in each hand, arms down, and trunk steady. Curl by bending the elbows; turn the palms upward if comfortable, then lower slowly.

            • Practice

              • Keep the upper arms mostly still and the wrists aligned with the forearms. Reduce the load if you must swing the torso or move the elbows far forward to finish.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

            • Cues and Variations

              • A seated curl or cable curl can make the starting position easier to repeat. An EZ-bar is another grip option.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's curl, 4:44. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms.

    • Triceps: two exercises

      • Tuesday's dips, on a day you do them, also work the triceps.

      • Compound — overhead cable triceps extension: 2–3 work sets of 6–12 reps

        • Huberman treats this as the compound triceps movement.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Overhead Cable Triceps Extension

          • Overhead Cable Triceps Extension

            • Practice extending the elbows overhead against a cable to stretch and train the long head of the triceps.
              • Day 7 at 8:12 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Cable Machine

            • Setup

              • Set the cable above and behind you, face away from the stack, and hold the handles behind the head. Step far enough from the machine to feel a stretch on the triceps at the start. Lock the lats and brace the core.

            • Practice

              • Extend the elbows to press the handles forward and overhead, like a soccer throw-in, then return to the stretched position. The movement is mostly at the elbow; as the set gets harder, let the shoulders come back a little while keeping the stretch.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Huberman usually does three sets to or near failure. Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

            • Cues and Variations

              • Reduce the load if the trunk arches or the stretch disappears. Exercise/Body/Strength/Triceps Pressdown trains the triceps with the arms at the sides.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's overhead cable triceps extension, 7:40. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms.

      • Isolation — triceps pressdown: 2 work sets of 8–15 reps

        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Triceps Pressdown

          • Triceps Pressdown

            • Practice elbow extension against resistance from a high cable pulley.
              • Day 7 at 8:48 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Cable Machine

            • Setup

              • Attach a rope or bar to a high cable pulley. Stand with feet planted and knees softly bent, elbows beside the torso, and hands holding the attachment securely.

            • Practice

              • Extend the elbows to press the attachment down, then bend them slowly to return. Keep the upper arms in place and the shoulders quiet.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

            • Cues and Variations

              • Avoid using bodyweight to shove the handle downward. Reduce the load if the elbows drift forward or the torso rocks. This is an elbow-extension exercise; Huberman's overhead cable extension uses a different shoulder position.

            • Sources

              • Source: Mayo Clinic's Strength Training for Adults, triceps pushdown.
              • Demonstration: Huberman's triceps isolation, 8:43. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms.

    • Abdominals and core

      • Hanging leg raise: 2 sets

        • The week's second core session, at the progression stage reached on Tuesday.
        • Exercise/Body/Strength/Hanging Leg Raise

          • Hanging Leg Raise

            • Practice raising the legs while hanging from a bar to train the abdominals and grip together.
              • Day 4 at 12:36 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Pull-Up Bar
              • Day 4 at 13:30 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles
              • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Pull-Up Bar

            • Setup

              • Hang from a secure pull-up bar with an overhand grip, arms straight, and the shoulders engaged rather than slumped toward the ears. Let the body settle so it is not swinging.

            • Practice

              • Brace the abdominals and lift the legs in front of you without swinging, then lower them slowly to a still hang before the next repetition. The progression stage sets how high and how straight the legs go.

            • Dose and Progression

              • Huberman's progression has three stages:
                • 1. Knee raise: bend the knees and lift them until the thighs are level with the hips, at 90 degrees.
                • 2. L-sit: lift straight legs to horizontal and hold the L position.
                • 3. Pike: lift straight legs all the way up toward the bar.
              • His goal is three sets of five pikes. Move to the next stage once the current one is controlled for the planned repetitions.

            • Cues and Variations

              • If the body swings, pause in a still hang between repetitions or return to the previous stage. Grip often fails before the abdominals at first; that is part of the training effect Huberman describes. Straps or a captain's-chair station with forearm pads take grip out of the movement.

            • Sources

              • Demonstration: Huberman's hanging core progression, 11:49. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles.

    • Optional forearms: 3–4 sets of 12–30 reps

      • Flexor and extensor movements from Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/18/12/The Perfect Forearm Workout: Exercise/Body/Strength/Prone Wrist Curl, Exercise/Body/Strength/Reverse Curl, Exercise/Body/Strength/Reverse Wrist Roll, Exercise/Body/Strength/Wrist Deviation, Exercise/Body/Strength/Forearm Rotation, Exercise/Body/Strength/Farmer's Carry, or Exercise/Body/Strength/Dead Hang.

    • Optional neck

      • The book lists neck work as optional on this day too. Huberman points to Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/16/11/Neck Exercises that Kill Your Neck: Exercise/Body/Strength/Plate Neck Raise and Exercise/Body/Strength/Chin Tuck.

    • Optional standing calf raise: 2 work sets of 8–15 reps

      • Huberman does calf raises twice a week, one of those days being leg day. Include it if Sunday's work has recovered.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Standing Calf Raise

        • Standing Calf Raise

          • Practice raising and lowering the heels against resistance through a controlled ankle range.
            • Day 2 at 11:36 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles
            • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Angled Calf Raise Machine
            • Day 2 at 13:00 in Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles
            • Equipment: Equipment/Exercise/Dumbbell

          • Setup

            • Use a calf-raise machine or stand on a stable, nonslip step with light hand support. Place the balls of the feet securely on the surface and begin with the knees straight but not forced backward.

          • Practice

            • Lower the heels through a comfortable stretch, pause, then rise as high as you can without bouncing. Keep the ankles controlled and the same foot position throughout.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Begin on both feet with bodyweight if needed. A single-leg version increases the challenge; a dumbbell can add resistance once balance and range are consistent. Shorten the range if the Achilles or ankle is uncomfortable.

          • Sources

            • Demonstrations: Huberman's calf raise, 10:32 and single-leg version, 12:26. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles.
