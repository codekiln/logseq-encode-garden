    • Banning AI from using specific words or phrases

      • I'd like to make a system that makes it very easy for me to ban AI from using words and phrases with me or when creating documents.
        • My first thought about this was to make an AI/Agent/Skill in rulesync which would let me quickly add a word or phrase to a part of the knowledge garden that catalogs the words or phrases that I'd like AI to avoid when communicating with me or on my behalf, following progressive disclosure.         • Later, I was thinking of using Claude/Code/Hooks for this.

    • Convert #tag links to [[wikilink]] form on commit

      • I'd like a pre-commit converter, built like the text:fix-invisible mise task, that rewrites Logseq #tag and #[[Page]] links into [[...]] form — one obvious way to link (Py/Zen of Python), and the shape Looksyk can import, since it reads [[...]] but not #tag.
      • Not as safe a rewrite as invisible whitespace, so it stays deferred. A # also begins headings, hex colors, shell comments, and URL fragments, so the converter has to skip fenced and inline code, ignore # before a space or another #, and treat # as a link only at a word boundary. Scope it to .md files under pages/ and journals/, where well over a thousand inline # links live today.

  • so much Word/Salad

    • ugh. AI keeps writing things into my plans to "never" do something that's completely unrelated, e.g. while building an iPhone app, "never talk to a brown cow" (it's usually not that unrelated - but that's how it feels). So often, AI expressions collapse into an always/never binary.
    • so many double negatives.

    • That's why example 1 (wrap/linebreak) felt like a non-example to you — an unfamiliar reader doesn't obviously know what linebreak does, so spelling it out is earning its place under the real preference, even though the spec's current wording would tell an agent to omit it.

    • regarding My/AI/Rule/No Recipe in the Cake - new My/AI/Rule/Prune useless commandments
      • ❯ in what universe does this make sense?

  > ### Requirement: Link rationale is not narrated into the config

  > A config comment carries the garden link. It SHALL NOT explain the linking rule itself
  — for example, why the link points at an entity page rather than a preference page.
  Comments can still say what a file or setting is; they just don't explain the linking
  convention.


  to me, this is just terrible. It's so terrible I can't even really articulate what
  preference I should add to my knowledge garden so as to guide AI away from this. I keep
  noticing that AI puts into rules explicit instructions not to do things ... that it
  should be obvious one should never do. Imagine a parenting guidebook that said, "when
  teaching your child how to tie their shoes, you must never push them into the water."
  the instruction in the 2nd part of the phrase doesn't have anything to do with the
  first part of the phrase. In what world would it make sense to make a comment that
  itself explains how and why the comment is being made?

⏺ You've named the actual disease, not just a symptom. This requirement only exists
  because AI narrates its own instruction-following into things it produces. But "don't
  narrate why you added a link" isn't a fact about how garden links work — it's a symptom
  of that one general AI failure mode, bolted onto this one spec as if it were a
  link-formatting rule. Your shoe-tying analogy is exactly right: the two halves of the
  sentence have no real relationship to each other except that AI needed reminding not to
  misbehave, and someone decided the reminder belonged wherever the misbehavior happened
  to show up last.

  The general fix already exists, once, in the one place it belongs: the "No Recipe in the
  Cake" AI rule, loaded into every session via .claude/rules/. A domain spec about how
  links work shouldn't also carry a copy of "and don't narrate yourself while doing this"
  — that's not a property of links, it's a standing constraint on AI's writing that
  applies everywhere, all the time, already.

  I think the right move is to delete this requirement entirely from garden-entity-links

2026-06-27 Sat

  • The instant of installation is madness [1]

    • Q: Is there a 2026 install-time tool that tells a non-expert how much supply-chain risk they are adding, without handing them a pile of specialist audit tools?
    • This morning I noticed that hacker-news-tui published a Security/Attack/Chain/Supply policy and avoided extra dependencies (cool!). I was looking for Spotify TUI Options it turns out ncspot and spotify-player each bring in more than 500 dependencies throug Rust/crates (ugh!).
    • Asked ChatGPT whether there's a CLI that can help do security audits for packages. I was really thinking of something like Snyk, but I didn't say that because I was lazy. It suggested I roll my own AI skill using cargo audit, cargo deny, cargo geiger, cargo supply-chain, cargo vet, Semgrep, and CodeQL (ugh!)
      • IT pointed to arXiv/Article/26/02/Auditing Rust Crates Effectively for an idea of how a Cargo Scan tool can narrow down the scope of the source you need to read to validate Rust crates by looking for dangerous effects instead of only checking known vulnerabilities. That's cool, but not helpful.
    • So is there no CLI or AI agent skill that analyzes supply chain of a package and helps an end user evaluate the safety of a project?
    • It then strongly recommended something called https://agentaudit.dev/ that claims to be a security registry for AI agent packages, MCP servers, and AgentSkills. It has no visible public members, commits are attributed only to agentaudit-dev and Claude, has very few GitHub/Stars, and it's even unclear whether it does anything (https://agentaudit.dev/findings doesn't list anything). (ugh!)
    • As with many things with AI, this whole thing seems kafka-esque. People are worried about AI deepfakes. I'm more worried about how basically all software now is potentially vulnerable, and there's no easy way to even find out the risk profile of the software you want to use. So, what's to be done? It just seems so crazy that if I ask "testing framework XYZ" I get tons of great results (jest, cyprus, vitest, etc), but if I say "security testing framework XYZ" I get vaporware, roll-your-own, and requests convince your CSO -> CEO -> CFO to get an enterprise subscription for devops to be able to generate SBOMs for auditors.
    • I came across Security-Phoenix-demo/security-skills-claude-code which looks a bit more grounded in a dedicated provider: it is tied to Phoenix Security, which at least names people and links to LinkedIn profiles. The look and feel of the website has that real-ish cringe of security-consulting marketing at least. But there are so many products embedded that it's difficult to really see if it's useful; the Phoenix "Platform" consists of Orange, Purple, Blue, and Green products that may or may not have open source equivalents. (ugh!)

    • [1] This title riffs on Person/Jacques Derrida/Essay/63/Cogito and the History of Madness's quotation of Person/Søren Kierkegaard: "The instant of decision is madness."
