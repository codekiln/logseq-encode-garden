  • Choose Clip Gain or Mixer Track Volume and unlink it from the sample. The envelope's loop braces turn coloured and its own loop and region controls become active; the sample keeps looping while the envelope plays once.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/09 Compressor
  • Attack — time to reach full compression once over the threshold; Release — time to recover after the signal drops. Auto Release sets release from the material.
    • 10–50 ms of attack lets the start of each hit through and keeps it lively; very short attack can sound lifeless or buzzy.
    • Short release can cause pumping, sometimes used on drums on purpose.
    • The manual suggests practicing with a drum loop, adjusting Attack, Release, Threshold and Gain.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/10 Corpus/05 Sidechain Parameters
  • Off Decay — Note Off damps the resonance; its slider sets how much. At 0% only Decay matters, like a marimba or glockenspiel; at 100% Note Off cuts the sound at once.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/11 Delay
  • Jump — switches at once, possibly clicking
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/13 Dynamic Tube
  • A — clean at low Bias, adding bright harmonics once the input passes a threshold
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/15 EQ Eight

Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/26 Multiband Dynamics
  • Multiband Dynamics is a dynamics processor aimed at mastering. It compresses and expands, upward and downward, in up to three bands with adjustable crossovers and per-band envelopes. Each band has an upper and a lower threshold, so two kinds of processing can run in each band at once.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/26 Multiband Dynamics/01 Dynamics Processing Theory
  • With three bands and two thresholds each, one Multiband Dynamics can apply six kinds of processing at once.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/26 Multiband Dynamics/02 Interface and Controls
  • Above thresholds — Attack is the time to full effect once the signal rises past the threshold; Release the time to recover after it falls back
  • Below thresholds — Attack is the time to full effect once the signal drops below; Release the time to recover after it rises again
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/33 Roar/01 Input Section

Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/33 Roar/03 Modulation Section

Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/33 Roar/04 Feedback Section

Ableton/Live/UG/36 Push 2/08 Working with Samples
  • Simpler sets itself up from the sample's length: short samples play once, long ones loop and warp. Warped samples play at the Set tempo on any note, and warp settings from clips or files carry over; see Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping.
Ableton/Live/UG/36 Push 2/14 Clip Mode
  • The second upper button toggles Loop. On: Loop Position (where the loop starts), Loop Length, and Start Offset (where playback starts within the loop; it moves with Loop Position when they coincide). Off: Start and End of the region played once. Shift adjusts in 16ths.
Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources/03 The Overload Indicator
  • The Overload indicator in the Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar shows "Disk" when the disk falls behind and, once CPU warnings are on, "CPU" when the processor is overloaded.
Ableton/Live/UG/39 Audio Fact Sheet/02 Neutral Operations/03 Unstretched Beats-Tones-Texture-Re-Pitch Warping
  • Tempo changes stretch only while they last. Tracks recorded at 120 BPM play back neutrally again once the tempo returns to 120 BPM.
Ableton/Live/UG/39 Audio Fact Sheet/03 Non-Neutral Operations/04 Dithering
  • Any gain change in Live produces 32-bit audio. Apply dither only once; unless you master in Live, render at 32 bits and skip dither.
Ableton/Live/UG/39 Audio Fact Sheet/03 Non-Neutral Operations/10 Grooves
  • A warped clip at the Set's tempo normally plays neutrally, but once a groove is applied playback is non-neutral at every tempo.
Ableton/Live/UG/40 MIDI Fact Sheet/03 Live's MIDI Solutions
  • During audio dropouts Live still receives and records MIDI correctly, even if playthrough sounds wrong; playback is accurate once the system recovers.
Ableton/Manual/06/01 Arrangement View Navigation

  • To have the Arrangement display follow the song position and scroll automatically, turn on the Follow switch, or use the Follow command from the Options menu. Follow will pause if you make an edit or scroll the view horizontally in the Arrangement, or if you click on the beat-time ruler. Follow will start again once you stop or restart playback, or click in the Arrangement or clip scrub area. If you zoom the Arrangement while Follow is enabled, Live will zoom to the playhead.
