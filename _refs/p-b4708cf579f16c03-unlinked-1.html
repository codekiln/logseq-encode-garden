Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/07 Clip Gain and Pitch
  • Gain, in dB, sets clip gain; split handles show the range when several clips differ.
Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/02 External Audio In-Out/01 Mono-Stereo Conversions
  • Mono becomes stereo by copying the signal to both sides. Routing to a mono output sums left and right and lowers the result 6 dB to avoid clipping.
Ableton/Live/UG/18 Mixing/01 The Live Mixer/01 Additional Mixer Features
  • Live's 32-bit floating-point engine has so much headroom that tracks can go well above 0 dB without clipping. Levels over 0 dB only matter where audio leaves Live: physical inputs and outputs, the Main track, and files you save or export.
Ableton/Live/UG/24 Plug-Ins
  • Plug-ins installed or removed while Live runs appear after a restart, or after Rescan in the Plug-Ins Settings. Option/Alt+Rescan deletes the plug-in database and scans from scratch.
Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/03 Mixer and Device Clip Envelopes/01 Modulating Mixer Volumes and Sends
  • Send modulation is also a percentage: it can lower a send to −∞ dB but never past the Send knob's setting.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/02 Auto Filter/01 Filter Types
  • Low-pass — cuts above the cutoff; 12 or 24 dB slope
  • High-pass — cuts below the cutoff; 12 or 24 dB
  • Band-pass — passes a band around the cutoff; 12 or 24 dB
  • Notch — cuts a narrow band; 12 or 24 dB
  • Morph — sweeps from low-pass through band-pass to high-pass; 6, 12, 24 or 48 dB
  • Notch + LP — a notch plus a low-pass roll-off; 12 or 24 dB
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/04 Auto Shift/01 Input Section
  • Input Gain — −24 to +24 dB
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/07 Channel EQ
  • Low — ±15 dB low shelf at 100 Hz; the curve adapts to the amount of gain
  • Mid — ±12 dB sweepable peak; the slider above it sets the frequency, 120 Hz to 7.5 kHz
  • High — boosts up to 15 dB with a high shelf. Cutting also lowers a low-pass filter from 20 kHz toward 8 kHz as it goes to −15 dB.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/09 Compressor
  • Threshold sets where compression starts. Ratio sets how much: at 3, a 3 dB rise above the threshold comes out as 1 dB. A ratio of 1 does nothing.
  • The orange Gain Reduction meter shows the current reduction. The manual warns that more than about 6 dB changes the sound a lot and can flatten dynamics in a way that cannot be undone later — especially on the Main track.
  • Knee — how gradually compression starts. 0 dB is a hard knee, which can sound harsh at high ratios. A 10 dB knee with a −20 dB threshold starts gently at −30 dB and reaches full compression at −10 dB.
  • Expand — raises level above the threshold instead: at 1:2, each dB over becomes 2 dB. See Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/26 Multiband Dynamics for the types of dynamics processing.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/15 EQ Eight
  • Each band's chooser offers, top to bottom: low cut (48 or 12 dB/octave), low shelf, peak, notch, high shelf, high cut (12 or 48 dB/octave). Each band has an on/off switch; turn unused bands off to save CPU. Stacking identical bands gives very steep filtering.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/16 EQ Three
  • Each band's gain goes from −∞ to +6 dB, so a bass line or kick can be removed entirely.
  • An LED per band lights when there is signal in it above −24 dB, even when the band is off.
  • 24 dB / 48 dB — crossover steepness; 48 dB is steeper and uses more CPU.
  • The filters are made to sound like a powerful analog filter cascade, so 48 dB mode colors the sound slightly even with every band at 0 dB. For more neutral behavior use 24 dB mode or Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/15 EQ Eight.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/19 Filter Delay
  • Volume per delay, up to +6 dB, makes up for heavy filtering.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/20 Gate
  • Floor — how much a closed gate turns the signal down; −∞ dB mutes, 0 dB does nothing
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/21 Glue Compressor
  • Range — limits how much compression can happen. −60 to −70 dB behaves like the original hardware; −40 to −15 dB works as an alternative to Dry/Wet; 0 dB does nothing.
  • Soft clip — a fixed waveshaper capping output at −0.5 dB to catch loud transients. It colors and distorts the sound, so the manual suggests leaving it off unless that is wanted. With Oversampling, peaks can still pass 0 dB.
  • The needle shows gain reduction in dB. The Clip LED turns red above 0 dB, or yellow when Soft clip is catching peaks.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/21 Glue Compressor/01 Sidechain Parameters
  • Oversampling — processes at twice the sample rate to reduce aliasing and harsh transients, for slightly more CPU. Output may then exceed 0 dB even with Soft clip on.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/23 Hybrid Reverb/04 Algorithmic Reverb Engine
  • Decay — time for the tail to fall by 60 dB
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/23 Hybrid Reverb/05 EQ Section
  • Four bands. The low and high bands switch between pass filters (6 to 96 dB/octave) and shelves; the two middle bands are peaks covering the whole range, from wide boosts to fairly narrow cuts.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/24 Limiter
  • Anything after Limiter can raise the level again. To be sure the output never clips, put Limiter last on the Main track and keep the Main volume below 0 dB.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/26 Multiband Dynamics/02 Interface and Controls
  • In the display, large bars show each band's output and small bars its input; with no processing the input meters line up with the tops of the output meters. The scale is in dB.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/28 Pedal/01 Pedal Tips
  • Fuzz, Gain 50%, Sub off; Bass and Mid at −100%, Mid Frequency to taste, Treble 100%; Output −20 dB. Then raise Dry/Wet from 0% until the drums fizz enough.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/32 Reverb/05 Global Settings
  • Decay — time for the tail to fall by 60 dB
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/34 Saturator
  • Bass Shaper — like Analog Clip with smoother harmonics on heavily driven lows; made for 808 kicks and synth bass. Bass Shaper Threshold (0 to −50 dB) goes from soft clipping (low values, best for bass with high Drive) to hard clipping (high values).
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/38 Spectrum
  • Spectrum analyzes frequencies in real time and draws level (dB, vertical) against frequency or pitch (horizontal). Peaks stay on the graph until the song restarts. It measures only and never changes the sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/40 Utility
  • Gain — −∞ to +35 dB. Handy for automating fades while leaving the track's Volume for mixing. Arrow keys step 1 dB from −18 to +35 dB and speed up below −18 dB.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/03 Noise Generator
  • White noise through its own -6 dB/octave low-pass filter, with an on switch and level.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/03 Drift/02 Oscillator Section
  • With filtering on, raising gain drives the filter's two saturation points, before and after the filter, for analog-style distortion: the first engages above the default -6.0 dB, the second above 0.0 dB.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/03 Drift/03 Filter Section
  • Freq — low-pass cutoff. Type — Type I, 12 dB/octave DFM-1 that feeds distortion back internally, from gentle sweeps to warm drive; or Type II, 24 dB/octave Cytomic MS2, a Sallen-Key design with soft clipping that limits resonance.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/04 Drum Sampler/01 Sample Controls Section
  • Sample Gain — -70 to 24 dB.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/04 Drum Sampler/03 Filter Section
  • Filter On and four types: 12 dB low-pass, 24 dB low-pass, 24 dB high-pass and peak. Low- and high-pass have Resonance and Frequency; peak has Gain and Frequency.
