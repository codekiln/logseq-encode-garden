Instrument/Ondes Martenot
  • The Ondes Martenot is an early electronic musical instrument invented in 1928 by French cellist and radio engineer Maurice Martenot. Known for its ethereal, voice-like timbre, it became a favorite among avant-garde composers and remains one of the most expressive electronic instruments of the 20th century.

Microfreak/UG/02 Installation/04 Precautions

Page reference

  • Read and understand all instructions. Follow the instructions printed on the instrument.
  • Keep the instrument away from water and moisture. Place it somewhere stable where it cannot fall.
  • Do not put heavy objects on the instrument or block its ventilation openings. Keep it away from heat vents and places with poor air circulation.
  • Do not open the instrument or insert objects into it; this can cause fire or electric shock. Do not spill liquid on it.
  • If the instrument malfunctions, take it to a qualified service center. Opening and removing the cover voids the warranty; improper testing may cause electric shock or other malfunctions.
  • Do not use the instrument during thunderstorms, in hot sunlight, or near a gas leak.

Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc
  • The Digital Oscillator is the very heart of the MicroFreak. It's the digital circuit that generates the core sound of this instrument. The other parts of the MicroFreak - the Analog Filter, the Envelopes, and the LFO - exist only to shape/mangle/wobble the sound of the Digital Oscillator.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/05 KarplusStr
  • Description: Karplus-Strong is a sound synthesis method developed by Kevin Karplus and Alex Strong at Stanford University. It creates realistic drum and plucked-string sounds by looping a short noise burst through a filtered delay. This physical modeling approach recreates an instrument's physical characteristics digitally, such as string bow position, striking force, and the materials' diffusion and damping.
  • In physical modeling, an exciter creates vibrations in a resonator. The exciter can be a bow or a strike, and the resonator can emulate many instrument shapes.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/12 Modal
  • Description: A modal resonator imitates how an object's shape amplifies some harmonics and dampens others when it vibrates. This lets it mimic instrument bodies such as woodwinds, strings, and drums. It needs an exciter signal to produce sound, as a real instrument needs a bow, drumstick, or breath. Its harmonic structure and the instrument shape it mimics can change as a knob turns or a Matrix source such as the LFO or Envelope modulates it.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/01 What an Envelope Does
  • An instrument's envelope and timbre help make it recognizable. An organ reaches full volume at once, stays there while a key is held, then decays quickly. A piano has a slower attack and longer decay. A string section builds and fades more gradually.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard
  • A capacitive keyboard makes the player part of its electrical circuit. Changing how much skin touches a key changes its resistance and the pressure signal it sends. Finger contact alone can reach about 30% of the pressure range; pressing more of the finger onto the key can reach 100%. This touch response helps make the MicroFreak a performance instrument.
Microfreak/UG/14 Config/02 MIDI Control Center/01 Device Tab
  • Local control: With Local Off, the keyboard and panel controls send MIDI but do not directly play the MicroFreak. In a DAW, this lets the selected track route MIDI back to the MicroFreak or to another instrument. The MicroFreak can also play recorded MIDI while its keyboard controls another instrument.
  • Arp/Seq MIDI out: On or Off. Sends arpeggiator and sequencer notes to another instrument or a DAW.
