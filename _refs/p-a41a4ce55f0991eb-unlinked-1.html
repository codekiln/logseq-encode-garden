  • The concept of context rot has really come to shape my thoughts about AI assisted dev recently. It's given me a word to apply to that all-too-familiar feeling of when, after the LLM has started to work on a coding task for a while, ingesting the context of the codebase and the back-and forth, it stops adhering to prompting guidelines (CursorAI/.cursor/rules, Claude/Code/Claude.md files, etc). As you add tokens, it gradually fills up the "short term memory" in such a way that decreases the amount of "attention" it can spend on a task. By the time you get to 50% of the context window, the amount of "space" the LLM has to reason is often too small for the level of accuracy needed in coding.This is why it's not currently a viable technique to just say "go do this" when working on AI coding. Instead, one should break the task into a plan and then into steps that can each be achieved with a certain margin of error, what I'll call the "context buffer," the unused context which is like "space" that allows the model to actually "think."   • Prompt Engineering

    • Reddit/User/SaulFontaine says that there is an order to the prompting components for AI/LLMs, and that later prompts that try to modify behavior can be "shadowed" by earlier prompts. They are specifically referring to OpenAI here.

      • Here is roughly how the pieces get stitched together from highest to lowest priority:

        • 1 - OpenAI's built-in system prompt ("You are ChatGPT, a large language model trained by OpenAI...")

        • 2 - Customer-supplied system message (if you are using the API and inject one)

        • 3 - Developer message(s) (the instructions the product team gave)

        • 4 - Memory/context layers (persistent memories, user bio)

        • 5 - User-preference prompts (your typography and style rules)

        • 6 - Recent conversation turn(s)

        • 7 - Current user query

        • 8 - OpenAI's rollout-level "finalizing" instructions and decoding defaults

      • It looks like the typography rules you supply at step 5 can get overshadowed by the built-in system prompt at step 1 or by step 8's decoding defaults (where smart-quote normalization lives). To permanently override that you need to elevate your ASCII-only rule into the very first system message. For example:

      • System message: All output must use only ASCII punctuation. Do not apply any smart-quote or typographic substitutions. Replace any U+2018, U+2019, U+201C or U+201D characters with the ASCII single quote (') or double quote (") before finalizing output.

AI/Agent/Agentic/Engineer/ing/Q/What is fait accompli and amor fati, how are these related if at all, and why are more and more podcasts related to agentic engineering talking about these terms?

  • What is fait accompli and amor fati, how are these related if at all, and why are more and more podcasts related to Agentic Engineering talking about these terms?

    • AI Answer

      • Short answer: Fait accompli is a French phrase for an irreversible done deal — something already decided or executed, so later objection is mostly theater. Amor fati is Latin for "love of fate" — a Stoic / Nietzschean stance of actively affirming what has happened (and what you cannot control), not merely enduring it. They are related as situation vs stance: a fait accompli is the irreversible fact; amor fati is one response to living with irreversible facts. Agentic-engineering discourse leans on both because agents keep producing irreversible outcomes faster than humans can deliberate, and the cultural shock of that speed needs a vocabulary for both the done deal and how to live with it.

      • Fait accompli

        • Literal sense: "accomplished fact" — a state of affairs already brought about, so negotiation after the fact is weak.
        • In agent workflows it names the failure mode where the model plans or implements inside its opaque loop and returns a finished artifact (diff, deployed change, inferred action) before a human formed or approved intent. Reviewing afterward is arguing with a document; the decision already happened.
        • Typical mitigations people contrast with that pattern: approve scope/spec before build, plan mode as a reviewable contract, issue-first "no code until the design is written," hard gates / human-in-the-loop at irreversible seams — steer intent instead of auditing a fait accompli.
        • External framing of the pattern: When AI Acts Before You Decide, Verifying AI-generated code: evidence, not vibes, Claude Code: We've Got Issues.

      • Amor fati

        • Classical sense (Stoic lineage; Nietzsche's sharper formulation): do not only tolerate necessity — love it. Affirm what is, including what hurts or upends prior identity, without resenting reality for refusing your preferred script.
        • Stoic and Nietzschean takes diverge on what "fate" is (rational cosmos vs chaotic flux), but popular usage collapses them into: accept what you cannot control; put energy into judgment, craft, and response.
        • In the agentic era it is often applied to industry-scale fate: models will keep advancing; nobody is fully in charge of the curve; clinging to hand-crafted identity or a paused timeline breeds misery. Person/DHH's Singularity & Serenity and his Lex Fridman Podcast #501 both treat amor fati as the liberating frame for loving the jump in capability — including the irony that messy Linux surfaces become agent-friendly — rather than resenting the end of a prior craft identity.
        • Adjacent engineering metaphor: treat failures and interruptions as expected fate and design for idempotency / reroute / learn from the sprint outcome (Amor Fati in agile; Stoic Unix / Amor Fati → idempotency).

      • How they relate

        • Not synonyms. Fait accompli names an external irreversible state (often unwanted when it short-circuits consent). Amor fati names an internal affirmative posture toward necessity.
        • Sequence that shows up in practice: agent (or market) produces a fait accompli → operators either (a) fight the already-done outcome with delayed review, or (b) redesign process so irreversible steps require prior consent, and/or (c) practice amor fati toward the larger shift they cannot undo (agents writing most of the code; singularity-shaped uncertainty).
        • One French essay on Nietzsche puts the temporal cut cleanly: amor fati loves what has already taken place (the accomplished fact) while still willing a different tomorrow — love of the past as material, not passivity about the future (AMOR FATI, le grand oui à la vie).

      • Why agentic-engineering podcasts keep saying these words

        • Speed creates accomplished facts. Coding agents collapse "decide → implement → review" into long autonomous runs; hosts need a short phrase for "the work already happened without a real decision point" → fait accompli.
        • Identity shock needs a philosophy, not only a workflow tip. When influential practitioners ([Person/DHH], others on the Lex / agentic circuit) describe loving the upheaval instead of mourning hand-crafted exclusivity, amor fati travels as memeable Stoicism for the paradigm shift — paired with Serenity-Prayer / dichotomy-of-control language.
        • Mimetic amplification. Once a high-signal episode (e.g. Lex #501 with DHH on Agentic Engineering vs vibe coding, Omarchy, and amor fati) frames the emotional problem of the era, adjacent shows and essays reuse the same Latin/French tags because they compress a shared felt experience: irreversible agent output + irreversible industry change.
        • Practical dual use. Governance writing uses fait accompli as a warning (don't let the agent decide first). Culture writing uses amor fati as a prescription (don't waste life resenting the curve). Same discourse community; complementary jobs.

AI/Coding/Concept/Balancing Agentic and DIY

  • The importance of #Balancing DIY Software Engineering and AI Coding

    • AI Development Phases and Building the Coding Engine

      • While working on AI/Coding/Technique/Phased Planning/ai-coding dir, had a thought about AI Software Engineering
        • Feels like building an engine—a coding engine. Software/Engineering is now the act of building and tuning a specific coding engine, tailored to a specific domain or problem.
          • The engine consists of prompts meant to be invoked at specific phases in the software development lifecycle.
          • If done well, it could function like a checklist.
            • This could increase quality and consistency. Book/The Checklist Manifesto.

    • Traversal and Resolution in Learning

      • Driving everywhere limits the ability of the traveler to notice details in the landscape.
      • Slower traversals increase the resolution of time, life, and awareness.
      • Agentic coding feels like driving—accelerated but less granular.
      • Before motor cars became common, people walked and biked more.
      • After cars, personal exploration of the landscape diminished.
      • Coding is approaching a similar shift, where implementation is rapid but potentially less thoughtful.

    • What is the right ratio of AI:DIY coding?

      • Implementing something from scratch yields a more detailed mental map.
      • Classical Software Engineering fosters struggle, which often leads to better abstractions and simplifications.
        • Without this struggle or the signal of felt pain, one may not recognize patterns that lead to cleaner designs.
      • It's not a fixed thing. It changes with the season, the repo, the project, the issue.
      • Cultivate mindfulness to tune into the signal of when the fulcrum needs to shift

AI/Coding/History
  • AI/Coding/History/Phase/5 - Context Engineering
    • After agentic coding became the dominant paradigm, it took a while for engineers to understand how to let the agentic coding tools take actions in outside of the local filesystem, such as checking CI logs, reading JIRA or GitHub tickets, or opening PRs. After the introduction of MCP made this possible, there was a period of time where developers struggled to understand how agentic tools quickly filled up the AI/Context/Window and led to Context Rot. The term Context Engineering emerged as a way to signal the importance for minimizing the context needed to solve the problem at hand while still providing access to powerful tools.
    • Sub-Phases
      • AI/Coding/History/Phase/5 - Context Engineering/MCP Servers
        • After March 2025, many IDEs such as CursorAI and some CLIs such as Claude/Code implemented an MCP/Client into the agentic coding chat interface, so developers could give AI MCP Servers to which enabled them to ask the AI to both sense and act on external context, for example
          • GitHub/MCP included the ability open and update PRs, to read CICD logs and GitHub/Issues
          • Atlassian/MCP included the ability to edit and read Confluence, JIRA
          • Playwright/MCP included the ability to check out a web application's display looked like in the browser, giving it the ability to "see"
