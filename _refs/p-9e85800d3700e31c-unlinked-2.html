Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/01 Layout
  • Clips sit in each track's main lane (Ableton/Arrangement View/Track/Display Area); comping moves clips there from take lanes.
  • Arrangement Track Controls hold volume, pan, I/O and more; choose them under View → Arrangement Track Controls.
  • Tracks stack vertically and reorder by dragging. Dropping an instrument or MIDI effect into the Mixer Drop Area below the tracks creates a MIDI track; an audio effect creates an audio track.
  • Ableton/Arrangement View/Optimize/Height and Ableton/Arrangement View/Optimize/Width, or H and W, fit every track to the view.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/02 Navigation and Zooming
  • Option-scroll (Mac) / Alt-scroll (Win) in a main lane zooms the track vertically, or every track with selected content.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/03 Transport and Playback
  • Playback starts from the flashing blue Ableton/Live/UI/Arrangement View/Marker/Insert Marker, at the Arrangement start by default. Click in a track to move it; double-click Stop, or press Home (Win) / Fn+Left (Mac), to send it back to the start.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/05 Time Signature Changes
  • Markers aren't quantized, only constrained by the grid, so one placed before a bar ends leaves a crosshatched fragmentary bar. Its context menu offers Delete Fragmentary Bar Time, which removes the extra time, or Complete Fragmentary Bar, which inserts time to finish the bar. Both change the length of every track.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/07 Moving and Resizing Clips
  • Drag a clip by its bar to move it to another time or track; drag its left or right edge to resize it. The waveform or MIDI display isn't a drag handle.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/08 Audio Clip Fades and Crossfades
  • Audio clips have fades at their start and end; neighbouring clips on a track can crossfade.
  • Hover over a clip, in a tall enough track, to see the fade handles as small squares at its edges. In Automation Mode, hold F over an automation lane to show them.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/09 Selecting Clips and Time
  • To select time inside a clip, unfold its track with the button by its name or U; Option+U (Mac) / Alt+U (Win) unfolds all. Resize by dragging the split line, with Option +/- (Mac) / Alt +/- (Win), or Option with a pinch; holding Option (Mac) / Alt (Win) while resizing one track resizes all.
  • 0 deactivates the selected material, or the selected track. R or Reverse Clip(s) reverses a selection of audio, across clips; MIDI can't be reversed this way.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/11 Using the Time Commands
  • Cut, Copy and Paste act on the selection; their "… Time" versions insert or delete time across every track, time signature markers included.
    • Cut Time and Delete Time remove the selected time and close the gap, shortening the Arrangement.
    • Paste Time and Duplicate Time insert time, lengthening it.
    • Insert Silence adds a chosen amount of empty time at the insert marker.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/13 Consolidating Clips

Page reference

  • Consolidate, Cmd+J (Mac) / Ctrl+J (Win), the Edit menu or the main lane's context menu, joins selected adjacent clips into one, per track, across several tracks if selected. Useful for turning a looped passage into a single loop clip.
  • For audio it writes a new sample per track, recorded from the warping engine before the track's effects and mixer: it includes clip gain, warping, pitch and clip envelopes but no effects. Use Export Audio/Video for a post-effects file.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/14 Linked-Track Editing

  • 6.14 Linked-Track Editing

    • Linked-track editing applies comping and other phase-locked edits to several tracks together, for example the microphones on a drum kit. See Ableton/Linked-Track Editing and Ableton/YouTube/21/04/Learn Live Linked-Track Editing.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/14 Linked-Track Editing/01 Linking and Unlinking Tracks

      • 6.14.1 Linking and Unlinking Tracks

        • Link selected tracks with Link Tracks in a track header's context menu, or link a Group Track's tracks from the group header. Linked tracks show an indicator button in their headers.
        • A Set can have several linked groups, but a track belongs to only one. Hover the indicator to highlight a group's tracks; click it to select them.
        • To add tracks to a group, select them and Cmd-click (Mac) / Ctrl-click (Win) Link Tracks in a linked track's header. Any mix of linked and unlinked tracks can be linked into a new group.
        • Unlink Track(s) in the header's context menu removes tracks from their group.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/14 Linked-Track Editing/02 Editing Linked Tracks

      • 6.14.2 Editing Linked Tracks

        • These apply to every track in the group at once:
          • Moving and resizing clips; selecting clips and time; the "… Time" commands; splitting and consolidating.
          • Clip fades, for fades that start at the same position.
          • Arming and disarming.
          • Renaming, inserting and deleting take lanes, and Audition Mode, even where take lanes are hidden.

Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/15 The Mixer in Arrangement View

Page reference

  • Arrangement View has two sets of mixer controls: the mixer, and the Arrangement track controls beside each track. They share values.
  • Open the mixer with View → Mixer, Cmd+Option+M (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+M (Win), or the mixer view control at the bottom right. View → Mixer Controls and the Arrangement track controls submenu choose what shows.
  • Performance Impact meters, track delays and the crossfader exist only in the mixer.
Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View
  • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/03 The Track Status Fields

    • 7.3 The Track Status Fields

      • The Track Status field above each track's mixer controls shows what the track is doing:
        • A pie chart: a looping Session clip, with the loop length in beats on the right and the number of repetitions on the left. On a Group Track, without numbers, it means a track inside is playing.
        • A progress bar: a one-shot clip, with time remaining in minutes:seconds.
        • A microphone (audio track) or keyboard (MIDI track): the track is monitoring its input.
        • A miniature of clips: the track is playing the Arrangement.
Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/01 Session View Clips
  • Click a clip's triangular launch button to start it, or select the clip by its name and press Enter; arrow keys move to neighbouring clips. A square Clip Stop button in a slot or in the Track Status field below the grid stops the track.
  • Group Track slots are shaded, in the color of the leftmost clip, when any track in the group has a clip in that scene. Their launch buttons launch those clips together, empty ones hold stop buttons, and clicking one selects its clips.
Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/02 Tracks and Scenes

Page reference

  • A track plays one clip at a time, so put alternatives in the same column: song parts, variations of a drum loop.
  • Drag the edges of a track's title bar to resize it, narrow enough to show only launch buttons and key controls; Option (Mac) / Alt (Win) resizes all tracks. 0 on a selected track header deactivates the track.
  • Rows are scenes. A Scene Launch button in the Main track column launches the whole row. Cancel Scene Launch in the Main track's context menu cancels a pending one.
Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/02 Tracks and Scenes/01 Editing Scene Tempo and Time Signature Values
  • Drag the left edge of the Main track's header to show the hidden Scene Tempo and Scene Time Signature fields. Launching the scene switches the Set to those values.
Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/02 Tracks and Scenes/02 Scene View
  • The Scene View opens when you select scenes, click a Scene Tempo or Time Signature field, or click the Main track's title bar.

Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/04 Setting Up the Session View Grid

Page reference

  • Several files dropped at once stack in one track, vertically in Session View; hold Cmd (Mac) / Ctrl (Win) while dropping to spread them across tracks. This works for raw audio and MIDI files, not Live Clips.
Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/04 Setting Up the Session View Grid/01 Select on Launch
  • Launching a clip also selects it so the Clip View shows it. Turn off Select on Launch in Launch Settings to keep the current view, for example a return track's devices, when launching clips or scenes.

Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/05 Recording Sessions into the Arrangement
  • A track plays either its Session clip or its Arrangement, not both. Launching a Session clip, or pressing a Clip Stop button, takes the track off the Arrangement until you press Ableton/Live/UI/Arrangement View/Button/Back to Arrangement, which lights up while what you hear differs from the Arrangement.
  • Stop All Clips sits in the Main track's status field. Session and Arrangement clips are independent, so you can record improvisations into the Arrangement again and again.
  • Create → Consolidate Time to New Scene turns a selected Arrangement range into one clip per track in a new scene, writing new samples for audio tracks.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout
  • Clicking a track's status display in Session View opens the clip playing on that track.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout/01 Clip Title Bar
  • New clips take their track's color. Pick another from the title bar or clip context menu.
  • Assign Track Color to Clips, in a track header's context menu, resets them; on a Group Track, Assign Track Color to Grouped Tracks and Clips colors everything inside. Each affects only the view you run it in, Session or Arrangement.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/03 Extended Clip Properties/01 Follow Action and Launch Controls
  • Empty slots split a track's clips into groups; Follow Actions set how clips in a group trigger one another.
Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo/04 Clip Tempo Followers and Leaders
  • Many clips can lead, but only one sets the tempo at a time: the playing clip on the lowest track.
  • Live writes read-only tempo automation on the Main track, which moves with the leader clips. Unfollow Tempo Automation in the tempo field's context menu turns all leaders into followers and makes the automation editable.
Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping/02 Importing Samples
  • Drag samples in from your folders, or with an audio track selected use Create → Import Audio File… for MP3, AAC, Ogg Vorbis, Ogg FLAC or FLAC; it lands at the insert marker or in the selected slot.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/02 Zooming and Navigating in the MIDI Note Editor
  • Keys: Page Up/Page Down move an octave, with Shift one key track; +/- zoom around the selection; Z zooms fully to it, X shows the whole clip; Option (Mac) / Alt (Win) with +/- zooms the editor.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/03 Creating a MIDI Clip
  • MIDI clips come from recording on a MIDI track or Capture MIDI, double-clicking an empty Session slot or a MIDI track's display in the Arrangement, or Create → Insert Empty MIDI Clip(s), Cmd+Shift+M (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+M (Win), on a selected slot or timespan.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/04 Adding MIDI Notes
  • Notes arrive by recording on an armed track, by Capture MIDI, by double-clicking in the editor, or by drawing in Draw Mode.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/04 Adding MIDI Notes/01 Draw Mode
  • "Draw Mode with Pitch Lock" in Display & Input Settings keeps drawing on one key track, and Option (Mac) / Alt (Win) switches to freehand melodic drawing; with the setting off, the roles swap. Melodic drawing erases notes it crosses; pitch-locked drawing erases as you move back toward the first note. Menus show the current state as "Draw Mode (Pitch Lock On/Off)".
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/04 Adding MIDI Notes/02 Previewing Notes
  • With an instrument on the track, the Preview switch plays notes as you add, select or move them. On an armed track it also lets you step-record notes. The switch applies to every MIDI track in the Set.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/05 Editing MIDI Notes/02 Selecting Notes and Timespan
  • Click a note to select it, or with Option (Mac) / Ctrl (Win), up and down select the nearest note and step through notes in time, and left and right step along the same key track.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/05 Editing MIDI Notes/08 Note Operations
  • Cmd+J (Mac) / Ctrl+J (Win), or Join Notes, merges selected notes on one key track into one, joining their MPE data too.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/05 Editing MIDI Notes/12 Editing Velocities
  • Click a piano key to select one key track's notes, then draw a ramp just for them.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/06 Folding and Scales
  • Fold to Notes, with the Fold button, F, or the View menu, hides key tracks without notes, useful for drum kits that use a few keys spread over the keyboard. On a Drum Rack track with it off, pads that hold devices are shown.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/08 Multi-Clip Editing
  • With several MIDI clips selected, the editor shows their notes together, to compare and edit material across tracks and scenes, or successive variations in one track.
    • Edit notes from all of them at once, or one at a time with Focus Mode.
    • A loop bar per clip, in its color, sits above the editor. Click a clip's note or loop bar to switch to it; drag a loop bar's marker to change that clip's loop, Cmd/Ctrl-click or Shift-click to edit several (not in Focus Mode); Cmd+D (Mac) / Ctrl+D (Win) duplicates them. Drag above the scrub area to resize the bars.
    • The title bar names the clip being edited, or its track if the clip has no name.
    • Loop, time signature, groove and scale controls, and Fold to Notes and Fold to Scale, apply to all selected clips.
    • Velocity and Chance edits apply only to the foreground clip.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/08 Multi-Clip Editing/02 Multi-Clip Editing in the Session View
  • Up to eight looped clips; loop bars ordered by track, then scene.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/08 Multi-Clip Editing/03 Multi-Clip Editing in the Arrangement View
  • Clips from up to eight tracks within a time selection; loop bars ordered by track vertically and by time horizontally.
Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE
  • To receive MPE from a controller, turn on MPE Mode for it in Settings → Link, Tempo & MIDI. A track whose input is an MPE controller always listens on All Channels.
Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/01 Viewing MPE Data
  • MIDI track meters light their lowest dot blue when per-note controller changes pass through.
Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/06 MPE-Multi-channel Settings
  • It opens from the Ext. Instrument device, from a MIDI track's I/O section in the mixer, or from the context menu of an MPE-enabled plug-in.
Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/06 MPE-Multi-channel Settings/01 Accessing the MPE-Multi-channel Settings Dialog
  • Mixer I/O section: with the target device chosen in the track's MIDI To, choose MPE in the second menu, then reopen it and choose MPE Settings…, in either the Session or Arrangement mixer.
Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/06 MPE-Multi-channel Settings/02 The MPE-Multi-Channel Settings Dialog
  • A track outputs to one zone only, so using both zones takes two tracks. Zones apply only to MPE output.
Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI
  • Commands that turn audio into MIDI: slicing, and converting harmony, melody or drums into a new MIDI track.
  • With an audio clip selected, the Create menu and the clip's context menu offer four commands: Slice to New MIDI Track, and Convert Harmony, Melody or Drums to New MIDI Track.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/01 Slice to New MIDI Track

    • 13.1 Slice to New MIDI Track

      • Slicing cuts the audio into pieces of time, one per MIDI note, without analysing pitch or rhythm. The pieces keep playing the original sound.
      • The dialog slices by beat division, by transients or by Warp Markers, and offers a Slicing Preset. A Rack holds at most 128 chains, so Live refuses settings that would make more than 128 slices.
        • Your own slicing presets go in the Defaults folder of the User Library.
        • Preserve warped timing keeps timing changes from warping; with it off, slices sound like the raw audio.
      • The result:
        • a new MIDI track with a clip holding one note per slice, in a rising chromatic sequence;
        • a Ableton/Instrument/Drum Rack on that track with one chain per slice, each a Ableton/Instrument/Simpler loaded with its slice;
        • the Drum Rack's Ableton/Macro controls mapped to envelope, loop and crossfade settings across every Simpler, as set by the slicing preset.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/01 Slice to New MIDI Track/01 Resequencing Slices

        • 13.1.1 Resequencing Slices

          • The sliced clip forms a rising "staircase" of notes. Editing the notes, or dragging Drum Rack pads onto each other to swap their notes, plays the slices in a new order.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/01 Slice to New MIDI Track/02 Using Effects on Slices

        • 13.1.2 Using Effects on Slices

          • Each slice has its own Drum Rack chain, so it can take its own audio effects. To share effects across several slices, select their chains and press Cmd+G (Mac) / Ctrl+G (Windows) to group them into a nested Rack, then add effects after it.
          • MIDI effects placed before the Drum Rack, such as Arpeggiator or Random, rearrange the slices as they play.
          • Slicing suits drum loops but also works on voices and ambient textures.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/02 Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track

    • 13.2 Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track

      • Detects the pitches in polyphonic audio and writes them to a clip on a new MIDI track, which comes with an Instrument Rack playing a piano sound.
      • Unlike slicing, the Convert commands don't play the original audio; the notes drive a different instrument.
      • Works on recordings from your collection, or on your own playing of guitar, piano or other harmonic instruments.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/03 Convert Melody to New MIDI Track

    • 13.3 Convert Melody to New MIDI Track

      • Detects the pitches in monophonic audio and writes them to a new MIDI track with a synth Instrument Rack. Its "Synth to Piano" Macro moves the sound from an analog-style synth to an electric piano.
      • Singing, whistling or a solo instrument can all become MIDI notes this way.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/04 Convert Drums to New MIDI Track

    • 13.4 Convert Drums to New MIDI Track

      • Extracts rhythms from unpitched, percussive audio onto a new MIDI track with a Drum Rack, trying to sort hits into kick, snare and hi-hat.
      • Adjusting the audio clip's transient markers before converting changes where notes land.
      • Suits breakbeats, beatboxing or tapping on a surface.
