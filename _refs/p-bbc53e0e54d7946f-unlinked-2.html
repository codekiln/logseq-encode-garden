Library/Science/Classification
  • Library classification (often bibliographic classification) assigns notation — usually a call number — so items can be shelved in a predictable subject order and browsed alongside neighbors that treat the same topic.
  • It is one kind of knowledge organization system; it supports collocation (related works together) and a stable shelf address, distinct from the vocabulary used in subject headings or abstracts.
  • Relative indexes (DDC) and subject-heading cross-references help mapping, but classification and subject headings answer different retrieval jobs.
  • “Call number encodes year or edition” — Usually false; it is primarily subject + author/title key; local policy varies.

Logseq/Entity/Discussion
  • In this garden, Discussion pages model the reasoning behind one subject page: the principles that shaped it, the alternatives weighed and the history of decisions about it. The subject page keeps only what is true of the subject.
  • A page at <Subject>/Discussion whose content is the reasoning behind <Subject>: why the subject is shaped the way it is, what else was considered, and what was decided when.
  • The subject can be any page: an entity definition such as Logseq/Entity/Music/Scale, a principle such as My/Principle/Simplify/Prefer Standards and Defaults, or a preference such as My/Pref/Dev/mise/Tasks. The subject page may exist only as a logical page, as Logseq/Entity/Hierarchy does.
  • One Discussion page per subject, at <Subject>/Discussion: Logseq/Entity/Definition/Discussion discusses Logseq/Entity/Definition.
  • The subject page links its Discussion in see-also::.
  • H1 — the subject page's H1 followed by Discussion: - # Music Scale Discussion for Logseq/Entity/Music/Scale, whose H1 is Music Scale. When the subject has no page file, the H1 names the subject in Title Case without the Logseq/ prefix, as - # Entity Hierarchy Discussion does for Logseq/Entity/Hierarchy. Logseq/Entity/Definition/Discussion keeps the H1 codekiln wrote for it.
  • An analysis is one author's written reasoning about the subject at one time, usually an AI model's. Each analysis has its own page, nested under the Discussion it belongs to:
    • <Subject>/Discussion/Analysis/<Model>/YY/MM/DD/HHMM ET <slug>
    • <Model> is the last segment of the author's Logseq/Entity/AI/Model page: Opus for Anthropic/Model/Claude/5/5/Opus, Fable for Anthropic/Model/Claude/5/1/Fable.
    • YY/MM/DD is the two-digit year, month and day the analysis was written, and HHMM ET the 24-hour Eastern time.
    • <slug> states the analysis's main claim as a short sentence: Mark each page with the most specific entity in each lineage.

My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/A pointer carries a link, an id with a slug, and a reason

  • An id that is the subject of the sentence stays verbatim

    • A digest, a version string, a UUID, a key type, quoted terminal output: these are the thing under discussion, so a slug and a link would be wrong on them. Rewriting quoted output would also make the transcript false.
    • The test is what the id is doing in the sentence. If I am meant to go somewhere and look at something, it is a pointer and it needs the link, the slug and the reason. If I am meant to look at the id itself, leave it alone.
  • Weak: git/reflog says rediscover commit 84f0092 in lazygit. Seven hex characters, no subject, no link. I cannot tell what that commit did without running git show.
My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/No Grammar Knots
  • Keep the subject near its verb. Put background information after the main clause or in a separate sentence.
    • Before: “Maya, after checking the date and correcting the address, sent the invitation.”
    • After: “Maya sent the invitation after checking the date and correcting the address.”
My/Pref/Writing/Give every sentence a subject that can do its verb

  • A subject that can do its verb

    • When describing responsibility for work, name the person or group of people responsible: “I propose…,” “The developer will…,” or “The support team decided….”
    • Use a verb to describe the proposed action: “I propose writing a script to find channels affected by failed AI turns.” Distinguish what someone proposes, what exists, and what has been tested.
    • The test is a picture. If I cannot see the subject doing the verb, the sentence needs a different subject.
    • This holds in every document I read, an OpenSpec proposal and a heading included (see My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/Use Headings to Serve a Purpose).

    • Why this matters

      • Reading generated design documents on 2026-09-10 Thu, I said:

        • I can't stand writing like this. The subjects of the sentences are not entities. a "release" doesn't do "building" and a "ticket" doesn't do "touching" or "saying."

        • "A ticket whose own files run nothing" is one of the worst starts to a sentence I've ever read. My eyes are bleeding.

        • terrible, terrible writing. goes on the wall of shame ... I'm already lost.

      • When the subject cannot act, I have to work out who acted, and the writer already knew. That is the same work My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/Never make the reader resolve a reference asks the writer to do once so I never do it.
      • For automated work, name the program or command that performs the operation. For work performed by people, name the people responsible.

    • Name the people responsible

      • A person: a developer, a tester, a reviewer, me.
      • A group of people: the support team, the developers, the reviewers.
      • Files, directories, tickets, proposals, and system components cannot intend, propose, decide, or approve. Name the people who do.

    • Describe automated behavior

      • Name the program or command and the operation it performs: “The build script builds an image.” Use a verb that describes the actual operation.
      • “The finder decides which channels need recovery” → “The recovery script lists channels that meet these conditions.” State the actual conditions in the surrounding text.

    • A coined noun used as a name is the same fault

      • You keep using "The triage" as though it were a proper noun like the title of a movie. The reader has no idea what you are talking about with The Triage. Just say what you mean, don't invent bigrams to stand in the way.

      • there is no "triage" and it's not doing any "asking"

      • A phrase invented inside the document picks up capital letters and then a verb. Now it is both a thing I have never heard of and an actor that cannot act. See My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/Do not coin phrases unless asked.

    • Examples

      • A release builds and deploys the whole service.

        • → The build script builds an image of the whole service. The DevOps team deploys the image.

      • The files a ticket touched say what that ticket changed in the build.

        • → Read a developer's commits to see which files they changed.

      • A ticket that changed no file that runs while serving a request says so in its QA field.

        • → When a developer changed no file that runs while serving a request, nobody has an area to test.

      • The triage asks what the release deploys.

        • → Check whether the DevOps team deploys a build that includes the developer's work.

      • for a ticket that ships nothing

        • → for a ticket with no areas for QA to test. "Shipping nothing" is weird and unnecessary.
