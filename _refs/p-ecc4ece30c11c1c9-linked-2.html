31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/01 General Overview

    • 31.8.1 General Overview

      • Up to two polyphonic engines, in four sections: engines, modulation, filters and global mix.
      • Starting points: combine different oscillator types, move each engine's macros, map parameters in the Modulation Matrix, or automate the macros with an LFO device.
      • Toggle Expanded View in the header shows every modulation source and target. A and B switch engines; Copy to A / Copy to B copy modulation values; X clears all modulation.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/02 Oscillators

    • 31.8.2 Oscillators

      • Each engine has its own on switch; turning an engine off also turns off its filter, but a filter can be turned off alone.
      • Octaves, Semitones, Cents per engine. With Use Current Scale on, semitones (st) become scale degrees (sd).
      • 24 oscillator types per engine, six of them scale-aware (marked ♭♯), from plain sine to wave swarms, FM, noise loops and ambient generators. Choose from the menu or step with the arrows.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/03 Oscillator Macros

    • 31.8.3 Oscillator Macros

      • Each engine's two macro knobs change with its oscillator type. All four can be mapped to a MIDI controller, driven by an LFO device, or modulated in the matrix.
      • Macros by oscillator type:
        • Basic Shapes — Shape (sine → triangle → saw → square), Tone (pulse width).
        • Dual Basic Shapes — Shape, Detune (adds a detuned copy).
        • Noisy Shapes — Shape, Rough (noise distortion).
        • Square Sync — Freq 1, Freq 2 of two synced squares.
        • Square 5th — 5th Amt (morph to a square a fifth up), P Width.
        • Sub — Tone (sine toward square), Aux (adds a lower subharmonic sine).
        • Swarm Sine / Triangle / Saw / Square — Motion (modulates the swarm), Spacing (moves through more complex chords).
        • Harmonic Fm — Amount, Ratio.
        • Fold Fm — Amount, Shape (carrier shape).
        • Squelch — Amount (FM depth), Feedback.
        • Simple Fm — Amount, Ratio.
        • Chip — Tone (pitch and pulse width), Rate (speed of the chip interval).
        • Shepard's Pi — Rate (below 50 falls, above 50 rises, 50 still), Width (octaves used).
        • Tarp — Decay, Tone.
        • Extratone — Pitch, Env Amount.
        • Noise Loop — Rate (how often noise-loop fragments change; high values become plain noise), Fade (graininess).
        • Filtered Noise — Freq, Width.
        • Bitgrunge — Freq (square frequency), Mult (number of sub-octaves; maximum gives none, minimum many).
        • Crackle — Density, Intensity (spread of loudness and brightness).
        • Rain — Tone (resonance of drops and wind, making it pitched), Rate (drop density).
        • Bubble — Density, Spread (randomness of bubble size).
        • Chord — four saws; Shape (intervals), Inversion. With Use Current Scale it builds chords from the set scale, otherwise from a major scale on the played note.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/04 Envelopes Tab

    • 31.8.4 Envelopes Tab

      • Every envelope has Attack, Decay, Sustain and Release, set by number fields, typing, or dragging breakpoints. Red Attack, Decay and Release Slope values, or the diamonds between breakpoints, shape the curves.
      • Loop modes for Amplitude and Modulation envelopes: Trigger plays every segment once and ignores sustain; Loop repeats the whole envelope; AD Loop repeats attack and decay.
      • The Modulation envelope adds Initial, Peak and Final levels.
      • Link Envelopes ties the engines' envelopes together so they act as one instrument.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/05 LFOs Tab

    • 31.8.5 LFOs Tab

      • Two LFOs per engine, each in Hz or synced, with Phase Offset and Retrigger.
      • LFO 1 types: Basic Shapes, Ramp, Wander, Alternate, Euclid, Pulsate, each with Rate and two macros. The LFO 1 FX panel chains two of 18 effects (FX1, FX2) onto it, each with a macro. LFO 1 and LFO 1 FX are separate sources in the matrix.
      • LFO 2 — Sine, Tri, Saw Up, Saw Down, Rectangle, Random S&H; a third matrix source.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/06 Matrix Tab

    • 31.8.6 Matrix Tab

      • Routes sources (columns) to targets (rows) within an engine, such as LFO 1 to Volume or the Modulation envelope to filter frequency. Drag a cell up or down to set the amount.
      • On envelope and LFO timing targets, negative values speed them up and positive values slow them down. Some targets add modulation; others multiply.
      • Click a parameter to add it to the current engine's matrix; an unmodulated row disappears when you click another parameter.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/07 MIDI and MPE Tabs

    • 31.8.7 MIDI and MPE Tabs

      • MIDI and MPE data become modulation sources:
        • Velocity, Pitch — the note's value, held for its duration.
        • Random — a new value per note.
        • Pitch Bend, Press, Modulation Wheel — standard controller sources.
        • Note Pitch Bend, Slide, Press — MPE sources. Without a controller, clip envelopes can drive them.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/08 Settings Tab

    • 31.8.8 Settings Tab

      • Per-engine settings: Osc Key Tracking, Scale Awareness and Glide.
      • Osc Key Tracking — off plays C3, or the scale root in that octave, for every note; useful for drones and percussion.
      • Phase Reset — fixed start phase per note. Phase Spread then spreads start phase by the matrix's Spread source; off starts at zero.
      • Oscillator Scale Awareness — keeps scale-aware oscillators in scale: Dual Basic Shapes, the four Swarms and Chip.
      • Filter Scale Awareness — keeps Plate Resonator and Membrane Resonator in scale.
      • Glide — Glide Time for overlapping notes, in Mono or Poly. Porta slides smoothly; Gliss steps, by scale degree when scale-aware. Needs Glide Time above zero.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/09 Filters

    • 31.8.9 Filters

      • Filter A and Filter B have their own on switches, a Frequency knob, 17 types and two macros per type, most often Q (emphasis at cutoff) and Drive (input saturation).
      • Types:
        • SVF 12dB / 24dB — state variable; L-B-H-N morphs low-pass, band-pass, high-pass and notch.
        • MS2 — low- and high-pass from a Sallen-Key design in a well-known Japanese semi-modular mono synth, with soft clipping on resonance.
        • OSR — band-pass from a rare British mono synth, resonance limited by a hard-clipping diode.
        • LP Crunch 12dB — feeds its own distortion back in.
        • LP Switched Res — resistors replaced by fast switches for downsampling artifacts; Lofi high is crushed, low is smoother.
        • Filther — low-pass with hard diode clipping in and soft saturation out.
        • Eq Peak / Eq Notch — Boost or Cut around the cutoff.
        • Phaser — six-stage with Feedback and notch Spread.
        • Redux — resampler and bitcrusher; Crush sets bit depth, Lofi mixes filtered downsampled signal with raw artifacts.
        • Vowel — formant filter; Morph moves between vowels.
        • Comb + / Comb - — feedforward and feedback combs with Feedback and Damp.
        • Plate Resonator — 32 modes of a rectangular plate; size, resonance and ratio. Scale-aware.
        • Membrane Resonator — 32 modes of a round membrane; size, resonance and high-frequency damping. Scale-aware.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/10 Mix Section

    • 31.8.10 Mix Section

      • Per engine: Volume, Pan and Tone Filter, which cuts lows at positive values and highs at negative values.
      • Limit — a per-voice limiter after both engines are mixed and Drive is applied.
31.8 Meld
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/08 Meld/11 Global Controls

    • 31.8.11 Global Controls

      • Mono/Poly — Mono offers Legato (new notes continue the held note's envelope); Poly sets 2 to 12 voices.
      • Spread — range of the matrix's Spread source: an offset between stacked voices, or across a chord's notes when stacking is off. Does nothing unless routed.
      • Stacked Voices — duplicates both engines per note, filters and modulation included; heavy on CPU.
      • Mixer Drive — per-voice saturation after the mixer and before the limiter.
      • Volume — output.
      • Use Current Scale — follows Live's Scale Mode, so transposition, including the Pitch Quant target, moves in scale degrees.

31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/01 General Overview

    • 31.9.1 General Overview

      • A central display sits between two sides of shell. The left shell has the four oscillators; the right has LFO, filter, pitch and global. Touching a shell control shows that section in the display, so levels and frequencies are reachable together while envelopes and waveforms sit in each display.
      • The triangle at the top left folds the display away.
      • Each oscillator either outputs audio or modulates another. Eleven algorithms, picked in the global display, set the connections; signal flows top to bottom in each icon. The algorithm can be mapped, automated and modulated.
      • Beyond the usual FM sines, Operator offers other waveforms, two noises and a drawable partial editor. Ableton suggests exploring FM without the filter first, since many classic FM synths had none.
31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/02 Oscillator Section

    • 31.9.2 Oscillator Section

      • 31.9.2.1 Built-in Waveforms

        • From the Wave chooser: pure Sine, the usual first choice for FM; Sine 4 Bit and Sine 8 Bit for a C64-style sound; Saw D and Square D digital shapes for bass; resynthesized square, triangle and saw whose number (e.g. Square 6) is the harmonic count, with lower numbers mellower and less prone to aliasing; Noise Looped, a noise loop; and Noise White, true random noise.

      • 31.9.2.2 User Waveforms

        • User, or any built-in wave, can be edited by drawing harmonic amplitudes with the pencil; Shift locks to one harmonic. The status bar shows harmonic number and amplitude.
        • Edit the first 16, 32 or 64 harmonics. Repeat extends them upward with a fade: low values are brighter, high values roll off more and favor the fundamental; Off truncates.
        • The context menu limits drawing to even or odd harmonics and toggles Normalize, which keeps level constant; with it off, output can get very loud.
        • Export as Ableton/File/.ams to Samples/Waveforms in the User Library; drop .ams files back on an oscillator, or into Simpler or Sampler. The Osc Preview's context menu copies waves between oscillators.

      • 31.9.2.3 More Oscillator Parameters

        • Coarse and Fine set frequency relative to the note. Fixed ignores note pitch and uses Freq times Multi, down to 0.1 Hz; only the timbre changes across keys, as for drums.
        • Osc < Vel shifts frequency with velocity; Q quantizes the shift to whole ratios, otherwise it detunes into inharmonic tones.
        • Amplitude comes from Level and the oscillator's envelope, scaled by Vel and Key.
        • Phase, and R to restart the phase per note or run free.
        • When modulating, the modulator's level and the frequency ratio set the result. An oscillator nothing modulates can modulate itself with Feedback.

      • 31.9.2.4 Aliasing

        • Digital synthesis aliases at high frequencies, and FM, with its many harmonics, does so readily; complex waves like Saw 32 more than sines. A little can be useful; too much makes high notes fold to unrelated pitches.
        • Antialias mode, on by default for new patches, reduces it. Tone in the global section also tames it, sometimes like a low-pass filter. To hear aliasing, turn Tone up and play very high notes.
31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/03 LFO Section

    • 31.9.3 LFO Section

      • The LFO runs up to audio rate, so it works almost like a fifth oscillator modulating the others' frequency.
      • Dest. A buttons send it to each oscillator and the filter, with one amount; Dest. B adds another target with its own amount.
      • Waveforms: classic shapes, S&H for random steps, and band-pass filtered Noise. The manual's tip: LFO noise is the key to FM hi-hats and snares.
      • Rate with a Low, High or Sync range; Rate < Key makes it follow pitch; R retriggers.
      • LFO Amount scales both destinations, modulated by Amt < Vel and the LFO's envelope.
31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/04 Envelopes

    • 31.9.4 Envelopes

      • Seven envelopes: one per oscillator, plus filter, pitch and LFO. All can loop; filter and pitch envelopes have adjustable slopes.
      • Oscillator envelopes have three rates and three levels: from Initial (often -inf dB) at the attack rate to Peak, at the decay rate to Sustain, and after note-off at the release rate back to silence. Drag breakpoints or nudge selected ones with arrow keys. Copy shapes between oscillators from the context menu.
      • Slopes (filter, pitch) — positive moves fast then slow, negative slow then fast, zero linear.
      • Loop — an envelope that reaches sustain while held restarts; Loop Time sets the return. Fast loops give effects you wouldn't expect from an envelope, and are the key to endless evolving FM sounds.
      • Beat — restarts after the Repeat beat time without quantizing the first note. Sync — quantizes the first repeat to the nearest 16th so repeats follow the song; acts like Beat when stopped.
      • Looped envelopes restart from their current level at the attack rate, so they don't click.
      • Trigger — ignores note-off; good for percussion.
      • Global Time scales all rates except Beat and Sync times; Time < Key speeds envelopes for higher notes; Time < Vel per envelope.
      • The pitch envelope has Destination A–D and LFO buttons with an amount, an on switch in the shell, and a Dest. B target with Amt. B.
      • Pitch and filter envelopes add End, the level after release.
      • Tip from the manual: a looping pitch envelope routed only to the LFO acts as a second LFO on its rate, and the LFO's own looping envelope as a third on its depth.
31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/05 Filter Section

    • 31.9.5 Filter Section

      • The filter shapes the oscillators' output; with classic waveforms Operator can work as a subtractive synth.
      • Types: low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, notch and Morph, at 12 or 24 dB, with circuit models made with Cytomic:
        • Clean — efficient, same as EQ Eight's filters; all types.
        • OSR — state variable with a hard-clipping diode, from a rare British mono synth; all types.
        • MS2 — Sallen-Key with soft clipping, from a famous Japanese semi-modular; low- and high-pass.
        • SMP — a custom design between MS2 and PRD; low- and high-pass.
        • PRD — a ladder with no resonance limiting, from an American dual-oscillator mono synth; low- and high-pass.
      • Frequency and Resonance; non-Clean low-, high- and band-pass add Drive.
      • Morph sweeps low-pass → band-pass → high-pass → notch → low-pass; its context menu snaps to each.
      • The display switches between filter envelope and response curve; drag the curve to set cutoff and resonance.
      • Cutoff modulation: Freq < Vel, Freq < Key, Envelope, and the LFO via Dest. A "FIL" or Dest. B. Play by Key in the Frequency context menu sets Freq < Key to 100% and cutoff to 466 Hz.
      • Shaper curve with Shp. Drive and Dry/Wet after the filter; 0% wet bypasses it.
31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/06 Global Controls

    • 31.9.6 Global Controls

      • Voices — up to 32; 6 to 12 is practical for CPU. At 1, overlapping notes play legato without retriggering envelopes, as for a flute.
      • Volume in the shell; Pan in the display with Pan < Key and Pan < Rnd.
      • MIDI routing: Velocity, Key, Aftertouch, Pitch Bend and Mod Wheel each reach two targets with amounts. Time < Key and the pitch bend range are fixed, though those sources can still take another target.
31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/07 Glide and Spread

    • 31.9.7 Glide and Spread

      • Glide — polyphonic: new notes start at the last note's pitch and slide over Glide Time.
      • Spread — two detuned voices per note panned left and right for chorus. It is read at note-on, so a sequence can make only some notes stereo. Costs CPU.
      • Transpose — global.
31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/08 Strategies for Saving CPU Power

    • 31.9.8 Strategies for Saving CPU Power

      • Turn off unused features, especially the filter and LFO, and keep voices at 6 to 12. Use Spread sparingly, and switch off Interpolation and Antialias if needed.
      • Turning off oscillators doesn't save CPU.
31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/09 Finally

    • 31.9.9 Finally…

      • Operator comes from a love of hardware FM synths such as the Yamaha SY77, the Yamaha TX81Z and the NED Synclavier II.
      • John Chowning first explored FM musically in the mid-1960s. In 1973 he and Stanford began working with Yamaha, which led to the DX7.
31.9 Operator
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/10 The Complete Parameter List

    • 31.9.10 The Complete Parameter List

      • Every parameter, grouped by where it sits; the Info View also explains any control under the mouse.

      • 31.9.10.1 Global Shell and Display

        • Time — scales all envelope rates. Tone — high-frequency content; brighter means more aliasing risk. Volume. Algorithm — oscillator connections.
        • Voices — oldest notes are cut when exceeded. Retrigger (R) — retrigger a playing note instead of adding a voice.
        • Interpolation — off sounds rougher, especially noise, and saves CPU. Antialias — off saves CPU.
        • Time < Key — faster envelopes for higher notes. PB Range.
        • Pan, Pan < Key (low notes left, high right, as for pianos), Pan < Random.

      • 31.9.10.2 Modulation Targets

        • For MIDI routings, the LFO's Dest. B and the pitch envelope's Dest. B: Off; OSC Volume A–D; OSC Crossfade A/C and B/D; OSC Feedback (only unmodulated oscillators); OSC Fixed Frequency; FM Drive (level of all modulators); Filter Frequency; Filter Q (Legacy); Filter Res; Filter Morph; Filter Drive (not with Morph); Filter Envelope Amount; Shaper Drive; LFO Rate; LFO Amount; Pitch Envelope Amount; Volume; Panorama; Tone; Time.

      • 31.9.10.3 Pitch Shell and Display

        • Pitch Envelope On — off saves CPU. Pitch Env — ±100%; 100% follows the envelope's levels, negative inverts.
        • Spread, Transpose (affects playing notes), Glide (G) and Glide Time; legato glides don't retrigger envelopes.
        • Destination A–D and LFO switches with Amount A; Destination B with Amount B.

      • 31.9.10.4 Filter Shell and Display

        • Filter On (off saves CPU), Filter Type, Circuit Type, Freq, Res (resonance for low- and high-pass, width for band-pass and notch).
        • Freq < Vel; Freq < Key, where 100% doubles cutoff per octave around C3; Envelope, where 100% can shift about nine octaves.
        • Flt. Drive, Morph, Shaper, Shp. Drive, Dry/Wet.

      • 31.9.10.5 LFO Shell and Display

        • LFO On (off saves CPU). Waveform — all band-limited to avoid clicks.
        • Range — Low covers 50 seconds to 30 Hz, Hi 8 Hz to 12 kHz, or Sync to tempo.
        • R, Rate, Amount, Destination A–D and FIL with Amount A, Destination B with Amount B.
        • Rate < Key — at 100% the LFO doubles per octave like an oscillator. Amt < Vel.

      • 31.9.10.6 Oscillators A-D Shell and Display

        • Osc On. Coarse — whole-number ratios for harmonic relationships. Fine — fractions for inharmonic ones.
        • Fixed, Freq and Multi — a constant frequency, Freq × Multi in Hz.
        • Level — on a modulator, higher levels usually mean brighter or noisier sounds.
        • Envelope/Oscillator display switches; 16/32/64 partials; Wave; Repeat.
        • Feedback — self-modulation of an unmodulated oscillator, also shaped by its level and envelope.
        • Phase (one full cycle), R.
        • Osc < Vel with Q; Vel and Key for level, centered on C3. Velocity on modulators makes timbre follow touch.

      • 31.9.10.7 Envelope Display

        • Attack — linear on oscillator envelopes, shapeable on filter and pitch. Decay — exponential on oscillators. Release — from the current level at note-off to End, or to -inf dB on oscillators.
        • Initial, Peak, Sustain, and End (LFO, filter and pitch only).
        • Loop mode — Loop, Beat, Sync or Trigger. Repeat — beat time for Beat and Sync. Loop Time — return from sustain to initial.
        • Time < Vel — scales segments, not Beat or Sync times.
        • A.Slope, D.Slope, R.Slope — filter and pitch envelopes.

      • 31.9.10.8 Context Menu Options for Operator

        • Copy parameters between oscillators; set all envelope levels to maximum, minimum or middle; harmonics editor options (even or odd, Normalize, export .ams); Play By Key; and Enable Per-Note Pitch Bend, on by default.

      • Pick a preset from the folders under Sampler in the browser; imported third-party libraries appear in its Imports folder. Arm the track so you can hear what you play, then play.
31.10 Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler/02 Multisampling

    • 31.10.2 Multisampling

      • Multisampling records an instrument at many pitches and dynamics instead of transposing one sample, so the timbre changes the way the real instrument's does. A piano, for example, might have three or more samples per key for soft to very loud playing.
      • Sampler works with multisample presets, imported third-party multisamples, your own multisamples, or a single sample run through its modulation.
31.10 Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler/03 Title Bar Options

    • 31.10.3 Title Bar Options

      • Title bar context menu entries beyond the usual Cut, Copy, Rename, Edit Info Text and Delete:
        • Group — wraps Sampler in a new Instrument Rack.
        • Fold — shows only the title bar; double-click to unfold.
        • Show Preset Name — off titles the device "Sampler" instead of the top sample's name.
        • Lock to Control Surface — keeps Sampler on a control surface set in Link, Tempo & MIDI, wherever focus goes; happens automatically when the track is armed. A hand icon marks it.
        • Save as Default Preset.
        • Use Constant Power Fade for Loops — on by default; off uses linear loop crossfades.
        • Sampler -> Simpler — converts the preset, for sharing with Live editions that lack Sampler.

31.10 Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler/05 The Zone Tab

    • 31.10.5 The Zone Tab

      • The Zone tab opens the Zone Editor above the Device View, for mapping samples across key, velocity and sample-select ranges.
      • The sample layer list on the left holds every sample, or layer, of the multisample. Buttons above it:
        • Auto — selects the layers that play each incoming note while it sounds.
        • Lin/Pow — linear or constant-power zone fades.
        • RR — Round Robin on or off.
        • Key/Vel/Sel — which zone editor to show. Right-click an editor to zoom Small, Medium or Large.

      • 31.10.5.1 Round Robin Sample Playback

        • Plays a different sample on each note, cycling through layers that share a key zone; good for natural variation in repeated drums or notes, or for deliberate surprises.
        • Round Robin Mode — Forward (top to bottom), Backward, Other (random, never the same twice running), Random (repeats allowed).
        • Reset Interval — restarts the cycle at a set interval, such as each bar, from the first sample of the mode.

      • 31.10.5.2 The Sample Layer List

        • Layers can be named, for example by root key; hovering shows details in the Status Bar, and selecting one opens it in the Sample tab.
        • Context menu: Delete, Duplicate, Rename; Distribute Ranges Equally (C-2 to G8) and Distribute Ranges Around Root Key; zoom; Show in Browser; Manage Sample; Normalize Volume (peaks to full headroom); Normalize Pan (an even stereo spread, not necessarily center); Select All With Same Range; and sorting by name, key, velocity or selector, ascending or descending.

      • 31.10.5.3 Key Zones

        • The note range, up to all 127, that triggers each sample. A sample stays accurate a few semitones around its root key, and other samples cover the rest.
        • New samples span the full range. Move and resize zones like Arrangement clips; drag a top corner to fade across semitones and crossfade neighbors, linear or exponential per Lin/Pow.

      • 31.10.5.4 Velocity Zones

        • The note-on velocities, 1–127, each sample answers, so different dynamics can use different recordings. Edited like key zones.

      • 31.10.5.5 Sample Select Zones

        • A 0–127 filter not tied to any MIDI input, like a Rack's Chain Select zones. Only samples whose zone overlaps the sample selector can play.
        • Moving the selector doesn't switch a sample already playing.
31.10 Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler/06 The Sample Tab

    • 31.10.6 The Sample Tab

      • Playback settings for the selected sample, around its waveform. Most values apply to that sample only; the Sample chooser switches layers. Cmd-scroll (Mac) / Ctrl-scroll (Win) zooms.
      • Reverse — global and modulatable; plays from Sample End back through the Sustain Loop to Sample Start without writing a new file.
      • Snap — snaps start and end points to zero crossings of the left channel to avoid clicks; a small Crossfade may still help. Right-click a loop brace for Snap Marker.
      • RootKey, Detune (±50 cents), Volume (up to +24 dB), Pan.

      • 31.10.6.1 Sample Playback

        • These work with the global volume envelope. Envelopes here use Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release with A., D. and R. Slope; Initial, Peak and End levels; Time < Vel; Loop modes (Loop, Beat, Sync, Trigger); Repeat; and Loop Time, as in Operator.
        • Times show in samples or min:sec:ms, switched from any value's context menu.
        • Sample Start and Sample End — where playback starts and stops. A slow attack can delay the audible start.
        • Sustain Mode — no loop; a forward loop between Loop Start and Loop End; or a back-and-forth loop, each while the note sustains. Link sets Sample Start to Loop Start.
        • Release Mode, with a Sustain Loop active:
          • Off — the release happens inside the sustain loop.
          • Release — playback runs on toward Sample End.
          • Release Loop — runs to Sample End, then loops from Release Loop to Sample End.
          • Back-and-Forth Release Loop — the same, bouncing back and forth.
        • Crossfade — smooths loop joins, constant-power unless turned off in the title bar menu.
        • Detune — corrects the pitch shift that short loops can cause.
        • Interpol — transposition quality; Good and Best cost a lot of CPU.
        • RAM — loads the whole multisample into memory, which helps when modulating start and end points but eats RAM.
        • The waveform's context menu repeats the layer list's file and normalize options and zooms to playing or loop regions. On the right, Vertical Zoom and B/M/L/R choose display height and channels.
31.10 Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler/07 The Pitch-Osc Tab

    • 31.10.7 The Pitch/Osc Tab

      • 31.10.7.1 The Modulation Oscillator (Osc)

        • One modulation oscillator per voice performs FM or AM on the samples; it is never heard directly. Subsonic AM varies volume; audible AM creates composite waves.
        • 21 waveforms in Type, a loopable amplitude envelope with the usual levels, and:
          • Volume — modulation depth; Vol < Vel scales it with velocity.
          • Fixed with Freq × Multi — a constant frequency.
          • Coarse (0.125–48) and Fine (0–1000) — tuning when not fixed.

      • 31.10.7.2 The Pitch Envelope

        • A multi-stage envelope on the sample's pitch, and the modulation oscillator's; drag breakpoints or use sliders. Amount sets its range in semitones.
        • Global controls on the right:
          • Spread — two detuned voices per note; doubles CPU.
          • Transp (semitones) and Detune (cents).
          • Zn Shft — shifts which key zone plays without changing pitch, for interesting multisample artifacts.
          • Glide — Glide is monophonic, Portamento polyphonic; Time sets duration.
31.10 Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler/08 The Filter-Global Tab

    • 31.10.8 The Filter/Global Tab

      • 31.10.8.1 The Filter

        • A polyphonic filter with the same types and Cytomic circuits as Operator: low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, notch and Morph at 12 or 24 dB, with Clean, OSR, MS2, SMP and PRD circuits. See Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/05 Filter Section.
        • Frequency, Resonance, Drive on non-Clean low-, high- and band-pass, and Morph for the Morph type.
        • F. Env — a filter envelope with levels, ADSR, loop and slopes; its Amount must be non-zero to act.
        • Shaper — Soft, Hard, Sine or 4bit, with Amount. An arrow sets whether it comes before or after the filter.

      • 31.10.8.2 The Volume Envelope and Global Controls

        • The global ADSR volume envelope. Loop and Trigger restart from Initial if a note outlasts Decay, taking Time to return; Beat and Sync restart after Repeat.
        • Pan and Pan < Rnd; Time scales every envelope; Time < Key scales them by pitch.
        • Voices up to 32. R retriggers playing notes instead of adding voices, saving CPU with long releases played quickly.
31.10 Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler/09 The Modulation Tab

    • 31.10.9 The Modulation Tab

      • One loopable Aux envelope and three LFOs, each able to modulate many parameters, including each other. LFOs can run free or sync, and LFOs 2 and 3 can modulate in stereo.

      • 31.10.9.1 The Auxiliary Envelope

        • Same design as the other envelopes, routed to two of 29 destinations through the A and B choosers, each with an amount.

      • 31.10.9.2 LFOs 1, 2 and 3

        • Type — Sine, Square, Triangle, Saw Down, Saw Up, Sample and Hold.
        • Rate — Hz with Freq (0.01–30 Hz), or beat sync with Beats (64th notes to 8 bars).
        • Attack — fade-in time, for delayed vibrato. Retrig and Offset — reset and starting phase. Key — rate follows pitch.
        • LFO 1 has direct depths for Vol, Pan, Filter (0–24) and Pitch.
        • LFOs 2 and 3 have A and B destinations and a Stereo mode: Phase offsets the right channel; Spin runs it up to 50% faster.
31.10 Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler/10 The MIDI Tab

    • 31.10.10 The MIDI Tab

      • Key, Velocity, Release Velocity, Aftertouch, Mod Wheel, Foot Controller and Pitch Bend each map to two destinations with Amount A and Amount B. Example: Velocity to Loop Length at 100 gives longer loops for harder notes.
      • Pitch Bend Range — up to 24 semitones.
      • Clicking the Sampler picture scrolls its credits.

31.11 Simpler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/11 Simpler/01 Playback Modes

    • 31.11.1 Playback Modes

      • The mode switch, left of the Sample tab or along the bottom of the expanded view, sets how Simpler treats the sample:
        • Classic (default) — pitched melodic and harmonic instruments, full ADSR, looping, polyphonic.
        • One-Shot — monophonic drum hits or short phrases; simple envelope, no loop, plays the whole sample by default.
        • Slicing — non-destructive slices played chromatically, set automatically or by hand; suited to drum breaks.

      • 31.11.1.1 Classic Playback Mode

        • The left and right flags bound the usable region. Start and Length are percentages of it; Length 50% plays half. Loop sets how much loops, when the Loop switch is on.
        • Very short loops turn glitchy or granular, or pitched at audio rates, and can load the CPU heavily, especially in Complex warp modes.
        • Snap puts markers on zero crossings of the left channel, so stereo samples can still click. Fade crossfades loop end into start, useful for long textures.
        • Gain — sample level, separate from the final Volume after the filter.
        • Voices — when exceeded, the oldest voices are stolen.
        • Retrig — replaying a sounding note cuts it off; off lets copies overlap. Audible only with long releases and more than one voice.

      • 31.11.1.2 One-Shot Playback Mode

        • Flags set the region; no Loop, Length or Voices, and always monophonic.
        • Trigger — plays on after note-off, so hold length doesn't matter. Gate — starts fading at note-off over the Fade Out time.
        • Fade In — time to full volume; Fade Out — starts that long before the region end.
        • Snap applies to the start and end flags only.

      • 31.11.1.3 Slicing Playback Mode

        • Slice By:
          • Transient — at transients; Sensitivity sets how many, up to 64.
          • Beat — at a beat Division.
          • Region — a number of equal Regions.
          • Manual — double-click to add slices.
        • Playback — Mono plays one slice at a time; Poly plays several, with Voices and Retrig; Thru is monophonic but plays on to the end of the region.
        • Trigger/Gate as in One-Shot. Fades span each slice in Mono and Poly, or from the triggered slice to the region end in Thru.
        • Automatic slices are blue and manual ones white. Drag to move, double-click to delete; in Transient mode, Option-click (Mac) / Alt-click (Win) toggles a slice between manual and automatic. Manual slices survive Sensitivity changes.

    • 31.11.2 Warp Controls

      • Warp off plays the sample faster or slower with pitch, like a classic sampler. On, it follows the Set's tempo whatever the note, with the same Warp Modes as audio clips.
      • Warp as… fits the sample to a number of bars or beats; ÷2 and ×2 fix a wrong guess.

    • 31.11.4 Envelopes

      • Three ADSR envelopes, for amplitude, filter and pitch, chosen with buttons and edited with knobs or handles. Filter and pitch have amount controls at the top right.
      • The amplitude envelope can loop: Loop and Trigger restart from the start if the note outlasts Decay, taking Time to return; Beat and Sync restart after Rate.
31.11 Simpler

    • 31.11.5 LFO

      • Sine, square, triangle, saw down, saw up and random, free from 0.01 to 30 Hz or synced. Runs per voice.
      • Attack — time to full depth. R — reset to Offset on each note; Offset does nothing otherwise.
      • Key — higher notes get faster LFOs; at zero all voices share a rate.
      • Volume, Pitch, Pan, Filter — modulation depths.
31.11 Simpler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/11 Simpler/06 Global Parameters

    • 31.11.6 Global Parameters

      • Pan, with Random > Pan and LFO modulation.
      • Spread — two detuned voices per note panned apart, read at note-on, so some notes can be stereo and others mono.
      • Volume and Velocity > Volume; LFO on volume gives tremolo.
      • C3 plays the original pitch. Transpose ±48 semitones, Detune ±50 cents, pitch bend ±5 semitones; the pitch envelope suits percussive sounds.
      • Glide (mono) or Portamento (poly) with Time.

      • From the sample display or title bar:
        • Use Constant Power Fade for Loops — off gives linear crossfades. Fade is unavailable while warping.
        • Simpler -> Sampler — converts for multisample use; warping and slicing don't carry over, so such presets behave very differently.

  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/11 Simpler/08 Strategies for Saving CPU Power

    • 31.11.8 Strategies for Saving CPU Power

      • Complex and Complex Pro warp modes cost much more CPU than the others.
      • Turn the filter off if unused; 24 dB slopes cost more than 12 dB.
      • Turning off the LFO helps a little.
      • Stereo samples need about twice the processing of mono.
      • Lower Voices, and set Spread to 0% if unused.

31.12 Tension
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/12 Tension/01 Architecture and Interface

    • 31.12.1 Architecture and Interface

      • An exciter (hammer, pick or bow) sets the string vibrating. The fret or finger termination sets its length and so its pitch; a damper shortens its decay. The body, through the bridge or a pickup, radiates the sound. Tension adds a filter between string and body.
      • String tab: Exciter, Damper, String, Vibrato, Termination, Pickup and Body. Filter/Global tab: Filter, MPE and global performance controls.
      • Every section except String and Keyboard can be turned off, which saves CPU.
31.12 Tension
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/12 Tension/02 String Tab

    • 31.12.2 String Tab

      • The string's physical properties and how it is played.

      • 31.12.2.1 The Exciter Section

        • With the Exciter off, only the damper can move the string. With Exciter and Damper both off there is no sound.

      • 31.12.2.2 Exciter Types

        • Bow — sustained vibration from stick-and-slip friction, as on violin, viola and cello. Damping is unavailable.
        • Hammer — strikes from below once and falls away, as in a piano.
        • Hammer (bouncing) — dropped from above and may bounce, as on a hammered dulcimer.
        • Plectrum — snaps the string, as on a guitar or harpsichord.

      • 31.12.2.3 Exciter Parameters

        • Type-specific: Bow has Force (more is scratchier) and Friction (more gives a faster attack); the Hammers have Mass and Stiffness; Plectrum has Protrusion (less is thinner) and Stiffness.
        • Shared: Velocity — how fast the exciter hits. Position — 0% at the termination, 50% at the midpoint. Damping — how much impact force the exciter absorbs; for the bouncing hammer it sets the stiffness of its spring, and more makes a shorter, louder, brighter contact.
        • Fix. Pos — keeps the contact point fixed regardless of string length, like a guitar pick. Leave it off for a piano, whose hammers strike about 1/7 of the length.
        • Vel and Key modulate by velocity and pitch. Some combinations produce no sound.

      • 31.12.2.4 The Damper Section

        • Dampers mute the string, like felt in a piano or a player's fingers, and make sounds of their own.
        • Mass — how hard it presses; more mutes faster. Stiffness — felt to metal. Very high values can shorten the string and change its tuning.
        • Velocity — how fast the damper lands at key release and lifts at key press; very high values can make a loud release. Works only with Gated on; with Gated off the damper stays on the string.
        • Position, Fix. Pos and Key sliders as in the Exciter.
        • Damping — overall absorption. Above 50% the damper gets stiff enough to bounce, which lengthens decay again; try it on one repeated key.

      • The String Section

        • Decay with < Key. Ratio — shortens release decay relative to onset decay.
        • Inharm — real strings are inharmonic, more so when thicker; raising it detunes upper partials.
        • Damping with < Key — more high partials at higher values.

      • The Vibrato Section

        • An LFO on pitch: Delay, Attack, Rate, Amount, < Mod (mod wheel depth relative to Amount) and Error (random variation of rate, amount, delay and attack).

      • 31.12.2.5 The Termination Section

        • Models finger, fret and string, which set the string's working length. Finger Mass (with Vel and Key), Finger Stiff and Fret Stiff.

      • 31.12.2.6 The Pickup Section

        • An electromagnetic pickup. Position 0% at the termination is brighter and thinner; toward 50% is fuller.

      • The Body Section

        • The body radiates and filters the string's energy; some, like guitars, have an air cavity that boosts lows.
        • Body Type after real instruments, Body Size XS to XL (larger is lower), Decay, Str/Body (dry string versus body; fully left bypasses the body), Low Cut and High Cut.
        • Volume sets the overall output and is also on the Filter/Global tab.
31.12 Tension
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/12 Tension/03 Filter-Global Tab

    • 31.12.3 Filter/Global Tab

      • A polyphonic filter with envelope, LFO and MIDI modulation, keyboard and portamento options, and a Unison effect.
      • Filter — between String and Body, with its own on switch; 2nd- or 4th-order low-pass, band-pass, notch, high-pass or formant. Freq and Res (vowels for formant), each modulated by LFO, envelope and pitch; the LFO and Env sliders need those sections on.
      • Filter Envelope — ADSR with Vel on Attack (faster at higher velocity) and Vel on Sustain (higher at higher velocity).
      • Filter LFO — sine, triangle, rectangle, stepped random or smooth random; Rate in Hz or synced; Attack and Delay.
      • MPE — Pressure and Slide each reach two destinations with amounts; activity LEDs; global Pitch Bend and per-note Note PB ranges.
      • Keyboard — Octave, Semi, Detune (±50 cents), Voices, Stretch tuning (sharper highs and flatter lows, or the reverse when negative), Error (random tuning per note) and Priority (High, Low or Last).
      • Portamento — Time, Legato (only overlapping notes slide) and Prop. (time scales with the interval).
      • Unison — two or four stacked Voices, Detune from subtle chorus to wide, and Delay between voices.
      • Volume — shared with the String tab.
31.12 Tension
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/12 Tension/04 Sound Design Tips

    • 31.12.4 Sound Design Tips

      • Tension's parts affect each other, so it is easy to find settings that make no sound, or very loud ones; watch levels.
      • Think of the sections as one physical object: a slow bow can start an undamped string, but a heavy damper needs a faster bow.
      • The presets show it can do much more than strings.
