Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types
  • The oscillator models from V.Analog through Noise were developed by Mutable Instruments and introduced in its Plaits module in 2018. Arturia included these open-source designs in the MicroFreak. For more detail about the waveforms, see the Plaits manual.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/12 Modal
  • Description: A modal resonator imitates how an object's shape amplifies some harmonics and dampens others when it vibrates. This lets it mimic instrument bodies such as woodwinds, strings, and drums. It needs an exciter signal to produce sound, as a real instrument needs a bow, drumstick, or breath. Its harmonic structure and the instrument shape it mimics can change as a knob turns or a Matrix source such as the LFO or Envelope modulates it.
  • The resonator also controls how quickly the sound fades. Its damping can change with a knob or a Matrix source.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/13 Noise
  • Description: This oscillator mixes a noise source with a sine, triangle, or sawtooth oscillator. Noise particles are tiny noise fragments that originate when noise is sampled down.
  • Wave: Selects the noise source, from particle noise through white noise to metallic noise. Turning the Wave knob clockwise morphs the resulting wave from particle noise to white noise to metallic noise.
Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/02 LFO Rate
  • Matching the LFO rate to the MicroFreak sequencer, a DAW, or another clock source can create rhythms at multiples or divisions of the clock.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/02 Gates and Triggers
  • The keyboard is the MicroFreak's primary gate source. Touching and holding a key starts the envelope's Attack stage, followed by Decay/Release and Sustain. The envelope stays at the Sustain level while the key is held. Releasing the key ends the gate, and the level falls to zero at the rate set by Decay/Release.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/05 Amp Mod Button
  • Turning on Amp Mod activates the Envelope Generator to shape the volume with those gates. The envelope output also always goes to the filter; Filter Amount sets its effect on cutoff. The envelope is also available as a Matrix source for controlling other MicroFreak modules.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/01 Gates and Triggers
  • The envelope section introduced gates and triggers. These signals also matter when playing the keyboard, which is the MicroFreak's primary source of gates.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/02 Keyboard Responsiveness

  • Note/Info Assign Press as the Matrix source and the Envelope's Sustain knob as a modulation target to experiment with keyboard and volume effects.

Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/03 Glide
  • Pressure can modulate Glide: set Glide as a Matrix destination and Pressure as the source, then adjust the Matrix attenuator. Different LFO waveforms produce different glide intensity and slope. With LFO Sync on, each note gets a different glide shape; changing the LFO rate changes the synchronized rate and the glide speed.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/05 LFO Speed Tutorial
  • Set an Assignable Matrix destination to LFO Rate and connect keyboard Pressure as its source.
Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/07 Arp Fun
  • The Arpeggiator can act as a modulation source. The effect is subtle, so increase the modulation amount and extend the octave range. Routing it to two destinations can make the movement easier to hear.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/04 Copy
  • Hold the source pattern's A or B icon, then press the destination pattern's icon. To copy A to B, hold A and press B; to copy B to A, hold B and press A. Check the display for confirmation.
Microfreak/UG/14 Config/01 Utility & MCC/02 Global
  • Source: Internal, USB, MIDI, Clock, or Auto; chooses the tempo source.
Microfreak/UG/14 Config/02 MIDI Control Center/01 Device Tab
  • MIDI clock source: USB, MIDI, or Sync. USB is the built-in computer connection; MIDI uses the 5-pin DIN input.
Microfreak/UG/14 Config/02 MIDI Control Center/02 Wavetables Tab/01 Management
  • Each group of 2,048 source samples counts as a cycle.
  • The source's first and last cycles become cycles 1 and 32.
  • With more than eight source cycles, MicroFreak distributes them evenly across the wavetable and crossfades between them.
  • With fewer than eight source cycles, it spreads them across the slots so the Wave knob can reach every cycle.
Microfreak/UG/19 Appendix B Vocoder/08 Global Settings
  • Auto Gain is the default and suits average use with the Vocoder Edition microphone. A lavalier microphone or external audio source may need manual gain.
Microfreak/UG/19 Appendix B Vocoder/08 Global Settings/03 Set Gain/03 External Sources
  • An external audio source can modulate the vocoder, including a guitar, drum machine, phone, tablet, or Eurorack mixer. Match its output level to the MicroFreak input: these sources are usually much louder than a microphone.
  • Use a headphone/microphone splitter that connects the source to the mic contact of the MicroFreak’s 3.5 mm TRRS jack.
  • Select a vocoder preset, hold a key, and slowly raise Mic Gain. If there is no sound, slowly raise the source device’s output level.
