Microfreak/UG/01 Welcome and Introduction
  • The MicroFreak is a compact, versatile, semi-modular synthesizer with many distinctive features. It lets you experiment with modular sound construction without patch cords.
Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/01 Top Row/03 Panel Select
  • A loaded preset recalls saved parameter values, which may differ from the panel's current knob positions. Press Panel to apply the current knob positions to the sound, so subsequent edits match what you hear.
Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/02 Middle Row/02 Digital Osc
  • The Digital Oscillator generates the MicroFreak's core sound. The Analog Filter, Envelopes, and LFO shape it. Type, Wave, Timbre, and Shape control its parameters; Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc explains them in detail.
Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/02 Middle Row/03 Analog Filter
  • The Analog Filter emphasizes or suppresses harmonics in the Digital Oscillator sound, changing its timbre. Cutoff sets where filtering begins; Resonance emphasizes frequencies near that point. Microfreak/UG/07 Filter explains the filter in detail.
Microfreak/UG/04 Presets/01 Load Presets

  • Note/Warning By default, loading another preset clears unsaved changes without warning. Turn on Utility > Global > Controls > Click to Load to browse without replacing the current sound; click the encoder to save the current changes and load the selected preset.

  • Press the Preset encoder quickly three times to reset the current sound to its initial empty state. This also clears an existing preset's sound.
Microfreak/UG/04 Presets/04 Panel
  • After loading a preset, the MicroFreak plays the sound stored in memory. The panel's current knob positions may differ from those saved positions, so moving a knob may not immediately match the sound you hear.
  • Press Panel once after loading a preset to apply all current knob positions to the sound. The preset may sound different, but the panel positions will then correspond to the sound.
Microfreak/UG/04 Presets/05 Digital Control
  • Every MicroFreak module, including the analog filter, is controlled digitally. This combines the warmth and direct control of analog sound with the ability to save and recall presets and settings.
  • In the default mode, move the knob past its stored value to hook it. Press Panel to bypass the loaded preset and hear a sound based on the panel's current knob and slider positions.
Microfreak/UG/05 Connections/01 Control Signals
  • The Matrix connects control signals from the MicroFreak's modules. These signals move more slowly than audio and are suited to controlling sound.
Microfreak/UG/05 Connections/02 Matrix and Encoder/01 Sources and Destinations
  • Pitch, Wave, and Timbre modify the Digital Oscillator's sound.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc
  • The Digital Oscillator is the very heart of the MicroFreak. It's the digital circuit that generates the core sound of this instrument. The other parts of the MicroFreak - the Analog Filter, the Envelopes, and the LFO - exist only to shape/mangle/wobble the sound of the Digital Oscillator.
  • The Oscillator in the MicroFreak is unique in that it can emulate many different synthesis models. In the history of electronic music, many gifted sound engineers developed unique ways of generating sounds. Voltage Controlled Oscillators, the pitch of which could be controlled with an external voltage, FM oscillators that created timbres by modulating two or more oscillators, Harmonic oscillators use combinations of harmonics to create complex

    timbres. You'll find many of these Oscillator models in the MicroFreak, and we hope you'll have as much fun with them as the engineers who designed them.

Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/01 as Soundgen

  • 06.01 The oscillator as a sound generator

    • When you hear a sound, you hear air vibrating against the eardrum of your ear. As humans, we can hear frequencies in the range of 20 to 20,000 Hz. As you age your ability to hear high frequencies will gradually diminish. At 65 you'll probably hear only frequencies up to 6000 Hz. But that is enough to be able to enjoy timbral variations in music.
    • The ear can perceive sound around 50 Hz as bass (think huge organ pipes), but around 30 Hz it's difficult for the ear to hear a sound as a pitch; it's perceived as a low rumble. In electronic music, low-frequency sounds are often used as voltage or control signals to modulate another module. An LFO is an oscillator specifically designed to generate frequencies in this range. The LFO in the MicroFreak can generate signals in the range from 0.1Hz to 100Hz. Please refer to Microfreak/UG/08 LFO for details.

    • Note/Info Modulation is not limited to this range; some audio oscillator models in the MicroFreak use a second oscillator to modulate their own frequency.

    • The Digital Oscillator
    • The Digital Oscillator can play notes in the range from C-2 to G8. Although the MicroFreak keyboard spans only two octaves, you can shift the range it plays up and down.
    • Freaky idea: Applying a (very) small dose of randomness to the pitch of the digital oscillator will make someone who listens to your track sit up and pay attention.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/02 Param Controls
  • Each model has three sound-shaping parameters: Wave, Timbre, and Shape. Their effects depend on the model. Turning a knob shows its current function on the display; the oscillator overview uses those display names and puts the model's screen name in parentheses.
  • Wave, Timbre, and Shape can each be a modulation destination in the Matrix, so a sound can change as the modulation changes.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/02 Param Controls/01 Knob Speed
  • Slow allows precise sound-design edits; Fast suits broader changes during a performance. In either mode, the knobs accelerate when turned more quickly.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/01 BasicWaves
  • Description: Every sound consists of a series of harmonics. The first harmonic is the fundamental, which determines the pitch you hear. The second harmonic is twice as high in pitch, the third is three times as high, and so on. On a guitar, touching the exact middle of a string produces the second harmonic; dividing the string into three parts produces the third harmonic.
  • The second and higher harmonics determine a sound's timbre. The second, fourth, sixth, and eighth harmonics are even; the third, fifth, seventh, and ninth are odd. Odd harmonics can add a more dissonant timbre.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/02 SuperWave
  • Description: This is a digital waveform animator that creates copies of a waveform and detunes them. Detuning them creates a very fat, lush sound. Unlike more traditional waveform animators that multiply a sawtooth wave, this model allows you to select from four different waveforms.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/04 Harmo
  • Description: Harmonic oscillators recreate sound by creating and summing harmonics. Varying the amplitude of individual harmonics changes the timbre. This oscillator sums complete waveforms, not just up to eight harmonics, producing more complex sounds than traditional harmonic oscillators.
  • Chorus: Adds chorus to the oscillator sound, making it wider.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/05 KarplusStr
  • Description: Karplus-Strong is a sound synthesis method developed by Kevin Karplus and Alex Strong at Stanford University. It creates realistic drum and plucked-string sounds by looping a short noise burst through a filtered delay. This physical modeling approach recreates an instrument's physical characteristics digitally, such as string bow position, striking force, and the materials' diffusion and damping.
  • Position: Controls where and how hard the resonator is struck. It does not affect the bowed part of the sound.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/07 Waveshaper
  • Description: Combines a waveshaper and a wavefolder. The waveshaper changes a wave's rising and falling stages: it can steepen a triangle's rise into a falling saw, or change the curves of either stage. These changes affect the number and amplitude of harmonics, with more harmonics producing a richer, sometimes sharper, sound. The original model was based on a feature of the Serge waveshaper.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/10 Chords
  • A major chord may sound forceful and happy, while a minor chord may sound sad to listeners raised with Western music. Responses to major and minor scales differ across cultures.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/11 Speech
  • Description: The Speech oscillator draws on speech synthesis research begun by Texas Instruments in the late 1970s, which led to the Speak & Spell talking toy. Vowels use unrestricted airflow while the throat and tongue shape their sound; consonants delimit and shape vowels.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/12 Modal
  • Description: A modal resonator imitates how an object's shape amplifies some harmonics and dampens others when it vibrates. This lets it mimic instrument bodies such as woodwinds, strings, and drums. It needs an exciter signal to produce sound, as a real instrument needs a bow, drumstick, or breath. Its harmonic structure and the instrument shape it mimics can change as a knob turns or a Matrix source such as the LFO or Envelope modulates it.
  • The resonator also controls how quickly the sound fades. Its damping can change with a knob or a Matrix source.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/19 Scan Grains
  • The Scan grains oscillator plays short slices (grains) of a loaded sample. Each grain has a volume envelope for a smooth sound, and the oscillator scans from the sample's start to its end at a user-defined speed.
Microfreak/UG/07 Filter

  • 07 The Filter: sound in close-up

    • A filter emphasizes or suppresses harmonics in a sound, changing its timbre. It is traditionally paired with an oscillator; the MicroFreak's analog filter shapes the sound of its Digital Oscillator.
    • The Analogue Filter
    • Think of the Analog Filter as a searchlight moving over the oscillator's waveform, revealing its harmonic content. Its beam can be broad or focused; the focused width is called Q or resonance.
    • A sound contains sine-wave frequencies at different loudness levels. These frequencies often form families around a fundamental frequency, with related harmonics above it. The mix of odd and even harmonics largely determines a sound's character. A filter favors some frequencies and attenuates others.
    • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound

      • 07.01 Modifying sound

        • The MicroFreak has a Low Pass Filter (LPF), a Band Pass Filter (BPF), and a High Pass Filter (HPF). The Type button switches between them.
        • A low-pass filter attenuates frequencies above its cutoff; a high-pass filter attenuates frequencies below it; a band-pass filter attenuates frequencies on both sides of the cutoff.
        • With a low-pass filter open at maximum cutoff, all frequencies pass. Lowering the cutoff removes the highs first, then the midrange; closing it completely leaves silence. In high-pass mode, maximum cutoff removes all frequencies; lowering it lets frequencies above the cutoff pass. In band-pass mode, frequencies near the cutoff remain audible, and resonance sets the width of that range.
        • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/01 Low Pass Filter

          • 07.01.01 Low Pass Filter

            • A Low Pass Filter removes frequencies above its cutoff. It is central to subtractive synthesis and selectively reduces a sound's high frequencies. Modulating the cutoff over time varies the sound's timbre.

            • Note/Info The MicroFreak filter has a 12 dB-per-octave roll-off. Its slope is gentler than a 24 dB filter, which suits oscillators with rich, complex harmonics such as grains, wavetables, shapers, and FM.

        • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/02 Band Pass Filter

          • 07.01.02 Band Pass Filter

            • A Band Pass Filter lets only a narrow frequency range pass. At minimum resonance, all frequencies pass; turning Resonance clockwise narrows the range. At its maximum, the filter self-oscillates and can act as a sine-wave oscillator, while blocking the Digital Oscillator's sound.
        • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/03 High Pass Filter

          • 07.01.03 High Pass Filter

            • A High Pass Filter removes frequencies below its cutoff. Raising the cutoff removes the lows first, then the midrange, leaving the highest harmonics.
            • Use it to accent beats in an arpeggio or bass loop, or to clear low frequencies from a solo sound that competes with a bass part in a mix.
            • Its main controls are Cutoff frequency and Resonance.
        • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/04 Cutoff Frequency

          • 07.01.04 Cutoff frequency

            • The cutoff frequency is where filtering takes place. Feeding a filter's output back into itself creates a resonance peak around the cutoff. The Resonance knob controls this effect; the LFO or envelopes can also modulate resonance when assigned in the Matrix.
            • Filters attenuate frequencies gradually above or below the cutoff. This slope, or roll-off, depends on the number of poles: a four-pole filter has a steeper roll-off than a two-pole filter. The MicroFreak filter rolls off at 12 dB per octave.
            • The Cutoff knob moves the cutoff point from about 30 Hz at its fully counter-clockwise position to above 15 kHz at its fully clockwise position.
        • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/05 Resonance

          • 07.01.05 Resonance or Q

            • Resonance, also called Emphasis or Q (for quality of filtering), amplifies frequencies close to the cutoff. Turning the Resonance knob clockwise makes the filter more selective and gives the sound a stronger ringing character. At its extreme setting, the filter self-oscillates.
    • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/02 Animating Sound

      • 07.02 Animating sound

        • Changing the cutoff and resonance dynamically makes the filter a musical tool. The MicroFreak can use an LFO, an Arpeggiator, or an Envelope to control these parameters. See Microfreak/UG/08 LFO for the LFO; the Arpeggiator and Envelope chapters cover the other modulators.
        • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/02 Animating Sound/01 Cutoff Modulation

          • 07.02.01 Cutoff Modulation

            • Moving the cutoff changes the sound's timbre. The most effective and musically pleasing way to modulate it is with an envelope generator. The MicroFreak connects the main envelope to filter cutoff; the Filter Amt knob beside Sustain sets the modulation amount.

            • Note/Info Changing Filter Amt lights the corresponding point on the Matrix. The filter amount can also be fine-tuned there.

        • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/02 Animating Sound/02 Emphasis Resonance Modulation

          • 07.02.02 Emphasis/Resonance Modulation

            • Increasing emphasis focuses the filter's response around the cutoff, boosting nearby frequencies.

            • Note/Info A sequencer modulation track can create stepped harmonic effects by assigning a different filter-cutoff modulation amount to each step.
