31.1 Analog
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/01 Architecture and Interface

    • 31.1.1 Architecture and Interface

      • Two oscillators and a noise generator feed two multi-mode filters, each followed by an amplifier. The filters can run in series or in parallel.
      • Two LFOs modulate oscillators, filters and amps, and each filter and amp has its own envelope.
      • The interface is a central display surrounded by the shell. The shell holds each section's main controls; the display shows the selected section's visualizations and extra controls.
      • A Global section holds volume, vibrato, polyphony and similar settings, and an MPE section maps pressure, slide and per-note pitch bend.
31.1 Analog
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/02 Oscillators

    • 31.1.2 Oscillators

      • Modeled oscillators, so no aliasing. Each has an on switch (Osc 1, Osc 2) and a level slider.
      • F1/F2 — balance between Filter 1 and Filter 2; centered sends equally.
      • Shape — sine, sawtooth, rectangular or white noise. Rectangular enables Pulse Width (100% is a square with odd harmonics only; low values sound thin), which an LFO can modulate.
      • Octave, Semi, Detune — tuning; Detune moves in cents, up to 300 cents either way.
      • Pitch Mod — LFO amount, and Key: how far pitch follows the keyboard. 100% is equal temperament, 0% ignores note pitch. C3 always plays the same frequency, so oscillators with different Key values move further out of tune with each other the farther you play from C3.
      • Pitch Env — Initial pitch and the Time to glide to the final pitch.
      • Sub/Sync — Sub adds an oscillator an octave down (square for saw or rectangle, sine for sine, off for noise) at a set Level. Sync restarts the waveform from a hidden oscillator whose rate rises with Ratio; 0% has no effect. Mapping Ratio to a mod wheel is a suggested use.
31.1 Analog
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/03 Noise Generator

    • 31.1.3 Noise Generator

      • White noise through its own -6 dB/octave low-pass filter, with an on switch and level.
      • F1/F2 — balance between the filters.
      • Color — the low-pass frequency; higher is brighter.
      • Noise has shell controls only, so the display doesn't change.
31.1 Analog
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/04 Filters

    • 31.1.4 Filters

      • Two multi-mode filters with independent settings, flexible routing, saturation and modulation.
      • Fil 1 / Fil 2 — on switches; the chooser beside each picks 2nd- or 4th-order low-pass, band-pass, notch, high-pass or formant.
      • Freq and Reso — cutoff and resonance. With a formant filter, Reso moves between vowel sounds. Filter 1's Freq: Ableton/Instrument/Analog/Filter/1/Freq
      • To F2 (Filter 1) — how much of Filter 1's output feeds Filter 2.
      • Follow (Filter 2) — Filter 2's cutoff tracks Filter 1, including its modulation; Filter 2's Freq then sets the offset.
      • Freq Mod and Res Mod — modulation of cutoff and resonance by LFO, note pitch and filter envelope; negative values lower them.
      • Drive — saturation after the filter: Off, three symmetrical Sym modes, or asymmetrical Asym modes; higher numbers distort more.
31.1 Analog
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/05 Amplifiers

    • 31.1.5 Amplifiers

      • After each filter an amplifier sets level and pan, with its own envelope.
      • Amp 1 / Amp 2 — on switches; Level and Pan knobs.
      • Pan Mod and Level Mod — modulation by LFO, note pitch and amp envelope. With note pitch on Level, middle C stays the same and positive values make higher notes louder.
31.1 Analog
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/06 Envelopes

    • 31.1.6 Envelopes

      • Besides the oscillator pitch envelopes, each filter and amp has an ADSR envelope, all four with the same controls in the display, plus velocity and looping.
      • Attack, with Att < Vel to shorten it at higher velocities; Decay; Sustain (fully left removes sustain, fully right removes decay); Release.
      • Env < Vel — velocity scales the whole envelope.
      • S.Time — lets the sustain level fall while the key is held; lower is faster.
      • Slope — linear or exponential segments.
      • Legato — a note played while another is held continues that note's envelope.
      • Free — skips sustain and goes straight from decay to release, so notes have equal length; suits percussion.
      • Loop:
        • Off — plays once.
        • AD-R — attack and decay repeat until release.
        • ADR-R — attack, decay and release repeat while held.
        • ADS-R — no looping, but attack and release play once more on key release, which can mimic audible dampers.
        • With Free on, AD-R and ADR-R act as if the key is always held.
31.1 Analog
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/07 LFOs

    • 31.1.7 LFOs

      • Two independent LFOs that modulate oscillators, filters and amps.
      • LFO 1 / LFO 2 switches, Rate in Hz or tempo-synced divisions.
      • Wave — sine, triangle, rectangle, stepped random noise or smoothed noise. Width shapes triangle (toward up or down saw) and rectangle (pulse width); 50% gives a pure triangle or square.
      • Delay — time before the LFO starts; Attack — time to full depth.
      • Retrig — restart at the same phase on each note; Offset shifts the phase.
31.1 Analog
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/08 Global Parameters

    • 31.1.8 Global Parameters

      • Volume, vibrato, unison, glide, routing presets and keyboard settings.
      • Volume — overall output.
      • Vib — a dedicated pitch LFO on both oscillators with amount and Rate. Display adds Delay, Attack, Error (random variation per voice) and Amt < MW (mod wheel control of depth).
      • Uni — stacks voices per note with Detune; the display picks two or four Voices and a Delay between them.
      • Gli — glide with Time; with Legato, glide only on overlapping notes. Glide Mode Const keeps time fixed; Prop scales it with the interval.
      • Quick Routing — four buttons set common routings without touching levels, tuning or waveforms: each oscillator to its own filter and amp; both oscillators split across both filters; everything through Filter 1 and Amp 1; or serial Filter 1 into Filter 2 and Amp 2.
      • Keyboard section:
        • Octave, Semi, Detune (±50 cents) for the whole instrument.
        • PB Range — pitch bend range in semitones.
        • Stretch — stretch tuning as on pianos: positive raises high notes and lowers low ones; negative does the reverse.
        • Error — random tuning per note.
        • Voices and Priority — polyphony, and which notes get cut when it runs out: High keeps higher notes, Low keeps lower, Last keeps the newest.
31.1 Analog
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/09 MPE Sources

    • 31.1.9 MPE Sources

      • The MPE switch in the Global display shows three MPE sources: Pressure, Slide and NotePB (per-note pitch bend).
      • Pressure and Slide each have two destination choosers with amount sliders, and activity LEDs.
      • Note PB — per-note pitch bend range in semitones.

31.2 Collision
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/01 Architecture and Interface

    • 31.2.1 Architecture and Interface

      • Two exciters, Mallet and Noise, feed two stereo resonators that can be independent or linked. The exciters start the sound; the resonators shape most of its character. With both exciters off there is no sound.
      • The interface has Mallet and Noise sections; Resonator 1 and Resonator 2 tabs; an LFO tab with two LFOs; a MIDI/MPE tab for pitch bend, mod wheel, aftertouch and their MPE versions; and global settings for voices, retrigger, resonator structure and volume.
      • Turning off unused sections saves CPU.
31.2 Collision
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/02 Mallet Section

    • 31.2.2 Mallet Section

      • Simulates a mallet striking a surface; the controls set the mallet's physical properties. The Mallet button turns it on.
      • Volume — output level.
      • Stiffness — soft mallets give fewer highs and a longer, blurrier hit; harder ones a shorter, brighter hit.
      • Noise — impact noise in each strike, like the "chiff" of a felt mallet.
      • Color — noise frequency; higher removes lows. No effect when Noise is 0.
      • Volume, Stiffness and Noise can follow pitch and velocity through the Key and Vel sliders in the MIDI tab.
31.2 Collision
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/03 Noise Section

    • 31.2.3 Noise Section

      • A second exciter: white noise through a multimode filter with its own envelope, usable instead of or with the Mallet. The Noise button turns it on.
      • Filter types LP, HP, BP and LP+HP. Freq sets cutoff or center; Res sets resonance, or bandwidth in LP+HP. Cutoff can follow note pitch, velocity or the envelope.
      • Volume — output level, modulated by pitch and velocity in the MIDI tab.
      • Env Amt with A, D, S, R — an ADSR envelope on the filter. S at 0 removes sustain; at 100 removes decay.
31.2 Collision
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/04 Resonator Tabs

    • 31.2.4 Resonator Tabs

      • The resonators define most of the sound. Each tab has an on switch; with both off there is no sound.
      • Resonance Type:
        • Beam — beams of various materials and sizes.
        • Marimba — a Beam variant with the overtone tuning of arch-cut marimba bars.
        • String — strings of various materials and sizes.
        • Membrane — a rectangular membrane like a drum head.
        • Plate — a flat rectangular plate.
        • Pipe — a tube open at one end with an adjustable opening at the other.
        • Tube — a tube closed at both ends.
      • Quality — Eco to High, trading overtone detail for CPU. Not on Pipe or Tube.
      • 1 → 2 / 2 → 1 — copy one resonator's settings to the other.
      • X-Y Controller — horizontal sets decay, vertical sets Material or Radius.
      • Decay — internal damping.
      • Material — low values let lows ring longer (wood, rubber, nylon); high values let highs ring longer (glass, metal). Pipe and Tube use Radius instead: larger means longer decay and more sustained highs, and at extremes a pitch change.
      • Ratio (Membrane, Plate) — proportion of the object's two sides.
      • Brightness — louder highs at higher values. Not on Pipe or Tube.
      • Inharm — negative compresses partials downward, positive stretches them upward. Not on Pipe or Tube.
      • Opening (Pipe) — 0% closed at one end, 100% open at both.
      • Hit — strike point, center at 0% toward the edge at higher values; Rnd randomizes it. Not on Pipe or Tube.
      • Note Off — how much note-off mutes the resonance; 0% ignores it like a real marimba, 100% mutes at once.
      • Pos. L / Pos. R — where vibrations are picked up on each side, center to edge. Pipe and Tube are always measured at the open end.
      • Decay, Material/Radius, Inharm and Opening can be modulated in the MIDI tab.

      • 31.2.4.1 Tuning Section

        • Tune in semitones and Fine in cents, up to ±50.
        • Key in the MIDI tab sets keyboard tracking: 100% is equal temperament, 200% makes each half step a whole step, negative values fall in pitch as you play higher.
        • Pitch Env and Time — a starting pitch that glides to the final pitch; velocity can scale the start.

      • 31.2.4.2 Mixer Section

        • Gain and Pan per resonator; pan can follow note pitch.
        • Bleed — mixes in the dry exciter signal, which helps restore highs lost at low tuning or quality.
31.2 Collision
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/05 LFO Tab

    • 31.2.5 LFO Tab

      • Two LFOs modulate Mallet, Noise and resonator parameters, and each other.
      • Waveforms: sine, square, triangle, saw up, saw down, stepped noise and smooth noise.
      • Offs. — phase offset, used when Retrigger restarts the LFO on each note.
      • Two Destination choosers per LFO, each with an amount relative to the LFO's Amount.
      • Rate in Hz or synced divisions; Rate can follow pitch and Amount velocity in the MIDI tab.
31.2 Collision
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/06 MIDI-MPE Tab

    • 31.2.6 MIDI/MPE Tab

      • Maps pitch bend (including per-note), mod wheel, pressure and slide to two destinations each, with amounts.
      • Key and Vel sliders let note pitch and velocity modulate further Mallet, Noise, resonator and LFO parameters.

      • 31.2.6.1 The Global Section

        • Voices — maximum polyphony.
        • Retrig. — retriggers a playing note instead of adding a voice, saving CPU.
        • Structure — 1 > 2 (serial): both exciters feed Resonator 1, whose mono mix also feeds Resonator 2 while Resonator 1 reaches its own mixer in stereo. 1 + 2 (parallel): the exciter mix feeds both resonators.
        • Volume — overall output.
31.2 Collision
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/07 Sound Design Tips

    • 31.2.7 Sound Design Tips

      • For a realistic marimba, follow the physical chain: the Mallet strikes a tuned bar (Resonator 1), and a tube (Resonator 2) amplifies it, in serial 1 > 2.
      • For unrealistic sounds:
        • Use the Noise exciter with long envelopes for washy, near-granular textures, or effects like bowed vibraphone and crystal glasses.
        • Try the parallel 1 + 2 structure.
        • Modulate with the LFOs and MIDI or MPE controllers.
      • The models are idealized and can respond far more strongly than real objects. Some settings jump sharply in volume, so keep levels low while experimenting.

31.3 Drift
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/03 Drift/01 Subtractive Synthesis

    • 31.3.1 Subtractive Synthesis

      • Subtractive synthesis starts from a harmonically rich waveform and carves it with filters. Drift adds modulation on top.
      • The Drift control gives each voice small pitch and filter variations, so the tone wavers slightly out of tune.
31.3 Drift
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/03 Drift/02 Oscillator Section

    • 31.3.2 Oscillator Section

      • Two oscillators, pitch modulation, a waveform display, an oscillator mixer and a noise generator.

      • 31.3.2.1 Oscillator 1

        • Waveforms: Sine, Triangle, Shark Tooth, Saturated, Saw, Pulse, Rectangle. Shark Tooth is based on a classic Moog shape; Saturated suits bass.
        • Oct transposes by octaves. Shape alters the harmonics, somewhat like pulse-width modulation, differently per waveform; the Waveform Display shows the result.
        • Shape Mod Source — Env 1, Env 2/Cyc, LFO, Key (more modulation for higher notes when positive), Velocity, Modwheel, Pressure or Slide, with an amount of -100% to 100%. Shape Mod can move the waveform even when Shape is 0%.

      • 31.3.2.2 Oscillator 2

        • Waveforms: Sine, Triangle, Saturated, Saw, Rectangle. Oct in octaves, Detune in semitones.

      • 31.3.2.3 Pitch Mod

        • Two sources modulate both oscillators' pitch, chosen from the same list as Shape Mod, each with -100% to 100%. An LFO in Ratio mode on pitch can produce FM tones.

      • 31.3.2.4 Waveform Display

        • Shows the combined output of Osc 1, Osc 2 and noise as you edit.

      • 31.3.2.5 Oscillator Mixer

        • Switches and gain for Osc 1, Osc 2 and white Noise.
        • With filtering on, raising gain drives the filter's two saturation points, before and after the filter, for analog-style distortion: the first engages above the default -6.0 dB, the second above 0.0 dB.
        • Arrow toggles beside each gain send that source through the filter; off bypasses it.
        • R — Retrigger resets both oscillators' phase on each note; off lets them run free.
31.3 Drift
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/03 Drift/03 Filter Section

    • 31.3.3 Filter Section

      • A switchable low-pass filter with key tracking and resonance, a high-pass filter, and two modulation slots.
      • Freq — low-pass cutoff. Type — Type I, 12 dB/octave DFM-1 that feeds distortion back internally, from gentle sweeps to warm drive; or Type II, 24 dB/octave Cytomic MS2, a Sallen-Key design with soft clipping that limits resonance.
      • Key — 0.00 ignores note pitch; 1.00 makes cutoff follow it.
      • Res — low-pass resonance. HP — high-pass cutoff.
      • Clicking the section shows an X-Y view: the left dot sets HP, the right dot sets LP cutoff horizontally and resonance vertically.
      • Freq Mod — two sources for LP cutoff, each -100% to 100%.
31.3 Drift
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/03 Drift/04 Envelopes Section

    • 31.3.4 Envelopes Section

      • Envelope 1 shapes amplitude; Envelope 2 is for modulation. Click a section or the 1 and 2 toggles to show one in the display.

      • 31.3.4.1 Envelope 1

        • ADSR on the oscillator section's amplitude, set by knobs or in the display. Attack goes from start to peak, Decay from peak to Sustain, Sustain holds until note-off, Release returns to zero.

      • 31.3.4.2 Envelope 2

        • Another ADSR, not on amplitude by default, available as a modulation source everywhere in Drift.
        • The switch left of Attack turns it into a Cycling Envelope, which acts like an LFO restarted by each note.
        • Tilt moves the midpoint, and at extremes the slopes. Hold sets time at maximum. Time modes: Rate (Hz), Ratio, Time (ms) or Sync (beat divisions).
31.3 Drift
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/03 Drift/05 LFO Section

    • 31.3.5 LFO Section

      • One LFO with the same four time modes as the Cycling Envelope: Rate, Ratio, Time and Sync.
      • Waveforms: Sine, Triangle, Saw Up, Saw Down, Square, Sample & Hold, Wander (S-curves between random values), Linear Envelope and Exponential Envelope (one-shot decays).
      • R — retrigger on each note, or free-running.
      • Amount — overall depth; a modulation source and amount can modulate the LFO itself.
31.3 Drift
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/03 Drift/06 Mod Section

    • 31.3.6 Mod Section

      • Three free slots route a source to a destination with -100% to 100%.
      • Sources: Env 1, Env 2/Cyc, LFO, Key, Velocity, Modwheel, Pressure, Slide.
      • Destinations: Osc 1 Gain, Osc 1 Shape, Osc 2 Gain, Osc 2 Detune, Noise Gain, LP Frequency, LP Resonance, HP Frequency, LFO Rate, Cyc Env Rate, Main Volume.
31.3 Drift
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/03 Drift/07 Global Section

    • 31.3.7 Global Section

      • Mode — voice mode:
        • Poly — one voice per note, up to 32.
        • Mono — one note at a time drawn with four voices; Thickness raises the three extra voices from silent at 0. A new note chokes a held one.
        • Stereo — two voices per note panned apart by Spread.
        • Unison — four voices per note detuned by Strength.
      • Voices — total voices. At 32: Poly plays 32 notes, Stereo 16, Unison 8.
      • Drift — scales each voice's own random offsets to oscillators and filter; more sounds more out of tune.
      • Legato (Mono) — new notes change pitch without restarting envelopes; Glide sets the slide time.
      • Volume and Vel > Vol — level and how much velocity affects it.
      • Transpose — ±48 semitones. Note PB — per-note pitch bend on or off, so an MPE controller can be used without finger position changing pitch. PB Range — pitch bend range.

31.4 Drum Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/04 Drum Sampler/01 Sample Controls Section

    • 31.4.1 Sample Controls Section

      • Load a sample by double-clicking it in the browser or dropping it on the waveform.
      • Sample Start and Sample Length — as percentages of the sample; Shift-drag Start for fine steps and a zoomed view.
      • Sample Gain — -70 to 24 dB.
      • Attack, Hold, Decay — the envelope. Hold can be inf to play the whole sample, and is unused in Gate mode.
      • Envelope Mode — Trigger plays through the Hold time after note-off; Gate fades by the Decay time at note-off.
      • Transpose ±48 semitones and Detune ±50 cents.
      • Hovering the waveform shows Swap to Next/Previous Similar Sample and the hot-swap button. Right-click for Show Similar Files, Return to Reference and Save as Similarity Reference. The title bar's hot-swap button replaces the whole instrument. Shortcuts: Ableton/Live/UG/42 Keyboard Shortcuts/23 Similar Sample Swapping.
31.4 Drum Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/04 Drum Sampler/02 Playback Effects Section

    • 31.4.2 Playback Effects Section

      • One effect at a time, turned on with the Playback Effect toggle and picked from a menu. Each has two parameters, on knobs or an X/Y pad (vertical first, horizontal second).
      • Stretch — changes length without pitch, with grainy lo-fi artifacts; Factor and Grain Size.
      • Loop — repeats part of the sample; Loop Offset and Loop Length.
      • Pitch Env — pitch sweep; Amount ±100% and Decay back to base pitch.
      • Punch — fixed-attack ducking that brings out the transient; Amount and Release.
      • 8-Bit — filtering plus bit and sample-rate reduction; Sample Rate and the filter's Decay Time.
      • FM — a sine modulates pitch; Amount and Frequency.
      • Ring Mod — Amount and Frequency; low frequencies give tremolo, high ones the usual ring-mod artifacts.
      • Sub Osc — a 30–120 Hz sub layer on the device's envelope; Amount and Frequency.
      • Noise — a noise layer on the device's envelope; Amount and Color.
      • Time and frequency controls in most effects follow the played note and global pitch. FM and Ring Mod modulation decays with the global Decay, so short decays touch only the transient.
31.4 Drum Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/04 Drum Sampler/03 Filter Section

    • 31.4.3 Filter Section

      • Filter On and four types: 12 dB low-pass, 24 dB low-pass, 24 dB high-pass and peak. Low- and high-pass have Resonance and Frequency; peak has Gain and Frequency.
31.4 Drum Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/04 Drum Sampler/04 Global Section

    • 31.4.4 Global Section

      • Volume -36 to 36 dB, Pan, and Velocity to Volume.
      • Modulation Source — Velocity or Slide (MPE), sent to one destination: Filter cutoff, Attack, Hold, Decay, or FX1 and FX2 (the current playback effect's two parameters), with a Modulation Amount.
31.4 Drum Sampler
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/04 Drum Sampler/05 Context Menu Options for Drum Sampler

    • 31.4.5 Context Menu Options for Drum Sampler

      • Enable Per-Note Pitch Bend — on by default.
      • Envelope Follows Pitch — scales the envelope with pitch so it covers the same part of the sample when transposed.
      • Drum Sampler > Simpler — swaps in Ableton/Instrument/Simpler, keeping start and length.
      • In a Ableton/Instrument/Drum Rack, a pad's Save as Default Pad makes Drum Sampler load for every sample dropped on an empty pad.

31.5 Electric
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/05 Electric/01 Architecture and Interface

    • 31.5.1 Architecture and Interface

      • A key drives a hammer into a fork, and a magnetic pickup amplifies the fork, much like an electric guitar. The fork has a tine bar, which the hammer strikes, and a tone bar, a tuned resonator. It rings for a long time until the key's release brings a damper down.
      • Four sections: Hammer, Fork, Damper/Pickup and Global. Click a section, or its icon, to show all its parameters.
31.5 Electric
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/05 Electric/02 Hammer Section

    • 31.5.2 Hammer Section

      • Stiffness — harder hammers sound brighter, softer ones mellower; follows velocity and pitch via Vel and Key.
      • Noise — impact noise, with Pitch (center frequency), Decay and Key (pitch scaling of noise volume).
      • Force — how hard the hammer hits; Amount plus Vel and Key.
31.5 Electric
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/05 Electric/03 Fork Section

    • 31.5.3 Fork Section

      • Tine — the struck part. Color balances low and high partials; Decay sets fade while held; Key scales level by pitch.
      • Tone — the fork's secondary resonance, with its own Decay.
      • Release — fade after key release, for both tine and tone.
31.5 Electric
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/05 Electric/04 Damper-Pickup Section

    • 31.5.4 Damper/Pickup Section

      • 31.5.4.1 Pickup Parameters

        • Symmetry — vertical position; 50% is in front of the tine and brightest, lower is below, higher is above.
        • Distance — farther is cleaner; closer overdrives more.
        • Type — R is electro-dynamic, W is electro-static.
        • Input and Output — signal into the pickup sets distortion; output sets level. Low input with high output is cleaner than the reverse. Key scales output by pitch.

      • 31.5.4.2 Damper Parameters

        • Dampers make a small noise when they leave and touch the fork; these simulate it.
        • Tone — soft to hard dampers, mellow to bright.
        • Level — amount of damper noise.
        • Att/Rel — -100 only on attack, 100 only on release, center both.
31.5 Electric
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/05 Electric/05 Global Section

    • 31.5.5 Global Section

      • Volume and Voices — more voices cost more CPU.
      • Semi and Detune (±50 cents).
      • Stretch — stretch tuning. Real tines and strings don't behave like the ideal model, so equal temperament can sound off on pianos; stretching sharpens high notes and flattens low ones. Negative values do the reverse.
      • Pitch Bend — global bend range; Note PB — MPE per-note bend range.

31.7 Impulse
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/07 Impulse/01 Sample Slots

    • 31.7.1 Sample Slots

      • Drag samples into slots from the browser or the Session and Arrangement Views, or hot-swap them; Delete (Mac) / Backspace (Win) removes one.
      • Slots map to C3 through the octave above, leftmost first. With Fold on, the MIDI Editor lists all eight slots. A Pitch device transposes the mapping; a Scale device rearranges it.
      • Each slot has its own parameters, shown when you click it. Changes, including automation and clip envelopes, apply only from the next note; for continuous changes during a note use Ableton/Instrument/Simpler.
      • Slot 8's Link button ties it to slot 7 so each cuts the other off, as a closed hi-hat silences an open one.
      • Hovering a slot shows play, solo, mute and hot-swap.
31.7 Impulse
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/07 Impulse/02 Start Transpose and Stretch

    • 31.7.2 Start, Transpose and Stretch

      • Start — offset into the sample, up to 100 ms.
      • Transp — ±48 semitones, modulated by velocity or random.
      • Stretch — -100% to 100%; negative shortens, positive lengthens. Mode A suits low sounds like toms and bass; Mode B suits high ones like cymbals. Modulated by velocity.

  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/07 Impulse/04 Saturator and Envelope

    • 31.7.4 Saturator and Envelope

      • Saturator — fatter, rounder sound; Drive adds gain and distortion, so lower the slot's volume to match. Heavy drive on low sounds gives overdriven analog drum tones.
      • Decay — up to 10.0 seconds. Trigger mode decays with the note; Gate mode waits for note-off, which suits hi-hats with varying lengths.
