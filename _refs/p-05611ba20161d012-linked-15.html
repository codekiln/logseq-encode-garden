10.2 Keyboard Responsiveness
  • Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/02 Keyboard Responsiveness/01 Using Responsiveness

    • 10.2.1 Using Keyboard Responsiveness

      • The small variations of a capacitive keyboard make timbre and pitch useful modulation targets. Modulating the Cycling Envelope's rise and fall times can also produce interesting results.
      • The Matrix Assign buttons open up several options:
        • Modulate Oscillator Type with pressure.
        • Modulate Wave, Timbre, and Shape together with pressure to make abrupt timbre changes.
        • Modulate Glide with pressure or velocity.
        • Modulate envelope attack and decay times with pressure for a natural response.
      • Keyboard Pressure or Velocity can control modular gear. Set the keyboard's output voltage at Utility → CV/Gate → Pressure Range, from 0V to 10V. The signal is available at the MicroFreak's rear Pressure output and can open an external filter, change a sequencer's speed, or alter a resonator's damping.

      • Note/Warning Some Eurorack modules cannot accept 10V input and may clip above their maximum input voltage.

11 Using the Icon Strip
  • Microfreak/UG/11 Icon Strip/01 Key Hold Button

    • 11.1 The Key Hold Button

      • Key Hold locks a key or chord so both hands are free to adjust the MicroFreak's controls.

      • Note/Info The HOLD state is not saved with the preset.

      • The Hold Icon
      • Pressing Hold once keeps the held keys active after the fingers lift. In paraphonic mode, playing additional notes adds them to the current chord.
      • In Arp mode, Hold keeps the arpeggiated notes playing after keys are released. Turning off Arp or Hold ends them. Playing new keys replaces the notes currently playing.

      • Note/Info Hold does not work with external MIDI. To hold external MIDI notes, send the MicroFreak a Sustain message.

      • In Sequencer mode, the Hold Icon has alternate functions.
      • The alternative functions of the Hold Icon
      • In Step-record mode, Hold adds a tie or silence.
      • In Real-time recording mode, Hold clears the content as the sequence records.
      • With Seq Mod, Hold clears sequence modulation; with A or B, it clears the selected sequence.
      • When the Sequencer is disabled, Hold returns to its usual Key Hold function.

      • Note/Info Key Hold can help create a generative, self-evolving patch. Modulating pitch with independent rates from sources such as the LFO and Cycling Envelope makes the pitch change continuously without a sequencer.

11 Using the Icon Strip
  • Microfreak/UG/11 Icon Strip/03 Touch Strip

    • 11.3 The Touch Strip

      • The touch strip has three functions. Select Spice, Dice, or Bend by pressing its corresponding icon.
      • Spice and Dice and Bend
      • Microfreak/UG/11 Icon Strip/03 Touch Strip/01 Spice and Dice

        • 11.3.1 Spice & Dice

          • Spice and Dice work together, though their musical effect can be enjoyed without knowing the details.
          • Dice changes the gates and triggers of the playing Arpeggio or Sequence. It varies their timing and can shorten, lengthen, or omit gates.
          • Activate the Arpeggiator or Sequencer first. Select Dice, then touch the strip to set the amount of randomness: the left end adds none and the right end adds the most.
          • The Dice setting is silent until Spice is added. Select Spice and move right on the strip to increase the variation's intensity. Spice and Dice can be set in either order. With Spice held at a chosen amount, each Dice touch generates a different trigger variation at that intensity.
          • Spice and Dice also subtly vary octaves, velocity, and the Amp envelope's release time. Spice sets how far a parameter deviates from its normal value; Dice updates the random value per step.
          • Pattern randomizes chord pitches; Spice and Dice randomize gates and triggers. Together they can make an Arpeggio or Sequence sound very different from its starting pattern.

          • Note/Info Technical detail

            • A Sequence's Status is its series of gate lengths. New sequences start at a default gate length of 45%; Utility > Preset > Default gate length sets it from 5% to 85%.
            • A gate length of 0 is silent; 100 is a tie that continues into the next step without a noticeable pause. Values between them set the gate length percentage.
            • Spice moves gate lengths from the default toward the current Status. At maximum Spice, the gate lengths follow the Status exactly.
            • When Dice is lit, touching the strip changes the Status by adding a bipolar value to each step's gate length. A touch near the left makes small changes; a touch near the right can reshuffle the Status.
            • When the finger lifts, the last gate-length pattern heard becomes the new Status, which can then change again with another Dice touch.

          • Note/Info Spice and Dice settings are not saved with a preset. They are intended for live playing, and the generated variations cannot be repeated. Recording the performance into a DAW preserves it.

      • Microfreak/UG/11 Icon Strip/03 Touch Strip/02 Bend

        • 11.3.2 Bend

          • Pitch bending raises or lowers a note. Press the Bend icon to enable bending.
          • The Bend Strip
          • The center is neutral. Moving left lowers the pitch; moving right raises it. Touching another point jumps directly to that pitch.
          • In standard mode, pitch follows the strip's absolute position. In relative mode, finger movement is added to or subtracted from the current pitch, regardless of where the finger first touches. Enable relative mode at Utility > Misc > Relative bend.

          • Note/Info Six W-shaped marks on the strip help guide accurate bends.

          • The default bend range is 24 chromatic steps: 12 to either side of center. Utility > Preset > Bend range sets the range, up to 48 steps (four octaves).
          • Tapping between two points can alternate pitches quickly. When the finger lifts, pitch returns to the center. Moving a finger slightly on the strip also creates vibrato.

          • Note/Info Indian music and instruments such as the sarod and sitar offer examples of expressive pitch-bending traditions beyond Western music.

12 The Arpeggiator
  • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/01 Using Patterns

    • 12.1 Using Patterns

      • Press Pattern to generate a semi-random arpeggio from keys played legato. Each new key press generates a new pattern.
      • The pattern draws notes from the octave range selected with Oct | Mod. It plays the lowest note twice as often as each higher chord note, emphasizing the root.
      • Set the generated pattern length with Utility > Preset > Seq length. This also sets the length used by the Sequencer and Spice & Dice. The default is 16 steps; the range is 4–64.
      • When a pattern sounds right, press Hold before releasing the keys. Touching the keyboard again creates a new pattern. With Hold active, the arpeggio continues while you lift your fingers; deactivate Hold to clear it.
      • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/01 Using Patterns/01 Creating Pattern Variations

        • 12.1.1 Creating Pattern Variations

          • Add or remove a finger while playing to generate a new pattern variation.
          • Patterns are performance material and are not saved with a preset.
12 The Arpeggiator
  • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/02 Gates and Triggers

    • 12.2 Gates and Triggers revisited

      • Pressing a key produces a gate that stays high while the key is held. The Arpeggiator and Sequencer also generate gates.
      • With Amp | Mod off, the Arpeggiator or Sequencer gates control the amplifier directly. With it on, those gates trigger the Envelope, which controls the amplifier.
      • Set gate length in Utility > Preset > Default gate length. To hear its effect on the Arpeggiator, turn Amp | Mod off, hold a chord, and adjust the setting.
12 The Arpeggiator
  • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/03 Arpeggio Rate

    • 12.3 Arpeggio Rate

      • Rate sets the arpeggio speed. With Sync off, the display shows BPM; the default is 120 BPM, and the Arpeggiator runs independently of internal or external clock.
      • With Sync on, the Arpeggiator follows the internal clock and Rate selects a time division. For example, 1/2 gives each note a two-beat duration; 1/4 gives quarter notes, or four notes per measure.
      • Available divisions:
        • 1 whole note
        • 1/2 note and 1/2 note triplet
        • 1/4 note and 1/4 note triplet
        • 1/8 note and 1/8 note triplet
        • 1/16 note and 1/16 note triplet
        • 1/32 note and 1/32 note triplet
      • A 1/4 division corresponds to a standard metronome tick.
      • The Arpeggiator and the Rate knob
      • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/03 Arpeggio Rate/01 Using Sync

        • 12.3.1 Using Sync

          • Sync coordinates the timing of modules or connected instruments. The MicroFreak can sync to a DAW or external synthesizer, with Rate setting divisions such as double speed or half speed.
          • The LFO, Arpeggiator, and Sequencer can sync to the clock. Their synchronized rhythms can add accents and rhythmic shifts.
12 The Arpeggiator
  • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/04 Making it Swing

    • 12.4 Making it Swing

      • Hold Shift and turn the Rate encoder to adjust Swing. The range is 50–75%; 50% is the default straight rhythm. Swing shifts notes around the beat and can loosen a rhythm, especially when combined with straight notes.
12 The Arpeggiator
  • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/05 Arpeggio Range

    • 12.5 Arpeggio Range

      • The Arpeggiator initially plays the held notes within one octave. Press Oct | Mod to choose how many octaves above the held notes are added:
        • 1 plays only notes held on the keyboard.
        • 2 adds the same notes one octave above.
        • 3 adds them two octaves above.
        • 4 adds them three octaves above.
      • The Arpeggiator and range
      • While an arpeggio plays, pressing Octave Up or Octave Down keeps the pitches already in the arpeggio and adds newly played notes in the new octave.
      • To transpose a held arpeggio, activate Hold, press Shift, and play a key to set the transposition. The display shows the amount.
      • In Scale mode, notes outside the selected scale are forced into it. Different notes can collapse to the same pitch, creating repeated-note or ratcheting effects. Keep Utility > Preset > Scale open while adjusting the scale and transposition.
12 The Arpeggiator
  • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/06 Transfer to Seq

    • 12.6 Transferring an arpeggio to the Sequencer

      • To transfer an arpeggio to a sequence pattern:
        • Activate the Arpeggiator with Arp | Seq and play until you find an arpeggio you like. This also works in Paraphonic mode.
        • Press Hold and adjust Spice & Dice if desired.
        • Press Shift + Up | A or Order | B to transfer it to sequence pattern A or B. Playback switches to the Sequencer.
      • The transferred pattern extends to the current sequence length. A three-note arpeggio transferred to an eight-step pattern becomes an eight-step sequence.
      • Spice & Dice effects transfer with the arpeggio, providing those effects in a sequence. Recording in Sequencer mode disables Spice & Dice.
      • Chords created in Chord mode also transfer to the sequence.
12 The Arpeggiator
  • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/07 Arp Fun

    • 12.7 Arpeggiator fun

      • The Arpeggiator can act as a modulation source. The effect is subtle, so increase the modulation amount and extend the octave range. Routing it to two destinations can make the movement easier to hear.
      • Example patch:
        • Set the oscillator type to Waveshaper.
        • Activate the envelope with Amp | Mod. Set Attack to 0 ms, Decay to 100 ms, Sustain to 30%, and Filter Amt to maximum.
        • In the Matrix Key | Arp row, assign Assign 1 to Glide with modulation amount about 0.2. Set Glide between 1/32 and 1/16.
        • Assign Assign 2 to envelope Attack with modulation amount 10.
        • Press Pattern to generate a new arpeggio pattern when you lift a finger from the keyboard.
      • To vary the pattern, set Spice with the Spice icon and touch strip, then roll Dice on the strip. This varies the Glide and slows the Attack in the higher octaves.
      • An external delay works well with an arpeggio.
      • Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/07 Arp Fun/01 Spicing up Arpeggios

        • 12.7.1 Spicing up your Arpeggios

          • Use the Bend strip to change the pitch of a playing arpeggio. For strong variation across an octave, set Utility > Preset > Bend range to 12 and make sure Utility > Misc > Relative bend is off.
          • A single held note can make a rhythmic pattern: set a medium arpeggio rate, then lift and replace the finger rhythmically. Vary timbre, envelope stages, or volume with modulation or keyboard pressure while keeping the pitch fixed. The manual calls repeating one pitch while changing its other qualities Hoketus.
          • A small amount of pitch modulation from the LFO or Cycling Envelope can make a sustained note resemble a monochord: several strings tuned to the same pitch, with occasional strings a step above or below.

13. The Sequencer
  • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use

    • 13.1. Using the Sequencer

      • The icon strip changes function with Arp | Seq. In arpeggiator mode, its icons control Hold, Order, Random, and Pattern. After Shift + Seq activates the sequencer, they control Tie/Rest, pattern A, pattern B, Record/Stop, and Play/Stop.
      • The Sequencer Controls
      • The Sequencer Controls: Stop and Start
      • The Tie/Rest Icon
      • Tie/Rest: During step recording, extend a note across steps or enter silence.
      • A and B: Select the pattern.
      • Record (O): Start step recording while playback is stopped. During playback, press Record to start real-time recording.
      • Play (>): Start or stop playback; also ends step recording.

      • Note/Info During playback, the MicroFreak sends MIDI and analog clock signals. Starting or stopping playback also sends MIDI start or stop messages to external sequencers.

      • A preset can contain monophonic or paraphonic patterns. In paraphonic mode, the sequencer can play up to four voices; with Paraphony off, it plays only the lowest note of each step.
      • Keyboard notes take priority over sequence notes. If you hold two keys in four-voice paraphonic mode, two voices remain for the sequence, which plays its lowest two notes. Incoming MIDI notes have the same priority as keyboard notes; sequence notes have the lowest priority.

      • Note/Info The pitch CV output sends the lowest note in a sequence step. During keyboard playing, it sends the most recently played note.

      • MIDI sends all notes in a played chord, including velocity and aftertouch, even when the chord has more than four notes.
      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/01 Select & Play

        • 13.1.1. Selecting and Playing a Sequence Pattern

          • A new preset has empty patterns. A factory or saved preset may contain data in A or B. Press A or B to load that pattern; the display shows “Sequence X Loaded.”
          • During playback, press A or B to switch patterns. Both patterns have the same length within a preset and play one at a time. Recorded patterns are saved with their preset.
          • To discard recent changes and reload the last saved version of a pattern, hold Shift and press its A or B icon.
          • To erase a pattern, hold its A or B icon and press Hold. Both buttons blink until the erase finishes.

          • Note/Info You can erase or reload A while B plays, and the reverse.

          • Once a pattern contains notes, pressing a keyboard key starts the currently selected pattern; you need not press Play first.
          • A pattern can hold both notes and modulation data.
      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/02 Keyboard

        • 13.1.2. The Sequencer and the Keyboard

          • In monophonic mode, holding a keyboard key pauses a playing sequence. The sequence resumes when you release the key. In paraphonic mode, it continues alongside your playing.
          • The sequencer can play Unison voices or trigger chords in Chord Mode. Voice priority is keyboard notes, then incoming MIDI notes, then sequencer notes.
      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record

        • 13.1.3. Recording a Sequence

          • A pattern stores its length, notes and velocity for each step, ties and rests, and up to four recorded modulation parameters. A and B share their length.

          • Note/Info Gate length is stored in the sequence. Set a global gate length for each preset in Utility; identical sequences in different presets can then have different gate lengths.

          • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/01 Step Time

            • 13.1.3.1. Step-Time Recording a Sequence

              • Stop playback and press Record. Hold one or more notes for the current step. You can change the notes while holding keys; the first four notes held before all keys are released are stored. Releasing all keys advances to the next step.

              • Note/Info Recording new information in a step overwrites its previous contents.

              • Press Tie/Rest without holding notes to insert a silent step and advance.
              • To sustain a note or chord across steps, hold it and press Tie/Rest for each additional step. Releasing the keys advances again.
              • The display shows the current step and its recorded notes. After the final step, the MicroFreak enters Play mode automatically.
              • In step recording, an incoming MIDI Start/Play message switches the sequencer to real-time recording.
          • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/02 Edit

            • 13.1.3.2. Editing a Sequence

              • Select A or B, stop playback, and press Record. Turn Rate/Swing to move through the steps. The MicroFreak plays each step's notes and recorded modulation as you reach it, looping to the beginning after the final step.
              • At the desired step, hold new notes to replace the stored notes. Press Hold to replace the step with a rest.

              • Note/Info Step recording also accepts notes and velocity from a keyboard connected to MIDI In.

              • Note/Info In step mode, Shift + Rate/Swing changes the sequence length while you work.

          • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/03 Real Time

            • 13.1.3.3. Real-Time Recording a Sequence

              • Start playback, then press Record. A blinking Record icon means the sequencer is waiting for notes or knob movements.
              • During note recording, each pass through the loop replaces notes played at the corresponding steps in the previous pass. Modulation recording runs for one cycle and then stops recording.
              • Playing a note replaces the recorded note at that point. Pressing Hold while recording replaces notes or modulation events at that point with silence; you can fill the gap on the next pass.
              • A DAW can play notes into a MicroFreak set to real-time recording. You can later add knob movement to the modulation tracks.
              • Tip: A longer sequence provides finer recording resolution. Set its length in Utility > Preset > Seq Length; a slower recording rate can make note and modulation placement easier.

              • Note/Info You can start in step recording and press Play to record the remainder in real time. The sequencer cannot switch back to step recording during that take.

          • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/04 Copy

            • 13.1.3.4. Copying a Sequence

              • Hold the source pattern's A or B icon, then press the destination pattern's icon. To copy A to B, hold A and press B; to copy B to A, hold B and press A. Check the display for confirmation.
              • Copying a pattern lets you keep its melody while recording different modulation movements in the other pattern, then alternate between the two during performance.
          • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/05 Arp

            • 13.1.3.5. Using the Arpeggiator to Create Sequences

              • Create an arpeggio and transfer it to a sequence to retain its Spice & Dice variations. Standard sequence recording disables Spice & Dice, so transferring an arpeggio is the way to include those effects in a sequence. See Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator for the transfer procedure.
13. The Sequencer
  • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/02 Mod Tracks

    • 13.2. The Modulation Tracks

      • Each pattern has four modulation tracks. The note sequence records pitch and velocity; each modulation track records the position of one control at each step. A track can hold as many values as the sequence has steps, up to 64.
      • In step recording, record the notes first, then capture control positions at the desired steps. Notes and modulation can also be recorded together by holding notes while turning controls.

      • Note/Info Step recording cannot capture or edit pressure, so pressure data is not saved with the preset when recording in this mode.

      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/02 Mod Tracks/01 Step Time

        • 13.2.1. Step-Time Recording of Modulation

          • Select a pattern, stop playback, and press Record. Turn Rate/Swing to select the step where modulation should begin.
          • Turn a control to audition its value. The display shows the step, modulation slot, parameter, and amount. Release the control to store that position with the step. Move to another step with Rate/Swing and set the next value. Repeat across the pattern.

          • Note/Info Touching a control assigns it to the first free modulation track. A fifth control produces a “Memory full” message.

          • You can fill one control's track across all steps before assigning another control, or set up to four controls at each step before moving on.
          • The Rate Knob
          • Turning Rate/Swing through the steps plays their stored notes and modulation, so you can hear and edit each value. Turn its assigned control to replace a stored position. Oct | Mod cycles through active modulation tracks.

          • Controls available for modulation tracks

            • Oscillator: Type, Wave, Timbre, and Shape. With sample selection enabled, Type controls sample selection.
            • Filter: Cutoff and Resonance.
            • Envelope: Attack, Decay, Sustain, and Filter Amount.
            • LFO: Rate.
            • Cycling Envelope: Rise, Fall, Hold, and Amount sent to the Matrix.
            • Matrix: Modulation amount of a matrix point.
            • Glide: Glide amount.

          • Note/Info Shift + knob parameters, control buttons, and icon buttons cannot be assigned to modulation tracks.

          • To clear a track, press Oct | Mod until its light blinks, then hold Oct | Mod and press Hold. Press Hold repeatedly while holding Oct | Mod to clear further tracks.
      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/02 Mod Tracks/02 Real Time

        • 13.2.2. Real-Time Recording of Modulation

          • Start Play and Record, then move a control. Recording begins at the first step that captures a movement, continues for one full loop, and stops just before that step on the next pass. Record turns off automatically.
          • To erase the selected modulation track, hold Oct | Mod and press Hold. Keep Oct | Mod held and press Hold again to erase each following track.

          • Note/Info The sequencer can record control movements from external controllers. External devices such as a DAW can record the MicroFreak's control movements.

      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/02 Mod Tracks/03 Smoothing

        • 13.2.3. Smoothing

          • Smoothing, or slew, makes changes between stored modulation values gradual, much as Glide smooths changes in pitch. Set each track separately in Utility > Preset > Seq (X) smooth, where X is the modulation track number from 1 to 4.
          • A new track recorded step by step has smoothing off by default. A new track recorded in real time has smoothing on by default. Overwriting an existing track keeps its smoothing setting.
13. The Sequencer
  • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/03 Fun

    • 13.3. Fun with Sequences

      • Rests and ties can give a sequence more varied rhythm. For example, a 32-step sequence with rests and ties played at double speed can be more varied than 16 filled steps at normal speed.
      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/03 Fun/01 Hidden Feature

        • 13.3.1. A Hidden Feature

          • Patterns A and B share the effects of their modulation tracks when you switch between them. Suppose A changes oscillator type on steps one and four. If you switch to B after step four, B starts with the oscillator type last set by A; switching before step four leaves B with the type set on step one.

          • Note/Info To make more patterns that use the same sound, copy a preset and record new patterns in the copy. Switching presets gives access to the patterns and modulation tracks in each.

    • 13.1.3. Recording a Sequence

      • A pattern stores its length, notes and velocity for each step, ties and rests, and up to four recorded modulation parameters. A and B share their length.

      • Note/Info Gate length is stored in the sequence. Set a global gate length for each preset in Utility; identical sequences in different presets can then have different gate lengths.

      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/01 Step Time

        • 13.1.3.1. Step-Time Recording a Sequence

          • Stop playback and press Record. Hold one or more notes for the current step. You can change the notes while holding keys; the first four notes held before all keys are released are stored. Releasing all keys advances to the next step.

          • Note/Info Recording new information in a step overwrites its previous contents.

          • Press Tie/Rest without holding notes to insert a silent step and advance.
          • To sustain a note or chord across steps, hold it and press Tie/Rest for each additional step. Releasing the keys advances again.
          • The display shows the current step and its recorded notes. After the final step, the MicroFreak enters Play mode automatically.
          • In step recording, an incoming MIDI Start/Play message switches the sequencer to real-time recording.
      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/02 Edit

        • 13.1.3.2. Editing a Sequence

          • Select A or B, stop playback, and press Record. Turn Rate/Swing to move through the steps. The MicroFreak plays each step's notes and recorded modulation as you reach it, looping to the beginning after the final step.
          • At the desired step, hold new notes to replace the stored notes. Press Hold to replace the step with a rest.

          • Note/Info Step recording also accepts notes and velocity from a keyboard connected to MIDI In.

          • Note/Info In step mode, Shift + Rate/Swing changes the sequence length while you work.

      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/03 Real Time

        • 13.1.3.3. Real-Time Recording a Sequence

          • Start playback, then press Record. A blinking Record icon means the sequencer is waiting for notes or knob movements.
          • During note recording, each pass through the loop replaces notes played at the corresponding steps in the previous pass. Modulation recording runs for one cycle and then stops recording.
          • Playing a note replaces the recorded note at that point. Pressing Hold while recording replaces notes or modulation events at that point with silence; you can fill the gap on the next pass.
          • A DAW can play notes into a MicroFreak set to real-time recording. You can later add knob movement to the modulation tracks.
          • Tip: A longer sequence provides finer recording resolution. Set its length in Utility > Preset > Seq Length; a slower recording rate can make note and modulation placement easier.

          • Note/Info You can start in step recording and press Play to record the remainder in real time. The sequencer cannot switch back to step recording during that take.

      • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/04 Copy

        • 13.1.3.4. Copying a Sequence

          • Hold the source pattern's A or B icon, then press the destination pattern's icon. To copy A to B, hold A and press B; to copy B to A, hold B and press A. Check the display for confirmation.
          • Copying a pattern lets you keep its melody while recording different modulation movements in the other pattern, then alternate between the two during performance.

13.2. The Modulation Tracks
  • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/02 Mod Tracks/01 Step Time

    • 13.2.1. Step-Time Recording of Modulation

      • Select a pattern, stop playback, and press Record. Turn Rate/Swing to select the step where modulation should begin.
      • Turn a control to audition its value. The display shows the step, modulation slot, parameter, and amount. Release the control to store that position with the step. Move to another step with Rate/Swing and set the next value. Repeat across the pattern.

      • Note/Info Touching a control assigns it to the first free modulation track. A fifth control produces a “Memory full” message.

      • You can fill one control's track across all steps before assigning another control, or set up to four controls at each step before moving on.
      • The Rate Knob
      • Turning Rate/Swing through the steps plays their stored notes and modulation, so you can hear and edit each value. Turn its assigned control to replace a stored position. Oct | Mod cycles through active modulation tracks.

      • Controls available for modulation tracks

        • Oscillator: Type, Wave, Timbre, and Shape. With sample selection enabled, Type controls sample selection.
        • Filter: Cutoff and Resonance.
        • Envelope: Attack, Decay, Sustain, and Filter Amount.
        • LFO: Rate.
        • Cycling Envelope: Rise, Fall, Hold, and Amount sent to the Matrix.
        • Matrix: Modulation amount of a matrix point.
        • Glide: Glide amount.

      • Note/Info Shift + knob parameters, control buttons, and icon buttons cannot be assigned to modulation tracks.

      • To clear a track, press Oct | Mod until its light blinks, then hold Oct | Mod and press Hold. Press Hold repeatedly while holding Oct | Mod to clear further tracks.
13.2. The Modulation Tracks
  • Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/02 Mod Tracks/02 Real Time

    • 13.2.2. Real-Time Recording of Modulation

      • Start Play and Record, then move a control. Recording begins at the first step that captures a movement, continues for one full loop, and stops just before that step on the next pass. Record turns off automatically.
      • To erase the selected modulation track, hold Oct | Mod and press Hold. Keep Oct | Mod held and press Hold again to erase each following track.

      • Note/Info The sequencer can record control movements from external controllers. External devices such as a DAW can record the MicroFreak's control movements.

13.2. The Modulation Tracks

      • A new track recorded step by step has smoothing off by default. A new track recorded in real time has smoothing on by default. Overwriting an existing track keeps its smoothing setting.

              • The buttons above the computer and MicroFreak panes move and manage wavetables.
              • Wavetable management buttons

              • Import opens three choices:

              • Import choices
              • When importing WAV or AIFF audio, MicroFreak converts it into an MFW wavetable of 32 cycles, each 2,048 samples long:
                • Each group of 2,048 source samples counts as a cycle.
                • The source's first and last cycles become cycles 1 and 32.
                • With more than eight source cycles, MicroFreak distributes them evenly across the wavetable and crossfades between them.
                • With fewer than eight source cycles, it spreads them across the slots so the Wave knob can reach every cycle.
              • An audio file can therefore become a usable wavetable without manually preparing each cycle.
              • Export saves a selected wavetable as MFW or a selected bank as MFWB. Choose a location in the system save dialog.

14.2 MIDI Control Center
  • Microfreak/UG/14 Config/02 MIDI Control Center/01 Device Tab

    • 14.2.1 Device Tab

      • The Device tab contains MIDI and configuration settings for the MicroFreak.
      • Left column of MicroFreak Device Tab in MIDI Control Center

      • MIDI

        • MIDI Input Channel: All, 1–16, or None. Sets the receive channel on the MicroFreak's 16-channel MIDI port.
        • MIDI Output Channel: 1–16. Sets the transmit channel.
        • MIDI Output Destination: Off, USB, MIDI, or MIDI + USB. USB connects directly to a computer; MIDI cables can run over longer distances.
        • Local control: With Local Off, the keyboard and panel controls send MIDI but do not directly play the MicroFreak. In a DAW, this lets the selected track route MIDI back to the MicroFreak or to another instrument. The MicroFreak can also play recorded MIDI while its keyboard controls another instrument.
        • Arp/Seq MIDI out: On or Off. Sends arpeggiator and sequencer notes to another instrument or a DAW.
        • MIDI through: When On, incoming MIDI echoes to MIDI Out.
        • Knob sends CCs: On or Off. Sends knob control-change data to external synths.
        • MIDI Merge: USB+KBD, MIDI+KBD, or BOTH+KBD. Chooses how keyboard data merges into the MIDI stream.

      • Clock and sync

        • MIDI clock source: USB, MIDI, or Sync. USB is the built-in computer connection; MIDI uses the 5-pin DIN input.
        • Sync Clock In/Out settings: One Step, 2 PPQ, 24 PPQ, or 48 PPQ. The Sync port can connect to older, pre-MIDI devices such as Korg and Roland drum machines.
        • Global Tempo: When On, preset tempos are ignored and the most recently set tempo remains in use.

      • CV and gate

        • CV pitch format: 1 V/oct, Hz/V, or 1.2 V/oct. Sets the pitch CV output format; 1 V/oct is used by Eurorack, and 1.2 V/oct by Buchla.
        • CV Gate format: S-Trig, V-Trig 5 V, or V-Trig 12 V. Sets the Gate output format.
        • CV Press range: 1–10 V. Sets the Pressure output voltage range.
        • CV 0 V reference: C−1 to G8. Sets the note that outputs zero volts in volts-per-octave pitch formats.
        • CV 1 V reference: C−1 to G8. Sets the note that outputs one volt in Hz/V pitch format.
      • Right column of MicroFreak Device Tab in MIDI Control Center

      • Controls and browsing

        • Knob Catch sets how a knob's physical position meets its stored value when sending MIDI:
          • Jump sends the physical position as soon as the knob moves, possibly changing the value abruptly.
          • Hook waits until the knob passes the stored value before sending changes.
          • Scaled moves the stored value up or down with the knob, regardless of its position. This is the default. At a physical limit, the knob must turn back before it can continue changing the value in the desired direction.
        • Click to Load Preset: On requires an extra click to load a selected preset; Off loads it while scrolling.
        • Osc Knob Speed: Slow allows fine edits to Wave, Timbre, and Shape; Fast suits broad sweeps in performance.
        • Oct LED Blink: When Off, active octave-shift buttons glow steadily rather than blinking.
        • Master Tuning: Sets tuning deviation in cents.
        • Memory protection: Off allows all presets to be overwritten; Factory Only protects factory presets; All protects user presets too.

      • Keyboard response

        • Keyboard sensitivity: 10–100%. Sets the response of Pressure and Velocity.
        • Aftertouch curve: Linear, Logarithmic, or Exponential. Adjusts how aftertouch responds to playing force.
        • Velocity curve: Adjusts the keyboard's response to playing force:
          • Linear (default) has an even response across the dynamic range.
          • Logarithmic reaches louder notes with less force, but makes low-level dynamics harder to control.
          • Exponential gives finer control at low levels, but needs more force for high levels.
        • Velocity curve settings
        • Relative Bend: When Off, the bend strip's physical center is zero bend. When On, the first point of contact becomes zero, allowing a wider gesture such as a dive bomb.

      • Scale and microphone

        • Scale: Off plays the chromatic scale. Selecting a scale prevents notes outside it; see Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales.
        • Root Note: Sets the key for the selected scale.
        • Mic Gain, Noise Gate, and Mic Detection control the 3.5 mm TRRS input used with the Vocoder Oscillator. They match the Utility settings for Vocoder configuration.
