Music/Scale/Minor/Harmonic
Harmonic Minor ScaleHistory
  • "Harmonic minor" is a nineteenth-century pedagogical name. It collects the pitches of the chords of a minor key, with the dominant and leading-tone chords built on the raised seventh.
Harmonic Minor ScaleUse
  • Nineteenth-century exoticism made that augmented second a marker of the East, Spain or Romani music.
Music/Scale/Minor/Hungarian
Hungarian Minor ScaleHistory
  • The name comes from the nineteenth-century style hongrois: the verbunkos idiom that Romani bands played in Hungarian cities, which Person/Franz Liszt, Person/Johannes Brahms and Person/Franz Schubert borrowed. Liszt's book on the subject, Des Bohémiens et de leur musique en Hongrie (18/5/9), credited that music to the Roma.
Music/Scale/Pentatonic/Major
Major Pentatonic ScaleHistory
  • European art music took it up as a marker of the exotic or the rural. Person/Claude Debussy heard the Javanese gamelan at the 18/8/9 Exposition Universelle in Paris, and "Pagodes" (Estampes, 1903) is built on pentatonic collections. Javanese slendro tuning divides the octave into five near-equal steps, so the Western pentatonic is only an approximation of what he heard.
Music/Scale/Pentatonic/Minor
Minor Pentatonic ScaleHistory
  • Person/Béla Bartók and Kodály's fieldwork after 1905 found that the oldest layer of Hungarian peasant song is in a minor pentatonic, often with a melody that repeats a fifth lower in its second half. Bartók set out that finding in The Hungarian Folk Song (1924). It overturned the nineteenth-century assumption that Hungarian music meant the style hongrois of Music/Scale/Minor/Hungarian.
Music/Scale/Whole Tone
Whole Tone ScaleHistory
  • Glinka used a descending whole-tone scale for the sorcerer Chernomor in Ruslan and Lyudmila (18/4/2), and Russian composers after him kept it for the supernatural.
