Ableton/Live/UG/03 Live Concepts/08 Audio and MIDI
  • Live handles two kinds of signal. Audio is a series of numbers approximating a waveform; MIDI is a series of commands, like "play a C4 at mezzo piano", closer to a written score.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/02 Main Clip Properties Panel/04 Clip Scale
  • In audio clips the scale doesn't change playback; it's passed to scale-aware devices. In MIDI clips, the scale's keys are highlighted in the piano ruler. See Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/01 The MIDI Note Editor Layout
  • Notes go in the grid. The time ruler runs across the top; the note ruler down the side shows C-2 to C8, a tuning system's pitches, or a Drum Rack's pads, beside a piano ruler.
  • The Preview switch above the piano ruler sounds a pitch or pad when you click a key or add or move a note.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/05 Editing MIDI Notes/02 Selecting Notes and Timespan
  • Drag to select several notes; Shift-click adds or removes one; Shift-click a piano key toggles all notes on that key. Option+Shift (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift (Win) with up and down keeps adding notes.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/05 Editing MIDI Notes/03 Find and Select Notes
  • With filters showing, Shift-click a piano key to add that key's notes, or Shift-drag to make evenly repeated time selections.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/05 Editing MIDI Notes/12 Editing Velocities
  • Click a piano key to select one key track's notes, then draw a ramp just for them.
Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/06 Folding and Scales
  • With Scale Mode on, set in the Main Clip Properties panel or Control Bar along with Root Note and Scale Name, the scale's keys are highlighted in the piano ruler. Highlight Scale, with its button, K, or the menus, also highlights the key tracks and marks the root; it applies everywhere.
  • The piano ruler's context menu spells notes with flats, sharps, both, Auto (by the root's place on the circle of fifths), or MIDI note numbers. With Scale Mode on, it affects only notes outside the scale.
Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools/01 Rhythm
  • Generates a repeating rhythm on one pitch or drum pad, chosen with Pitch or by Option-clicking (Mac) / Alt-clicking (Win) the piano ruler.
Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools/02 Seed
  • Generates random notes within ranges. Set the pitch range with its sliders or handles, or Option-drag (Mac) / Alt-drag (Win) on the piano ruler; overlapping the handles gives a single pitch. The slider is purple when a scale is active.
Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools/03 Shape
  • Generates a run of notes that follows a shape, chosen from Shape Presets or drawn in the display, within a pitch range set by sliders or by Option-dragging (Mac) / Alt-dragging (Win) the piano ruler. Purple means a scale is active.
Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools/04 Stacks
  • Chord Root sets the root, limited to the clip scale's notes and defaulting to its root; Option-click (Mac) / Alt-click (Win) the piano ruler or use the arrow keys. Chord Inversion cycles inversions, negative values an octave lower. Chord Duration and Offset adjust length and position in eighths of the chord.
Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/02 Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track
  • Detects the pitches in polyphonic audio and writes them to a clip on a new MIDI track, which comes with an Instrument Rack playing a piano sound.
  • Works on recordings from your collection, or on your own playing of guitar, piano or other harmonic instruments.
Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/03 Convert Melody to New MIDI Track
  • Detects the pitches in monophonic audio and writes them to a new MIDI track with a synth Instrument Rack. Its "Synth to Piano" Macro moves the sound from an analog-style synth to an electric piano.
Ableton/Live/UG/15 Tuning Systems/01 Loading a Tuning System
  • With a tuning loaded, the piano roll shows the tuning's notes instead of standard MIDI notes. Hovering over a note shows its pitch and frequency in the Status Bar.
Ableton/Live/UG/15 Tuning Systems/03 MIDI Track Options for Tuning Systems/01 Bypass Tuning
  • Bypass Tuning makes one MIDI track ignore the tuning; its clips show 12TET notes in the piano roll.
Ableton/Live/UG/15 Tuning Systems/03 MIDI Track Options for Tuning Systems/02 MIDI Controller Layouts
  • Track Tuning MIDI Controller Layout chooses which controller keys play which notes of the tuning, so an external keyboard lines up with the piano roll:
    • All Keys — every key plays a tuning note.
    • Black Keys Only — centred on C#3.
    • White Keys Only — centred on C3.
    • Closest in Pitch to Keyboard — each key plays the nearest tuning pitch.
    • Custom Controller Layout — set your own with the … button, which opens Configure MIDI Layout.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/31 Resonators
  • Decay — how long the resonators ring; longer is more sustained and tonal, like an undamped piano string. Low notes ring longer unless Const is on.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/05 Electric
  • Electric is an electric piano modeled on 1970s instruments, made with Applied Acoustics Systems. Every component is physically modeled in real time, with no samples, and parameters reach beyond what the real instruments allow.
  • Garden notes: Ableton/Instrument/Electric/E-Piano MKI Simple
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler/02 Multisampling
  • Multisampling records an instrument at many pitches and dynamics instead of transposing one sample, so the timbre changes the way the real instrument's does. A piano, for example, might have three or more samples per key for soft to very loud playing.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/12 Tension/02 String Tab
  • Hammer — strikes from below once and falls away, as in a piano.
  • Fix. Pos — keeps the contact point fixed regardless of string length, like a guitar pick. Leave it off for a piano, whose hammers strike about 1/7 of the length.
  • Dampers mute the string, like felt in a piano or a player's fingers, and make sounds of their own.
Ableton/Live/UG/40 MIDI Fact Sheet/01 Ideal MIDI Behavior
  • Playthrough — MIDI from hardware passes through the DAW in real time to a synth or plug-in, ideally feeling as immediate as a piano.
Ableton/Live/UG/40 MIDI Fact Sheet/02 MIDI Timing Problems
  • Latency — a constant delay. Audio has to be buffered on its way in and out of an interface. Players adapt to small delays as long as they stay the same; even a piano has some delay between key and string.
Ableton/Note

  • On the occasion of Luigi Nono’s birth centenary, I respond to his classic work for piano and tape, “..sofferte onde serene..” (1976-77), with a new work, which extends Nono’s techniques through live AI tools and interactive multimedia. The work reflects on problems of authorship, authenticity and authority in musical AI, namely the disturbing potential of human inferiority – the fact that we all are potentially “bugs” (with its multiple connotations), and we might have to accept a lower status in a new (or not so new) hierarchy. To do so, the work assumes an AI, as if alien, invasion of sorts, inspired by the “dark forest hypothesis”, term coined by the Chinese sci-fi author Liu Cixin as a response to the Fermi paradox. The musical score consists of a dense interactive video featuring borrowed cinematic materials of authoritative figures conducting, instructing, ordering, insulting and abusing the performer; augmented interactive tablatures; music notation and video synths, projected in real-time, and driving a combination of improvised, comprovised and fixed musical materials. These include the author’s previous recordings of works by Luigi Nono, Iannis Xenakis, Maurice Ravel, which are remixed through a combination of SOMAX2 and GesTCom, and live controlled through inertial sensors and an MPE controllers. Thus, both human and machine are trained and constrained, and sparks of resistance are not missing: More than following, GesTCom here has a function of unfollowing: the learned gestures are intentionally obstructed, or the tolerance of the motion follower algorithm is set extremely low, so that the system cannot follow, thus sabotaging its initial intent. As a result, various drones occur, as the supervp.scrub~ object allows resynthesized audio output to be time-stretched, transposed in pitch and undergo several spectral envelope transformations. The virtuosity involved in controlling the SOMAX2 in live settings is nontrivial, and so is the possibility to enter a dialogue with materials already learned and recorded, but also to surprise the system as well as the human performer with alien materials not featuring in the Nono score or in the recordings. Several degrees of distancing (same, similar and alien material) of the human pianist from the original material used to train SOMAX2 result in a variety of interactions, reflecting the echoes and resonances of Nono’s own political aesthetics.

      • Short

        • Inspired by the Japanese post-war art movement; contact-miked piano frame drives feedback into four corner loudspeakers while transducers excite the soundboard with processed harmonics.

      • Long

        • Gutai is a piece written as an exercise in self listening for the performer and an immersive listening experience for the audience.
        • the performer reads a graphic score, and sculpts an improvisation through it.
        • Every musical choice has potential to come back later through electronics.
        • responsibility of the performer to always be listening and to respond to the decisions they made previously in the performance. Simultaneously, these captured bits of the improvisation will be played back by the speakers surrounding the audience, giving them an inside look at the creation of the improvisation and surrounding them with the pianist’s choices. The hope is that with each performance, the player will be able to understand themselves as an improvisor through coming to terms with the consequences of their own musical decisions and that the listeners can experience the creation of the work.

  • Upwelling is a multimedia composition that weaves together the artist's experiences with the transformative capabilities of digital technology. Since relocating to the Fox River Valley, 45 miles west of Chicago, in 2019, I have been captivated by the interplay of images and sounds, exploring their convergence in a digital realm through Max/MSP and TouchDesigner. Upwelling unfolds as a visual narrative, where footage I filmed along the river and during travels becomes dynamic texture material mapped onto geometric shapes. These visuals undergo various manipulations and interactions, creating a synthesis of the familiar and the abstract. The work also incorporates home movies I digitized for my parents, invoking a contemplative reflection on the passage of time and the individuals who have shaped my life. The audio composition is a sonic collage of field recordings, synthesized elements, and acoustic instruments. Improvised piano, guitar, violin, and drums are sampled to act as vessels for memory and emotion—symbols of missed opportunities, including a wish that I had spent more time learning to play them earlier in life. The audio is processed through a series of filters, allowing the intensity of distinct frequency bands to influence visual parameters. This dynamic relationship between sound and image produces a shifting tapestry of modulations and displacements, transforming the familiar into the unfamiliar. Upwelling contemplates the unpredictable nature of memory—how it can resurface randomly or be triggered by specific events, shaping our present experiences and influencing our identities, cultural values, and relationships. In this composition, the artist invites the audience to navigate the fluid boundaries between the tangible and the ephemeral, the past and the present, offering a poignant reflection on the interconnectedness of our memories and the profound role they play in shaping the human experience.
