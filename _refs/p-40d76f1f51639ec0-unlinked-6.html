Job/to be Done
  • The Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) framework is a way of understanding why people choose products, services, or behaviors. The core claim is simple:
    • People do not fundamentally buy products. They “hire” them to make progress in a particular situation.
  • A person can “hire” radically different products in different contexts.
  • The same person might:
    • hire Apple/Notes for quick capture
    • hire Notion for project planning
    • hire paper notebooks for emotional reflection
    • hire voice memos while driving
  • People resist change more than they seek novelty
    • Christensen framed adoption as a battle between forces:
      • push of the current problem
      • pull of a new solution
      • anxiety about the new thing
      • habit/inertia of the old thing
    • This is one reason many technically superior products fail. They solve the wrong struggle or introduce too much behavioral friction.
    • JTBD became influential because it changes how innovation is approached.
      • Instead of asking:
        • “What features do customers want?”
        • “What market segment are we targeting?”
        • “How do we beat competitors?”
      • You ask:
        • “What progress is the person trying to make?”
        • “What circumstances create the struggle?”
        • “What prevents satisfactory progress today?”
        • “What would a better resolution look like?”
  • Still, the framework remains influential because it gives a disciplined language for understanding motivation without reducing people to demographics or feature checklists.
  • People are not primarily optimizing for products.

  • Each page should list only its most specific entities. A musician who is also a knowledge gardener lists Logseq/Entity/Person/Musician and Logseq/Entity/Person/Knowledge Gardener and leaves out Logseq/Entity/Person, because each of those is already a kind of person. Musician, then Person, is one lineage: an entity followed by each entity it is a kind of. This narrows the hypothesis codekiln wrote in the 2026-10-01 Thu journal: the rule covers only a child entity that is a kind of its parent, and it lets one page carry two sibling entities. The Musician, Knowledge Gardener and User Guide definition pages currently say to mark each instance with the parent entity as well.
  • Is-a. Every instance of the child is also an instance of the parent: Logseq/Entity/Article/Blog, Logseq/Entity/Repo/Awesome, the Logseq/Entity/Book/Section levels, Logseq/Entity/Person/Musician, Logseq/Entity/Person/Knowledge Gardener, Logseq/Entity/Book/User Guide.
  • I recommend lineage: an entity and its chain of is-a parents up to the topmost defined entity, so a musician's lineage is Musician, then Person. It is an ordinary word, it reads well in a rule ("two entities in one lineage"), and it leaves out part-of children, since an episode does not descend from a show. Clade names the group in the other direction: Person together with every entity that descends from it, Musician and Knowledge Gardener included.
  • Taken literally, "more than one entity with the same entity ancestry" also forbids a person who is both a musician and a knowledge gardener, since Logseq/Entity/Person/Musician and Logseq/Entity/Person/Knowledge Gardener both descend from Person. Neither entity implies the other, so marking both carries information.
  • The redundancy is between an entity and its own ancestor: Musician already implies Person. The rule I would write is that each page carries the most specific entity it has in each lineage, so a page marked Musician leaves out Person.
  • Logseq/Entity/Person/Knowledge Gardener and Logseq/Entity/Person/Musician each say "Frontmatter lists both types, Person first". Logseq/Entity/Book/User Guide marks a guide with both User Guide and Book, child first.
  • The likely reason for marking both is backlinks: the backlinks of Logseq/Entity/Person list a musician only when the musician's page links Person directly. Moving to child-only markers would mean:
    • rewriting the Musician, Knowledge Gardener and User Guide definition pages to mark the child alone;
    • changing logseq-entity:: on the eight instance pages;
    • adding the lineage rule to Logseq/Entity, together with the statement that an instance of a child entity is an instance of every ancestor;
    • listing all people with a query over Person and every entity that descends from it, because backlinks alone would miss the musicians.

Logseq/Entity/Identity
  • In this garden, Identity pages model a public name under which a person or collective publishes, performs or makes work.
  • A stage name, pen name, named artistic project or collective with a recognizable body of work. The identity has a page separate from the biographical people behind it.
  • A person's ordinary biographical name alone does not require a second page. A spelling variant or abbreviation that names the same identity can remain an alias.
  • A person can have several identities, and a collective identity can have several people behind it. Identity pages carry identity entities; biographical pages carry person or maker entities.
  • Logseq/Entity/Identity/Frontmatter/identity-of links an identity to known people behind it. Unknown people are left unstated.
  • Logseq/Frontmatter/created-by-identities links a work or release to its credited identities. A link to an identity does not imply that every associated person contributed to every work.
  • A work's created-by:: links the known people who made that particular work. Identity membership alone is insufficient evidence for a personal credit.
  • Search published names, existing titles, aliases, official URLs and person-page mentions before creating an identity.
  • When the identity exists only as an alias on a person page, retain the biographical page and separate the identity from that alias before creating its page. Other aliases and the person's biographical information remain with the person.
  • One linked H1 with the published name and official URL when known, a short description, and verified links to the people behind the identity.
Logseq/Entity/Identity/Frontmatter/identity-of

  • identity-of:: — people behind a public identity

    • On a Logseq/Entity/Identity instance, links the known real people behind that public identity.

    • Values

      • One or more comma-separated links to person hubs, including Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker instances. Omit the property when the people are unknown.
      • A solo identity normally links one person; a collective identity may link several. The property is neither an exhaustive membership history nor a claim of current membership.
      • Specific membership periods and roles belong in the identity's body. A work's individual credits require evidence about that work.

    • Example

      • On Bibio, identity-of:: links Person/Stephen Wilkinson.
