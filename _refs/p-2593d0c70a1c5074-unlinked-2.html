  • Far — balanced, with some room sound
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/08 Chorus-Ensemble
  • Vibrato — modulates pitch more strongly with no added delayed copy; the wave morphs from sine to triangle, which can make a police-siren sound
  • Feedback — output fed back to input for a more extreme, brighter sound that keeps echoing after playback stops. Feedback Invert gives a hollow sound at high feedback. Not available in Vibrato.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/08 Chorus-Ensemble/01 Chorus-Ensemble Tips
  • On dry guitar, Ensemble at 1–1.8 Hz with Amount 100% gives a surf-rock sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/09 Compressor
  • The orange Gain Reduction meter shows the current reduction. The manual warns that more than about 6 dB changes the sound a lot and can flatten dynamics in a way that cannot be undone later — especially on the Main track.
  • Knee — how gradually compression starts. 0 dB is a hard knee, which can sound harsh at high ratios. A 10 dB knee with a −20 dB threshold starts gently at −30 dB and reaches full compression at −10 dB.
  • 10–50 ms of attack lets the start of each hit through and keeps it lively; very short attack can sound lifeless or buzzy.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/10 Corpus/05 Sidechain Parameters
  • Off Decay — Note Off damps the resonance; its slider sets how much. At 0% only Decay matters, like a marimba or glockenspiel; at 100% Note Off cuts the sound at once.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/11 Delay
  • Fade — crossfades old and new times; gradual changes can sound granular
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/12 Drum Buss
  • Transients — above 100 Hz: positive values add attack and sustain for a fuller, punchier sound; negative values add attack but cut sustain for tighter, drier drums
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/16 EQ Three
  • The filters are made to sound like a powerful analog filter cascade, so 48 dB mode colors the sound slightly even with every band at 0 dB. For more neutral behavior use 24 dB mode or Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/15 EQ Eight.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/21 Glue Compressor
  • Soft clip — a fixed waveshaper capping output at −0.5 dB to catch loud transients. It colors and distorts the sound, so the manual suggests leaving it off unless that is wanted. With Oversampling, peaks can still pass 0 dB.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/22 Grain Delay
  • Frequency — sets grain size and length, and so shapes how Pitch and Spray sound
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/23 Hybrid Reverb/03 Convolution Reverb Engine
  • Convolution reverb uses recordings of real spaces, called impulse responses, to put a sound in that space. Any recording can be an IR — a famous hall, a snare drum, a garbage can, a voice.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/23 Hybrid Reverb/04 Algorithmic Reverb Engine
  • Freeze — stops input and makes the decay infinite so the reverb sustains; Freeze In keeps adding input to the frozen sound, building it up
  • A bright, clearly artificial diffuse reverb based on spectrally flat "velvet" noise. It adds depth without getting in the way, suits short non-linear decays on drums, and gives a clean digital sound on longer decays.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/29 Phaser-Flanger
  • Feedback — output fed back for a stronger comb-filter sound; in Doubler it also keeps echoing after playback stops. Ø inverts it for a hollow sound. High feedback can jump in level quickly — protect ears and equipment.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/30 Redux
  • Redux degrades sound with downsampling and bit reduction, from harsh aliasing to warm 8-bit grit. Noise, stereo width and filtering can be added, and the quantizer curve allows gentle or extreme bitcrushing.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/32 Reverb
  • Reverb imitates sound echoing in a space. Real reverb comes from reflections off hard surfaces, shaped by absorbent materials; Reverb approximates it with filters and modulated delays, for anything from a small room to a canyon.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/32 Reverb/03 Diffusion Network
  • Diffusion and Scale set density and coarseness; in very small rooms they strongly color the sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/32 Reverb/05 Global Settings
  • Size — room volume; very large gives a shifting, diffuse delay, very small a colored, metallic sound
  • Freeze — holds the diffuse sound almost forever.
    • Flat — bypasses the shelf filters while frozen, so the frozen sound does not lose energy in those bands
    • Cut — stops new input adding to the frozen sound
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/33 Roar/01 Input Section
  • Parallel — two independent shapers; Blend goes from Stage 1 to Stage 2. Two very different curves (such as Half Wave Rectifier and Fractal) with Blend modulated make the sound move between them.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/33 Roar/02 Gain Stage Section
  • Shaper Bias — offsets the signal for asymmetric distortion; high values sound like a broken circuit, extreme values silence it
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/36 Spectral Resonator
  • Spectral Resonator adds tuned resonances to the input's spectrum, from short percussive ringing to long washes. Pitch comes from an internal setting or a MIDI sidechain, polyphonically with up to 16 voices, like a vocoder. Modulation modes add movement or break the sound into textures. It supports scale awareness and tuning systems.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/36 Spectral Resonator/02 Frequency Section
  • Stretch — spacing of the harmonics; below 0% closer, above 0% wider. At 100% only odd harmonics remain, a square-wave sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/36 Spectral Resonator/06 Spectral Resonator Tips
  • Reverb-like sound: low Freq, more Unison and Uni. Amt, Wander with low Mod Rate and Pch. Mod; Decay sets the tail.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/37 Spectral Time
  • Spectral Time combines spectral freezing and spectral delay. Filters, delays and frequency shifting resynthesize the input: sustain a sound forever, add timed fades, smear frequencies over time, or add metallic echoes.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/38 Spectrum
  • Spectrum analyzes frequencies in real time and draws level (dB, vertical) against frequency or pitch (horizontal). Peaks stay on the graph until the song restarts. It measures only and never changes the sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/39 Tuner
  • It measures only and never changes the sound. It works best on a clean, single-note signal; chords, noise or very rich harmonics can confuse it.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/41 Vinyl Distortion
  • Pinch Effect — odd harmonics, typically 180° out of phase for a wider stereo image; same controls, different sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/42 Vocoder
  • Pitch Tracking — a monophonic oscillator that follows the modulator's pitch, limited by High and Low. Saw or three pulse waves, with Pitch for coarse tuning. It holds each detected pitch until it finds a new one, so changing its settings or resetting it (for instance by grouping the track) can change the sound unexpectedly. Best on single melodic lines or voice; unpredictable but interesting on chords and drums.
  • Enhance — normalizes the carrier's spectrum and dynamics for a brighter sound, useful with external carriers
  • Range — frequency span of the filters; narrow it if the sound gets too piercing or boomy
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/42 Vocoder/01 Vocoder Tips
  • 5. Play the synth while speaking: the speech rhythm comes through with the synth's tone. Solo the voice track to hear only the vocoded sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/30 MIDI Effect Reference/01 Arpeggiator
  • Gate sets note length as a percentage of Rate. Over 100% the notes overlap for a legato sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog/02 Oscillators
  • Shape — sine, sawtooth, rectangular or white noise. Rectangular enables Pulse Width (100% is a square with odd harmonics only; low values sound thin), which an LFO can modulate.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/07 Sound Design Tips

    • 31.2.7 Sound Design Tips

      • For a realistic marimba, follow the physical chain: the Mallet strikes a tuned bar (Resonator 1), and a tube (Resonator 2) amplifies it, in serial 1 > 2.
      • For unrealistic sounds:
        • Use the Noise exciter with long envelopes for washy, near-granular textures, or effects like bowed vibraphone and crystal glasses.
        • Try the parallel 1 + 2 structure.
        • Modulate with the LFOs and MIDI or MPE controllers.
      • The models are idealized and can respond far more strongly than real objects. Some settings jump sharply in volume, so keep levels low while experimenting.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/01 Architecture and Interface
  • Two exciters, Mallet and Noise, feed two stereo resonators that can be independent or linked. The exciters start the sound; the resonators shape most of its character. With both exciters off there is no sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/02 Collision/04 Resonator Tabs
  • The resonators define most of the sound. Each tab has an on switch; with both off there is no sound.

Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/05 Electric/02 Hammer Section
  • Stiffness — harder hammers sound brighter, softer ones mellower; follows velocity and pitch via Vel and Key.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/05 Electric/05 Global Section
  • Stretch — stretch tuning. Real tines and strings don't behave like the ideal model, so equal temperament can sound off on pianos; stretching sharpens high notes and flattens low ones. Negative values do the reverse.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/06 External Instrument
  • A routing utility, not a sound source: it sends MIDI to a hardware synth or a multitimbral plug-in and brings its audio back.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/07 Impulse/04 Saturator and Envelope
  • Saturator — fatter, rounder sound; Drive adds gain and distortion, so lower the slot's volume to match. Heavy drive on low sounds gives overdriven analog drum tones.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/09 Operator/02 Oscillator Section
  • From the Wave chooser: pure Sine, the usual first choice for FM; Sine 4 Bit and Sine 8 Bit for a C64-style sound; Saw D and Square D digital shapes for bass; resynthesized square, triangle and saw whose number (e.g. Square 6) is the harmonic count, with lower numbers mellower and less prone to aliasing; Noise Looped, a noise loop; and Noise White, true random noise.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/10 Sampler
  • Sampler is Live's multisampling instrument. It handles libraries of several gigabytes, imports common library formats, and has a modulation system that reaches nearly every part of its sound.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/12 Tension
  • Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/12 Tension/04 Sound Design Tips

    • 31.12.4 Sound Design Tips

      • Tension's parts affect each other, so it is easy to find settings that make no sound, or very loud ones; watch levels.
      • Think of the sections as one physical object: a slow bow can start an undamped string, but a heavy damper needs a faster bow.
      • The presets show it can do much more than strings.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/12 Tension/01 Architecture and Interface
  • An exciter (hammer, pick or bow) sets the string vibrating. The fret or finger termination sets its length and so its pitch; a damper shortens its decay. The body, through the bridge or a pickup, radiates the sound. Tension adds a filter between string and body.
Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/12 Tension/02 String Tab
  • With the Exciter off, only the damper can move the string. With Exciter and Damper both off there is no sound.
  • Vel and Key modulate by velocity and pitch. Some combinations produce no sound.

  • Distance — meters or feet; for timing offsets between speakers in a PA. A Temperature setting (°C or °F) matches the room, since sound travels at different speeds in warm and cold air.
