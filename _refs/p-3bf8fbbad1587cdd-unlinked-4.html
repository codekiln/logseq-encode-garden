Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/06 MPE-Multi-channel Settings/02 The MPE-Multi-Channel Settings Dialog
  • The dialog chooses the MPE zone (lower or upper) and how many note channels it uses, or switches to multi-channel mode with any range of note channels.
  • Each zone has a global channel for controls that aren't per-note: channel 1 for the lower zone, channel 16 for the upper. The number of note channels sets the zone's polyphony, for example 1–11 for the lower zone and 12–16 for the upper.

Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/01 Groove Pool/01 Adjusting Groove Parameters
  • Base — the note grid the groove is measured against, such as 1/4 or 1/8. Groove notes that fall on that grid don't move your notes.
  • Quantize — straight quantization applied before the groove: 100% snaps to the Base grid, 0% leaves notes where they were.
Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/01 Groove Pool/02 Committing Grooves
  • Commit, above the Clip Groove chooser, writes the groove into the clip — moving MIDI notes, or adding Warp Markers to audio — and then sets the clip's groove to None.
Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/02 Editing Grooves
  • What a groove does depends on its Groove Pool settings and on the note positions inside the groove file.
  • Drag a groove from the browser or Groove Pool onto a MIDI track to get its notes as an ordinary MIDI clip, edit them, then turn the clip back into a groove.
Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/03 Groove Tips/03 Creating Texture With Randomization
  • Duplicate a track, apply a groove with Random turned up to one copy, and play both: every note lands slightly apart from its twin, which thickens single string voices into a section.
Ableton/Live/UG/15 Tuning Systems
  • The Info View describes a tuning, including its notes per octave, when you hover over it in the browser or over a loaded tuning's name.
Ableton/Live/UG/15 Tuning Systems/01 Loading a Tuning System
  • With a tuning loaded, the piano roll shows the tuning's notes instead of standard MIDI notes. Hovering over a note shows its pitch and frequency in the Status Bar.
  • Changing or removing a tuning leaves notes where they are, so they may now play different pitches. With Retune Set On Loading Tuning Systems on in the Options menu, Live moves notes to the closest pitches in the new tuning after a confirmation dialog.
    • Retuning can delete or shorten a note when two overlapping notes map to the same new pitch.
Ableton/Live/UG/15 Tuning Systems/02 The Tuning Section
  • The triangle next to the tuning's name expands settings for the reference pitch and the note range:
    • Tuning — the tuning system's name.
    • Octave and Note — which note is the reference.
    • Ref. Pitch/Freq — the reference frequency. Changing it raises or lowers every note in the Set.
    • Lowest Note and Highest Note — what the lowest and highest notes play. Changing one moves the other, keeping the number of notes between them.
  • Only Ref. Pitch/Freq changes the sound. Changing Octave or Note updates the displayed frequency without an audible jump until the frequency itself is changed.
Ableton/Live/UG/15 Tuning Systems/03 MIDI Track Options for Tuning Systems/01 Bypass Tuning
  • Bypass Tuning makes one MIDI track ignore the tuning; its clips show 12TET notes in the piano roll.
Ableton/Live/UG/15 Tuning Systems/03 MIDI Track Options for Tuning Systems/02 MIDI Controller Layouts
  • Track Tuning MIDI Controller Layout chooses which controller keys play which notes of the tuning, so an external keyboard lines up with the piano roll:
    • All Keys — every key plays a tuning note.
    • Black Keys Only — centred on C#3.
    • White Keys Only — centred on C3.
    • Closest in Pitch to Keyboard — each key plays the nearest tuning pitch.
    • Custom Controller Layout — set your own with the … button, which opens Configure MIDI Layout.
Ableton/Live/UG/16 Launching Clips/02 Launch Modes
  • Launch Mode sets how a clip responds to mouse clicks, computer keys and MIDI notes:
    • Trigger — press starts the clip; release does nothing.
    • Gate — press starts it; release stops it.
    • Toggle — press starts it; the next press stops it.
    • Repeat — while held, the clip retriggers at the clip quantization rate.
Ableton/Live/UG/16 Launching Clips/05 Velocity
  • Velocity Amount sets how much MIDI note velocity affects the clip's volume: at 0 it has no effect; at 100% the softest notes play silently. Playing clips from MIDI is covered in Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote.
Ableton/Live/UG/16 Launching Clips/07 Follow Actions/04 Adding Variations in Sync
  • Identical melody clips in Legato Mode can hand over to each other without the melody changing; editing each clip's settings, envelopes or notes a little at a time then makes the melody change gradually.
Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/03 External MIDI In-Out/01 MIDI Port Inputs and Outputs
  • Sends or receives notes and CC messages.
  • Turn it on for an input to play or record notes, or to record CC from a hardware synth into clips.
  • Turn it on for an output to play external hardware, send notes to another app through a virtual MIDI bus, or send CC automation out.
Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/05 Internal Routings/02 Making Use of Internal Routing
  • When a complex instrument turns one note into something closer to a passage, record its audio: one MIDI track hosts the instrument, and an audio track taking input from it records the result.
  • To double a strings part with brass, add a MIDI track with a brass instrument whose Input Type taps the strings track at Post FX. MIDI from another track is tapped after its MIDI effects and just before its instrument, so the brass gets the same notes.
Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/05 Recording Quantized MIDI Notes

    • 19.5 Recording Quantized MIDI Notes

      • Edit → Record Quantization lines up recorded MIDI notes to a chosen subdivision.
      • In the Arrangement, record quantization is its own Undo step, so Undo can remove the quantization and keep the take.
      • It can't be changed while recording, except when overdubbing a looping clip, where changes apply at once and can't be undone separately.
      • Notes can also be quantized afterwards with Edit → Quantize; see Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI.
Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/03 Recording/03 Overdub Recording MIDI Patterns
  • Set Global Quantization to one bar, and choose a Record Quantization value if notes should be quantized as they're recorded.
  • Arm the track and press Session Record. Notes are added on every loop pass.
  • Undo removes the last pass; notes can be drawn, moved or deleted in the MIDI Note Editor while recording continues.
Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/03 Recording/04 MIDI Step Recording
  • Step recording enters notes with the transport stopped, at your own pace.
  • Hold notes and press the right arrow to enter them and move the insert marker one grid step; keep holding and press it again to lengthen them. Hold the notes and press the left arrow to delete them.

Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/10 Capturing MIDI/01 Starting a New Live Set
  • Live sets the tempo (within 80–160 BPM), the loop and the notes' grid positions. Ending on the downbeat of the next bar helps it find the phrase;
  • notes played before the phrase stay in the clip ahead of the start marker, so the loop can be moved. Crop Clip discards the rest.
  • If the first capture is a single note, the loop spans that note and the tempo is set to make it one, two, four or eight bars.
Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/10 Capturing MIDI/02 Adding Material to an Existing Live Set
  • Playing over a clip on the same track and pressing Capture MIDI adds the new notes on top, for building a pattern in layers.
  • Earlier notes are kept ahead of the start marker here too.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/01 An Overview of Racks/01 Signal Flow and Parallel Device Chains
  • In a Drum Rack, each chain receives only its assigned MIDI note.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/04 Chain List
  • Each chain has an Activator, Solo and Hot-Swap. Instrument, Drum and Audio Effect Rack chains add volume and pan; Drum Rack chains add sends and MIDI note settings.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/04 Chain List/01 Auto Select
  • With Auto Select on, chains are selected as they process signal: in a Drum Rack, when their note arrives; in other Racks, according to their zones. Useful for troubleshooting.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones
  • The Key, Vel and Chain buttons above the Chain List open their editors; Hide closes them. Audio Effect Racks have only chain select zones; Drum Racks have no zones and filter by note in the chain list.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones/01 Signal Flow through Zones
  • Before entering a chain, a note must pass that chain's key zone, then its velocity zone, then its chain select zone. Failing any one keeps it out.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones/02 Key Zones
  • The Key Zone Editor shows each chain's range across the full MIDI note range, almost 11 octaves. Chains respond only to notes in their zone, which makes keyboard splits.
  • Fade ranges lower the velocity of notes entering the chain.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones/03 Velocity Zones
  • In Instrument and MIDI Effect Racks, each chain also responds only to a range of Note On velocities, 1–127, edited like key zones.
  • Fade ranges lower the velocity of notes entering the chain.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones/04 Chain Select Zones
  • In Instrument and MIDI Effect Racks they filter only notes by default; Chain Selector Filters MIDI Ctrl in the ruler's context menu makes them filter all MIDI.
  • In MIDI Effect Racks fades lower note velocity. In Instrument and Audio Effect Racks fades lower each chain's output volume: moving the selector off a zone that ends in a fade silences the chain, while a zone without a fade lets reverb tails and delays ring out.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/06 Drum Racks
  • Input/Output: Receive sets the incoming note the chain answers (with note names, numbers and General MIDI drum names); Play sets the note sent on to its devices; Choke puts it in one of sixteen choke groups, where triggering one chain silences the others, such as a closed hi-hat cutting an open one. With Receive on All Notes, Play and Choke are disabled. The Preview button fires a test note.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/06 Drum Racks/01 Pad View
  • Pad View shows one pad per MIDI note (128 in all). Shift the visible 16 pads by dragging in the overview or with the arrow keys; hold Cmd (Mac) / Alt (Windows) to move one row.
  • Drop almost anything from the browser on a pad and it maps to that note: a sample makes a chain with a Ableton/Instrument/Simpler; an audio effect goes after it; another sample replaces only the Simpler and its sample, keeping effects. In Hot-Swap, D switches the target between the Rack and the last selected pad.
  • Dragging one pad onto another swaps their notes, so existing clips play different sounds; Cmd/Alt-dragging layers both pads. See also Ableton/How To/Preserve Name of Clip when Dragging into Drum Rack.
  • Receive choosers in the chain list remap notes too; chains sharing a note all fire from its pad.
  • A pad stands for a note, not a chain — every chain at any depth that receives that note:
    • an empty pad shows its note, and its General MIDI suggestion in the Status Bar;
    • a pad with one chain shows its name and gives mute, solo, preview, Hot-Swap, rename and delete;
    • a pad with several shows "Multi"; mute, solo, preview and delete affect them all.
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/08 Mixing With Racks/01 Extracting Chains
  • A drum chain dragged from the mixer to a new track takes its MIDI notes along — for example, moving the snare from a drum loop to its own track with its devices and notes. Drag from the chain list to move only the devices.
Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/01 Recording Automation in Session View
  • Setting Record Session automation in to All Tracks (Record, Warp & Launch Settings) records into every playing clip, armed or not — for instance adding automation to a MIDI clip without recording notes.
Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/05 Viewing Automation
  • An automation envelope is a line of breakpoints drawn over the clip or track: value on the vertical axis, time on the horizontal. Drawing it over the content helps line breakpoints up with audio or notes.
Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/02 Audio Clip Envelopes
  • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/02 Audio Clip Envelopes/02 Changing Pitch and Tuning per Note

    • 27.2.2 Changing Pitch and Tuning per Note

      • With Warp on, choose Clip → Transposition and change the pitch of individual notes in a loop while it plays.
      • Draw steps along the grid in Draw Mode, then switch Draw Mode off to move breakpoints and soften the steps into ramps.
      • Warp settings decide how closely the envelope is followed: lower Grain Size in Tones or Texture mode, or a smaller Granulation Resolution in Beats mode, for a quicker response.
      • Shift while drawing or dragging gives finer steps for tuning corrections. Cmd+Option-drag (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt-drag (Windows) scrolls.
      • The envelope adds to the Transpose control, and the total stays within −48 to +48 semitones.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/02 Audio Clip Envelopes/03 Muting or Attenuating Notes in a Sample

    • 27.2.3 Muting or Attenuating Notes in a Sample

      • Choose Clip → Gain and draw any volume shape onto the sample.
      • The envelope is a percentage of the Clip Gain setting, so it can turn notes down to silence but never above the gain slider.

Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/04 Auto Shift
  • Auto Shift tracks and corrects pitch in real time, with vibrato, pitch shifting and formant shifting. Built mainly for vocals, it works on any monophonic audio. It corrects to a scale or to MIDI notes from another track, and has its own LFO.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/04 Auto Shift/01 Input Section
  • Input Pitch shows the detected pitch in notes and cents.
  • The Latency Readout shows latency in ms. Live Mode in the title bar lowers latency for performance, at the cost of small glitches at note starts and fast pitch changes.
  • MIDI input corrects pitch to notes from another MIDI track instead of to a scale.
  • External Source picks the track; Tapping Point takes the notes before (Pre FX) or after (Post FX, Post Mixer) that track's MIDI effects.
  • Mono — one note at a time; Glide sets how long overlapping notes take to slide
  • With MIDI input on, Auto Shift is silent unless notes arrive.
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/04 Auto Shift/02 Quantizer Tab
  • The Quantizer corrects pitch to the notes of a scale.
  • Pitch Correction Meter — how far, in cents, the input is being moved, with the target note below it
  • Smooth and Smoothing Time (0–200 ms) — smooth transitions between notes and keep natural vibrato. Together with Correction Strength they range from subtle to hard, stepped correction.
  • Included Notes in Scale — click notes to build a scale, or use Root and Scale
  • Use Current Scale in the title bar follows the clip's scale, shows its notes in purple, and disables Root and Scale.
