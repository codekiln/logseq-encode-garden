Ableton/Live/UG/24 Plug-Ins/03 VST Plug-Ins/02 VST Presets and Banks

  • 24.3.2 VST Presets and Banks

    • Each VST instance owns a fixed-size bank of presets, chosen below the title bar. Edits always change the selected preset.
    • Unlike Live presets, which every instance and Set share, VST presets belong to that one instance.
    • Rename the current preset with Edit → Rename Plug-In Preset while the title bar is selected.
    • Load Preset or Bank imports a preset or bank file (on Windows choose the file type in the dialog). Save Preset or Bank saves the current preset or the whole bank, picking VST Preset or VST Bank as the format.
Ableton/Live/UG/24 Plug-Ins/04 Audio Units Plug-Ins

  • 24.4 Audio Units Plug-Ins

    • Audio Units run on macOS only and behave much like VST plug-ins.
    • To activate them, press Activate under the browser's Plug-Ins label, or turn on Use Audio Units in Plug-Ins Settings → Plug-In Sources.
    • Some AU plug-ins have device modes, such as reverb quality, set only in the plug-in's own window.
    • AU presets work like Live presets, though some are read-only and can't be moved in the browser. They are .aupreset files stored in (Home)/Library/Audio/Presets/(Manufacturer)/(Plug-in).
Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks

  • 25. Instrument, Drum and Effect Racks

    • Instrument, Drum and Effect Racks: chains, zones, Macro Controls and mixing inside a Rack.
    • A Rack holds effects, plug-ins and instruments in a track's device chain. Racks can build complex processors, playable performance instruments and stacked synths, while gathering the most important controls in one place. Even a single device gains from a Rack, through new control relationships between its parameters.
    • Saved Racks are Ableton/File/.adg files.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/01 An Overview of Racks

      • 25.1 An Overview of Racks

        • How signals flow through a Rack's parallel chains, and what Macro Controls are.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/01 An Overview of Racks/01 Signal Flow and Parallel Device Chains

          • 25.1.1 Signal Flow and Parallel Device Chains

            • A track's devices normally run in series, left to right, in one chain. A Rack adds parallel chains.
            • In Instrument and Effect Racks, every chain receives the same input at once, processes it through its own devices in series, and the chains' outputs are mixed into the Rack's output.
            • In a Drum Rack, each chain receives only its assigned MIDI note.
            • A whole Rack behaves as a single device: it can go anywhere in a chain, Racks can nest without limit, and devices after it receive its output.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/01 An Overview of Racks/02 Macro Controls

          • 25.1.2 Macro Controls

            • Macro Controls are a bank of knobs, each able to control any number of parameters across the Rack's devices — to bring one parameter forward, to morph many at once, or to hide a complex instrument behind a small custom panel. See Ableton/Macro.
            • Mapping the Macros to a hardware controller makes them playable.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/02 Creating Racks

      • 25.2 Creating Racks

        • Four kinds of Rack, each with its own rules:
          • MIDI Effect Rack — MIDI effects only, on MIDI tracks.
          • Audio Effect Rack — audio effects only, on audio tracks or after an instrument on MIDI tracks.
          • Instrument Rack — instruments, plus MIDI effects before them and audio effects after.
          • Drum Rack — like an Instrument Rack, plus up to six return chains of audio effects with per-chain send levels. See Ableton/Instrument/Drum Rack.
        • Create an empty Rack by dragging a generic Rack preset (such as "Audio Effect Rack") from the browser, then drop devices into its Chain List or Devices view.
        • To wrap existing devices, select their title bars, right-click and choose Group (Cmd+G / Ctrl+G) or Group to Drum Rack. Grouping again, or grouping chains, nests a Rack inside a Rack; the Device View shows each level as a pair of brackets.
        • Ungroup on a Rack's title bar takes it apart.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/03 Looking at Racks

      • 25.3 Looking at Racks

        • A column at the Rack's left holds selectors for its views: Macro Controls, Chain List (with return chains in a Drum Rack), Devices, and the Drum Rack's Pad View.
        • Rounded corners bracket a Rack's contents; with Devices shown, the closing bracket moves to keep nesting clear.
        • Select a Rack by its own title bar to move, copy, delete, rename it, or edit its info text.
        • Hiding every view folds the Rack into its view column, the same as Fold or double-clicking its title bar.
        • Right-click the Device View selector for a list of every device in the track, nested Racks included; choose one to jump to it.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/04 Chain List

      • 25.4 Chain List

        • The Chain List is where incoming signals branch: each parallel chain is one entry. Drop presets, devices or whole chains into the area below to add chains. Racks, chains and devices drag freely between Racks and tracks; hovering over another track while dragging opens its Device View.
        • The selected chain is what the Devices view shows; arrow keys flip through chains. Several chains can be selected for copying and regrouping.
        • Each chain has an Activator, Solo and Hot-Swap. Instrument, Drum and Audio Effect Rack chains add volume and pan; Drum Rack chains add sends and MIDI note settings.
        • Chains save as presets in the browser, and can be renamed, given info text, or coloured from the context menu.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/04 Chain List/01 Auto Select

          • 25.4.1 Auto Select

            • With Auto Select on, chains are selected as they process signal: in a Drum Rack, when their note arrives; in other Racks, according to their zones. Useful for troubleshooting.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones

      • 25.5 Zones

        • Zones are filters at the input of each chain in Instrument and Effect Racks that decide which values reach it. They pass everything until you change them.
        • The Key, Vel and Chain buttons above the Chain List open their editors; Hide closes them. Audio Effect Racks have only chain select zones; Drum Racks have no zones and filter by note in the chain list.
        • Each zone has a lower bar to move or resize it and a thin upper bar for fade ranges. Drag edges to resize, the middle to move.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones/01 Signal Flow through Zones

          • 25.5.1 Signal Flow through Zones

            • In a MIDI Effect Rack with four chains, all chains receive the same MIDI.
            • Before entering a chain, a note must pass that chain's key zone, then its velocity zone, then its chain select zone. Failing any one keeps it out.
            • The outputs of all chains are mixed and passed to whatever follows the Rack.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones/02 Key Zones

          • 25.5.2 Key Zones

            • The Key Zone Editor shows each chain's range across the full MIDI note range, almost 11 octaves. Chains respond only to notes in their zone, which makes keyboard splits.
            • Fade ranges lower the velocity of notes entering the chain.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones/03 Velocity Zones

          • 25.5.3 Velocity Zones

            • In Instrument and MIDI Effect Racks, each chain also responds only to a range of Note On velocities, 1–127, edited like key zones.
            • Fade ranges lower the velocity of notes entering the chain.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/05 Zones/04 Chain Select Zones

          • 25.5.4 Chain Select Zones

            • Chain select zones let one parameter, the draggable Chain selector above the 0–127 scale, choose which chains can play. Only chains whose zone overlaps the selector's value produce output.
            • In Instrument and MIDI Effect Racks they filter only notes by default; Chain Selector Filters MIDI Ctrl in the ruler's context menu makes them filter all MIDI.
            • In MIDI Effect Racks fades lower note velocity. In Instrument and Audio Effect Racks fades lower each chain's output volume: moving the selector off a zone that ends in a fade silences the chain, while a zone without a fade lets reverb tails and delays ring out.

            • 25.5.4.1 Making Preset Banks Using Chain Select

              • Chain select zones default to a length of 1 at value 0. Place four chains with different effects at values 0, 1, 2 and 3 with no overlap, and map the Chain selector to a hardware encoder: turning it switches between four setups.

            • 25.5.4.2 Crossfading Preset Banks Using Fade Ranges

              • Lengthen the zones and add fade ranges so neighbouring presets overlap, keeping one value where each plays alone. Moving the selector then crossfades between presets; wider fades make smoother transitions.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/06 Drum Racks

      • 25.6 Drum Racks

        • Ableton/Instrument/Drum Racks share most features with other Racks but are laid out for drum kits.
        • Four extra toggles in the view column show the Input/Output, Send and Return sections and Auto Select.
        • Input/Output: Receive sets the incoming note the chain answers (with note names, numbers and General MIDI drum names); Play sets the note sent on to its devices; Choke puts it in one of sixteen choke groups, where triggering one chain silences the others, such as a closed hi-hat cutting an open one. With Receive on All Notes, Play and Choke are disabled. The Preview button fires a test note.
        • Mixer: besides volume, pan and Hot-Swap, each chain has post-fader sends to the return chains, once they exist.
        • Return chains: up to six chains of audio effects at the bottom of the list, fed by the sends. Each return's Audio To goes to the Rack's output or straight to the Set's return tracks.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/06 Drum Racks/01 Pad View

          • 25.6.1 Pad View

            • Pad View shows one pad per MIDI note (128 in all). Shift the visible 16 pads by dragging in the overview or with the arrow keys; hold Cmd (Mac) / Alt (Windows) to move one row.
            • Drop almost anything from the browser on a pad and it maps to that note: a sample makes a chain with a Ableton/Instrument/Simpler; an audio effect goes after it; another sample replaces only the Simpler and its sample, keeping effects. In Hot-Swap, D switches the target between the Rack and the last selected pad.
            • Dropping several samples maps them upward chromatically from the pad. Cmd/Alt-dragging them layers all on one pad in a nested Instrument Rack.
            • Dragging one pad onto another swaps their notes, so existing clips play different sounds; Cmd/Alt-dragging layers both pads. See also Ableton/How To/Preserve Name of Clip when Dragging into Drum Rack.
            • Receive choosers in the chain list remap notes too; chains sharing a note all fire from its pad.
            • A pad stands for a note, not a chain — every chain at any depth that receives that note:
              • an empty pad shows its note, and its General MIDI suggestion in the Status Bar;
              • a pad with one chain shows its name and gives mute, solo, preview, Hot-Swap, rename and delete;
              • a pad with several shows "Multi"; mute, solo, preview and delete affect them all.
            • With a natively supported pad controller selected in Settings → Link, Tempo & MIDI, its pads play the pads visible on screen and follow when you scroll.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/07 Using the Macro Controls

      • 25.7 Using the Macro Controls

        • A Rack can show up to 16 Macro Controls; new Racks show eight. Buttons in the view column add or remove visible Macros, and the count is saved with the Set.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/07 Using the Macro Controls/01 Map Mode

          • 25.7.1 Map Mode

            • The Map button in the Macro view turns on Macro Map Mode: mappable parameters get a coloured overlay, Map buttons appear under each Macro, and the Mapping Browser opens.
            • To map, click a device parameter, then a Macro's Map button. The Macro takes the parameter's name and units. Set the range with Min and Max in the Mapping Browser; Min above Max, or Invert Range, inverts it. Click Map again to finish.
            • A mapped parameter is greyed out, controlled by the Macro, though clip envelopes can still modulate it.
            • Edit or delete mappings in the Mapping Browser while Map Mode is on.
            • A Macro with several parameters reverts to a generic name (such as "Macro 3") and a 0–127 scale unless all share the same units and range.
            • Macros can be renamed, coloured and given info text from the Edit or context menu.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/07 Using the Macro Controls/02 Randomizing Macro Controls

          • 25.7.2 Randomizing Macro Controls

            • The Rand button in the title bar randomises every mapped Macro.
            • Exclude Macro from Randomization in a Macro's context menu protects it. Macros mapped to Volume in Instrument Rack presets are excluded by default.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/07 Using the Macro Controls/03 Macro Control Variations

          • 25.7.3 Macro Control Variations

            • Variations store snapshots of a Rack's Macro settings — for capturing sound design states, comparing mix settings, or jumping between settings for builds and drops while performing or recording.
            • Open them with the Show/Hide Macro Variations button. New stores the current state as "Variation 1", "Variation 2" and so on; rename, duplicate or delete from the Edit or context menu.
            • Launch Macro Variation recalls one; Overwrite Macro Variation replaces it with the current state.
            • Exclude Macro From Variations in a Macro's context menu keeps it unchanged when variations launch.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/08 Mixing With Racks

      • 25.8 Mixing With Racks

        • Instrument and Drum Racks with more than one chain can be shown in the Session mixer: a button in the track's title bar, and on nested chains, folds the Rack's chains in or out.
        • Chains look like tracks without clip slots. Their mixer controls mirror the chain list, and reordering, renaming and regrouping work in either place. Clicking a chain's title bar shows its devices.
        • Adjusting one of several selected chains in the Session mixer changes them all; this doesn't happen in the chain list.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/25 Racks/08 Mixing With Racks/01 Extracting Chains

          • 25.8.1 Extracting Chains

            • Chains can be dragged out of Racks into other tracks or Racks from the chain list or the mixer. Drum Rack return chains dragged to the mixer become return tracks.
            • A drum chain dragged from the mixer to a new track takes its MIDI notes along — for example, moving the snare from a drum loop to its own track with its devices and notes. Drag from the chain list to move only the devices.

Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation

  • 26. Automation

    • Recording, drawing, editing and deleting automation in both views, and overriding it by hand.
    • Automation records changes to a parameter over time so they replay the same way on every playback.
    • Almost every mixer and device control can be automated, including tempo. An automated parameter shows a coloured LED, usually red depending on theme; blue LEDs mean modulation, not automation.
    • Automation is stored as breakpoint envelopes: one of the clip envelope types in Session View, and automation lanes in Arrangement View.
    • It can be recorded in Session clips or Arrangement tracks, or drawn and edited by hand.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/01 Recording Automation in Session View

      • 26.1 Recording Automation in Session View

        • Session View automation can go into Session clips, or into the Arrangement's automation lanes when recording the Session to the Arrangement. Only the second can automate tempo.
        • To record into Session clips, turn on Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Button/Automation Arm in the Control Bar and arm the tracks.
        • Setting Record Session automation in to All Tracks (Record, Warp & Launch Settings) records into every playing clip, armed or not — for instance adding automation to a MIDI clip without recording notes.
        • Press Session Record and move controls. Press it again to stop recording and keep playing, or press Stop to stop both.
        • Edit the result in Clip View's Envelope Editor.
        • Copying Session clips into the Arrangement turns their automation into track automation. Recording the Session into the Arrangement captures existing clip automation along with live changes.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/02 Recording Automation in Arrangement View

      • 26.2 Recording Automation in Arrangement View

        • In Arrangement View, automation records on all tracks during Arrangement recording when Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Button/Automation Arm is on. Individual track arming is unnecessary for automation.
        • Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Button/Arrangement Record starts transport and recording; then changes to tempo, mixer and devices are recorded.
        • Press Arrangement Record again to stop recording but keep playing, or Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Button/Stop to stop both.
        • The result appears in the tracks' automation lanes.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/03 Overwriting Automation

      • 26.3 Overwriting Automation

        • Recording over existing automation for the same parameter replaces it, including on later passes of a looping clip.
        • In Session View, mouse moves behave like "touch": the value returns to the recorded automation on release. Hardware knobs and faders behave like "latch": the last value holds until the clip or loop ends. A first recording into a clip always latches.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/04 Overriding Automation

      • 26.4 Overriding Automation

        • Moving an automated parameter while not recording overrides its automation without erasing it. The LED turns grey.
        • The Control Bar's Re-Enable Automation button lights up when anything is overridden; click it to bring all automation back.
        • Re-Enable Automation in a parameter's context menu restores just that one. Relaunching a Session clip with automation also restores it.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/05 Viewing Automation

      • 26.5 Viewing Automation

        • An automation envelope is a line of breakpoints drawn over the clip or track: value on the vertical axis, time on the horizontal. Drawing it over the content helps line breakpoints up with audio or notes.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/05 Viewing Automation/01 Viewing Automation in Session View

          • 26.5.1 Viewing Automation in Session View

            • In Clip View's Envelope Editor, turn on the Automation toggle and pick a device and control in the choosers beside it.
            • The Device chooser lists the track's devices, the mixer and MIDI Ctrl (MIDI controller clip envelopes). LEDs mark automated controls and the devices holding them. A control without automation shows as a dashed line at its current value.
            • Show Adjusted Envelopes Only shortens the lists to one combined Automated Control chooser; Show All Envelopes brings the full lists back.
            • With Clip and Device View stacked, clicking any device or mixer control opens its envelope — unless Show Adjusted Envelopes Only is on and the control has no automation.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/05 Viewing Automation/02 Viewing Automation in Arrangement View

          • 26.5.2 Viewing Automation in Arrangement View

            • Turn on Ableton/Live/UI/Arrangement View/Toggle/Automation Mode above the track headers, or press A, to open each track's main automation lane.
            • The Device and Automation Control choosers in the lane pick what to show; Device set to None hides the envelope. LEDs and dashed lines work as in Session View, and Show Automated Parameters Only / Show All Parameters shorten or restore the lists.
            • In Automation Mode, clicking a control shows its envelope. Show Automation or Show Automation in New Lane in a parameter's context menu turns on Automation Mode and focuses its lane.

            • More lanes

              • Add Automation Lane moves the current envelope to its own lane and sets the main lane to None, ready for another parameter.
              • A toggle in the main lane hides or shows the extra lanes; drag to reorder them.
              • Up and down arrows move between lanes; left goes back to the main lane; left and right on the main lane hide or show extras.
              • Option-click (Mac) / Ctrl-click (Windows) Add Automation Lane duplicates the main lane's envelope, up to two duplicates. Cmd-click (Mac) / Alt-click (Windows) adds a lane for every automated parameter.
              • Remove Automation Lane removes a lane, or all selected lanes; Cmd/Alt-click removes it and every lane below.
              • See also Ableton/Clip/Q/How can I see all MIDI CC clip envelopes stacked in Arrangement View?

            • 26.5.2.1 Context Menu Options for Automation Lanes

              • Lane header context menus offer Add Automation Lane (main lane), Add Lane for Each Automated Envelope, Remove Automation Lane (extra lanes), and commands to clear all envelopes for the track or one of its devices.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/06 Creating Automation Envelopes

      • 26.6 Creating Automation Envelopes

        • Besides recording, envelopes can be built by adding breakpoints with mouse or keyboard, drawing in Draw Mode, or inserting shapes.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/06 Creating Automation Envelopes/01 Adding Automation Envelope Breakpoints

          • 26.6.1 Adding Automation Envelope Breakpoints

            • Select the parameter, then click the dashed line to add the first breakpoint; the line turns solid. Drag before releasing to set the value, holding Shift for fine control. Double-click away from the line to add a breakpoint there.
            • Breakpoints snap to the grid; hold Cmd (Mac) / Alt (Windows) to place them freely.
            • Values show on hover and while dragging. Right-click the line → Add Value to place a breakpoint at an exact value.
            • On/off and other stepped parameters get stepped envelopes instead of slopes.
            • Keyboard entry is covered in Ableton/Live/UG/41 Accessibility and Keyboard Nav.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/06 Creating Automation Envelopes/02 Drawing Automation Envelopes with Draw Mode

          • 26.6.2 Drawing Automation Envelopes with Draw Mode

            • In Draw Mode, drag to draw the whole envelope freehand. Toggle it from the Options menu, the Control Bar, or B; hold B to use it temporarily.
            • Drawing makes steps as wide as the visible grid. Change the grid with shortcuts or the Options or context menu.
            • For smooth lines, turn off Snap to Grid (Cmd+4 / Ctrl+4), or hold Cmd (Mac) / Alt (Windows) while drawing.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/06 Creating Automation Envelopes/03 Inserting Automation Shapes

          • 26.6.3 Inserting Automation Shapes

            • Shapes from the Envelope Editor's or lane's context menu fill the time selection, or one grid step at the insert marker, scaled to the parameter's range. Useful for rhythmic patterns, swells and builds.
            • The top row holds sine, triangle, sawtooth, inverse sawtooth and square; the bottom row holds two sets of ramps and an ADSR.
            • Waveforms go in as drawn; ramps connect to the values on either side of the selection, so they look different depending on their surroundings.
            • Stepped parameters accept only the square shape.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/07 Editing Automation

      • 26.7 Editing Automation

        • Existing envelopes can be edited by moving breakpoints and segments, by stretching or skewing, or by simplifying.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/07 Editing Automation/01 Editing Breakpoints and Segments

          • 26.7.1 Editing Breakpoints and Segments

            • Drag a breakpoint to move it; a vertical line shows its grid position. Cmd/Alt bypasses snapping; Shift locks horizontal moves and gives fine vertical control.
            • Click a breakpoint to delete it; its neighbours join up.
            • Drag in the background to select several breakpoints, then drag one to move them all, or Delete from the context menu.
            • Drag near a segment to move it, or Shift-click it, then release Shift and drag. Moving a segment in or out of a time selection adds breakpoints at the selection's edges.
            • Dropping onto existing breakpoints overwrites them, but only when you release.
            • Option-drag (Mac) / Alt-drag (Windows) a diagonal segment to curve it; double-click with the same key to straighten it.
            • Edit Value in the context menu types an exact value; other selected breakpoints move by the same amount.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/07 Editing Automation/02 Stretching and Skewing Envelopes

          • 26.7.2 Stretching and Skewing Envelopes

            • Hovering over a time selection shows handles around it.
            • Top and bottom centre handles stretch vertically (Shift for fine control); left and right centre handles stretch in time; corner handles skew. An outline previews the result and snaps to the grid unless you hold Cmd/Alt.
            • Option/Alt-drag mirrors the move on the opposite handle.
            • Dragging over breakpoints outside the selection removes them.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/07 Editing Automation/03 Simplifying Envelopes

          • 26.7.3 Simplifying Envelopes

            • Simplify Envelope in the context menu, on a time selection, replaces dense breakpoints with the fewest straight or curved segments that keep the shape.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/08 Deleting Automation

      • 26.8 Deleting Automation

        • Delete Automation in a parameter's context menu, or Cmd+Delete (Mac) / Ctrl+Backspace (Windows) with it selected, removes all its automation; the LED goes away and the value returns to default.
        • Context menus in the Envelope Editor or lane headers also offer Clear Envelope, Clear All Envelopes of… (one device; lane headers only) and Clear All Envelopes (the whole Set).
        • To remove only part of an envelope, edit its breakpoints.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/09 Additional Tips for Working with Automation

      • 26.9 Additional Tips for Working with Automation

        • Tempo automation, locking envelopes to the timeline, and Edit menu commands.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/09 Additional Tips for Working with Automation/01 Automating the Tempo

          • 26.9.1 Automating the Tempo

            • Tempo can be automated like any control: tempo changes made while recording the Arrangement are saved.
            • To see or draw it, turn on Automation Mode and choose Mixer → Song Tempo in the Main track's lane.
            • The Tempo Minimum and Maximum sliders set the BPM range shown, which is also the range of a MIDI controller mapped to tempo.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/09 Additional Tips for Working with Automation/02 Locking Envelopes in Automation Lanes

          • 26.9.2 Locking Envelopes in Automation Lanes

            • Moving an Arrangement clip normally moves its automation. Ableton/Live/UI/Arrangement View/Toggle/Lock Envelopes, or Options → Lock Envelopes, keeps automation at its timeline position instead.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/09 Additional Tips for Working with Automation/03 Using Edit Menu Commands

          • 26.9.3 Using Edit Menu Commands

            • With a time selection in an automation lane, Cut, Copy, Duplicate and Delete act only on the envelope inside it, leaving clips alone. A selection across several lanes affects all of them.
            • Envelope segments can be pasted to a different parameter, with unpredictable results.

Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation/07 Editing Automation/01 Editing Breakpoints and Segments

  • 26.7.1 Editing Breakpoints and Segments

    • Drag a breakpoint to move it; a vertical line shows its grid position. Cmd/Alt bypasses snapping; Shift locks horizontal moves and gives fine vertical control.
    • Click a breakpoint to delete it; its neighbours join up.
    • Drag in the background to select several breakpoints, then drag one to move them all, or Delete from the context menu.
    • Drag near a segment to move it, or Shift-click it, then release Shift and drag. Moving a segment in or out of a time selection adds breakpoints at the selection's edges.
    • Dropping onto existing breakpoints overwrites them, but only when you release.
    • Option-drag (Mac) / Alt-drag (Windows) a diagonal segment to curve it; double-click with the same key to straighten it.
    • Edit Value in the context menu types an exact value; other selected breakpoints move by the same amount.

  • 26.8 Deleting Automation

    • Delete Automation in a parameter's context menu, or Cmd+Delete (Mac) / Ctrl+Backspace (Windows) with it selected, removes all its automation; the LED goes away and the value returns to default.

Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes

  • 27. Clip Envelopes

    • Envelopes that belong to a clip and modulate or automate its sample, mixer, device and MIDI controller values.
    • Every clip can carry clip envelopes. Depending on the clip, they represent MIDI controller data or automate or modulate device parameters.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/01 The Clip Envelope Editor

      • 27.1 The Clip Envelope Editor

        • Open the Envelopes tab in Ableton/Clip/View.
        • The Device chooser picks a category:
          • audio clips — "Clip" (sample controls), each effect in the track, and the mixer;
          • MIDI clips — "MIDI Ctrl" (controller data), each device in the track, and the mixer.
        • The Control chooser picks a control within it. LEDs mark envelopes that have been changed; Only show adjusted envelopes shortens both lists.
        • Session clips show Automation and Modulation toggles below the choosers. Arrangement clips only take modulation here; their automation lives in the track's automation lanes.
        • Drawing and editing work as for automation envelopes; see Ableton/Live/UG/26 Automation.
        • Clear Envelope in the editor's context menu, also reached with Cmd+Delete (Mac) / Ctrl+Backspace (Windows), returns an envelope to default.
        • MIDI Envelope Auto-Reset (Options menu or Sample Editor context menu) resets certain MIDI controller values when a new clip starts.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/02 Audio Clip Envelopes

      • 27.2 Audio Clip Envelopes

        • With audio effects, clip envelopes make Live a sound design tool: many variations of one clip, from small corrections to unrecognisable new sounds, all in real time.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/02 Audio Clip Envelopes/01 Clip Envelopes are Non-Destructive

          • 27.2.1 Clip Envelopes are Non-Destructive

            • Envelopes are computed live and never change the sample on disk, so hundreds of clips can share one sample and all sound different.
            • To keep a result as audio, render, resample, or use Consolidate in the Arrangement.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/02 Audio Clip Envelopes/02 Changing Pitch and Tuning per Note

          • 27.2.2 Changing Pitch and Tuning per Note

            • With Warp on, choose Clip → Transposition and change the pitch of individual notes in a loop while it plays.
            • Draw steps along the grid in Draw Mode, then switch Draw Mode off to move breakpoints and soften the steps into ramps.
            • Warp settings decide how closely the envelope is followed: lower Grain Size in Tones or Texture mode, or a smaller Granulation Resolution in Beats mode, for a quicker response.
            • Shift while drawing or dragging gives finer steps for tuning corrections. Cmd+Option-drag (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt-drag (Windows) scrolls.
            • The envelope adds to the Transpose control, and the total stays within −48 to +48 semitones.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/02 Audio Clip Envelopes/03 Muting or Attenuating Notes in a Sample

          • 27.2.3 Muting or Attenuating Notes in a Sample

            • Choose Clip → Gain and draw any volume shape onto the sample.
            • The envelope is a percentage of the Clip Gain setting, so it can turn notes down to silence but never above the gain slider.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/02 Audio Clip Envelopes/04 Scrambling Beats

          • 27.2.4 Scrambling Beats

            • In Beats warp mode only, Clip → Sample Offset shifts where the clip reads from, which rearranges a rhythmic loop.
            • Picture a tape head moved by the envelope: positive values read ahead, negative values read behind. Each vertical grid line is a sixteenth note, from +8 to −8 sixteenths.
            • A descending staircase repeats the first step; a smooth downward ramp slows time, with a slurred sound when it is slightly off 45° (try a 1/32 Granulation Resolution).
            • Good for quick beat variations; precise cutting and splicing is easier in the Arrangement with Consolidate.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/02 Audio Clip Envelopes/05 Using Clips as Templates

          • 27.2.5 Using Clips as Templates

            • To hear a clip's envelopes with another sample, select the clip and drag a new sample onto Clip View. All settings and envelopes stay; only the sample changes.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/03 Mixer and Device Clip Envelopes

      • 27.3 Mixer and Device Clip Envelopes

        • Clip envelopes can automate or modulate mixer and device controls. Automation sets a control's absolute value; modulation only changes that value relatively, so both can act on one parameter.
        • Automation is drawn in red, modulation in blue. On knobs, automation moves the needle while modulation shows as a blue segment of the ring.
        • Example: volume automation fading out over four bars plus a modulation envelope rising over four bars first makes a crescendo, then fades once the falling automation limits the range.
        • Session clips have both kinds, switched with a toggle below the choosers; Arrangement clips have only modulation.
        • In the Control chooser, red LEDs mark automation and blue LEDs mark modulation; a parameter can show both.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/03 Mixer and Device Clip Envelopes/01 Modulating Mixer Volumes and Sends

          • 27.3.1 Modulating Mixer Volumes and Sends

            • Two modulation envelopes affect volume: Clip Gain, and Track Volume, which acts on the mixer after the effects. A small dot under the volume slider shows the modulated value and follows the slider.
            • Send modulation is also a percentage: it can lower a send to −∞ dB but never past the Send knob's setting.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/03 Mixer and Device Clip Envelopes/02 Modulating Pan

          • 27.3.2 Modulating Pan

            • Pan modulation scales with the Pan knob: centred, it can sweep fully left to right; turned towards either side, its range shrinks; fully left or right, it does nothing.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/03 Mixer and Device Clip Envelopes/03 Modulating Device Controls

          • 27.3.3 Modulating Device Controls

            • Every device on the clip's track is in the Device chooser. As with the mixer, a clip envelope can only move a device control relative to its current value, unlike a preset, which sets values outright.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/04 MIDI Controller Clip Envelopes

      • 27.4 MIDI Controller Clip Envelopes

        • Choose MIDI Ctrl in a MIDI clip's Device chooser and a controller in the Control chooser to draw or edit controller data, including CCs recorded live or imported from MIDI files (marked with LEDs).
        • Most controller numbers up to 119 are available. Receiving devices may not follow standard assignments, so "Pitch Bend" or "Pan" might do something else.
        • The unlinking techniques below also work for MIDI controller envelopes.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/05 Unlinking Clip Envelopes From Clips

      • 27.5 Unlinking Clip Envelopes From Clips

        • A clip envelope can be unlinked from its clip and given its own loop and region settings.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/05 Unlinking Clip Envelopes From Clips/01 Programming a Fade-Out for a Live Set

          • 27.5.1 Programming a Fade-Out for a Live Set

            • To fade out over eight bars with only a one-bar loop:
              • Choose Clip Gain or Mixer Track Volume and unlink it from the sample. The envelope's loop braces turn coloured and its own loop and region controls become active; the sample keeps looping while the envelope plays once.
              • Type 8 into the envelope's loop length field.
              • Drag up on the envelope's time ruler to zoom out fully.
              • Add a breakpoint at the region end and drag it to the bottom.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/05 Unlinking Clip Envelopes From Clips/02 Creating Long Loops from Short Loops

          • 27.5.2 Creating Long Loops from Short Loops

            • Turn on the envelope's own Loop switch and the eight-bar fade repeats over the one-bar sample. Any control works this way, such as a filter sweep every four bars.
            • Make room by dragging the loop braces past the edge of the view or typing values. Lengths can be odd, such as 3.2.1.
            • Several odd-length envelopes in one clip get complex; the start markers of sample and envelope give a shared reference for where playback begins.
            • Markers, loop braces and drawing snap to the zoom-dependent grid.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/05 Unlinking Clip Envelopes From Clips/03 Imposing Rhythm Patterns onto Samples

          • 27.5.3 Imposing Rhythm Patterns onto Samples

            • The reverse works too: a one-bar volume envelope loop over a sample several minutes long repeatedly cuts holes into it, for example removing every third beat.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/05 Unlinking Clip Envelopes From Clips/04 Clip Envelopes as LFOs

          • 27.5.4 Clip Envelopes as LFOs

            • A looping clip envelope acts as an LFO synced to tempo, or unsynced with an odd loop length. Hide the grid to set loop points freely.
            • Stretch and skew handles and automation shapes help design LFO shapes.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes/05 Unlinking Clip Envelopes From Clips/05 Warping Linked Envelopes

          • 27.5.5 Warping Linked Envelopes

            • Linked envelopes follow the clip's Warp Markers: moving a marker stretches or squeezes the envelope too. Warp Markers can also be adjusted from the envelope editor.
