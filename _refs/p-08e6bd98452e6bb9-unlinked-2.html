Ableton/Live/UG/05 Managing Files and Sets/01 Sample Files
  • A sample is an audio file. Live reads mono and stereo WAV, AIFF, AIFF-C, FLAC and OGG Vorbis, and imports MP3, plus M4A on Mac.
  • Samples play from disk, so large files don't fill RAM, though many at once can overload the disk (see Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources).
  • Ableton/Live/UG/05 Managing Files and Sets/01 Sample Files/03 Exporting Audio and Video

    • 5.1.3 Exporting Audio and Video

      • File → Export Audio/Video, or Cmd+Shift+R (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+R (Win), renders the Set's audio, and video too when the Set has video clips.

      • 5.1.3.1 Selection Options

        • Rendered Track chooses what to render, always post-fader:
          • Main: the Main output, the same signal you hear.
          • All Individual Tracks: one file per track, including groups, returns and Main, all the same length for easy alignment. MIDI tracks with no instrument or audio effects are skipped; a MIDI track with audio effects but no instrument exports silence.
          • Selected Tracks Only: the same, for tracks selected before opening the dialog.
          • A single named track.
        • Render Start and Render Length default to the whole Set. An Arrangement time selection made beforehand sets them.
        • The render captures what was playing: if Session clips are playing over the Arrangement, only each track's active clip is rendered.

      • 5.1.3.2 Rendering Options

        • Include Return and Main Effects: adds the returns a track uses and the Main track's effects when rendering individual tracks, for stems.
        • Render as Loop: renders in two passes so an effect tail that runs past the end wraps into the start, making a seamless loop of the same length.
        • Convert to Mono, and Normalize, which raises the gain so the highest peak reaches full scale.
        • Create Analysis File: writes an .asd alongside the render.
        • Sample Rate: rates at or above the Set's rate render directly; lower rates render at the Set's rate and are then downsampled with the SoX Resampler library.

      • 5.1.3.3 Encoding Options

        • Encode PCM (on by default) writes a lossless WAV, AIFF or FLAC file at 16, 24 or 32 bits.
        • Below 32 bits, choose dithering: No Dither, Rectangular, Triangular (the default), or POW-r 1, 2 or 3. Dither only once. Render at 32 bits if the file will be processed further, and use POW-r only on the final render, never for files headed to mastering.
        • Encode MP3 writes a 320 kbps CBR MP3, alone or with the PCM file. MP3 export adds a little silence at the start, which can throw off alignment in another DAW.
        • With both encoders off, Export is grayed out.

      • 5.1.3.4 Video Rendering Options

        • Create Video writes a video file next to the audio. It needs video clips in the Arrangement and a PCM audio file.
        • Video Encoder lists the encoders installed on the computer; Edit opens the encoder's settings when it has any.
        • Audio renders first, then video, possibly in several passes. Resolution matches the source unless the encoder settings change it. See Ableton/Live/UG/28 Video.

      • 5.1.3.5 Real-Time Rendering

        • Rendering is offline unless the signal passes through an External Audio Effect or External Instrument routed to hardware; then the Main output and the tracks using it render in real time.
        • A 10-second countdown lets external gear fall silent first. Change the seconds, Restart, or Skip.
        • Auto-Restart on drop-outs re-renders automatically if Live detects glitches. Restart starts over; Cancel stops and deletes the partial file. The dialog counts attempts; closing other CPU-heavy programs helps.

Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View
  • Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/08 Audio Clip Fades and Crossfades

    • 6.8 Audio Clip Fades and Crossfades

      • Audio clips have fades at their start and end; neighbouring clips on a track can crossfade.
      • Hover over a clip, in a tall enough track, to see the fade handles as small squares at its edges. In Automation Mode, hold F over an automation lane to show them.
      • Fade In Start and Fade Out End handles change a fade's length without moving its peak; the Fade Curve handle shapes it.
      • Selecting time that includes a clip's start or end and choosing Create → Create Fade In/Out, Cmd+Option+F (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+F (Win), sets the fade to that length.
      • Crossfade by dragging a fade handle over the neighbouring clip's edge, or select across the boundary and choose Create Crossfade. The slope handle shapes it.
      • Delete removes a selected fade, unless Create Fades on Clip Edges is on in Record, Warp & Launch Settings; then it resets to 4 ms, and adjacent clips get automatic 4 ms crossfades to prevent clicks.
      • Fades can't cross a clip's loop boundaries, and a clip's two fades can't overlap. A dotted line shows a selected handle's limit.
      • Fades belong to clips, not tracks, and are separate from automation.

Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View

Page reference

  • Clip properties and the clip editor: the panels for audio, pitch and time, transform and generate, plus looping, cropping and replacing clips.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel

    • 8.4 Audio Utilities Panel

      • Audio clips' panel for warping, reversing and external editing, tempo, gain and transposition, plus toggles for edge fades, RAM Mode and high-quality interpolation.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/01 Warp Controls

        • 8.4.1 Warp Controls

          • With Warp off, a sample plays at its own tempo, right for hits, atmospheres, effects and speech. With Warp on, rhythmic material follows the Set's tempo as it changes.
          • Live works out tempo and length for well-prepared loops; other samples may need hints. See Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/02 Reversing Samples

        • 8.4.2 Reversing Samples

          • Reverse writes a reversed copy of the sample and swaps it into the clip; it's saved under the Project's Samples/Processed/Reverse, or the Temporary Folder until the Set is saved.
          • Warp Markers stay fixed to the sample, so they flip (a marker on bar 2's downbeat ends up on the second-to-last bar), and loop and region settings flip too. Clip envelopes stay fixed in time.
          • Long samples show progress in the Status Bar and briefly lock editing, though playback continues. Live remembers both versions until it quits, so reversing again is instant.
          • Session View reverses one clip at a time; in Arrangement View, select a range and use Reverse Clip(s) or R. Avoid reversing during a performance: reapplying settings can glitch.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/03 Destructive Sample Editing

        • 8.4.3 Destructive Sample Editing

          • Edit opens the sample in the external editor set in File & Folder Settings. Playback must stop while you edit.
          • Warp Markers survive only if the length doesn't change, and the edit affects every clip using that sample.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/04 Clip Start and End Fades

        • 8.4.4 Clip Start and End Fades

          • The Clip Fade toggle adds a 0 to 4 ms fade, depending on the signal, at the clip's start and end to prevent clicks. Session clips only; Arrangement clips use their fade handles.
          • It's on by default when Create Fades on Clip Edges is on in Record, Warp & Launch Settings.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/05 Clip RAM Mode

        • 8.4.5 Clip RAM Mode

          • RAM Mode loads the clip's audio into memory instead of streaming it from disk. It helps when the disk can't keep up with many tracks, or when Legato Mode causes dropouts.
          • Use it sparingly: if RAM runs out the system swaps to disk, and swapped audio arriving late causes both mutes and rhythmic hiccups, worse than a disk overload's mutes.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/06 High Quality Interpolation

        • 8.4.6 High Quality Interpolation

          • High Quality uses a better sample-rate conversion algorithm at higher CPU cost, with less distortion, especially in the highs, when transposing or converting sample rates. Transposition of about 19 semitones either way stays free of audible aliasing.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/07 Clip Gain and Pitch

        • 8.4.7 Clip Gain and Pitch

          • Gain, in dB, sets clip gain; split handles show the range when several clips differ.
          • Pitch transposes in semitones, with a second field for cents (100 cents per semitone).

Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping

  • 9. Audio Clips, Tempo, and Warping

    • Tempo, Warp Markers and warp modes for fitting audio to the song's tempo.
    • Live 11 notes: Ableton/Manual/09
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo

      • 9.1 Tempo

        • Setting, tapping and nudging the Set's tempo, and letting Arrangement clips lead it.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo/01 Setting the Tempo

          • 9.1.1 Setting the Tempo

            • The Control Bar's tempo field sets the playback tempo, in whole BPM and hundredths, each separately key or MIDI mappable. Tempo can be automated for gradual or sudden changes.
            • Live can lead or follow external gear over MIDI Clock or Link, or follow an audio source with Tempo Follower. See Ableton/Live/UG/37 Sync with Link Tempo Follower and MIDI.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo/02 Tapping the Tempo

          • 9.1.2 Tapping the Tempo

            • Click Tap Tempo on every beat to set the tempo; the more taps, the more accurate.
            • To tap with a key: turn on the Key switch, select Tap Tempo, press the key, and turn the Key switch off. MIDI Map Mode assigns a note or controller, such as a footswitch.
            • With Start Playback with Tap Tempo on in Record, Warp & Launch Settings, tapping starts playback: four taps in 4/4. Link-connected apps start in phase.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo/03 Nudging the Tempo

          • 9.1.3 Nudging the Tempo

            • For sources not locked to one tempo, such as live players or turntables, the Phase Nudge Up and Down buttons briefly speed up or slow down Live to stay aligned. They can be key or MIDI mapped.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo/04 Clip Tempo Followers and Leaders

          • 9.1.4 Clip Tempo Followers and Leaders

            • Warped clips follow the Set's tempo by default. In Arrangement View, the Lead/Follow toggle in Audio Utilities makes a clip a tempo leader: the Set follows the clip's Warp Markers and the tempo field is disabled.
            • Many clips can lead, but only one sets the tempo at a time: the playing clip on the lowest track.
            • Live writes read-only tempo automation on the Main track, which moves with the leader clips. Unfollow Tempo Automation in the tempo field's context menu turns all leaders into followers and makes the automation editable.
            • Leader automation overrides Tempo Follower. With EXT on, Lead/Follow is disabled.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping

      • 9.2 Warping

        • Warping plays any sample in sync with the chosen tempo, for beat matching, rhythmic experiments and sound design. Its controls are in the Clip View's Audio Utilities panel.
        • With Warp off, the sample plays at its own tempo, right for one-shots, textures, effects and speech. Turn it on for loops and whole songs.
        • Live 11 notes: Ableton/Manual/09.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping/01 Warping Options in Settings

          • 9.2.1 Warping Options in Settings

            • Record, Warp & Launch Settings decide how new samples load, depending on whether Live judges them short or long:
              • Loop/Warp Short Samples: Unwarped One Shot, Warped One Shot, Warped Loop, or Auto (the default), which picks the best fit.
              • Auto-Warp Long Samples, on by default, warps long samples on load with a Warp Marker on each bar's first beat; off, they load unwarped.
              • Default Warp Mode: the stretching algorithm for newly warped samples, Beats at first.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping/02 Importing Samples

          • 9.2.2 Importing Samples

            • Drag samples in from your folders, or with an audio track selected use Create → Import Audio File… for MP3, AAC, Ogg Vorbis, Ogg FLAC or FLAC; it lands at the insert marker or in the selected slot.
            • A file is analyzed on first import and can't be played or edited until that finishes; afterwards it's usually already in sync.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping/03 Warp Markers

          • 9.2.3 Warp Markers

            • An Ableton/Warp/Marker pins a point in the sample, such as a transient, to a place on the timeline, to map the audio's timing or change its rhythm. Import adds some; add as many as you need.
            • Double-click the upper half of the Sample Editor to add one, or press Cmd+I (Mac) / Ctrl+I (Win) to add one at the insert marker. Move markers with the arrow keys or by dragging.
            • With a marker selected, Shift-drag moves the waveform under it to fine-tune the start point.
            • Double-click a marker, or select its time and press Delete (Mac) / Backspace (Win), to remove it.
            • Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Switch/Follow scrolls the editor during playback; it pauses on edits.

            • 9.2.3.1 Transients and Pseudo-Warp Markers

              • Live finds Ableton/Transients on load: amplitude peaks where notes or beats begin, shown as small gray markers at the top of the editor, and usually good spots for Warp Markers.
              • Add or remove transients from the Create menu, or with Cmd+Shift+I and Cmd+Shift+Delete (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+I and Ctrl+Shift+Backspace (Win). Reset Transients removes the ones you added.
              • With a time selection, Insert Warp Markers, Cmd+I (Mac) / Ctrl+I (Win), puts a marker on every transient in it, or at its ends if there are none.
              • Hovering a transient shows a gray Ableton/Warp/Marker/Pseudo. Double-click or drag it to make a real marker; if no marker follows, the clip's tempo changes. Cmd (Mac) / Ctrl (Win) also makes markers at the neighbouring transients; Shift-drag moves the transient itself.

            • 9.2.3.2 Saving Warp Markers with a Sample File

              • Warp Markers save with the Set. The Save button in the clip's title bar also stores them with the sample, so they return whenever the file is used. Only your own samples, not Core Library or Pack content.
              • A sample saved with markers isn't auto-warped, though the Warp From… commands still run Auto-Warp by hand.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping/04 Warping Short Samples

          • 9.2.4 Warping Short Samples

            • These cases assume Loop/Warp Short Samples warps new samples, the default.

            • 9.2.4.1 Even-Length Loops

              • Live assumes a cleanly cut loop of 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 bars, sets the tempo to match, and places markers at both ends.
              • The estimated tempo shows in the BPM field; type the real one, or Shift-drag for fine steps. In Arrangement View, Shift-drag a clip's edge to stretch it.
              • ×2 and ÷2 fix a tempo guessed at double or half speed.

            • 9.2.4.2 Odd-Length Loops

              • Loops with an odd number of bars play out of sync, since Live assumes an even number. Put a marker at the right bar line, for example the start of bar eight in a nine-bar loop.
              • If Live guessed too short, say eight bars for nine, drag the end marker right until the missing bar shows.

            • 9.2.4.3 Uneven-Length Loops

              • For samples not cut to loop or without a clear beat, put the insert marker on the first downbeat and choose Set 1.1.1 Here, then Warp From Here. A marker before trailing silence trims it from the loop.

            • 9.2.4.4 Multi-Clip Warping

              • With several clips of equal length selected, marker edits on one apply to all, for example to fix timing across a multitrack recording.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping/05 Auto-Warping Long Samples

          • 9.2.5 Auto-Warping Long Samples

            • These cases assume Auto-Warp Long Samples is on, the default.

            • 9.2.5.1 Adjusting Auto-Warp Results

              • Auto-Warp usually gets the tempo right. Turn on the metronome while correcting it.
              • If the downbeat is wrong: Shift-drag from a marker to slide the waveform; or put the insert marker on the downbeat and choose Set 1.1.1 Here; or zoom in, add a marker on the downbeat, and drag it to bar 1.
              • Warp Sample as… fits an already-looped sample to a suggested loop length. Warp Selection as… auto-warps a selected part, such as a break inside a song, setting loop markers to fit.
              • For tight control, warp section by section from left to right, pinning each good section with markers; Cmd-click (Mac) / Ctrl-click (Win) selects several markers to move together.
              • The Warp From Here… commands reset markers to the right of a point and leave those to the left alone:
                • Warp From Here: runs Auto-Warp from that point.
                • Warp From Here (Start At …): uses the Set's tempo as the starting guess. Unwarp the clip, tap the tempo to match it, rewarp, then run this.
                • Warp From Here (Straight): one marker using the estimated original BPM, for material with steady tempo.
                • Warp … BPM From Here: one marker assuming the clip matches the Set's tempo; type the known BPM first.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping/06 Manipulating Grooves

          • 9.2.6 Manipulating Grooves

            • Markers can also bend rhythm on purpose: pin a late transient and drag it onto the beat, pinning its neighbours so the change stays local. Combined with grooves this becomes a creative technique.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping/07 Quantizing Audio

          • 9.2.7 Quantizing Audio

            • Quantize moves each transient to the nearest grid line. Click in the Sample Editor, then use Edit → Quantize or Cmd+U (Mac) / Ctrl+U (Win).
            • The Quantize panel sets the grid size or a specific division, triplets included. Amount moves markers only part of the way for a subtler result.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes

      • 9.3 Warp Modes

        • Warp Modes stretch time with granular techniques, repeating or skipping small segments called grains. Each mode chooses, overlaps and crossfades grains differently, so each suits different material, and each can be used creatively.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/01 Beats Mode

          • 9.3.1 Beats Mode

            • For material with a strong rhythm, such as drum loops and most dance music; it keeps transients intact.
            • Preserve: Transients for percussive material, or a grid division to keep fixed beat divisions. Large divisions with transposition give rhythmic artifacts.
            • Transient Loop Mode, for the end of each segment between transients:
              • Loop Off: plays to its end, then silence until the next transient.
              • Loop Forward: jumps back to a zero-crossing near the segment's middle and loops until the next transient.
              • Loop Back-and-Forth: plays back and forth from a zero-crossing near the middle until the next transient; with preserved transients this often sounds best, especially at slow tempos.
            • Transient Envelope: the fade on each segment. 100 means no fade; lower values decay faster, from smoothing clicks to gating effects.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/02 Tones Mode

          • 9.3.2 Tones Mode

            • For audio with a clear pitch: vocals, monophonic instruments, basslines.
            • Grain Size sets the average grain size, adjusted by how clear the pitch changes are. Small grains suit distinct pitch movement; large ones reduce noise but can add artifacts.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/03 Texture Mode

          • 9.3.3 Texture Mode

            • For sounds without a clear pitch: polyphonic orchestral music, pads, noise, drones, and sound design.
            • Grain Size ignores pitch here. Fluctuation adds randomness; higher values vary more.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/04 Re-Pitch Mode

          • 9.3.4 Re-Pitch Mode

            • Changes playback speed so pitch moves with tempo, like a turntable or an old sampler: double speed is an octave up. Transposition controls are disabled.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/05 Complex and Complex Pro Mode

          • 9.3.5 Complex and Complex Pro Mode

            • For mixed material such as whole songs. Complex Pro is a variant that can sound better on polyphonic textures and full mixes.
            • Formants: at 100%, transposition keeps the original formants, the tone's resonances. No effect without transposition.
            • Envelope, default 128: lower for high-pitched material, higher for low-pitched.
            • Both can use more CPU; freeze or resample tracks to save it.
Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo

Page reference

  • 9.1 Tempo

    • Setting, tapping and nudging the Set's tempo, and letting Arrangement clips lead it.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo/01 Setting the Tempo

      • 9.1.1 Setting the Tempo

        • The Control Bar's tempo field sets the playback tempo, in whole BPM and hundredths, each separately key or MIDI mappable. Tempo can be automated for gradual or sudden changes.
        • Live can lead or follow external gear over MIDI Clock or Link, or follow an audio source with Tempo Follower. See Ableton/Live/UG/37 Sync with Link Tempo Follower and MIDI.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo/02 Tapping the Tempo

      • 9.1.2 Tapping the Tempo

        • Click Tap Tempo on every beat to set the tempo; the more taps, the more accurate.
        • To tap with a key: turn on the Key switch, select Tap Tempo, press the key, and turn the Key switch off. MIDI Map Mode assigns a note or controller, such as a footswitch.
        • With Start Playback with Tap Tempo on in Record, Warp & Launch Settings, tapping starts playback: four taps in 4/4. Link-connected apps start in phase.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo/03 Nudging the Tempo

      • 9.1.3 Nudging the Tempo

        • For sources not locked to one tempo, such as live players or turntables, the Phase Nudge Up and Down buttons briefly speed up or slow down Live to stay aligned. They can be key or MIDI mapped.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/01 Tempo/04 Clip Tempo Followers and Leaders

      • 9.1.4 Clip Tempo Followers and Leaders

        • Warped clips follow the Set's tempo by default. In Arrangement View, the Lead/Follow toggle in Audio Utilities makes a clip a tempo leader: the Set follows the clip's Warp Markers and the tempo field is disabled.
        • Many clips can lead, but only one sets the tempo at a time: the playing clip on the lowest track.
        • Live writes read-only tempo automation on the Main track, which moves with the leader clips. Unfollow Tempo Automation in the tempo field's context menu turns all leaders into followers and makes the automation editable.
        • Leader automation overrides Tempo Follower. With EXT on, Lead/Follow is disabled.

  • 9.2 Warping

    • Warping plays any sample in sync with the chosen tempo, for beat matching, rhythmic experiments and sound design. Its controls are in the Clip View's Audio Utilities panel.
    • With Warp off, the sample plays at its own tempo, right for one-shots, textures, effects and speech. Turn it on for loops and whole songs.
    • Live 11 notes: Ableton/Manual/09.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/02 Warping/01 Warping Options in Settings

      • 9.2.1 Warping Options in Settings

        • Record, Warp & Launch Settings decide how new samples load, depending on whether Live judges them short or long:
          • Loop/Warp Short Samples: Unwarped One Shot, Warped One Shot, Warped Loop, or Auto (the default), which picks the best fit.
          • Auto-Warp Long Samples, on by default, warps long samples on load with a Warp Marker on each bar's first beat; off, they load unwarped.
          • Default Warp Mode: the stretching algorithm for newly warped samples, Beats at first.
