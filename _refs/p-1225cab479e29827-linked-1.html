LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/01 Seeking Text
3.1 Seeking Text
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/01 Seeking Text/Invoke Flash Seek Mode

    • What Keyshort invokes nvim/Plugin/flash.nvim Seek mode in Normal mode? card

      • s — type contiguous target characters, then press the green label key beside the match to jump
3.1 Seeking Text
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/01 Seeking Text/End of Line Instead of Seek

    • 1. What Keyshort puts the cursor in Insert mode at the end of the current line, and 2. what Keyshort moves to end of line without entering Insert? card

      • 1. A — Append at end of line (Enter Insert)
      • 2. $ (Shift+4) — Normal-mode end-of-line motion when Seek won't label the target near a line end
LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/02 Scrolling the Screen
3.2 Scrolling the Screen
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/02 Scrolling the Screen/Half Page Scroll Keyshorts

    • 1. What Keyshort scrolls the window down half a screen, and 2. what Keyshort scrolls up half a screen? card

      • 1. <C-d> — down; cursor stays in the same spot relative to the window
      • 2. <C-u> — up; same relative cursor position as half-page down
3.2 Scrolling the Screen
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/02 Scrolling the Screen/Full Page Scroll Keyshorts

    • 1. What Keyshort scrolls forward a full page, and 2. what Keyshort scrolls backward a full page? card

      • 1. <C-f> — forward one screen; accepts a count (e.g. 5<C-f> for five pages)
      • 2. <C-b> — backward one screen; also count-prefixable unlike half-page scroll in the book's emphasis
3.2 Scrolling the Screen
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/02 Scrolling the Screen/Single Line Scroll Keyshorts

    • 1. What Keyshort scrolls the window down one line, and 2. what Keyshort scrolls up one line? card

      • 1. <C-e> — scrolls the window down one line (cursor moves only if it would scroll off screen)
      • 2. <C-y> — scrolls the window up one line; same cursor-stays-put advantage for subtle repositioning
3.2 Scrolling the Screen
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/02 Scrolling the Screen/Z Mode Line Position Keyshorts

    • 1. What Keyshort moves the cursor line to the top of the screen, 2. what moves it to the bottom, and 3. what moves it to the middle? card

      • 1. zt — top of screen (leaves a few lines of context above)
      • 2. zb — bottom of screen (context below)
      • 3. zz — middle of screen (double letter for easy typing)
LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/03 Counting
3.4 Counting
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/03 Counting/Count Prefix Movement

    • 1. What Keyshort sequence moves the cursor up 15 lines in Normal mode, and 2. what moves it five characters to the right? card

      • 1. 15k — count prefix before k repeats the up-line motion 15 times
      • 2. 5l — count prefix before l moves five characters right on the same line
3.4 Counting
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/03 Counting/Find LazyVim Config File

    • What Keyshort opens the file picker scoped to the LazyVim configuration directory (e.g. to reach options.lua)? card

      • <leader>fc — find config files under ~/.config/nvim; see LazyVim/Keyshort/Search
LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/04 Find Mode
3.5 Find Mode
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/04 Find Mode/Find Forward and Backward

    • 1. What Keyshort enters Find mode forward on the current line, and 2. what Keyshort finds backward? card

      • 1. f then a character — highlights matches after the cursor (e.g. fs for letter s)
      • 2. F (shifted) — same motion backward; also accepts a count prefix
3.5 Find Mode
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/04 Find Mode/Find Mode With Count

    • 1. How do you jump to the third highlighted s after fs, and 2. after overshooting with 18f, how do you step back three matches? card

      • 1. 3f — count before f jumps to the Nth match; Find mode stays active for another guess
      • 2. 3F — backward count corrects an overshoot (e.g. three too far after 18f)
3.5 Find Mode
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/04 Find Mode/To Mode for Operations

    • 1. What does t do differently from f in Find/To mode, and 2. what does d2ts delete? card

      • 1. t lands before the target character — easier combined with change/delete operators than f on the char itself
      • 2. d2ts — deletes from cursor up to (but not including) the second s ahead on the line
LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/05 Moving by Words
3.6 Moving by Words
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/05 Moving by Words/Web Words Forward

    • 1. What Keyshort moves to the beginning of the next word, and 2. what moves to the end of the current (or next) word? card

      • 1. w — start of the next word (count repeats for farther jumps)
      • 2. e — end of the current word, or end of the next word if already at the current word's end
3.6 Moving by Words
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/05 Moving by Words/Web Words Backward

    • 1. What Keyshort moves backward to word beginnings, and 2. what Keyshort pair moves to the end of the previous word? card

      • 1. b — beginning of the current word, or the previous word if already at the beginning (countable)
      • 2. ge — go to end of the previous word (g then e; not g4e)
LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/06 Moving by Words Only BIGGER
3.7 Moving by Words, Only BIGGER
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/06 Moving by Words Only BIGGER/W vs w Word Boundaries

    • 1. What Keyshort stops at punctuation-delimited words on myObj.methodName('foo'), and 2. what Keyshort jumps by whitespace-delimited words instead? card

      • 1. w — treats punctuation as word boundaries (many stops across methodName('foo', …))
      • 2. W — moves to just after the next whitespace character (fewer, bigger stops on the same line)
3.7 Moving by Words, Only BIGGER
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/06 Moving by Words Only BIGGER/BIG Word Backward and End

    • 1. What Keyshort is the shifted form of b for BIG words, 2. what is the shifted form of e, and 3. what is the shifted form of ge? card

      • 1. B — backward by whitespace-delimited words (BIG b)
      • 2. E — forward to end of a whitespace-delimited word (BIG e)
      • 3. gE — end of the previous BIG word (BIG ge)
LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/07 Line Targets
3.8 Line Targets
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/07 Line Targets/Line Start Keyshorts

    • 1. What Keyshort moves to the first non-whitespace character on the line, and 2. what moves to column zero? card

      • 1. ^ (Shift+6) — first text on the line; skips leading indentation
      • 2. 0 — the very beginning of the line (first column), including whitespace
3.8 Line Targets
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/07 Line Targets/Line End Keyshorts

    • 1. What Keyshort moves to the end of the line, and 2. what moves to the last non-blank character? card

      • 1. $ (Shift+4) — last character before the newline, regardless of trailing spaces
      • 2. g_ — last non-blank on the line (g then _; alternative combos include $ge or $be)
LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/08 Jump History
3.10 Jump History
  • LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around/08 Jump History/Jump List Back and Forward

    • 1. What Keyshort jumps back to where you came from in the jump list, and 2. what jumps forward again? card

      • 1. <C-o> — older position ("go to the place I jumped from")
      • 2. <C-i> — opposite of <C-o>; steps forward through jump history (same as <Tab> in many contexts)

  • Speaking of keyboard navigation, yes, j and k to move up and down can be super slow if there are a lot of files to navigate. All of the commands that we discussed in LV4AD/Ch/03 Getting Around can be used to move faster. For example, 10j will move the cursor 10 lines down with just three keystrokes compared to pressing j 10 times, and Control-d or Control-u can be used to scroll the tree down or up.
