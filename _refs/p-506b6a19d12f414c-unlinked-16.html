Microfreak/Q/What does it mean in the Microfreak when the Paraphonic button is blinking when I have the sequencer enabled?
  • Paraphonic Chord Mode: the button blinks while you set the mode up, then stays on and blinks slowly. One key now plays the chord you entered, built with the intervals of the current scale.
  • Telling them apart: play one key. A chord points to Chord Mode; a single thick note points to Unison. The guide gives no blink rate for Unison, so the rate alone does not separate them.

Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/02 LFO Rate/01 LFO Retriggering
  • LFO retriggering restarts the LFO each time you press a keyboard key or the arpeggiator or sequencer generates a gate signal. This can make the LFO add a small pitch rise at the start of each note.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/01 What an Envelope Does
  • An instrument's envelope and timbre help make it recognizable. An organ reaches full volume at once, stays there while a key is held, then decays quickly. A piano has a slower attack and longer decay. A string section builds and fades more gradually.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/02 Gates and Triggers
  • The keyboard is the MicroFreak's primary gate source. Touching and holding a key starts the envelope's Attack stage, followed by Decay/Release and Sustain. The envelope stays at the Sustain level while the key is held. Releasing the key ends the gate, and the level falls to zero at the rate set by Decay/Release.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/05 Amp Mod Button
  • The keyboard and sequencer gates control the internal Voltage Controlled Amplifier (VCA) directly: a held key turns the sound on, and releasing it turns the sound off.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/06 Cycling Envelope/01 Cycling Envelope Stages
  • In Env mode, a keyboard or external gate starts Rise. Sustain stays high while the key is held; releasing the key ends the gate and starts Fall. In Run and Loop modes, Hold stays high for the time set with the Hold knob before Fall begins.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/06 Cycling Envelope/03 Legato Options
  • A Utility setting controls how the Envelope and Cycling Envelope respond to legato in monophonic mode. When the setting is OFF, each added note restarts both envelopes. When it is ON, only the first key starts them; later keys use the contour already in progress.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard
  • A Key in Close-Up
  • A capacitive keyboard makes the player part of its electrical circuit. Changing how much skin touches a key changes its resistance and the pressure signal it sends. Finger contact alone can reach about 30% of the pressure range; pressing more of the finger onto the key can reach 100%. This touch response helps make the MicroFreak a performance instrument.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/01 Gates and Triggers
  • Touching and holding a key produces a gate. The gate ends when the finger lifts. When the Envelope's Amp | Mod button is off, the internal amplifier listens for a key press and turns on abruptly. This is an on/off response, but other settings can change how it feels.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/02 Keyboard Responsiveness
  • Hearing is more sensitive to pitch changes than to volume changes, so pitch can help when adjusting keyboard responsiveness. On an empty preset, assign pressure to pitch in the Matrix at the maximum amount. Hold one finger at a 90° angle against the upper edge of a key, then lower it so more skin touches the key. The increased contact sends more pressure and raises the pitch.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/03 Glide
  • The Glide knob controls how quickly pitch moves between notes. It smooths the abrupt pitch change from one key to another, with a time from off to about 10 seconds.

Microfreak/UG/11 Icon Strip
  • Microfreak/UG/11 Icon Strip/01 Key Hold Button

    • 11.1 The Key Hold Button

      • Key Hold locks a key or chord so both hands are free to adjust the MicroFreak's controls.

      • Note/Info The HOLD state is not saved with the preset.

      • The Hold Icon
      • Pressing Hold once keeps the held keys active after the fingers lift. In paraphonic mode, playing additional notes adds them to the current chord.
      • In Arp mode, Hold keeps the arpeggiated notes playing after keys are released. Turning off Arp or Hold ends them. Playing new keys replaces the notes currently playing.

      • Note/Info Hold does not work with external MIDI. To hold external MIDI notes, send the MicroFreak a Sustain message.

      • In Sequencer mode, the Hold Icon has alternate functions.
      • The alternative functions of the Hold Icon
      • In Step-record mode, Hold adds a tie or silence.
      • In Real-time recording mode, Hold clears the content as the sequence records.
      • With Seq Mod, Hold clears sequence modulation; with A or B, it clears the selected sequence.
      • When the Sequencer is disabled, Hold returns to its usual Key Hold function.

      • Note/Info Key Hold can help create a generative, self-evolving patch. Modulating pitch with independent rates from sources such as the LFO and Cycling Envelope makes the pitch change continuously without a sequencer.

Microfreak/UG/16 Paraphonic Chord Mode
  • Play a key to hear the chord with the same interval structure, transposed to that key and fitted to the current scale.
  • A chord's intervals come from scale steps. The root is its first note. A third three semitones above the root makes a minor chord; a third four semitones above makes a major chord. Adding notes shapes the chord further. Paraphonic Chord Mode analyzes the intervals in the chord you play, then rebuilds those intervals from each key you play. For example, a minor seventh chord becomes a minor seventh chord rooted at the played key.
Microfreak/UG/16 Paraphonic Chord Mode/03 Spread Modulation

Microfreak/UG/17 Ext Gear/05 Local Control
  • By default, the keyboard is connected to the sound engine (Local On), so pressing a key plays the MicroFreak. Turn Local Control off when controlling the MicroFreak externally over MIDI: the keyboard sends notes to MIDI out, but they do not reach the sound engine. The MicroFreak may then seem unresponsive.
