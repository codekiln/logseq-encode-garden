33.2 Max for Live Audio Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/01 Align Delay

    • 33.2.1 Align Delay

      • A utility that delays a signal by time, samples or distance.
      • Delay Mode:
        • Time — milliseconds; for lining up with A/V gear or slight stereo widening
        • Samples — for compensating latency from other devices
        • Distance — meters or feet; for timing offsets between speakers in a PA. A Temperature setting (°C or °F) matches the room, since sound travels at different speeds in warm and cold air.
      • Link L/R — the left channel's delay drives both channels and Delay Right is disabled
33.2 Max for Live Audio Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/02 Envelope Follower

    • 33.2.2 Envelope Follower

      • A modulator that follows the level of the incoming audio and turns it into a smooth control curve. Auto-wah is the classic use.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as described in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
      • Gain — input gain
      • Rise and Fall — smooth the attack and release of the curve
      • Delay — delays the curve, in time or synced divisions (Delay Mode buttons)
      • Sidechain: by default the curve comes from the track's own input. The triangle at the top left opens the Audio Routing panel.
        • turn on Sidechain, choose the source track in Audio From, and choose Pre FX, Post FX or Post Mixer (and a Rack channel if needed)
        • Direct and S.C. meters show the track and sidechain levels
        • Sidechain Mix — 0% uses only the track input, 100% only the sidechain; a Drum Rack as sidechain gives rhythmic ducking or gating
33.2 Max for Live Audio Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/03 LFO

    • 33.2.3 LFO

      • A modulator that drives mapped parameters with a low-frequency oscillator.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as described in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
      • Waveform — Sine, Up, Down, Triangle, Square, Random, Bin, Stray or Glider
      • Shape bends the wave; Steps quantizes it into up to 24 steps. Shape is disabled for Random, Bin, Stray and Glider; Steps for Random, Bin and Square.
      • Jitter adds randomness; Smooth softens sudden changes, including jitter.
      • Rate — in Hz or synced divisions (Time Mode toggles). In Hz, ×10 multiplies the rate.
      • Depth — modulation amount for all targets, 0% to 100%
      • Offset — moves the center of the output up or down; the horizontal line in the display marks the center
      • Phase — start position in the cycle, 0% to 100%
      • Hold — freezes the output; releasing Hold continues from the held value
      • R — retriggers from the Phase position
33.2 Max for Live Audio Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/04 Shaper

    • 33.2.4 Shaper

      • A modulator whose output is a breakpoint envelope drawn in its display.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as described in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
      • Drawing:
        • click to add a breakpoint; Option-drag (Mac) or Alt-drag (Windows) between points to curve a segment; Shift-click a point to delete it
        • Clear erases the envelope; six presets next to it start a new one
        • Grid sets grid divisions; Snap snaps points to the grid
      • The small display at the top right shows the output like an oscilloscope.
      • Jitter adds randomness; Smooth softens sudden changes.
      • Phase — start position in the cycle; R retriggers from there (disabled in Sync time mode)
      • Offset — moves the center of the output up or down
      • Trigger modes:
        • Loop — repeats at Rate
        • 1-Shot — plays once each time the mappable T button is pressed
        • Manual — the Manual control scrubs through the envelope
      • Rate — in Hz or synced divisions
      • Depth — modulation amount for all targets

33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • MIDI-triggered modulators (Envelope MIDI, Expression Control, Shaper MIDI) and MIDI utilities (MIDI Monitor, MPE Control, Note Echo). The modulators map targets the same way as the audio-effect modulators in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/01 Envelope MIDI

    • 33.3.1 Envelope MIDI

      • A modulator that drives mapped parameters with an ADSR envelope triggered by notes.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as in the audio-effect modulators.
      • Loop Mode, with an LED that flashes on each trigger:
        • Free — triggers on every note
        • Sync — retriggers at the Sync Rate division
        • Loop — loops at the rate set by Global Time
        • Echo — repeats every Env Echo Time; Env Feedback sets how long the echoes last
      • Global Time scales the whole envelope: 1.00 matches the ADSR times, higher stretches, lower compresses.
      • Amount — modulation amount for all targets
      • Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release, each with a Slope slider for curved stages; the handles in the display can be dragged too.
      • Velocity — scales the envelope peak by note velocity
      • Sustain toggle — off makes the envelope one-shot: Attack, Decay, then Release however long the note is held
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/02 Expression Control

    • 33.3.2 Expression Control

      • A modulator driven by MIDI and MPE sources.
      • Five Mod Source tabs, each set by its MIDI Input Source menu to one of: Velocity, Modwheel, Pitchbend, Pressure, Keytrack, Expression, Random, Increment, Slide, Sustain. Map assigns a target to each source.
      • The Modulation toggle at the top right:
        • Modulate — the target stays adjustable and the two values merge. A polarity toggle chooses Bipolar or Unipolar, and Output Range Max sets the output at full input.
        • Remote Control — Expression Control alone sets the value, between Output Range Min and Max.
      • Curve Type — linear (two breakpoints, shaped by Curve A) or S-shaped (three breakpoints; Curve A shapes the upper part, Curve B the lower). Curve Link ties both parts to Curve A, inversely. Min/Max and the middle point's X-Y sliders, or dragging in the display, also shape the curve.
      • Increment Steps (1–32), active when Increment is a source, sets how many notes it takes to sweep the whole range. Stopping transport resets the count.
      • Random Amount, active when Random is a source, sets how far each note's value can deviate.
      • Smoothing Type — linear or logarithmic; Rise and Fall set how fast values move up or down, 0–1000 ms.
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/03 MIDI Monitor

    • 33.3.3 MIDI Monitor

      • A utility that shows incoming MIDI and MPE data.
      • Note display — incoming notes on a keyboard, with root, chord name and velocity
      • Flow diagram — a running list of notes and other messages such as pitch bend and aftertouch; Freeze pauses it and Clear empties it
      • MPE display — a running view of note, velocity, slide, pressure and per-note pitch
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/04 MPE Control

    • 33.3.4 MPE Control

      • MPE Control reshapes incoming MPE data with curves, usually ahead of a device that uses that data. It can also turn MPE into ordinary channel-wide MIDI so an MPE controller can play instruments without MPE.
      • MPE sends controller data per note, so each note can be shaped on its own; ordinary MIDI CCs affect every note. More on MPE in the Editing MPE chapter.
      • Example: with Expression Control mapping Slide to an instrument's filter cutoff, MPE Control can shape the slide so only high slide values open the filter fully.
      • Three sources, each with its own curve and on/off toggle: Press (pressure), Slide, and NotePB (per-note pitch bend). The selected source's curve is drawn in front.
      • Curve types, chosen at the bottom left of the display:
        • Linear — two breakpoints. Curve bends it to compress (easier to stay low) or expand (easier to reach high values). Min and Max, or dragging the end points, set the range.
        • S-shaped — a third, movable breakpoint in the middle splits the curve in two. The halves are linked; Curve Link off gives each its own Curve dial. The X-Y controls also move the middle point.
      • The triangle at the bottom right opens the advanced settings. Each source has a Smooth toggle with its own Rise and Fall, plus source-specific controls.

      • 33.3.4.1 Press

        • MPE pressure: per-note pressure on a key or pad after the initial strike, like polyphonic aftertouch.
        • Default — a value to use for notes that carry no MPE data
        • Swap to Slide — sends pressure to the Slide source instead, for controllers that only have polyphonic aftertouch
        • Press to AT — converts pressure to channel aftertouch for instruments without MPE

      • 33.3.4.2 Slide

        • MPE slide: the finger's vertical position on a key or pad, sent as CC 74.
        • Default — a value for notes without MPE data
        • Centered — for pad controllers: the middle of the pad gives zero, rising as the finger moves away vertically
        • Mode:
          • Abs (default) — values are absolute
          • Rel — every note starts in the middle of the range and moves only as the finger slides
          • Ons — the value updates only on Note On; this turns off smoothing
        • Slide to Mod — converts slide to Mod Wheel (CC 1) for instruments without MPE

      • 33.3.4.3 NotePB

        • MPE per-note pitch bend: horizontal movement on a key or pad, bending only that note.
        • Pitch Range — matches a controller and an instrument with different bend ranges. At 2x, a controller with ±48 semitones drives an instrument set to ±24. When per-note bend is mapped to something other than pitch, higher values cover more range with less movement.
        • NotePB to PB — converts per-note bend to ordinary pitch bend for instruments without MPE
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/05 Note Echo

    • 33.3.5 Note Echo

      • A MIDI echo: repeats each note at set intervals with falling velocity.
      • Sync on — the delay is chosen in 16th notes from numbered switches; 4 is one beat. The Delay Time percentage nudges the repeats off the grid for a drum-machine swing.
      • Sync off — the delay is in milliseconds; drag the Delay Time slider or type a value.
      • Input — Thru plays the original note and the echoes; Mute plays only the echoes.
      • Pitch — transposition added on each repeat
      • Delay — velocity of the echoes; Fback — how much output feeds back into the delay
      • MPE — echoes MPE data with the notes (otherwise it is filtered out). Press, Slide and Note PB then set feedback for each; below 100% the echoed MPE data fades with each repeat.
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/06 Shaper MIDI

    • 33.3.6 Shaper MIDI

      • A modulator with a breakpoint envelope that restarts on every MIDI note and can respond to velocity — unlike the audio-effect Shaper.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as in the audio-effect modulators.
      • Drawing:
        • click to add a breakpoint; Cmd-click (Mac) or Ctrl-click (Windows) adds the single sustain breakpoint, drawn larger. The envelope holds there while the note is held.
        • Option-drag (Mac) or Alt-drag (Windows) between points to curve a segment; Shift-click a point to delete it
        • Clear erases the envelope; six presets next to it start a new one
        • Grid and Snap as in Shaper
      • The small display shows the output; its LED flashes on each trigger.
      • Jitter adds randomness; Smooth softens sudden changes.
      • Velocity — how much note velocity scales the envelope; 0% ignores velocity
      • Offset — moves the center of the output up or down
      • Loop — repeats the envelope at Rate while a note is held, ignoring the sustain breakpoint
      • Rate — in Hz or synced divisions
      • Depth — modulation amount for all targets
      • Echo — how much each echo of the envelope feeds the next; Time — the echo interval

34. MIDI and Key Remote Control
  • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/01 MIDI Remote Control

    • 34.1 MIDI Remote Control

      • Live can be remote-controlled from external MIDI control surfaces such as MIDI keyboards and controller boxes, and from Push 1, Push 2 and Push 3.
      • Remote control is separate from playing a MIDI track. A key or controller assigned to a Live control stops reaching the MIDI track: if C-1 launches a clip, it no longer plays C-1 on the instrument. The Control Bar's MIDI indicators show which way incoming MIDI went.
      • Set up control surfaces first, in the Link, Tempo & MIDI tab of Live's Settings (Cmd+, on Mac, Ctrl+, on Windows).
      • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/01 MIDI Remote Control/01 Natively Supported Control Surfaces

        • 34.1.1 Natively Supported Control Surfaces

          • Choose control surfaces in the Control Surface column of the Link, Tempo & MIDI tab. Live can use up to six at once.
          • If the controller is listed in the first Control Surface chooser, pick it by name and set its MIDI input and output ports in the next two columns. If it is not listed, set it up by hand instead — see Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/01 MIDI Remote Control/02 Manual Control Surface Setup.
          • Some controllers need a preset dump. The Dump button next to the choosers becomes active when one is needed. Put the hardware in a state to receive the dump (the hardware's own documentation says how), then press Dump.

          • 34.1.1.1 Instant Mappings

            • An instant mapping is Live's built-in assignment of its functions to a supported controller's knobs, sliders and buttons. The controls follow the selected device, reassigning themselves to whatever device is in focus.
            • Manual assignments override instant mappings. For that, also turn on the Remote switches for the controller's ports in the MIDI Ports section.
            • A supported controller can be locked to one device so it stays in reach wherever the focus moves: right-click the device title bar and pick the controller under Lock to…. A hand icon in the title bar marks a locked device.
            • By default, arming a MIDI track locks its instrument to the control surface.
            • Some control surfaces cannot lock to devices.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/01 MIDI Remote Control/02 Manual Control Surface Setup

        • 34.1.2 Manual Control Surface Setup

          • A controller that is not in the Control Surface chooser can still be mapped by hand from the MIDI Ports section of the Link, Tempo & MIDI tab.
          • To use an input port for remote control, set its Remote switch to On. Any number of ports can be on; Live merges their MIDI.
          • For a controller with motorized faders or LEDs, also turn on Remote for its output port so Live can send changed values back to it.
          • To test, send MIDI from the controller: the Control Bar's MIDI indicators flash when Live receives a message.
          • The manual suggests choosing a Takeover Mode before leaving Settings — Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/01 MIDI Remote Control/03 Takeover Mode. Mapping then happens one parameter at a time.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/01 MIDI Remote Control/03 Takeover Mode

        • 34.1.3 Takeover Mode

          • Takeover Mode decides what happens when a control that sends absolute values, such as a fader, is first moved after a bank switch has pointed it at a different parameter, so the hardware and the parameter disagree.
            • None — the new value goes straight to the parameter, usually as a sudden jump.
            • Pick-Up — moving the control does nothing until it passes the parameter's current value; from then on the parameter follows the control. Smooth, but the pick-up point is hard to predict.
            • Value Scaling — Live compares the two values and brings the parameter toward the control smoothly as the control moves, then follows it 1:1 once they meet.
34. MIDI and Key Remote Control
  • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/02 The Mapping Browser

    • 34.2 The Mapping Browser

      • The Mapping Browser manages every manual MIDI, computer-key and Macro Control mapping. It appears only while one of the three mapping modes is on, and shows the mappings for that mode.
      • Each row shows the control, the path to the mapped parameter, the parameter name, and the mapping's Min and Max. Min and Max can be edited at any time and swapped with a context menu command. Delete a mapping with Backspace (Windows) or Delete (Mac).
      • Instant mappings depend on context and are not listed here.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/02 The Mapping Browser/01 Assigning MIDI Remote Control

        • 34.2.1 Assigning MIDI Remote Control

          • After the controller is set up in Settings:
            • 1. Turn on MIDI Map Mode with the MIDI switch in Live's upper right corner (Cmd+M on Mac, Ctrl+M on Windows). Mappable controls turn blue and the Mapping Browser appears. Cmd+Opt+B (Mac) or Ctrl+Alt+B (Windows) opens the browser if it is closed.
            • 2. Click the Live control to map.
            • 3. Move a knob or press a key or pad on the controller. The new mapping appears in the Mapping Browser.
            • 4. Press the MIDI switch again to leave MIDI Map Mode. Entering it again shows the mappings.
          • Launchpad/Custom/Mode/1 Arrangement maps a Launchpad pad to Arrangement Record this way.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/02 The Mapping Browser/02 Mapping to MIDI Notes

        • 34.2.2 Mapping to MIDI Notes

          • A mapped MIDI note sends Note On and Note Off to the control:
            • Session View slots — clips respond according to their Launch Mode.
            • Switches — each Note On flips the switch.
            • Radio buttons — each Note On steps to the next option.
            • Variable parameters — one note flips the parameter between its Min and Max. A range of notes spreads evenly spaced values across the parameter's range, one per note.
          • A slot can be mapped to a note range for playing a clip chromatically: press the root key (default transposition), and while holding it, press one key below and one above to set the range limits.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/02 The Mapping Browser/03 Mapping to Absolute MIDI Controllers

        • 34.2.3 Mapping to Absolute MIDI Controllers

          • Absolute controllers send values from 0 to 127. The result depends on what they are mapped to:
            • Session View slots — 64 and above act as Note On, 63 and below as Note Off.
            • Track activators and device on/off switches — a value inside the mapping's Min–Max range turns the switch on; outside it turns it off. Setting Min above Max reverses that.
            • Other switches, such as transport controls — 64 and above on, below 64 off.
            • Radio buttons — 0–127 is spread across the options.
            • Continuous controls — 0–127 is spread across the parameter's range.
          • Pitch bend and 14-bit absolute controllers (0–16383) work the same way, with the center at 8191/8192.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/02 The Mapping Browser/04 Mapping to Relative MIDI Controllers

        • 34.2.4 Mapping to Relative MIDI Controllers

          • Relative controllers send "increase" and "decrease" messages instead of absolute values, so a parameter never jumps when the hardware and Live disagree. A relative pan knob just moves the pan a little from wherever it is.
          • Live supports four kinds — Signed Bit, Signed Bit 2, Bin Offset and Twos Complement — each also in a linear version. Some encoders accelerate, sending bigger steps when turned quickly.
          • For controllers that are not natively supported, Live tries to detect the kind and whether acceleration is used. Turning the control slowly to the left while mapping helps. Live's guess appears in the Status Bar's mode chooser, where it can be changed by hand.
          • What the messages do:
            • Session View slots — increase acts as Note On, decrease as Note Off.
            • Switches — increase turns on, decrease turns off.
            • Radio buttons — increase steps forward, decrease steps back.
            • Continuous controls — each kind reads the 0–127 range differently to tell increases from decreases.

          • 34.2.4.1 Relative Session View Navigation

            • Besides mapping single slots and scenes, controls can be mapped to move the highlighted scene and launch the highlighted clips. In MIDI Map Mode and Key Map Mode a strip of mappable controls appears below the Session grid.
            • The strip has:
              • buttons that move the highlighted scene up and down
              • a scene number box, best mapped to an endless encoder, for scrolling through scenes
              • a button that launches the highlighted scene; with Select Next Scene on Launch on in the Record, Warp & Launch Settings, each press moves on to the next scene
              • a button that cancels a triggered scene launch
              • one button per track that launches that track's clip in the highlighted scene
            • Live keeps the highlighted scene centered in Session View, which helps in large Sets.

          • 34.2.4.2 Mapping to Clip View Controls

            • Clip View shows whichever clip is selected, or several clips at once, so a mapping to a Clip View control can change any clip in the Set. The manual recommends relative controllers for Clip View controls to avoid jumps.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/02 The Mapping Browser/05 Computer Keyboard Remote Control

        • 34.2.5 Computer Keyboard Remote Control

          • 1. Turn on Key Map Mode with the KEY switch in the upper right corner (Cmd+K on Mac, Ctrl+K on Windows). Mappable controls turn red and the Mapping Browser appears.
          • 2. Click the control to map; only controls with a red overlay can be mapped.
          • 3. Press the computer key to assign. The mapping appears in the Mapping Browser.
          • 4. Press KEY again to leave Key Map Mode.
          • Mapped keys work like MIDI notes: slots follow their Launch Mode, switches flip, and radio buttons step through their options.
          • Key mapping is different from the computer MIDI keyboard, which turns keystrokes into MIDI notes for instruments.
          • Suggested key mappings for locators: Ableton/Manual/06/03

        • Variable parameters — one note flips the parameter between its Min and Max. A range of notes spreads evenly spaced values across the parameter's range, one per note.
      • A slot can be mapped to a note range for playing a clip chromatically: press the root key (default transposition), and while holding it, press one key below and one above to set the range limits.
34.2 The Mapping Browser
  • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/02 The Mapping Browser/03 Mapping to Absolute MIDI Controllers

    • 34.2.3 Mapping to Absolute MIDI Controllers

      • Absolute controllers send values from 0 to 127. The result depends on what they are mapped to:
        • Session View slots — 64 and above act as Note On, 63 and below as Note Off.
        • Track activators and device on/off switches — a value inside the mapping's Min–Max range turns the switch on; outside it turns it off. Setting Min above Max reverses that.
        • Other switches, such as transport controls — 64 and above on, below 64 off.
        • Radio buttons — 0–127 is spread across the options.
        • Continuous controls — 0–127 is spread across the parameter's range.
      • Pitch bend and 14-bit absolute controllers (0–16383) work the same way, with the center at 8191/8192.
34.2 The Mapping Browser
  • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/02 The Mapping Browser/04 Mapping to Relative MIDI Controllers

    • 34.2.4 Mapping to Relative MIDI Controllers

      • Relative controllers send "increase" and "decrease" messages instead of absolute values, so a parameter never jumps when the hardware and Live disagree. A relative pan knob just moves the pan a little from wherever it is.
      • Live supports four kinds — Signed Bit, Signed Bit 2, Bin Offset and Twos Complement — each also in a linear version. Some encoders accelerate, sending bigger steps when turned quickly.
      • For controllers that are not natively supported, Live tries to detect the kind and whether acceleration is used. Turning the control slowly to the left while mapping helps. Live's guess appears in the Status Bar's mode chooser, where it can be changed by hand.
      • What the messages do:
        • Session View slots — increase acts as Note On, decrease as Note Off.
        • Switches — increase turns on, decrease turns off.
        • Radio buttons — increase steps forward, decrease steps back.
        • Continuous controls — each kind reads the 0–127 range differently to tell increases from decreases.

      • 34.2.4.1 Relative Session View Navigation

        • Besides mapping single slots and scenes, controls can be mapped to move the highlighted scene and launch the highlighted clips. In MIDI Map Mode and Key Map Mode a strip of mappable controls appears below the Session grid.
        • The strip has:
          • buttons that move the highlighted scene up and down
          • a scene number box, best mapped to an endless encoder, for scrolling through scenes
          • a button that launches the highlighted scene; with Select Next Scene on Launch on in the Record, Warp & Launch Settings, each press moves on to the next scene
          • a button that cancels a triggered scene launch
          • one button per track that launches that track's clip in the highlighted scene
        • Live keeps the highlighted scene centered in Session View, which helps in large Sets.

      • 34.2.4.2 Mapping to Clip View Controls

        • Clip View shows whichever clip is selected, or several clips at once, so a mapping to a Clip View control can change any clip in the Set. The manual recommends relative controllers for Clip View controls to avoid jumps.
34.2 The Mapping Browser
  • Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote/02 The Mapping Browser/05 Computer Keyboard Remote Control

    • 34.2.5 Computer Keyboard Remote Control

      • 1. Turn on Key Map Mode with the KEY switch in the upper right corner (Cmd+K on Mac, Ctrl+K on Windows). Mappable controls turn red and the Mapping Browser appears.
      • 2. Click the control to map; only controls with a red overlay can be mapped.
      • 3. Press the computer key to assign. The mapping appears in the Mapping Browser.
      • 4. Press KEY again to leave Key Map Mode.
      • Mapped keys work like MIDI notes: slots follow their Launch Mode, switches flip, and radio buttons step through their options.
      • Key mapping is different from the computer MIDI keyboard, which turns keystrokes into MIDI notes for instruments.
      • Suggested key mappings for locators: Ableton/Manual/06/03

    • 35.2 Browsing and Loading Sounds

      • Browse Mode loads sounds and effects from Push 1 without Live's browser. The display shows devices, presets and the browser's Places in columns, one column per folder level; In and Out shift the columns to reach deeper folders or show more presets.
      • The Selection Control buttons (first row under the display) move up a level and the State Control buttons (second row) move down one. Core Library and Pack items preview when selected.
      • The green button on the right loads the selected preset; the one on the left loads the device's default preset. A loaded entry's button turns amber, and pressing it again loads the next entry, which makes auditioning quick. Encoders scroll quickly; Shift with up or down moves a page.
      • What Browse shows depends on the last selected device: replacement instruments for an instrument, effects for an effect, and everything loadable for an empty MIDI track. Folders appear only when they hold something loadable at that moment; samples show only when browsing a single Drum Rack pad.

  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats

    • 35.3 Playing and Programming Beats

      • Turn on Note Mode and load a Drum Rack preset from Browse. On a Drum Rack track, pressing Note cycles the 8x8 grid through three layouts.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats/01 Loop Selector

        • 35.3.1 Loop Selector

          • The grid splits into three parts so you can play, step-sequence and set the clip length at once.
          • The 16 drum pads use Drum Rack's 4x4 layout. Pad colors: bright yellow has a sound, dull yellow is empty, green is playing, light blue is selected, dark blue is soloed, orange is muted.
          • The touch strip or Octave Up and Down move through larger racks 16 pads at a time; add Shift to move by single rows.
          • Holding Note briefly shows the 16 Velocities layout; Shift + Note locks it, and Note again unlocks it.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats/02 16 Velocities Mode

        • 35.3.2 16 Velocities Mode

          • Here the bottom-right 16 pads are 16 velocities for the selected drum pad; tap one to enter steps at that velocity.
          • Holding Note briefly shows the loop length controls; Shift + Note locks them.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats/03 64-Pad Mode

        • 35.3.3 64-Pad Mode

          • Uses the whole 8x8 grid for playing drums, which suits large kits such as sliced samples. Press Note again to toggle to it, and again to return to Loop Selector. Pad colors match the three-part layout.
          • The 16 pads shown for step sequencing don't follow layout changes, so you may need the touch strip or Octave keys to bring the right ones into view.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats/04 Loading Individual Drums

        • 35.3.4 Loading Individual Drums

          • Browse can load or replace a single pad. Press Device to show the track's devices, tap the pad, then press the selection button under its name. Browse now targets that pad, and tapping other pads while browsing retargets.

          • 35.3.4.1 Additional Pad Options for Push 1

            • Hold Duplicate, press a pad, then press the destination pad to copy it. The destination's devices and sound are replaced, but its recorded notes stay.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats/05 Step Sequencing Beats

        • 35.3.5 Step Sequencing Beats

          • Tapping a pad also picks it for step sequencing; hold Select while tapping to pick it silently. Tap step pads to place notes; the clip starts as soon as you tap a step. Each step is a 16th note by default, changed with the Scene/Grid buttons.
          • A moving green pad marks the playing step, red while recording. Tapping a filled step deletes the note; hold Mute to deactivate a step instead, or Solo to solo a sound. Hold Delete and tap a pad to clear its notes within the loop.
          • Step colors: unlit is empty, blue has a note (darker means higher velocity), dull yellow is a muted note. With triplet steps the two right columns turn red and are unused.
          • Velocity and micro-timing per note are covered in Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/12 Step Sequencing Automation; loop length pads in Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/06 Step Sequencing Melodies and Harmonies/01 Adjusting the Loop Length.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats/06 Real-time Recording

        • 35.3.6 Real-time Recording

          • Turn on Metronome for a click if wanted, then press Record and play the pads. Record again stops recording but keeps playing; a third press starts overdub, and further presses toggle playback and overdub.
          • New stops the selected clip and readies a new one on the track, so you can practice first. By default it also copies the clips playing on other tracks to a new scene and keeps them playing; the Workflow preference changes this.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats/07 Fixed Length Recording

        • 35.3.7 Fixed Length Recording

          • Fixed Length makes new clips a set length; hold it to choose the length.
          • With it off, clips record until you press Record, New or Play/Stop. Turning it on mid-recording stops recording and loops the last few bars, per the Fixed Length setting.

      • Repeat and quantize options for recording on Push 1.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/04 Additional Recording Options/01 Recording with Repeat

        • 35.4.1 Recording with Repeat

          • With Repeat on, holding a pad plays an even stream of notes, handy for steady hi-hats; finger pressure sets their volume. The Scene/Grid buttons set the rate, and Push remembers Repeat per track.
          • A quick press latches Repeat on; holding it makes it momentary. The Swing knob adds swing, shown on the display while touched.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/04 Additional Recording Options/02 Quantizing

        • 35.4.2 Quantizing

          • Quantize snaps notes in the selected clip to the grid. Hold it for options:
            • Swing Amount — also on Encoder 1 or the Swing knob.
            • Quantize To — the nearest note value to snap to.
            • Quantize Amount — how far notes may move.
            • Record Quantize — quantize while recording, value on Encoder 8; mirrors the Record Quantization chooser in Live's Edit menu.

  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/05 Playing Melodies and Harmonies

    • 35.5 Playing Melodies and Harmonies

      • Add Track adds a MIDI track and opens Browse to load an instrument; hold it to choose Audio, MIDI or Return. Inside a Group Track, new tracks go into the group.
      • On an instrument track the grid plays notes, in C major by default. The bottom-left pad is C1; each row up is a fourth higher and each pad to the right is the next scale note. A scale is three pads in a row, then three in the next row, and so on.
      • Pad colors: blue is the key's root, white is in the scale, green is playing, red is playing while recording.
      • Holding Note briefly shows loop length controls in the top row; Shift + Note locks them.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/05 Playing Melodies and Harmonies/01 Playing in Other Keys

        • 35.5.1 Playing in Other Keys

          • Scales changes key and scale with the display and the Selection and State Control buttons. The first encoder or the two far-left buttons pick the scale type.
          • Fixed Y/N — with Fixed on, the grid stays put when the key changes and the bottom-left pad stays C, or the nearest key note. With it off, the bottom-left pad is always the root.
          • In Key/Chromatic — In Key folds the grid to scale notes only; Chromatic shows every note with in-key notes lit.
          • Shift in Scales offers more layouts: "4th" and "3rd" set the interval between rows, ^ and > set whether it runs up or right, and "Sequent" lays notes out in order with no duplicates for a wide range.
          • Key, scale, In Key/Chromatic and Fixed are saved with the Set.
          • Drum recording options (fixed length, Repeat, quantize) work here too. Hold Delete and tap a pad to remove every note of that pitch in the loop.

  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/06 Step Sequencing Melodies and Harmonies

    • 35.6 Step Sequencing Melodies and Harmonies

      • Press Note again for the Melodic Sequencer, where all eight rows place notes. With In Key each row is a scale pitch; with Chromatic, in-key rows are lit. The light blue row, bottom by default, is the root. Each column is a step at the Scene/Grid resolution.

      • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/06 Step Sequencing Melodies and Harmonies/01 Adjusting the Loop Length

        • 35.6.1 Adjusting the Loop Length

          • Each loop length pad is a page of steps; a page's length depends on the step size. Drums at 16ths show two pages, or two bars; the Melodic Sequencer shows one page of eight steps, or two beats.
          • Hold one pad and tap another to set the loop; double-tap a pad for a one-page loop.
          • Pages follow the play position by default. Single-tap a page to lock the view to it while the loop keeps its length; reselect the loop to follow again. Tapping a page outside the loop sets the loop to it.
          • Colors: unlit is outside the loop, white is in the loop but not shown, dull yellow is shown but not playing, green is playing.
          • Shift + Note locks the pads per track. Hold Duplicate and press two page pads to copy a page on top of the destination; hold Delete and tap a page pad to clear it first.

  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/14 Setting User Preferences

    • 35.14 Setting User Preferences

      • Hold User for preferences:
        • Pad Threshold — the lightest touch that triggers a note. Low settings can cause accidental or stuck notes.
        • Velocity Curve — from Linear to several Log curves; higher Log values give more range when playing softly.
        • Workflow — how Duplicate, New and the up and down arrows behave:
          • Scene Workflow (default) — Duplicate captures the playing clips into a new scene and keeps playing, like Create → Capture and Insert Scene; New does the same but leaves an empty slot on the current track; arrows move and launch whole scenes.
          • Clip Workflow — Duplicate copies the selected clip to the next slot (Shift + Duplicate captures a scene); New readies an empty slot on the current track only; arrows launch only the selected track's clip.
        • Aftertouch Threshold — the lowest aftertouch value, 0–127, that Push registers; values above it are rescaled across the full range. At 120, an input of 119 does nothing and 120–127 map onto 0–127.

  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/15 Push 1 Control Reference

    • 35.15 Push 1 Control Reference

      • What each section and control does.

      • 35.15.0.1 Focus/Navigation Section

        • Note — on an instrument track the pads play notes, and further presses switch between real-time playing and the melodic sequencer. On a Drum Rack track the lower-left 16 pads play, the lower-right 16 set loop length, and the top four rows step-sequence; press again for 64-pad mode.
        • Session — pads launch Session View clips.
        • Shift — hold for second functions.
        • Arrow keys — move around the Set in Session Mode, or between tracks and scenes in Note Mode.
        • Select — hold and press a clip to select it without launching and show its name, or a drum pad to select its notes silently.

      • 35.15.0.2 Add Section

        • Add Effect — opens Browse to add a device right of the selected one; Shift adds it to the left. For a MIDI effect, select the instrument and use Shift + Add Effect.
        • Add Track — adds a MIDI track to the right; hold to choose Audio, MIDI or Return. Inside a Group Track, the new track joins the group.

      • 35.15.0.3 Note Section

        • Scales — on an instrument track in Note Mode, picks the key and scale, with Fixed Y/N, In Key/Chromatic and scale type. No effect on Drum Rack tracks or in Session Mode.
        • User — turns off Push's built-in functions so it can be remapped; hold for configuration. Relative encoders work best as "Relative (2's Comp.)"; turn the encoder slowly left while mapping to get it.
        • Repeat — holding a pad retriggers it at the Scene/Grid rate.
        • Accent — every note plays at full velocity; hold for temporary accent.
        • Octave Up/Down — shift pads by octave on instrument tracks, by 16 pads on Drum Racks, and by eight scenes in Session Mode. Unlit when no further range exists.

      • 35.15.0.4 State Control Section

        • For tracks, the eight left buttons stop clips, mute or solo, depending on which of Stop, Mute or Solo is active. For devices they turn devices on and off. Other modes reuse them, for example for scale selection. Shift + Stop stops all clips.

      • 35.15.0.5 Selection Control Section

        • Chooses what the encoders and display edit. In and Out reach devices in Racks or more parameter banks, and in Browse shift the columns.

      • 35.15.0.6 Display/Encoder Section

        • Six buttons beside the display set the encoder mode; hold one to switch temporarily. The ninth encoder is Main track volume, or Pre-Cue volume with Shift. Shift with any of the first eight encoders gives fine control.
        • Volume — eight track volumes.
        • Pan & Send — pans, then each send in turn.
        • Track — one track's volume, pan and first six sends; Master selects the Main track.
        • Clip — for MIDI clips: Loop Start (or Clip Start), Position, Loop Length (or Clip End), Loop on/off. Audio clips add Warp Mode, Detune, Transpose and Gain.
        • Device — parameters of the selected device.
        • Browse — scroll devices and presets.

      • 35.15.0.7 Tempo Section

        • Tap Tempo — the Set's tempo follows your taps. With "Start Playback with Tap Tempo" on in the Record, Warp & Launch Settings, four taps in 4/4 also start playback.
        • Metronome — toggles Live's metronome.
        • Left encoder sets tempo in 1 BPM steps, or 0.1 BPM with Shift. Right encoder sets swing for Quantize, Record Quantize and Repeat.

      • 35.15.0.8 Edit Section

        • Undo — Live's own Undo, including actions not made on Push; Shift + Undo redoes.
        • Delete — in Note Mode deletes the selected clip; in Session Mode hold and press a clip. Hold with a Selection Control button to delete a device or track, or touch an encoder to clear its automation or reset it.
        • Quantize — press to quantize selected notes, or the whole clip; hold with a drum pad for that pad only. On audio clips it moves transients. Hold for settings.
        • Double — doubles the loop and its contents.

      • 35.15.0.9 Transport Section

        • Fixed Length — new clips get a set number of bars; hold to choose.
        • Automation — toggles Live's Automation Record for playing Session clips. Shift + Automation re-enables overridden automation; Delete + Automation clears all automation in a clip.
        • Duplicate — captures a scene in Scene Workflow or copies the selected clip in Clip Workflow; with a drum pad, copies that pad.
        • New — stops the selected clip and readies new recording. On an armed MIDI track, Record + New triggers Capture MIDI.
        • Record — record, then play, then overdub.
        • Play/Stop — Live's play button; Shift + Play/Stop returns to 1.1.1 without playing.

      • 35.15.0.10 Touch Strip

        • On instrument tracks it is pitch bend (default) or mod wheel while playing live, and the note range while step sequencing; Select + tap the strip toggles bend and mod. On Drum Rack tracks it picks the pad bank.

      • 35.15.0.11 Pad Section

        • Session Mode launches clips. Note Mode plays notes on instrument tracks or splits into play, loop length and sequencer areas on Drum Rack tracks, with 64-pad mode one Note press away.

      • 35.15.0.12 Scene/Grid Section

        • In Session Mode these launch scenes; hold Select to select a scene without launching. In Note Mode they set step size and Repeat rate.

      • 35.15.0.13 Using Footswitches with Push 1

        • Two jacks take momentary footswitches. Footswitch 1 is sustain. Footswitch 2 toggles Record on a single tap and acts as New on a double tap.
        • If a switch works backwards, plug it in while holding it down, or use a pedal with a polarity switch.

    • 35.3.5 Step Sequencing Beats

      • Tapping a pad also picks it for step sequencing; hold Select while tapping to pick it silently. Tap step pads to place notes; the clip starts as soon as you tap a step. Each step is a 16th note by default, changed with the Scene/Grid buttons.
      • A moving green pad marks the playing step, red while recording. Tapping a filled step deletes the note; hold Mute to deactivate a step instead, or Solo to solo a sound. Hold Delete and tap a pad to clear its notes within the loop.
      • Step colors: unlit is empty, blue has a note (darker means higher velocity), dull yellow is a muted note. With triplet steps the two right columns turn red and are unused.
      • Velocity and micro-timing per note are covered in Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/12 Step Sequencing Automation; loop length pads in Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/06 Step Sequencing Melodies and Harmonies/01 Adjusting the Loop Length.
35.3 Playing and Programming Beats
  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats/06 Real-time Recording

    • 35.3.6 Real-time Recording

      • Turn on Metronome for a click if wanted, then press Record and play the pads. Record again stops recording but keeps playing; a third press starts overdub, and further presses toggle playback and overdub.
      • New stops the selected clip and readies a new one on the track, so you can practice first. By default it also copies the clips playing on other tracks to a new scene and keeps them playing; the Workflow preference changes this.
35.3 Playing and Programming Beats
  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/03 Playing and Programming Beats/07 Fixed Length Recording

    • 35.3.7 Fixed Length Recording

      • Fixed Length makes new clips a set length; hold it to choose the length.
      • With it off, clips record until you press Record, New or Play/Stop. Turning it on mid-recording stops recording and loops the last few bars, per the Fixed Length setting.

35.4 Additional Recording Options
  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/04 Additional Recording Options/01 Recording with Repeat

    • 35.4.1 Recording with Repeat

      • With Repeat on, holding a pad plays an even stream of notes, handy for steady hi-hats; finger pressure sets their volume. The Scene/Grid buttons set the rate, and Push remembers Repeat per track.
      • A quick press latches Repeat on; holding it makes it momentary. The Swing knob adds swing, shown on the display while touched.
35.4 Additional Recording Options
  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/04 Additional Recording Options/02 Quantizing

    • 35.4.2 Quantizing

      • Quantize snaps notes in the selected clip to the grid. Hold it for options:
        • Swing Amount — also on Encoder 1 or the Swing knob.
        • Quantize To — the nearest note value to snap to.
        • Quantize Amount — how far notes may move.
        • Record Quantize — quantize while recording, value on Encoder 8; mirrors the Record Quantization chooser in Live's Edit menu.
      • Hold Quantize and press a drum pad to quantize only that drum's notes.

35.5 Playing Melodies and Harmonies
  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/05 Playing Melodies and Harmonies/01 Playing in Other Keys

    • 35.5.1 Playing in Other Keys

      • Scales changes key and scale with the display and the Selection and State Control buttons. The first encoder or the two far-left buttons pick the scale type.
      • Fixed Y/N — with Fixed on, the grid stays put when the key changes and the bottom-left pad stays C, or the nearest key note. With it off, the bottom-left pad is always the root.
      • In Key/Chromatic — In Key folds the grid to scale notes only; Chromatic shows every note with in-key notes lit.
      • Shift in Scales offers more layouts: "4th" and "3rd" set the interval between rows, ^ and > set whether it runs up or right, and "Sequent" lays notes out in order with no duplicates for a wide range.
      • Key, scale, In Key/Chromatic and Fixed are saved with the Set.
      • Drum recording options (fixed length, Repeat, quantize) work here too. Hold Delete and tap a pad to remove every note of that pitch in the loop.

35.6 Step Sequencing Melodies and Harmonies
  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/06 Step Sequencing Melodies and Harmonies/01 Adjusting the Loop Length

    • 35.6.1 Adjusting the Loop Length

      • Each loop length pad is a page of steps; a page's length depends on the step size. Drums at 16ths show two pages, or two bars; the Melodic Sequencer shows one page of eight steps, or two beats.
      • Hold one pad and tap another to set the loop; double-tap a pad for a one-page loop.
      • Pages follow the play position by default. Single-tap a page to lock the view to it while the loop keeps its length; reselect the loop to follow again. Tapping a page outside the loop sets the loop to it.
      • Colors: unlit is outside the loop, white is in the loop but not shown, dull yellow is shown but not playing, green is playing.
      • Shift + Note locks the pads per track. Hold Duplicate and press two page pads to copy a page on top of the destination; hold Delete and tap a page pad to clear it first.

35.7 Melodic Sequencer + 32 Notes
  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/07 Melodic Sequencer + 32 Notes/01 32 Notes

    • 35.7.1 32 Notes

      • The bottom half plays scale notes and selects them for sequencing; selected notes show in a lighter track color. Hold Select to select without playing.
      • Colors: blue is the root, blue-green is selected, green is playing, white is in scale.
      • Octave Up and Down shift the range; Shift with the touch strip moves by octaves and Shift with Octave moves by one scale note. The Scale menu applies here too.
35.7 Melodic Sequencer + 32 Notes
  • Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/07 Melodic Sequencer + 32 Notes/02 Sequencer

    • 35.7.2 Sequencer

      • Tapping a step in the top half adds all selected notes to it. Holding a step shows its notes in blue-green below; tap one to remove it. Holding several steps adds the notes to all of them.
      • Hold Duplicate, press a step, then another to copy notes on top of the destination.
      • Colors: blue has notes, green is playing, white is selected, dull yellow is muted, gray is empty; with triplets the two right columns go unlit and unused.
      • Two pages, or two bars, show at 16ths. Hold Note for loop length pads in the fifth row; Shift + Note locks them. Page copy and clear work as in Ableton/Live/UG/35 Push 1/06 Step Sequencing Melodies and Harmonies/01 Adjusting the Loop Length.
