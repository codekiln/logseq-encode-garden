Logseq/Entity
Logseq EntityEntity and type
  • The entity system borrows ideas from type systems, and a page may differ from its entity definition while codekiln's idea of that entity is still changing. When codekiln first described the entity system in type-system terms, AI agents checked pages against their definitions like a type checker and asked "are you sure?" over every difference. codekiln brings pages into line with their definitions over time. An agent filing a new page follows its definition, and leaves an older page that differs as it was filed until codekiln asks for a change. Logseq/Entity/Article/Blog and Logseq/Entity/Podcast/Episode already say this about pages filed under older paths.
Logseq/Entity/Article/Blog
Blog
  • In this garden, Blog pages model a single post on a person's blog, as a more specific Logseq/Entity/Article.
BlogWhat counts as a Blog entity
  • Not Blog entities: magazine or newspaper articles better filed as Logseq/Entity/Article; author hubs; the blog index itself.
BlogPage shape
  • Same as Logseq/Entity/Article: H1 title linked to the original post; Summary, Notes, Highlights, and Links as the source warrants.
Logseq/Entity/Feed/Subscribed
Feed SubscribedWhat counts as an instance
  • Not an instance: a single post — that is Logseq/Entity/Article or Logseq/Entity/Article/Blog. A feed read once and abandoned; drop the marker rather than recording a lapsed intent.
Feed SubscribedRelationship to other entity types
  • Logseq/Entity/Article/Blog — the individual posts that arrive through the feed; those pages are not instances of this type, their parent hub is.
Logseq/Entity/Hierarchy/Discussion/Analysis/Opus/26/10/01/0611 ET Mark each page with the most specific entity in each lineage
Mark each page with the most specific entity in each lineageA child entity is either a kind of its parent or a part of it
  • Is-a. Every instance of the child is also an instance of the parent: Logseq/Entity/Article/Blog, Logseq/Entity/Repo/Awesome, the Logseq/Entity/Book/Section levels, Logseq/Entity/Person/Musician, Logseq/Entity/Person/Knowledge Gardener, Logseq/Entity/Book/User Guide.
Logseq/Entity/Hierarchy/Frontmatter/Type
logseq-entity-hierarchy-type:: — how a child entity relates to its parent
  • This property says whether a child entity is a kind of its parent or a part of it, and it goes on the child's definition page, the one whose name nests under the parent's. Every blog post is an article, so the Logseq/Entity/Article/Blog definition page, which nests under Logseq/Entity/Article, would carry logseq-entity-hierarchy-type:: [[Logseq/Entity/Hierarchy/Frontmatter/Type/extends]].
Logseq/Entity/Hierarchy/Frontmatter/Type/extends
extends
  • The child entity is a kind of its parent: every instance of the child is also an instance of the parent, and the child definition page states only how its instances differ from the parent's. Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker extends Logseq/Entity/Person, and Logseq/Entity/Article/Blog extends Logseq/Entity/Article.
Logseq/Entity/Person/Professor
ProfessorResearch and Publications
  • A separately modeled publication uses its own entity, such as Logseq/Entity/Book or Logseq/Entity/Article, with created-by:: pointing to every credited author who has a person hub. The person's linked references collect those publications.
Logseq/Entity/Website
WebsiteWhen we treat something as a website
  • Not this type: a single post, essay, or article → Logseq/Entity/Article. A codebase, app, or tool that happens to have a URL → Logseq/Entity/Software/Project. The organization or company behind a site → Logseq/Entity/Organization or Logseq/Entity/Company. A person's personal site, which lives as a sub-page of that person, e.g. Person/Name/Web.
