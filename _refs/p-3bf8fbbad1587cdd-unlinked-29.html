Microfreak/Q/What does it mean in the Microfreak when the Paraphonic button is blinking when I have the sequencer enabled?
  • Unison: entered with Shift + Paraphonic. The button blinks, and every note plays on all four oscillators tuned slightly apart.
  • Telling them apart: play one key. A chord points to Chord Mode; a single thick note points to Unison. The guide gives no blink rate for Unison, so the rate alone does not separate them.
Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/02 Middle Row/01 Keyboard Glide
  • Glide smooths the pitch transition between notes. Fully counterclockwise, the transition is immediate; turning the knob clockwise increases the glide time. Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/03 Glide explains Glide in detail.
Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/03 Bottom Row/03 Arp Seq
  • The Arpeggiator plays notes derived from the held keys. Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator and Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer share several controls.
Microfreak/UG/04 Presets/03 Preset Config
  • Root Note: Selects C, C♯, D, D♯, E, F, F♯, G, G♯, A, A♯, or B.
Microfreak/UG/05 Connections/01 Control Signals
  • A gate rises at Note On and falls at Note Off. The keyboard sends gates that start the MicroFreak's envelope.
  • Note for advanced users: Analog synthesizers and modular systems use control voltages for modulation. Mostly digital synthesizers such as the MicroFreak use digital signals that mimic those voltages; this manual calls them control signals.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/01 as Soundgen
  • The Digital Oscillator can play notes in the range from C-2 to G8. Although the MicroFreak keyboard spans only two octaves, you can shift the range it plays up and down.
Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/10 Chords
  • The first note of a chord is its root. The third determines whether the chord is minor, three half-steps above the root, or major, four half-steps above it. Adding voices further shapes the minor or major feel.

  • Note/Info Paraphony is disabled in Chords mode. The last key pressed becomes the root note, and only one chord can play at a time.

Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/02 LFO Rate
  • The displayed ratio sets how the LFO cycle relates to the clock. At 8/1, the slowest rate, a cycle completes in eight pulses. Each step up doubles the LFO speed, except the 1/2t, 1/4t, and 1/8t triplet settings. At 1/4, one LFO cycle equals a quarter note.
Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/02 LFO Rate/01 LFO Retriggering
  • LFO retriggering restarts the LFO each time you press a keyboard key or the arpeggiator or sequencer generates a gate signal. This can make the LFO add a small pitch rise at the start of each note.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/06 Cycling Envelope/03 Legato Options
  • Legato connects each new note to the previous one without a gap. Staccato playing lifts the fingers before playing the next note.
  • A Utility setting controls how the Envelope and Cycling Envelope respond to legato in monophonic mode. When the setting is OFF, each added note restarts both envelopes. When it is ON, only the first key starts them; later keys use the contour already in progress.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/03 Glide
  • The Glide knob controls how quickly pitch moves between notes. It smooths the abrupt pitch change from one key to another, with a time from off to about 10 seconds.
  • Pressure can modulate Glide: set Glide as a Matrix destination and Pressure as the source, then adjust the Matrix attenuator. Different LFO waveforms produce different glide intensity and slope. With LFO Sync on, each note gets a different glide shape; changing the LFO rate changes the synchronized rate and the glide speed.
Microfreak/UG/11 Icon Strip/01 Key Hold Button
  • Pressing Hold once keeps the held keys active after the fingers lift. In paraphonic mode, playing additional notes adds them to the current chord.
  • In Arp mode, Hold keeps the arpeggiated notes playing after keys are released. Turning off Arp or Hold ends them. Playing new keys replaces the notes currently playing.

  • Note/Info Hold does not work with external MIDI. To hold external MIDI notes, send the MicroFreak a Sustain message.

Microfreak/UG/11 Icon Strip/03 Touch Strip/02 Bend
  • Pitch bending raises or lowers a note. Press the Bend icon to enable bending.
Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator
  • An arpeggiator breaks a chord into individual notes and plays them one by one.
  • The four icons above the keyboard select how the notes are played:
    • Up plays notes from low to high, regardless of the order in which the keys were pressed.
    • Order plays the notes in the order the keys were pressed.
    • Random plays the notes in a random order.
    • Pattern generates a semi-random pattern from legato key presses.
Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/01 Using Patterns
  • The pattern draws notes from the octave range selected with Oct | Mod. It plays the lowest note twice as often as each higher chord note, emphasizing the root.
Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/03 Arpeggio Rate
  • With Sync on, the Arpeggiator follows the internal clock and Rate selects a time division. For example, 1/2 gives each note a two-beat duration; 1/4 gives quarter notes, or four notes per measure.
  • 1 whole note
  • 1/2 note and 1/2 note triplet
  • 1/4 note and 1/4 note triplet
  • 1/8 note and 1/8 note triplet
  • 1/16 note and 1/16 note triplet
  • 1/32 note and 1/32 note triplet
Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/04 Making it Swing
  • Hold Shift and turn the Rate encoder to adjust Swing. The range is 50–75%; 50% is the default straight rhythm. Swing shifts notes around the beat and can loosen a rhythm, especially when combined with straight notes.
Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/05 Arpeggio Range
  • The Arpeggiator initially plays the held notes within one octave. Press Oct | Mod to choose how many octaves above the held notes are added:
    • 1 plays only notes held on the keyboard.
    • 2 adds the same notes one octave above.
    • 3 adds them two octaves above.
    • 4 adds them three octaves above.
  • While an arpeggio plays, pressing Octave Up or Octave Down keeps the pitches already in the arpeggio and adds newly played notes in the new octave.
  • In Scale mode, notes outside the selected scale are forced into it. Different notes can collapse to the same pitch, creating repeated-note or ratcheting effects. Keep Utility > Preset > Scale open while adjusting the scale and transposition.
Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/06 Transfer to Seq
  • The transferred pattern extends to the current sequence length. A three-note arpeggio transferred to an eight-step pattern becomes an eight-step sequence.
Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator/07 Arp Fun/01 Spicing up Arpeggios
  • A single held note can make a rhythmic pattern: set a medium arpeggio rate, then lift and replace the finger rhythmically. Vary timbre, envelope stages, or volume with modulation or keyboard pressure while keeping the pitch fixed. The manual calls repeating one pitch while changing its other qualities Hoketus.
  • A small amount of pitch modulation from the LFO or Cycling Envelope can make a sustained note resemble a monochord: several strings tuned to the same pitch, with occasional strings a step above or below.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer
  • The MicroFreak sequencer records and plays up to four notes at once in paraphonic mode. It captures pitch, velocity, and note duration, along with movements of up to four controls in modulation tracks.
  • Record one step at a time to adjust each step's notes, velocity, and modulation, or record in real time. When routed through the modulation matrix, sequence steps can also provide pitch and velocity as modulation sources.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use
  • Tie/Rest: During step recording, extend a note across steps or enter silence.
  • A preset can contain monophonic or paraphonic patterns. In paraphonic mode, the sequencer can play up to four voices; with Paraphony off, it plays only the lowest note of each step.
  • Keyboard notes take priority over sequence notes. If you hold two keys in four-voice paraphonic mode, two voices remain for the sequence, which plays its lowest two notes. Incoming MIDI notes have the same priority as keyboard notes; sequence notes have the lowest priority.

  • Note/Info The pitch CV output sends the lowest note in a sequence step. During keyboard playing, it sends the most recently played note.

  • MIDI sends all notes in a played chord, including velocity and aftertouch, even when the chord has more than four notes.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/01 Select & Play
  • Once a pattern contains notes, pressing a keyboard key starts the currently selected pattern; you need not press Play first.
  • A pattern can hold both notes and modulation data.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/02 Keyboard
  • The sequencer can play Unison voices or trigger chords in Chord Mode. Voice priority is keyboard notes, then incoming MIDI notes, then sequencer notes.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record
  • A pattern stores its length, notes and velocity for each step, ties and rests, and up to four recorded modulation parameters. A and B share their length.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/01 Step Time
  • Stop playback and press Record. Hold one or more notes for the current step. You can change the notes while holding keys; the first four notes held before all keys are released are stored. Releasing all keys advances to the next step.
  • Press Tie/Rest without holding notes to insert a silent step and advance.
  • To sustain a note or chord across steps, hold it and press Tie/Rest for each additional step. Releasing the keys advances again.
  • The display shows the current step and its recorded notes. After the final step, the MicroFreak enters Play mode automatically.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/02 Edit
  • Select A or B, stop playback, and press Record. Turn Rate/Swing to move through the steps. The MicroFreak plays each step's notes and recorded modulation as you reach it, looping to the beginning after the final step.
  • At the desired step, hold new notes to replace the stored notes. Press Hold to replace the step with a rest.

  • Note/Info Step recording also accepts notes and velocity from a keyboard connected to MIDI In.

Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/01 Use/03 Record/03 Real Time
  • Start playback, then press Record. A blinking Record icon means the sequencer is waiting for notes or knob movements.
  • During note recording, each pass through the loop replaces notes played at the corresponding steps in the previous pass. Modulation recording runs for one cycle and then stops recording.
  • Playing a note replaces the recorded note at that point. Pressing Hold while recording replaces notes or modulation events at that point with silence; you can fill the gap on the next pass.
  • A DAW can play notes into a MicroFreak set to real-time recording. You can later add knob movement to the modulation tracks.
  • Tip: A longer sequence provides finer recording resolution. Set its length in Utility > Preset > Seq Length; a slower recording rate can make note and modulation placement easier.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/02 Mod Tracks
  • Each pattern has four modulation tracks. The note sequence records pitch and velocity; each modulation track records the position of one control at each step. A track can hold as many values as the sequence has steps, up to 64.
  • In step recording, record the notes first, then capture control positions at the desired steps. Notes and modulation can also be recorded together by holding notes while turning controls.
Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer/02 Mod Tracks/01 Step Time
  • Turning Rate/Swing through the steps plays their stored notes and modulation, so you can hear and edit each value. Turn its assigned control to replace a stored position. Oct | Mod cycles through active modulation tracks.

Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales
  • Scales can shape the emotion of a melody. Adding chord notes from the melody's scale strengthens its character: major notes may sound forceful and happy, while minor notes may sound sad. Responses to major and minor scales vary across musical cultures.
  • The chromatic scale contains twelve notes: C, C♯, D, D♯, E, F, F♯, G, G♯, A, A♯, B. A scale selects notes from those twelve. Most scales use eight notes; pentatonic scales use five, and blues scales use six, combining five pentatonic notes with one chromatic note.
  • C major, also called C Ionian, uses the white piano keys: C, D, E, F, G, A, B. The gaps between selected notes are whole tones or semitones. From C to D is a whole tone; from E to F is a semitone.
Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales/01 Scale Settings

  • Note/Info Save the same preset several times with different Scale or Root note settings to keep multiple versions.

  • The Scale option filters chromatic notes into a scale. The manual lists these note sets:
    • Major: C, D, E, F, G, A, B
    • Minor: C, D, E♭, F, G, A, B♭, B
    • Harmonic Minor: C, D, E, F, G, A♭, B
    • Dorian: C, D, E♭, F, G, A, B♭
    • Mixolydian: C, D, E, F, G, A, B♭
    • Blues: C, D, E♭, F, G♭, G, A♭, A, B♭
    • Pentatonic: C, D, E, G, A
  • With Major selected, the white keys play the major scale. Black keys are also quantized into that scale: the key that normally plays C♯ plays C, and each black key is lowered by a semitone to fit the scale. Chords played on the keyboard therefore use notes from the selected major scale.
Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales/02 The Scale Root
  • Starting a scale on a different note changes its mood because the intervals are heard in a different order. Starting C major on C follows tone, tone, semitone, tone, tone, tone, semitone. Keeping that interval pattern and making D the root gives D, E, F♯, G, A, B, C♯, D.
Microfreak/UG/16 Paraphonic Chord Mode
  • A chord's intervals come from scale steps. The root is its first note. A third three semitones above the root makes a minor chord; a third four semitones above makes a major chord. Adding notes shapes the chord further. Paraphonic Chord Mode analyzes the intervals in the chord you play, then rebuilds those intervals from each key you play. For example, a minor seventh chord becomes a minor seventh chord rooted at the played key.
Microfreak/UG/17 Ext Gear/01 Computer Connection
  • Many devices have MIDI ports but no CV/Gate or USB connectors. The MicroFreak can send them notes and control them from its front panel with CC commands.
Microfreak/UG/17 Ext Gear/02 CV Gate/01 Pitch Gate and Pressure
  • When Sequence A or B is selected, or when you play the keyboard, notes are immediately converted to Control Voltage (CV) and Gate signals at the rear-panel outputs. In Paraphonic mode, notes played on the keyboard take priority over a running sequence.
  • Each note sends three independent voltages: Pitch, Gate, and Pressure. Pressure can send velocity or pressure, depending on the Utility setting or MIDI Control Center.
Microfreak/UG/17 Ext Gear/03 Clock Sources and Destinations
  • Clock input and output can synchronize with older clock formats: 24, 48, or 2 pulses per quarter note (ppq), or one pulse per step.
Microfreak/UG/17 Ext Gear/05 Local Control
  • By default, the keyboard is connected to the sound engine (Local On), so pressing a key plays the MicroFreak. Turn Local Control off when controlling the MicroFreak externally over MIDI: the keyboard sends notes to MIDI out, but they do not reach the sound engine. The MicroFreak may then seem unresponsive.
  • Local Off is useful when an external sequencer controls the MicroFreak. It prevents double triggering when the sequencer sends keyboard notes back to the MicroFreak sound engine, a condition called MIDI echo.
Microfreak/UG/17 Ext Gear/06 MIDI Channels/01 DAW Configuration
  • A DAW can play MIDI tracks on the MicroFreak when the track's MIDI channel matches the MicroFreak's input channel. The Utility settings also let you receive on one channel and send the keyboard's notes to the DAW on another. Set the input and output channels in Utility.
Microfreak/UG/17 Ext Gear/08 MIDI Tutorial VCV Rack
  • In the first position, use a MIDI-CV module to send MicroFreak note values to an oscillator's pitch and velocity values to an ADSR envelope.
Microfreak/UG/17 Ext Gear/09 MIDI CC Control
  • MicroFreak encoders send MIDI CC# data as you turn them. CC# codes control parameters on external synthesizers, modular systems, and software such as VCV Rack. They are parameter-control messages, separate from note messages.
Microfreak/UG/17 Ext Gear/10 Tutorial MIDI CC

  • Note/Info Like note and velocity values, CC# values range from 0 to 127.

  • Press a key while speaking or singing into the microphone to hear your voice resynthesized by the Vocoder Oscillator. The keyboard determines the output pitch; the vocoder follows the volume and timbre of your voice, not the notes you sing.
