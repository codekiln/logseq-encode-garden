Person/Philip Pullman/Book/Series/His Dark Materials/Concept/Daemon

  • Dæmon

    • Overview

      • A dæmon is a person's inner self made external as an animal: it speaks, advises, and has a life that walks beside its human.
      • It matters because Person/Philip Pullman/Book/Series/His Dark Materials treats a person as something that can walk beside you — identity made visible, answerable, and able to settle.

    • Context

      • In Lyra's world a dæmon is a person's nature given an animal body, usually of the opposite sex. Children's dæmons shift form; at adulthood they settle into the animal that fits the person's character.
      • Pullman's pictures for the idea include portraits where a sitter and a small animal share a psychological link — Leonardo's Lady with an Ermine, Holbein's Lady with a Squirrel and a Starling, Tiepolo's Young Woman with a Macaw — and Socrates' daimon, an inner voice given a body.
      • This garden already reaches for the idea when talking about a Horcrux: both are ways a person's essence lives outside the body. A Horcrux hides a torn fragment in an object. A dæmon is the person, walking and talking beside them.

    • Key Principles

      • Companion-soul — human and dæmon are one life in two bodies, not a pet and its owner. Kill the dæmon and the human dies; when the human dies the dæmon fades.
      • Settling is coming of age — form becomes fixed as identity does.
      • Distance is injury — a human and dæmon must stay near each other; forced separation is agony, and far enough apart it kills. Witches, shamans, and (after the Land of the Dead) some others can travel farther because they have already survived that tearing.

    • Mechanism

      • Children's dæmons change form at will. At adolescence the form locks to the animal that matches character.
      • For a human to touch another person's dæmon is taboo; dæmons may touch one another, and those contacts show the relationship between the humans.
      • Permanent tearing of human from dæmon (intercision) releases a burst of energy and usually kills; it is the method the General Oblation Board uses in the first book.

    • Examples

      • Person/Philip Pullman/Book/Series/His Dark Materials/Character/Pantalaimon — Lyra Belacqua's dæmon; also called Pan. Favored changing forms include a dark brown moth and a snow-white ermine; he settles as a red-gold pine marten with a cream-white throat.
      • Horcrux — the garden's other picture of identity stored outside the body; murder-made fragment versus living companion.

    • Misconceptions

      • "A dæmon is a familiar or a pet" — False; it is the person externalized.
      • "A dæmon is a separate character who happens to travel with someone" — Oversimplified; they argue and can be parted, but they are not two people who met.
