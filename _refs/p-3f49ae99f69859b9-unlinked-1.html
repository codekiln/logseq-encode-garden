Inflation/vs/Deflation
  • Inflation is a rising general price level; deflation is a falling general price level. The trade-off is not a choice between two equally desirable targets: rapid or unpredictable movement in either direction can disrupt decisions, while persistent deflation can worsen downturns and make debts harder to repay.
  • Modern central banks generally aim for low, stable inflation rather than a perfectly constant price level. That leaves room for relative prices to adjust while reducing the risks associated with high inflation and deflation.

  • Inflation/vs/Deflation/Inflation

    • Inflation/vs/Deflation/Inflation

      • Inflation

        • In this trade-off, inflation means a broad rise in prices over time. Mild, stable inflation gives households and businesses a predictable planning environment and lets relative prices adjust without requiring nominal prices and wages to fall.
        • When inflation is high or unpredictable, purchasing power shifts between borrowers and savers, contracts become harder to plan around, and people spend effort adjusting prices rather than producing.
  • Lower prices for a particular service are not deflation unless the overall price level is falling. Higher home values are asset-price inflation in ordinary speech, but they are not the same measure as consumer-price inflation.
  • Federal Reserve: Historical approaches to monetary policy describes the costs of unstable inflation and deflation, including harder debt repayment when prices fall unexpectedly.
  • St. Louis Fed: Inflation, disinflation, and deflation distinguishes inflation from disinflation and deflation.

Real/Estate

  • Real estate and inflation

    • A rise in home sale prices is an asset-price change, not by itself consumer-price inflation. Housing services and rents are included in consumer price measures, so a persistent rise in rents can contribute to measured inflation.
    • The Federal Reserve's inflation overview defines inflation as a general increase in the overall price level, rather than a change in one product or asset.
