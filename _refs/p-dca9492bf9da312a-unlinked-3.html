  • Risk vs Reward

    • Summary

      • Every choice that could pay off could also go wrong, and the two arrive together: the options with the largest upside carry the widest spread of outcomes, and the options with the narrowest spread pay the least. Weighing risk against reward means deciding how much uncertainty to accept in exchange for how much expected gain. The answer turns on how much a loss would cost, whether it could be recovered from, and how many more times the same choice will come around.
      • The tension appears in investing, where it is stated as a premium demanded for bearing volatility; in engineering, where a faster or cheaper design carries more ways to fail; in choosing tools and dependencies, where capability comes with surface area; and in personal decisions about what to publish, share or keep private.

    • The Trade-Off

      • Risk/vs/Reward/Risk

        • Risk/vs/Reward/Risk
          • Accepting risk means choosing an option whose outcomes spread wide, including outcomes that lose.
          • Gains — access to payoffs that safe options never reach. The spread is what makes the large upside available at all, and bearing it is what the reward pays for.
          • Costs — the chance of loss, the attention it takes to hold the exposure in mind, and the compounding damage when a loss cannot be recovered from.
          • Too far — ruin: a single bad draw that ends the game, so no later reward is ever collected. A portfolio, a project or a reputation staked on one outcome.

      • Risk/vs/Reward/Reward

        • Risk/vs/Reward/Reward
          • Reaching for reward means choosing the option with the largest expected gain.
          • Gains — the upside itself: return, capability, reach or learning that the cautious option leaves on the table.
          • Costs — the risk that comes attached, since the options with the most reward carry the widest spread, and the habit of counting the upside while discounting the downside.
          • Too far — chasing: taking on any exposure that promises a payoff, until the accumulated spread swamps the expected gain. Its mirror image is leaving reward on the table out of habit, taking only what is certain and small.

    • How the balance is struck

      • Size the bet by the worst case and the ambition by the best case. A loss that would end the game is never worth its expected value; a loss that is small and recoverable can be taken many times over.
      • Prefer choices whose risk is legible. Risk/Profile argues that a small, well-understood set of risks beats a larger set that is individually mild and collectively opaque.
      • Repeated choices favor the higher expected value. A one-shot choice favors the narrower spread.
      • Separate the risk that is paid for from the risk that is not. Exposure that could be avoided or spread out adds variance with no added reward.

    • In this garden

      • Risk/Profile describes the shape of the risk a tool, dependency or decision carries.
      • My/Principle/Balance/Risk vs Reward/wrt Privacy - Make Things As Public As They Can Be applies the trade-off to what to share in public.

  • Quick mental model: Software/Composition/Analysis (SCA) → risk in third-party components; Security/Application/SAST → risk in first-party source; Security/Application/DAST → risk in running behavior; Dependency/Update/Automation → operational mitigation once you know what to bump.
  • Security angle — They do not replace SCA; with policy + CI, they help close disclosed issues quickly (pin bumps, coordinated upgrades) and reduce stale transitive risk—see Dependency/Update/Automation.
  • “Renovate solved security” — False without Software/Composition/Analysis prioritization and risk review; freshness can still introduce breaking or malicious upgrades.

    • note/my/side clarifying heuristic on when to make things more private

      • this week I've been thinking of a Heuristic (see also Epistemics/Concept/Heuristic vs Axiom) that I should, by default, make information as public as I can, and make information more private when they are tied to specifically articulated perceived harms or Threat/Models. Why? because of the reasons defined in the principle, but also because privacy is expensive and complex, and making things public often simplifies a system.
      • I wish there was a term like the opposite of Privacy. Something like Publicity, but without that term's current connotations of, say, a marketing campaign. Of course, we can speak about Openness, but in my opinion, that has a slightly different valence than Privacy. Privacy sounds like a right, an ideal, a quality. One could make a claim that Openness is a right, an ideal or a quality, but those aren't really connotations that drip off of that term, that the concept just naturally comes with or wicks off.
