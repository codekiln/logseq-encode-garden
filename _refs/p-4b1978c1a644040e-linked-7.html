19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/01 Choosing an Input

    • 19.1 Choosing an Input

      • A track records whatever input its In/Out section shows (View → In/Out). In Arrangement View, unfold and enlarge the track to see all of it.
      • By default an audio track records mono from external input 1 or 2, and a MIDI track records all incoming MIDI from active input devices. The computer MIDI keyboard works when no controller is attached.
      • Any track can instead record another mono or stereo input, one channel of one MIDI device, or another track; see Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO.
19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/02 Arming (Record-Enabling) Tracks

    • 19.2 Arming (Record-Enabling) Tracks

      • Click a track's Arm button to record-enable it. Session and Arrangement share tracks, so either view works.
      • Armed tracks are monitored by default ("auto-monitoring"), so you hear the input through the device chain.
      • Arming a MIDI track locks a natively supported control surface to the track's instrument.
      • Arming one track disarms the others unless you hold Cmd (Mac) / Ctrl (Windows) or several tracks are selected. Arming also selects the track, showing its devices.
19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/03 Recording

    • 19.3 Recording

      • Record in Arrangement View to capture several tracks at once and watch the take build along the timeline. Record in Session View to split a take into clips on the fly or to record while launching clips.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/03 Recording/01 Recording Into the Arrangement

        • 19.3.1 Recording Into the Arrangement

          • Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Button/Arrangement Record starts recording. With Start Playback with Record on (Record, Warp & Launch Settings) it starts at once; with it off, recording waits for Play or a clip launch. Shift-click does the opposite of the setting.
          • Every armed track gets a new clip.
          • With MIDI Arrangement Overdub on, new MIDI clips combine what was already in the track with the new input. Audio tracks don't overdub.
          • Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Switch/Punch-In blocks recording before the Arrangement Loop's start, which protects earlier material and gives a pre-roll. Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Switch/Punch-Out stops it after the loop's end.
          • Recording into the Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Toggle/Arrangement Loop keeps every pass. Undo repeatedly to step back through them, or open the clip: the sample holds all passes, the loop brace marks the last, and moving the markers left plays earlier ones.
          • See also Ableton/Arrangement View/Q/Can I record in Arrangement View while playing Session View clips?
      • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/03 Recording/02 Recording Into Session Slots

        • 19.3.2 Recording Into Session Slots

          • Set Global Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Menu/Quantization to anything but None so clips are cut on time.
          • Arm the tracks; empty slots on armed tracks show Clip Record buttons.
          • Session Record records into the selected scene on every armed track; the new clips' launch buttons turn red. Press it again to switch straight to looped playback.
          • Or click one Clip Record button to record a single slot, and press the clip's Launch button to loop it.
          • A slot's Clip Stop button, or the Control Bar's Stop, stops the clip.
          • The New button stops clips on armed tracks and selects a scene ready for a new take, adding a scene if needed. It only appears in Key Map and MIDI Map modes, for mapping.
          • Launching a scene doesn't record into its empty armed slots unless Start Recording on Scene Launch is on in the Record, Warp & Launch Settings.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/03 Recording/03 Overdub Recording MIDI Patterns

        • 19.3.3 Overdub Recording MIDI Patterns

          • To build a drum pattern or melody layer by layer in a looping clip:
            • Set Global Quantization to one bar, and choose a Record Quantization value if notes should be quantized as they're recorded.
            • Double-click an empty Session slot on the instrument's MIDI track to make an empty one-bar clip; change its loop length if needed.
            • Arm the track and press Session Record. Notes are added on every loop pass.
            • Press Session Record to switch between overdubbing and playing along without recording.
          • Option/Alt-double-click an empty slot to create the clip, arm the track and launch it in one step.
          • Undo removes the last pass; notes can be drawn, moved or deleted in the MIDI Note Editor while recording continues.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/03 Recording/04 MIDI Step Recording

        • 19.3.4 MIDI Step Recording

          • Step recording enters notes with the transport stopped, at your own pace.
          • Arm the track, turn on Preview in the clip's MIDI Editor, and click where recording should start.
          • Hold notes and press the right arrow to enter them and move the insert marker one grid step; keep holding and press it again to lengthen them. Hold the notes and press the left arrow to delete them.
          • The step arrows can be MIDI mapped.
19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/04 Recording in Sync

    • 19.4 Recording in Sync

      • Live keeps recordings in sync even when the tempo changes later, so a hard part can be recorded slowly and sped up afterwards. That only works if the recording was made in time.
      • The easiest way is to play with the metronome: turn on Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Switch/Metronome and it ticks once Play is pressed or a clip launches.
      • The mixer's Preview Volume knob sets its level; the menu next to the switch holds further settings.
      • Warp Markers can later fix timing or change the feel of audio and MIDI recordings.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/04 Recording in Sync/01 Metronome Settings

        • 19.4.1 Metronome Settings

          • Open from the menu next to the metronome or its context menu.
          • Sets the recording count-in length and the tick sound.
          • Rhythm sets the tick's beat division. Auto follows the time signature's denominator; divisions that don't fit a bar are disabled. If a time signature change makes the chosen division not fit, the tick falls back to Auto, and returns once it fits again.
          • Enable Only While Recording keeps the metronome silent except while recording. With Punch-In on, it sounds only after the punch-in point.
19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/05 Recording Quantized MIDI Notes

    • 19.5 Recording Quantized MIDI Notes

      • Edit → Record Quantization lines up recorded MIDI notes to a chosen subdivision.
      • In the Arrangement, record quantization is its own Undo step, so Undo can remove the quantization and keep the take.
      • It can't be changed while recording, except when overdubbing a looping clip, where changes apply at once and can't be undone separately.
      • Notes can also be quantized afterwards with Edit → Quantize; see Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI.
19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/06 Recording with Count-in

    • 19.6 Recording with Count-in

      • Set a count-in from the menu next to the Metronome switch. Recording starts once it finishes.
      • The Control Bar's Arrangement Position shows the count-in in blue, counting from negative values (−2.1.1 for two bars) up to 1.1.1.
19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/07 Setting up File Types

    • 19.7 Setting up File Types

      • Record, Warp & Launch Settings choose the File Type and Bit Depth of recorded samples.
      • Choosing a default Warp Mode that fits what you record keeps quality good if the tempo changes later.
19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/08 Where are the Recorded Samples

    • 19.8 Where are the Recorded Samples?

      • Recordings go to the Project folder under Samples/Recorded. Before the Set is saved they live in the Temporary Folder (Settings → File/Folder), which should be on a drive with plenty of space.
19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/09 Using Remote Control for Recording

    • 19.9 Using Remote Control for Recording

      • Key Map Mode and MIDI Map Mode let you record without the mouse.
      • Map Arrangement Record, the transport and track Arm buttons; Launchpad/Custom/Mode/1 Arrangement maps a Launchpad pad to Arrangement Record.
      • For Session recording, map Session Record and New, individual slots, or the relative controls — for example one key for scene down and another to start and stop recording on a track.
      • Map the step recording arrows to foot pedals to move the insert marker while both hands play.
19. Recording New Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/10 Capturing MIDI

    • 19.10 Capturing MIDI

      • Live keeps listening to MIDI on armed or monitored tracks. Capture MIDI turns what you just played into a clip, for when you forgot to record or were improvising.
      • On Push 1 and Push 2, hold Record and press New; Push 3 has a Capture button.
      • What it does depends on the state of the Set.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/10 Capturing MIDI/01 Starting a New Live Set

        • 19.10.1 Starting a New Live Set

          • When the Set has no clips and the transport is stopped:
            • every monitored MIDI track gets a clip with the phrase, in whichever view has focus;
            • Live sets the tempo (within 80–160 BPM), the loop and the notes' grid positions. Ending on the downbeat of the next bar helps it find the phrase;
            • the transport starts and plays the loop, ready for overdubs;
            • notes played before the phrase stay in the clip ahead of the start marker, so the loop can be moved. Crop Clip discards the rest.
          • If the first capture is a single note, the loop spans that note and the tempo is set to make it one, two, four or eight bars.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/19 Recording New Clips/10 Capturing MIDI/02 Adding Material to an Existing Live Set

        • 19.10.2 Adding Material to an Existing Live Set

          • When the transport is running, the Set has clips, or tempo is automated, Capture MIDI keeps the tempo and finds a phrase within it.
          • Playing over a clip on the same track and pressing Capture MIDI adds the new notes on top, for building a pattern in layers.
          • Earlier notes are kept ahead of the start marker here too.

20. Bounce to Audio
  • Ableton/Live/UG/20 Bounce to Audio/01 Bouncing Individual Tracks

    • 20.1 Bouncing Individual Tracks

      • Bounce Track in Place replaces a track with an audio track of its output. It replaces Freeze and Flatten from earlier versions. Find it in the track title bar's or a clip's context menu.
      • Bounce to New Track renders selected clips or a time selection to a new audio track and mutes the originals. Context menu, or Cmd+B (Mac) / Ctrl+B (Windows).
      • Bounces are taken after the effects and before the mixer: device processing is printed, while volume, pan and sends carry over as settings on the new track.
      • A time selection across several Arrangement tracks makes one new audio track per track with a selected clip.
      • Bounce Track in Place affects both views and bounces both Session and Arrangement clips. Bounce to New Track bounces only the selection; the new track appears empty in the other view.
      • New tracks and clips are named after the source plus "(Bounce)" and take the source track's colour. Files go to the Project's Samples/Processed/Bounce folder.
20. Bounce to Audio
  • Ableton/Live/UG/20 Bounce to Audio/02 Bouncing Group Tracks

    • 20.2 Bouncing Group Tracks

      • Bounce Group in Place replaces a whole Group Track with one audio track. Find it in the group's title bar, a group slot, or the group's main lane context menu.
      • Bounce Group to New Track renders selected clips or a time selection in the group to a new track and mutes the originals; also Cmd+B / Ctrl+B.
      • Groups bounce after the mixer, from what reaches the Main output — including effects and sends inside the group, excluding the Main track's own effects.
      • Tracks routed outside the group are left out and may leave silence; return tracks count only if their output reaches Main.
20. Bounce to Audio
  • Ableton/Live/UG/20 Bounce to Audio/03 Pasting Bounced Audio

    • 20.3 Pasting Bounced Audio

      • Paste Bounced Audio bounces whatever you copied in its current state at the moment you paste, so you can change the source and paste again without recopying. Handy for variations with different effects, or for gathering bits of several tracks into one.
      • Copy clips in one Session track, or clips or a time selection in the Arrangement (a group's main lane works too). Then paste into an audio track, an empty MIDI track or a take lane from the main lane's context menu, or with Cmd+Option+V (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+V (Windows). An empty MIDI track becomes an audio track.
      • If the copied source is deleted, the command is unavailable until you copy again.

21. Comping
  • Ableton/Live/UG/21 Comping/01 Take Lanes

    • 21.1 Take Lanes

      • Every audio or MIDI track in Arrangement View has a main lane, heard by default, and any number of take lanes, heard only when auditioned.
      • Take lanes appear automatically when recording in the Arrangement and can be added by hand.
      • Show or hide them with Show Take Lanes in the track header's context menu, the toggle on the main lane, or Cmd+Option+U (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+U (Windows).
      • The left arrow key moves from a take lane to the main lane and folds the take lanes.
      • Take lanes are hidden in Ableton/Live/UI/Arrangement View/Toggle/Automation Mode; showing or inserting them leaves that mode.
21. Comping
  • Ableton/Live/UG/21 Comping/02 Inserting and Managing Take Lanes

    • 21.2 Inserting and Managing Take Lanes

      • Insert: Create → Insert Take Lane, the header context menu, or Shift+Option+T (Mac) / Shift+Alt+T (Windows), for one or several selected tracks. Max for Live can also create lanes and add clips.
      • Duplicate: Cmd+D / Ctrl+D or the header's Duplicate command.
      • Delete: Delete/Backspace or Edit → Delete. Delete All Take Lanes clears a track; Delete All Unused Take Lanes removes lanes that contribute nothing to the main lane's comp.
      • Resize: Option++/- (Mac) / Alt++/- (Windows), Option/Alt+mouse wheel, or drag a lane's edge; works on all selected lanes.
      • Reorder: drag, or Cmd/Ctrl+up/down arrow.
      • Rename: Cmd+R / Ctrl+R; several at once, with Tab and Shift+Tab to move between lanes.
21. Comping
  • Ableton/Live/UG/21 Comping/03 Recording Takes

    • 21.3 Recording Takes

      • Recording in Arrangement View adds take lanes to armed tracks and puts the new clips in them.
      • Each pass over existing material, whether separate passes or loop recording, gets a new lane. An existing lane is reused when nothing sits after the punch-in point.
      • The latest take is always copied to the main lane, so it plays back right away.
      • Takes share the track colour unless Clip Color is set to Random in Theme & Colors Settings.
21. Comping
  • Ableton/Live/UG/21 Comping/04 Inserting Samples

    • 21.4 Inserting Samples

      • Drag samples or MIDI files onto take lanes from the browser or Finder/File Explorer. Hold Cmd (Mac) / Ctrl (Windows) while dragging several to place each on its own track.
21. Comping
  • Ableton/Live/UG/21 Comping/05 Auditioning Take Lanes

    • 21.5 Auditioning Take Lanes

      • Click a take lane's speaker button, or press T, to audition it.
      • Lanes on different tracks can play at the same time, but only one lane per track. With several selected, the last-selected lane on each track plays.
21. Comping
  • Ableton/Live/UG/21 Comping/06 Creating a Comp

    • 21.6 Creating a Comp

      • Press Enter, or use Copy Selection to Main Lane, to copy selected take material to the main lane.
      • Select clips or a time range and press Cmd/Ctrl+up/down arrow to swap in the same span from the next or previous non-empty take. On a take lane the selection moves with it.
      • In Draw Mode, click and drag across a take to copy it to the main lane in one gesture, or click a take to fill a main-lane time selection from it.
      • Main-lane clips are independent copies, so either can be edited without changing the other. Take clips edit like any Arrangement clip and can be copied or dragged to Session slots.
      • Create Fades on Clip Edges (Record, Warp & Launch Settings) adds 4 ms crossfades between adjacent clips to prevent clicks; Cmd+Option+F / Ctrl+Alt+F adds them by hand.
21. Comping
  • Ableton/Live/UG/21 Comping/07 Source Highlights

    • 21.7 Source Highlights

      • Take material used in the main lane's comp is highlighted in the track colour; unused material is desaturated. Highlights only show when position and clip properties match.
      • Drag the edge of a highlight to move the split point between two adjacent parts of the comp.

22. Stem Separation
  • Ableton/Live/UG/22 Stem Separation/01 How Stem Separation Works in Live

    • 22.1 How Stem Separation Works in Live

      • Separation analyses the spectral and time characteristics of a signal and extracts the parts it detects as stems.
      • Live uses a deep learning model trained for source separation that runs locally, with no internet connection or extra tools.
      • Any mono or stereo file splits into four stems: Vocals, Drums, Bass and Others (everything else).
22. Stem Separation
  • Ableton/Live/UG/22 Stem Separation/02 Separating Audio Files and Clips

    • 22.2 Separating Audio Files and Clips

      • Choose Separate Stems to New Audio Tracks from the context menu or Create menu, on a file in the browser or a clip in either view.
      • In the dialog:
        • toggles pick which of the four stems to make;
        • Merge to Single Track combines two or three chosen stems into one;
        • the quality selector picks High Speed or High Quality;
        • Separate starts the process and stops playback.
      • Each stem gets its own track inside a new Group Track, coloured to match the dialog's toggles; the group takes the source track's colour. A single stem, or a merged one, goes on one track instead.
      • Only the clip's audible region is processed — between its start and end markers, or from the start marker through the loop of a looped warped clip.
      • In the Arrangement, Separate Stems for Time Selection on a clip splits it at the selection's edges and separates only that part.
      • Source track effects move to the Group Track; track automation is copied to the group, and clip envelopes and modulation to each stem clip.
      • The source clip is deactivated to avoid doubling; select it and press 0 to turn it back on.
      • From the browser, stems land in whichever view has focus. In an empty Set with warping on for samples, the Set tempo follows the source.
      • Files go to the Project's Samples/Processed/Stems folder (merged stems keep their individual files too), in the File Type from the Record, Warp & Launch Settings, at 44.1 kHz and the source's bit depth.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/22 Stem Separation/02 Separating Audio Files and Clips/01 Separation Speed vs. Quality

        • 22.2.1 Separation Speed vs. Quality

          • High Speed separates every stem in one pass. It can still take several minutes on older computers.
          • High Quality gives cleaner results (a higher signal-to-distortion ratio) by running separate passes for Vocals, Drums and Bass, then deriving Others from what remains.
          • Crop the clip first to save time: Crop Clip Sample to Time Selection in the Sample Editor, or Crop Clip in the Arrangement, both Cmd+Shift+J (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+J (Windows).
          • In High Quality, choosing fewer stems saves time unless Others is included, since Others needs all three other passes. Unused stems from those passes are still saved in the Stems folder.
          • Older Windows computers and Intel Macs separate on the CPU only, so they're slower than Apple silicon Macs, which also use the GPU.
          • Some sounds land in an unexpected stem, such as hi-hat highs in Others or chorused synths in Vocals. Trying both modes can show which suits the material.

22.2 Separating Audio Files and Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/22 Stem Separation/02 Separating Audio Files and Clips/01 Separation Speed vs. Quality

    • 22.2.1 Separation Speed vs. Quality

      • High Speed separates every stem in one pass. It can still take several minutes on older computers.
      • High Quality gives cleaner results (a higher signal-to-distortion ratio) by running separate passes for Vocals, Drums and Bass, then deriving Others from what remains.
      • Crop the clip first to save time: Crop Clip Sample to Time Selection in the Sample Editor, or Crop Clip in the Arrangement, both Cmd+Shift+J (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+J (Windows).
      • In High Quality, choosing fewer stems saves time unless Others is included, since Others needs all three other passes. Unused stems from those passes are still saved in the Stems folder.
      • Older Windows computers and Intel Macs separate on the CPU only, so they're slower than Apple silicon Macs, which also use the GPU.
      • Some sounds land in an unexpected stem, such as hi-hat highs in Others or chorused synths in Vocals. Trying both modes can show which suits the material.

23. Working with Instruments and Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/23 Instruments and Effects/01 Device View

    • 23.1 Device View

      • The Device View at the bottom of the window shows the selected track's devices; double-click a track's title bar to open it.
      • Normally Clip View and Device View take turns. The toggles next to their selectors at the bottom right stack Clip View above Device View so both stay open.
      • Cmd+Option+3 / Cmd+Option+4 (Mac) or Ctrl+Alt+3 / Ctrl+Alt+4 (Windows) show or hide Clip View and Device View.
      • Double-click a device's title bar, or choose Fold, to collapse it.
23. Working with Instruments and Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/23 Instruments and Effects/02 Using Devices

    • 23.2 Using Devices

      • The browser sidebar has labels for Live's devices, Max for Live devices and plug-ins.
      • Double-click a device to add it to the selected track (or a new track), select it and press Enter, or drag it to a track, a drop area or the Device View. Dropping a sample into a MIDI track's Device View creates a Ableton/Instrument/Simpler with it.
      • With Auto monitoring, a track must be armed to hear an external input through its devices; adding an instrument to a MIDI track usually arms it. See Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/01 Monitoring for other setups.
      • Signals run left to right, so order matters. In a MIDI track, everything before the instrument is MIDI and everything after is audio, so a chain can go MIDI effects → instrument → audio effects.
      • Select a device's title bar and press Delete to remove it; drag title bars to reorder, or into another track to move. Cut, copy, paste and duplicate work too; pasted devices go before the selected one, or at the end if you click after the last device. Changes don't interrupt the audio.
      • Meters between devices show where a signal drops out or gets too hot.
      • There's no clipping between devices; it only happens at physical outputs or in rendered files.
      • Clip envelopes can automate or modulate device parameters per clip; see Ableton/Live/UG/27 Clip Envelopes.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/23 Instruments and Effects/02 Using Devices/01 Device Title Bar

        • 23.2.1 Device Title Bar

          • The title bar turns the device on and off, saves and hot-swaps presets, saves defaults and opens the context menu. Some devices add controls, such as a scale awareness toggle or the Chord device's Learn toggle.
          • The Activator toggle switches a device off as if removed: the signal passes untouched and it uses no CPU. Freezing tracks helps with heavy devices.
          • An arrow next to the Activator opens a view above the Device View, such as Roar's Gain Stage and Modulation Matrix or EQ Eight's frequency display.
          • A triangle in the title bar expands a view inside the device, such as Phaser-Flanger's LFO and Envelope Follower.
          • Right-click the title bar or use the Show Options toggle for the context menu: general commands like Cut, Copy and Rename, plus device-specific ones such as Auto Filter's Mono Sidechain.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/23 Instruments and Effects/02 Using Devices/02 Device A-B Comparison

        • 23.2.2 Device A/B Comparison

          • Every built-in device holds two states, A and B, with separate parameter values, for comparing edits — useful for presets, EQ and compression.
          • On loading, A and B are the same. Edits go to A while B keeps the starting values.
          • Compare: Copy A to B (Edit menu, context menu or Show Options) copies values across; Compare: Switch to B, or P, switches state. The title bar shows "(B)" while B is active.
          • Not available for Racks, Max for Live devices or plug-ins.
          • Automation belongs to one state: switching disables it, and switching back doesn't re-enable it. Use Re-Enable Automation in the parameter's context menu in the state you want.
          • Copying a device, or saving it as a preset, takes only the active state's values and uses them for both A and B.
          • Max for Live can reach the states through the Live API.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/23 Instruments and Effects/02 Using Devices/03 Live Device Presets

        • 23.2.3 Live Device Presets

          • Each device shows as a folder in the browser's content pane. The folder itself is the default preset (factory or your own default); the presets inside have custom settings. Presets come from the Core Library, Packs, or your own saves.
          • Browse with the arrow keys: up and down to move, left and right to close and open folders, Enter to load onto the selected track. Double-click or drag also work.
          • Live device files (Ableton/File/.adg, Ableton/File/.adv, Ableton/File/.amxd) can be dragged in from Finder or Explorer, though the browser is preferred.
          • Drop a preset over an existing one in a device chain to replace it.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/23 Instruments and Effects/02 Using Devices/04 Hot-Swapping Presets

        • 23.2.4 Hot-Swapping Presets

          • Hot-Swap links a device to the browser so you can audition and load other presets in its place. Press Q or click the Hot-Swap Presets button in the title bar.
          • Q swaps the selected device, or else the first audio effect on an audio track or the instrument on a MIDI track.
          • The browser opens at the device's folder. Any preset of the same device type can be swapped in — an audio effect can't be swapped for a MIDI effect. Arrow keys browse; Enter or double-click loads. Load the parent folder for the device's default settings.
          • Adding -EnableHotSwapOnSelection to Options.txt loads each preset as soon as it's selected.
          • Leave with Q, Esc, the X in the browser's Hot-Swap bar or in the title bar, or by moving to another view.

          • 23.2.4.1 Hot-Swapping Samples

            • Drum Rack pads, Drum Sampler, Impulse, Sampler and Simpler have a Hot-Swap Sample button: bottom right of each pad, of Drum Sampler's waveform, of each Impulse slot and of Simpler's sample display; top left of Sampler's expanded Zone Editor. Except in Sampler, it appears only on hover.
            • It opens the sample's folder in the browser; load another with Enter or double-click.
            • Leave by clicking the button again, the X in the Hot-Swap bar, Esc, Q, or moving to another view.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/23 Instruments and Effects/02 Using Devices/05 Saving Presets

        • 23.2.5 Saving Presets

          • The Save Preset button in the title bar saves the current settings, and any Info View text, as a new preset in the User Library's Presets folder. Name it and press Enter, or Esc to cancel.
          • Dragging a device by its title bar into any folder under Places saves it there.
          • If Collect Files on Export (Library Settings) is set to Ask, saving a preset that uses samples asks whether to copy them.
          • More in Ableton/Live/UG/04 Browser and, for Project-specific presets, Ableton/Live/UG/05 Managing Files and Sets.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/23 Instruments and Effects/02 Using Devices/06 Defaults

        • 23.2.6 Defaults

          • Defaults apply your most-used settings automatically. They can:
            • load devices with set parameter values;
            • load VST and Audio Units plug-ins with set parameter configurations;
            • create audio and MIDI tracks with set devices and mixer settings;
            • choose the presets used for dropping samples, slicing and converting audio to MIDI.
          • They live in the User Library's Defaults folder. Delete one from its context menu, or select it and press Delete, to return to factory settings.

          • 23.2.6.1 Default Presets

            • Set a device up and choose Save as Default Preset from its Options or context menu (instruments, MIDI and audio effects, and Racks). It is saved under Defaults in Audio Effects, Instruments or MIDI Effects, and loads every time the device is added — samples included for instruments.
            • Dragging a device into those folders works too, as long as it keeps the device's own name, such as "Simpler". Saving a new default asks before overwriting.

          • 23.2.6.2 Default Plug-in Configurations

            • For a VST or Audio Units plug-in, add parameters in Configure Mode and choose Save as Default Configuration. It is saved to Plug-In Configurations → Audio Units or VSTs; VST and AU versions can have separate defaults.
            • Only the list of parameters is saved, not their values. To keep values too, put the plug-in in a Rack and save the Rack as a default preset.

          • 23.2.6.3 Track Defaults

            • Set up a track's devices and mixer settings (routing, volume, pan, sends) and choose Save as Default Audio Track or Save as Default MIDI Track from its title bar's context menu. It goes to Creating Tracks → Audio Track or MIDI Track and applies to every new track of that kind.
            • Dragging a track there also works if it is named Default Audio Track or Default MIDI Track.

          • 23.2.6.4 Sample Dropping Defaults

            • To choose the instrument made when a sample is dropped into an empty MIDI track's Device View or onto a Drum Rack pad, set up an empty Sampler or Simpler and drag it to Dropping Samples → On Device View or On Drum Rack. A pad's Save as Default Pad adds to On Drum Rack too.
            • If a folder holds several, the last one added is used.

          • 23.2.6.5 Slicing Defaults

            • Build an empty Drum Rack with one chain holding an empty Sampler or Simpler, plus any effects and Macro mappings, and drag it into the Slicing folder. It then appears in the Slicing Preset chooser of Slice to New MIDI Track; see Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI.

          • 23.2.6.6 Converting Audio to MIDI Defaults

            • Custom Racks for Harmony, Melody and Drums to MIDI go in the matching Audio to MIDI folder. Use an Instrument Rack (or a single instrument) for Harmony and Melody; Drums needs a top-level Drum Rack with Sampler or Simpler on its pads, not one nested in an Instrument Rack.
            • If a folder holds several, the last one added is used.

          • 23.2.6.7 Project-Specific Defaults

            • A Defaults folder, with the same subfolders, inside the Current Project overrides the User Library defaults for Sets in that Project. Create folders in Finder/Explorer or with New Folder in the browser, then drag devices or tracks in.
            • Plug-ins can't be dragged into the Project. Instead, create Plug-In Configurations/Audio Units and VSTs folders there, save a configuration as usual, and move its folder with the Default.appc file from the User Library into the Project.
            • The Save as Default menu commands always save to the User Library, not the Project.
23. Working with Instruments and Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/23 Instruments and Effects/03 Device Delay Compensation

    • 23.3 Device Delay Compensation

      • Live compensates automatically for processing delays from built-in devices and plug-ins, return tracks included, keeping tracks in sync with as little delay as possible between playing and hearing.
      • It's on by default; Options → Delay Compensation turns it off.
      • With it on, Reduced Latency When Monitoring (Options menu) skips compensation on input-monitored tracks for the lowest latency, at the risk of those tracks falling out of sync with compensated ones such as returns.
      • Tempo-synced effects placed after delay-causing devices can sound out of time.
      • Very large track delays or plug-in latencies can make Live sluggish. Turning compensation off is not usually advised and also disables Track Delay; adjusting track delays may help instead. Compensation can add CPU load.
