Ableton/Live/UG/03 Live Concepts
  • Ableton/Live/UG/03 Live Concepts/14 The Mixer

    • 3.14 The Mixer

      • A clip's signal passes through the track's device chain from left to right and then into the mixer, which Session and Arrangement share.
      • Device-to-device connections are always stereo; inputs and outputs can be set to mono in the Audio Settings.
      • The mixer has volume, pan and sends. Sends feed return tracks, which hold only effects, so every track can share them.
      • The crossfader works like a DJ mixer's but can fade between any number of tracks, returns included.
      • On a MIDI track, MIDI effects come first, then an instrument, then audio effects. A MIDI track with no instrument outputs plain MIDI and loses its mix and send controls.
      • Show or hide mixer sections from View → Mixer Controls or the Mixer Config Menu at the bottom right.

Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves

  • 14. Using Grooves

    • The Groove Pool: applying a groove's timing and feel to clips and editing grooves.
    • Grooves change the timing and feel of a clip. Live's library grooves are Ableton/File/.agr files in the browser.
    • Drag a groove from the browser onto a clip to apply it. To audition several, turn on the Hot-Swap button above the clip's Clip Groove chooser and step through grooves while the clip plays.
    • Grooves work on MIDI clips and on audio clips; on audio they act through warping, so Warp must be on.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/01 Groove Pool

      • 14.1 Groove Pool

        • The Groove Pool holds every groove in use or loaded by double-clicking it in the browser. Grooves not used by any clip show greyed-out parameters.
        • Open it from the browser's view control menu, or with Cmd+Option+6 (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+6 (Windows).
        • Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/01 Groove Pool/01 Adjusting Groove Parameters

          • 14.1.1 Adjusting Groove Parameters

            • Each groove in the pool has buttons to save and hot-swap it, and these parameters, which change playback in real time:
              • Base — the note grid the groove is measured against, such as 1/4 or 1/8. Groove notes that fall on that grid don't move your notes.
              • Quantize — straight quantization applied before the groove: 100% snaps to the Base grid, 0% leaves notes where they were.
              • Timing — how strongly the groove's timing moves the clip.
              • Random — random timing offsets, different for each voice; small amounts loosen tightly quantized loops.
              • Velocity — how much the groove's velocities affect the clip, from −100 to +100. Negative values invert loud and quiet.
              • Global Amount — scales Timing, Random and Velocity for every groove, up to 130%. It also appears in the Control Bar once any clip has a groove.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/01 Groove Pool/02 Committing Grooves

          • 14.1.2 Committing Grooves

            • Commit, above the Clip Groove chooser, writes the groove into the clip — moving MIDI notes, or adding Warp Markers to audio — and then sets the clip's groove to None.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/02 Editing Grooves

      • 14.2 Editing Grooves

        • What a groove does depends on its Groove Pool settings and on the note positions inside the groove file.
        • Drag a groove from the browser or Groove Pool onto a MIDI track to get its notes as an ordinary MIDI clip, edit them, then turn the clip back into a groove.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/02 Editing Grooves/01 Extracting Grooves

          • 14.2.1 Extracting Grooves

            • Drag any audio or MIDI clip into the Groove Pool, or choose Extract Groove from its context menu, to make a groove from its timing and volume.
            • Only the playing part of the clip counts.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/03 Groove Tips

      • 14.3 Groove Tips

        • Ways to get more from grooves.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/03 Groove Tips/01 Grooving a Single Voice

          • 14.3.1 Grooving a Single Voice

            • A groove applies to a whole clip, so placing one drum voice behind the beat needs that voice in its own clip.
            • In a Drum or Instrument Rack, extract the chain for that voice (the snare, say) to its own track and clip, then give it a different groove.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/03 Groove Tips/02 Non-Destructive Quantization

          • 14.3.2 Non-Destructive Quantization

            • Set a groove's Timing, Random and Velocity to 0% and use only Quantize and Base for quantization that can be changed or undone at any time. Which groove file you use then doesn't matter.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves/03 Groove Tips/03 Creating Texture With Randomization

          • 14.3.3 Creating Texture With Randomization

            • Duplicate a track, apply a groove with Random turned up to one copy, and play both: every note lands slightly apart from its twin, which thickens single string voices into a section.

Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference
  • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/14 Echo

    • 29.14 Echo

      • Echo is a modulated delay with two independent delay lines, envelope and filter modulation, and character controls.
      • Channel Mode — Stereo, Ping Pong or Mid/Side; Mid/Side replaces the Left and Right knobs with Mid and Side.
      • Left / Right — delay times, in beat divisions or ms depending on Sync
      • Sync Mode — Notes, Triplet, Dotted or 16th; only heard while Sync is on
      • Stereo Link — changes to time, Sync and Sync Mode apply to both sides
      • Delay Offset — nudges each line slightly for swing; independent even when linked
      • Input — gain on the dry signal; D distorts it
      • Feedback — output fed back into the delay lines; Ø inverts the fed-back signal
      • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/14 Echo/01 Echo Tab

        • 29.14.1 Echo Tab

          • The Echo Tunnel draws each repeat as a ring moving toward the center; spacing shows time between repeats, and white dots mark a fixed 1/8 grid. Drag in it to change delay times.
          • Filter turns on a high-pass and a low-pass:
            • HP and its Res — high-pass cutoff and resonance
            • LP and its Res — low-pass cutoff and resonance
          • The triangle button shows the Filter Display, where the filter dots can be dragged.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/14 Echo/02 Modulation Tab

        • 29.14.2 Modulation Tab

          • An LFO modulates filter frequency and delay time, and can be blended with an envelope follower.
          • Waves — sine, triangle, saw up, saw down, square, noise; drag the wave in the display to change its rate
          • Sync on — Rate in beat divisions; off — Freq in Hz
          • Phase — offset between left and right; 180° is fully opposite
          • Mod Delay — modulation of delay time; Modulation x4 multiplies it by four, giving deep flanging at short times
          • Mod Filter — modulation of the filter
          • Env Mix — 0% LFO only, 100% envelope follower only
      • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/14 Echo/03 Character Tab

        • 29.14.3 Character Tab

          • Dynamics and imperfections:
            • Gate — input gate with Threshold and Release
            • Ducking — turns the wet signal down while input is above Threshold; Release sets how fast it recovers
            • Noise — adds vintage-style noise; Amount and Morph between noise types
            • Wobble — irregular delay-time modulation like a tape delay; Amount and Morph between wobble types
            • Repitch — pitch bends when delay time changes; off, the change crossfades
          • To save CPU, Echo turns off at least eight seconds after its input goes silent — unless both Noise and Gate are on.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/14 Echo/04 Global Controls

        • 29.14.4 Global Controls

          • Reverb — reverb amount; Reverb Location puts it before the delay, after it, or inside the feedback loop; Decay sets its length
          • Stereo — width of the wet signal; 0% mono, above 100% wider
          • Output — output gain
          • Dry/Wet — set to 100% on a return track. Its context menu has Equal-Loudness, which makes a 50/50 mix as loud as either end.

    • Delay Offset — nudges each line slightly for swing; independent even when linked
    • Input — gain on the dry signal; D distorts it
    • Feedback — output fed back into the delay lines; Ø inverts the fed-back signal
    • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/14 Echo/01 Echo Tab

      • 29.14.1 Echo Tab

        • The Echo Tunnel draws each repeat as a ring moving toward the center; spacing shows time between repeats, and white dots mark a fixed 1/8 grid. Drag in it to change delay times.
        • Filter turns on a high-pass and a low-pass:
          • HP and its Res — high-pass cutoff and resonance
          • LP and its Res — low-pass cutoff and resonance
        • The triangle button shows the Filter Display, where the filter dots can be dragged.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/14 Echo/02 Modulation Tab

      • 29.14.2 Modulation Tab

        • An LFO modulates filter frequency and delay time, and can be blended with an envelope follower.
        • Waves — sine, triangle, saw up, saw down, square, noise; drag the wave in the display to change its rate
        • Sync on — Rate in beat divisions; off — Freq in Hz
        • Phase — offset between left and right; 180° is fully opposite
        • Mod Delay — modulation of delay time; Modulation x4 multiplies it by four, giving deep flanging at short times
        • Mod Filter — modulation of the filter
        • Env Mix — 0% LFO only, 100% envelope follower only
    • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/14 Echo/03 Character Tab

      • 29.14.3 Character Tab

        • Dynamics and imperfections:
          • Gate — input gate with Threshold and Release
          • Ducking — turns the wet signal down while input is above Threshold; Release sets how fast it recovers
          • Noise — adds vintage-style noise; Amount and Morph between noise types
          • Wobble — irregular delay-time modulation like a tape delay; Amount and Morph between wobble types
          • Repitch — pitch bends when delay time changes; off, the change crossfades
        • To save CPU, Echo turns off at least eight seconds after its input goes silent — unless both Noise and Gate are on.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/14 Echo/04 Global Controls

      • 29.14.4 Global Controls

        • Reverb — reverb amount; Reverb Location puts it before the delay, after it, or inside the feedback loop; Decay sets its length
        • Stereo — width of the wet signal; 0% mono, above 100% wider
        • Output — output gain
        • Dry/Wet — set to 100% on a return track. Its context menu has Equal-Loudness, which makes a 50/50 mix as loud as either end.
