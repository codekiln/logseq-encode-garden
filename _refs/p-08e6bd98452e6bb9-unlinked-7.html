Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/40 Utility

Page reference

  • 29.40 Utility

    • Utility handles common signal tasks, often alongside other devices.
    • Phase — one switch per channel to invert left or right
    • Channel Mode — use only Left or only Right (on both outputs), for stereo files with different material on each side
    • Width — stereo width; 0% mono, above 100% wider. Mid/Side Mode in its context menu swaps it for a Mid/Side control: toward 100M sums to mono, toward 100S leaves only the side signal (left and right 180° apart). Width and Mid/Side are disabled when Channel Mode is Left or Right.
    • Mono — sums the input to mono
    • Bass Mono — makes the lows mono below Bass Mono Frequency (50–500 Hz), so they hold up on mono playback; Bass Mono Audition plays only those lows for tuning the frequency
    • Gain — −∞ to +35 dB. Handy for automating fades while leaving the track's Volume for mixing. Arrow keys step 1 dB from −18 to +35 dB and speed up below −18 dB.
    • Balance — pans the signal
    • Mute — silences the input at Utility's position. Since a track's own mute is at the end of the chain, Utility before a delay or reverb can cut its input and let the tail continue.
    • DC — removes DC offset and inaudibly low frequencies; matters only when nonlinear devices such as compressors or waveshapers follow
    • Related question: Ableton/Track/Q/How can I turn a mono signal into stereo in a channel?
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/41 Vinyl Distortion

Page reference

  • 29.41 Vinyl Distortion

    • Vinyl Distortion imitates the distortions of vinyl playback, which come from the geometry of needle and groove, and adds crackle.
    • Tracing Model — even harmonic distortion. Drive or vertical drag in its X-Y display sets the amount; horizontal drag or the Freq field sets its frequency ("color"); Option-drag (Mac) / Alt-drag (Windows) vertically sets Q.
    • Pinch Effect — odd harmonics, typically 180° out of phase for a wider stereo image; same controls, different sound.
    • Drive (global) — overall distortion from both sections
    • Soft imitates a dub plate; Hard a standard record.
    • Stereo/mono switch — whether the Pinch distortion is stereo (more realistic) or mono
    • Crackle — noise with Density and Volume
Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/42 Vocoder

Page reference

  • 29.42 Vocoder

    • Vocoder is not in the Intro and Lite editions.
    • A vocoder imposes the level contour of one signal, the modulator (usually speech or drums), onto the frequencies of another, the carrier (usually a rich synth such as strings or a pad). The classic result is a talking synth or robot voice.
    • Both signals pass through banks of band-pass filters; the level in each modulator band sets the level of the matching carrier band.
    • Put Vocoder on the modulator's track. Carrier options:
      • Noise — internal noise; its X-Y display sets downsampling (horizontal, left lowers the sample rate) and density (vertical, down thins it)
      • External — any internal routing point; the usual choice for robot voices
      • Modulator — the modulator carries itself, a resynthesized version shaped by Vocoder's controls
      • Pitch Tracking — a monophonic oscillator that follows the modulator's pitch, limited by High and Low. Saw or three pulse waves, with Pitch for coarse tuning. It holds each detected pitch until it finds a new one, so changing its settings or resetting it (for instance by grouping the track) can change the sound unexpectedly. Best on single melodic lines or voice; unpredictable but interesting on chords and drums.
    • Enhance — normalizes the carrier's spectrum and dynamics for a brighter sound, useful with external carriers
    • Unvoiced — level of an extra noise source for pitchless sounds like "f" and "s"; Sens. sets detection (100% always on, 0% off); Fast/Slow how quickly it switches
    • The center display shows each band's level; click in it to lower bands.
    • Bands — number of filters; more is more accurate and uses more CPU
    • Range — frequency span of the filters; narrow it if the sound gets too piercing or boomy
    • BW — filter bandwidth; 100% is most accurate, other values can be interesting
    • Precise/Retro — equal gain and width for every band, or narrower, louder bands toward the top
    • Gate — bands below the threshold are silent
    • Level — output boost or cut
    • Depth — how much of the modulator's envelope reaches the carrier; 0% none, 100% classic vocoding, 200% only the loudest peaks
    • Attack and Release — response speed; very fast keeps transients but can distort
    • Channel switches — Mono (both mono), Stereo (mono modulator, stereo carrier), L/R (both stereo)
    • Formant — shifts the carrier's filter bank; small changes on a voice can change its apparent gender
    • Dry/Wet — balance of processed and dry signal
    • Ableton/Live/UG/29 Audio Effect Reference/42 Vocoder/01 Vocoder Tips

      • 29.42.1 Vocoder Tips

        • Setups for the most common uses.

        • 29.42.1.1 Singing Synthesizer

          • 1. Put Vocoder on the vocal track — a recorded clip, or a live mic chosen as the track input.
          • 2. Put a synth such as Ableton/Live/UG/31 Instrument Reference/01 Analog on another track, played from a clip or live.
          • 3. Set Carrier to External and pick the synth track in Audio From (Post FX in the lower chooser works best).
          • 4. For live input, arm both tracks.
          • 5. Play the synth while speaking: the speech rhythm comes through with the synth's tone. Solo the voice track to hear only the vocoded sound.
          • Bright, harmonically rich patches, such as saw-based ones, make the words clearer; Unvoiced and Enhance add more clarity.

        • 29.42.1.2 Formant Shifter

          • Carrier Modulator, Depth 100%, Enhance on — then turn Formant to change the source's character, and try the filter bank controls too.

Ableton/Live/UG/32 Max for Live/03 Editing Max for Live Devices
  • A Max device, or "patch", is a set of objects passing data to each other through virtual cables. In the default Max Audio Effect, the plugin~ object takes audio in from the device's place in the chain and passes it to plugout~, which sends it on to the next device.
  • To start a new device, drag Max Instrument, Max MIDI Effect or Max Audio Effect from the browser into the Set. Any existing Max device can be edited the same way.
  • Max devices are saved as separate Ableton/File/.amxd files, not inside the Live Set. By default each type goes to its own folder in the User Library, such as Max Audio Effect or Max MIDI Effect. Keep that default: moving or renaming an AMXD file breaks the path stored in the Set. The File Manager can find a missing file.
Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices
  • The Max for Live instruments, audio effects and MIDI effects that come with Live.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects

    • 33.2 Max for Live Audio Effects

      • A utility delay and three modulators. The modulators — Envelope Follower, LFO and Shaper — share one mapping system:
        • Map then click any automatable parameter to make it a target; Unmap removes it. Show/Hide Multimap reveals more Map buttons, up to eight targets.
        • Mod toggles between Modulation and Remote Control.
          • Modulation (default) — the target can still be moved by hand. Modulation Polarity chooses Bipolar (around the base value) or Unipolar (one direction from it); Modulation Amount sets the depth.
          • Remote Control — the modulator alone sets the value; Min and Max scale the range.
      • Ableton/Max for Live/Audio Effect
      • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/01 Align Delay

        • 33.2.1 Align Delay

          • A utility that delays a signal by time, samples or distance.
          • Delay Mode:
            • Time — milliseconds; for lining up with A/V gear or slight stereo widening
            • Samples — for compensating latency from other devices
            • Distance — meters or feet; for timing offsets between speakers in a PA. A Temperature setting (°C or °F) matches the room, since sound travels at different speeds in warm and cold air.
          • Link L/R — the left channel's delay drives both channels and Delay Right is disabled
      • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/02 Envelope Follower

        • 33.2.2 Envelope Follower

          • A modulator that follows the level of the incoming audio and turns it into a smooth control curve. Auto-wah is the classic use.
          • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as described in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
          • Gain — input gain
          • Rise and Fall — smooth the attack and release of the curve
          • Delay — delays the curve, in time or synced divisions (Delay Mode buttons)
          • Sidechain: by default the curve comes from the track's own input. The triangle at the top left opens the Audio Routing panel.
            • turn on Sidechain, choose the source track in Audio From, and choose Pre FX, Post FX or Post Mixer (and a Rack channel if needed)
            • Direct and S.C. meters show the track and sidechain levels
            • Sidechain Mix — 0% uses only the track input, 100% only the sidechain; a Drum Rack as sidechain gives rhythmic ducking or gating
      • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/03 LFO

        • 33.2.3 LFO

          • A modulator that drives mapped parameters with a low-frequency oscillator.
          • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as described in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
          • Waveform — Sine, Up, Down, Triangle, Square, Random, Bin, Stray or Glider
          • Shape bends the wave; Steps quantizes it into up to 24 steps. Shape is disabled for Random, Bin, Stray and Glider; Steps for Random, Bin and Square.
          • Jitter adds randomness; Smooth softens sudden changes, including jitter.
          • Rate — in Hz or synced divisions (Time Mode toggles). In Hz, ×10 multiplies the rate.
          • Depth — modulation amount for all targets, 0% to 100%
          • Offset — moves the center of the output up or down; the horizontal line in the display marks the center
          • Phase — start position in the cycle, 0% to 100%
          • Hold — freezes the output; releasing Hold continues from the held value
          • R — retriggers from the Phase position
      • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/04 Shaper

        • 33.2.4 Shaper

          • A modulator whose output is a breakpoint envelope drawn in its display.
          • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as described in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
          • Drawing:
            • click to add a breakpoint; Option-drag (Mac) or Alt-drag (Windows) between points to curve a segment; Shift-click a point to delete it
            • Clear erases the envelope; six presets next to it start a new one
            • Grid sets grid divisions; Snap snaps points to the grid
          • The small display at the top right shows the output like an oscilloscope.
          • Jitter adds randomness; Smooth softens sudden changes.
          • Phase — start position in the cycle; R retriggers from there (disabled in Sync time mode)
          • Offset — moves the center of the output up or down
          • Trigger modes:
            • Loop — repeats at Rate
            • 1-Shot — plays once each time the mappable T button is pressed
            • Manual — the Manual control scrubs through the envelope
          • Rate — in Hz or synced divisions
          • Depth — modulation amount for all targets

Ableton/Live/UG/37 Sync with Link Tempo Follower and MIDI/01 Synchronizing via Link
  • Ableton Link keeps beat, tempo and phase in step across apps and devices on a wired or wireless network. Link Audio adds real-time audio streaming between the peers in a Link session, with no extra hardware.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/37 Sync with Link Tempo Follower and MIDI/01 Synchronizing via Link/01 Setting up Link and Link Audio

    • 37.1.1 Setting up Link and Link Audio

      • Link and Link Audio have their own Link tab in Ableton/Settings.
      • The Link toggle sits in the Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar. Set Show Link Toggle to Hide to remove it.
      • The menu beside the toggle switches Link and Link Audio on or off, lists connected Link Audio peers, and opens the settings with Configure Link….
      • Settings on the Link tab:
        • Start Stop Sync — share start and stop between peers that also turn it on.
        • Audio — turns Link Audio on. It works only while the Link toggle is on.
        • Name — the name other peers see.
        • Latency — input latency for incoming audio; the peer list shows how much audio each peer buffers.
        • Sync to Incoming Audio — delays Live's playback by the latency so incoming Link Audio lines up with Live's clips. Only one peer in a session should turn it on.
        • Peers list — each connected Link Audio peer with its status and buffered audio.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/37 Sync with Link Tempo Follower and MIDI/01 Synchronizing via Link/03 Using Link Audio

    • 37.1.3 Using Link Audio

      • Link Audio streams audio between Link peers on the same network, so Live can monitor and record other devices and apps. Both Link and Link Audio must be on for every peer.
      • Each peer appears as an input source. In an audio track, choose the peer in the Input Type chooser and its source track in the Input Channel chooser. Monitoring needs In, or Auto on an armed track; recording needs the track armed.
      • Every track with audio output can be streamed to peers. Live and Push can send and receive; other apps may only send.

  • 37.1.3 Using Link Audio

    • Link Audio streams audio between Link peers on the same network, so Live can monitor and record other devices and apps. Both Link and Link Audio must be on for every peer.
    • Each peer appears as an input source. In an audio track, choose the peer in the Input Type chooser and its source track in the Input Channel chooser. Monitoring needs In, or Auto on an armed track; recording needs the track armed.
    • Every track with audio output can be streamed to peers. Live and Push can send and receive; other apps may only send.
Ableton/Live/UG/37 Sync with Link Tempo Follower and MIDI/02 Synchronizing via Tempo Follower
  • Tempo Follower listens to an incoming audio signal, works out its tempo in real time, and has Live follow it. It suits cases with no Link or MIDI Clock, or where Live should follow a drummer or a pair of turntables instead of a fixed clock.
Ableton/Live/UG/37 Sync with Link Tempo Follower and MIDI/02 Synchronizing via Tempo Follower/01 Setting Up Tempo Follower
  • In the Tempo Follower section of the Tempo & MIDI Settings, set Input Channel (Ext. In) to the audio interface input carrying the source, such as an overhead microphone or a DJ mixer's record output. Sources with a clear rhythm work best.
Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources

Page reference

  • 38. Computer Audio Resources and Strategies

    • How Live uses the CPU and disk, and what to do when the Overload indicator lights.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources/01 Managing the CPU Load

      • 38.1 Managing the CPU Load

        • Live performs a large number of calculations every second to keep audio flowing. When the processor falls behind, playback and recording get gaps or clicks.
        • What affects CPU performance:
          • Clock speed — instructions per second, in GHz.
          • Architecture — how efficiently the CPU runs instructions and talks to other parts.
          • Cache memory — how fast the CPU fetches data.
          • System bus bandwidth — how fast data moves between components.
          • Temperature — an overheating CPU throttles itself.
        • Live needs a multi-core processor and spreads instruments, effects and I/O across the cores.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources/01 Managing the CPU Load/01 The CPU Load Meter

          • 38.1.1 The CPU Load Meter

            • The CPU meter in the Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar compares the time Live takes to compute one audio buffer with the time that buffer takes to play. 50% means computing runs twice as fast as playback; over 100% is possible.
            • The operating system schedules threads, so other applications can cause spikes. The meter counts audio processing only, not work such as drawing Live's interface.
            • The meter's menu, opened from its triangle button or by right-clicking:
              • Average (default) — average load from audio processing.
              • Current — total current, or peak, load. With both on, the menu shows both values and the meter shows average in a lighter shade and current in a darker one.
              • Meter Text Shows — which of the two values appears as a number.
              • Warn on Current CPU Overload — off by default; turns on CPU warnings in the Overload indicator.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources/01 Managing the CPU Load/02 CPU Load from Multichannel Audio

          • 38.1.2 CPU Load from Multichannel Audio

            • Moving audio between the CPU and the audio hardware is a constant load. Turn off unused inputs and outputs with the Input Config and Output Config buttons in the Audio Settings.
            • Live does not turn them off itself, because some drivers glitch audibly whenever the audio configuration changes.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources/01 Managing the CPU Load/03 CPU Load from Tracks and Devices

          • 38.1.3 CPU Load from Tracks and Devices

            • Tracks and devices cost CPU only while they process audio. Adding devices while stopped adds little load; an effect with no input is essentially idle, and a reverb or delay stays active until its tail finishes.
            • That lowers average load but not peak load. To stress-test a Set before a show, play a clip on every track with all devices on; if the CPU meter's Current value stays well under 100%, the Set should hold up.
            • The mixer's Performance Impact indicators show each track's share of the load as six rectangles. Turn them on from the Show/Hide Mixer Config menu at the bottom right, or View → Mixer Controls.
            • Freeze a heavy track, or turn off some of its devices, to lower its impact.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources/01 Managing the CPU Load/04 Track Freeze

          • 38.1.4 Track Freeze

            • Freeze Track renders every Session and Arrangement clip on a track to a sample file, so playback stops recomputing its devices and clip settings. Run it from the Edit menu, a track or clip context menu, or Cmd+Option+Shift+F (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+Shift+F (Win).
            • Only tracks holding clips can be frozen, so Group Tracks, return tracks and the Main track cannot. Tracks using External Audio Effect or External Instrument freeze in real time.
            • A frozen track's device and clip settings are locked until Unfreeze Track. Bounce Track in Place commits a track to new audio for good, and also works on Group Tracks.
            • Still available on frozen tracks: clip launching, volume, pan and sends, and also:
              • cutting, copying, pasting, duplicating and trimming clips
              • drawing mixer automation and mixer clip envelopes
              • consolidating clips
              • recording Session launches into the Arrangement
              • creating, moving and duplicating scenes
              • dragging frozen MIDI clips onto audio tracks
            • Edits to a frozen track with time-based effects can sound different after unfreezing, because those effects are recomputed then.
            • Frozen Arrangement tracks keep effect tails as crosshatched temporary clips beside the original clips; select the tail along with its clip when moving it. Frozen Session clips hold only two loop cycles.
            • Dragging a frozen clip into a drop area makes a new frozen track with that clip, or only its selected part.
            • Freeze files go in the project's Samples/Processed/Freeze folder, or the Temporary Folder for an unsaved Set. Freezing also lets a slower computer, or one missing a device, play a Set.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources/02 Managing the Disk Load

      • 38.2 Managing the Disk Load

        • When the disk cannot read or write fast enough, audio drops out and the Overload indicator flashes. Disk traffic grows with the number of audio channels in use; a stereo sample costs more than a mono one.
        • Ways to lower it:
          • Put layered clips, such as stacked vocals, into a Group Track and bounce the group in place to one file.
          • Use RAM Mode on audio clips, so Live reads them from memory instead of disk.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/38 Computer Audio Resources/03 The Overload Indicator

      • 38.3 The Overload Indicator

        • The Overload indicator in the Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar shows "Disk" when the disk falls behind and, once CPU warnings are on, "CPU" when the processor is overloaded.
        • CPU warnings are off by default. Turn them on with Warn on Current CPU Overload in the CPU meter's menu or CPU Overload Indicator in the indicator's context menu; the two options share one state.
        • Both kinds of overload can cause dropouts or crackles while playing or recording.
