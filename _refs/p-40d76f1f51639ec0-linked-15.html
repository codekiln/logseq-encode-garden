Knowledge Gardens (KGs) and Knowledge/Base Knowledge Bases (KBs)Douglas Lenat's knowledge principles
  • Person/Douglas Lenat and Edward Feigenbaum's On the Thresholds of Knowledge offer a useful set of design principles for a knowledge garden:
    • Keep knowledge explicit and inspectable so it can be questioned, connected, and revised.
    • Grow breadth as well as depth; unexpected questions often require general knowledge and analogies to distant domains.
    • Preserve declarative knowledge even when some of it is compiled into faster operational forms.
    • Test the garden through real use on substantial problems, because small toy examples can conceal missing context.

Knowledge/Q/Did Douglas Lenat have a wiki?

Page reference

  • Did Person/Douglas Lenat have a wiki?

    • AI Answer

      • Short answer: I found no evidence that Douglas Lenat maintained a personal wiki or a public wiki under his own name.
      • His documented knowledge project was Cyc, a formal knowledge base and ontology developed through Cycorp. Cyc's official knowledge-base page and platform description describe the system as a large body of codified common-sense knowledge used for machine reasoning.
      • The pages titled Douglas Lenat on Open WIKI and DougLenat on MeatballWiki are third-party wiki entries about him. They are evidence that other people documented Lenat, not that he maintained a wiki.
      • The best-supported answer is therefore: Lenat built and led a formal knowledge base, especially Cyc, but no personal wiki has surfaced in the sources checked.

  • Person/Solomon Hykes, the founder of Docker and developer of Dagger explains why Container/iz/ation is key to AI Coding

    • [unsupported content]

      • AI/Summary of relevant parts at 11:25

        • Hykes explained that while Docker's foundational container technology revolutionized environment portability, the tools built around it—like Dockerfiles and Compose—were designed as temporary solutions a decade ago and "frozen in time," inadequately serving today's needs for agent-native developer workflows.
        • He emphasized that as AI coding agents become more central, environment reproducibility is critical so agents can operate in isolated, portable, and observable environments.
        • Dagger aims to solve these problems by building an agent-native, modular workflow engine on existing open standards (containers, Git, OpenAI APIs) instead of retrofitting legacy Docker interfaces.
        • Hykes noted that while Docker made containers ubiquitous, it isn't "agent native," and the next wave of developer tooling must focus on isolating agent environments to avoid vendor lock-in, enabling local and cloud execution, and providing reproducibility for both agents and humans across platforms and infrastructures

⋯ComparisonsDesign PatternsThe Entities System is a form of a collection or curation of Design/Patterns, just applied to Knowledge Gardening.
  • The evolution of a knowledge garden and its kinds of entities are alive in the sense that Person/Christopher Alexander/Book/77/A Pattern Language describes a system of Design/Patterns. Quoting that book:

    • […] each pattern represents our current best guess as to what arrangement of the physical environment will work to solve the problem presented. The empirical questions center on the problem—does it occur and is it felt in the way we describe it?—and the solution—does the arrangement we propose solve the problem? And the asterisks represent our degree of faith in these hypotheses. But of course, no matter what the asterisks say, the patterns are still hypotheses, all 253 of them—and are, therefore, all tentative, all free to evolve under the impact of new experience and observation.

⋯My NotesComparisonsEntity Relationship Diagrams
  • The Entities System is Similar to Diagram/Entity Relationship for a particular domain of inquiry or field of study. An Entity Relationship Diagram or ERD is a tool, a perspective for how to think about a system. It's key to keep in mind that it's a perspective, and as such, it's incomplete. Just as Person/Christopher Alexander talks about how each Design/Pattern is a hypothesis, the entities in a knowledge garden are first or second order approximations of the Latent Space in human Conscious/ness. They must always be considered to be lossy representations.
