Week/Review/26/09/27 Sun
⋯09:40Making MusicGoal: Decrease Friction to Making And Then Sharing Music
  • I've been trying to get a synth podcast up for quite a while. I made a bit of progress yesterday on GitP/A, but I got bogged down with the Distractors of Secrets Management and Knowledge Gardening.
    • When I first started Ghost in the Patch, in each "episode" I narrated my creation of Microfreak/UG/04 Presets step by step, using Jargon and key vocabulary. Usually, by the end of the episode, I had longer periods without narration as I just played. It was a bit like falling asleep or into a trance. That was nice, in a way, but it was more than just a bit tangled in approach. I knew that I might be able to use AI/Voice/to/Text turn my narration into a transcription, then relate that to keywords in the Microfreak/Docs and use that to examine the MIDI captures to provide more informed commentary or linkages between various sounds of different episodes.
      • I kind of planned to eventually use some kind of Embedding model to find similar parts from similar episodes, then use that to drive some kind of a knowledge garden and composition system.
    • The problem with this is that it got me out of the groove and made it harder to practice and express what I was feeling. Consider the difference between practicing piano and discussing detailed finger and hand movements and you'll likely be able to understand what I'm talking about.
    • Lately, including the episode in Music/Composition/Log/26/09/25 Fri, I didn't have any commentary at all. It's great, because I can get into the zone more, and focus on the practice of making music. That felt good, and it served my purpose of decreasing the friction of making and sharing music.
    • That's fine, but a raw audio recording of experimental live improv on a synth seems to be a pretty stark format for a podcast. So this morning I was thinking that I just need some custom software. Ideally, I'd record the synth. Then I'd go out listening with my earbuds in while walking around, and I'd comment on it in a way that would be recorded. I might do this multiple times. Then I'd have AI kind of stitch these layered commentaries together. That way it's as easy as possible to get a rich discussion podcast going while also honoring the sanctity of the space in which I make music.
Week/Review/26/10/04 Sun
⋯ChallengesMaking/MusicProcessing Microfreak/Presets
  • I do sense that I need more memorable names than just having a preset named after the GitP episode. When I'm improvising, I need to be able to call up sounds from memory, and preset names named after dates aren't useful to me in that regard. The live performances I've done lately do require me to remember the names of presets. Presets are actually a starting point for me, not a destination.
    • I've thought about using them as a waypoint, e.g. making an improvisational system that has multiple presets that I'd sequence and go through as a way of organizing compositional structures. That said, I haven't actually performed this way yet, it's still an idea.
⋯ThoughtsLayered, Cross-Sectional ThinkingIdea/My - App/Idea - Media/Layered
  • In early episodes of GitP, I talked through my creation of a Microfreak/Preset. The ones I published this week were just music, which has a certain kind of simplicity and mystery to it. Imagine this: you are listening to a podcast, and at any moment, you can pause and make a comment with your voice, which records a new layer. Then when the next person listens to that podcast, they hear a sound which indicates the presence of a media layer, an off-ramp. They can take some action (gestural with head / airpod, verbal, or with touch on the screen) to switch over to the layer, then perhaps they want to make a reply with their voice or with text, which becomes yet another layer. Perhaps some of these could be voted up like Reddit somehow, and perhaps some of these conversations would let a person bring in an AI/Agent/Personal to have a conversation with.

      • a new sort of representation of multidimensional streamed media, the top-down, multi-fingered river loom

        • I've been picturing that each piece would have a different sort of representation than the one most people are familiar with. Usually, one finds that audio files have a player with a play button on the left, a play bar in the center, and the play bar goes from left to right. Instead, I'd have something more like a type of loom that goes from top to bottom.
        • There would be several channels of audio vertically oriented, scrolling by like a river, as though the webpage were a portal onto a river that was flowing from top to bottom, only it would actually be multiple rivers - some of them audio, some of them control structures, for example, each manipulated or automated CC value would be its own river. it would change thickness, thinness based on its expression. For some di-pole controls like audio pan, it would flip right vs left with a line down the center. For example, on the microfreak control structures that can modulate one thing with another on a grid, it would be
        • For others it would be a thin river near CC value of 0 and a thick river near a CC value of 127.
        • Then in addition to the left to right layout of the audio and its tracks, we'd have layers of depth, and one could toggle layers of depth. At the "top" would be the final, official version, post omega (gitpo). Next layer "down" or "into" the third dimsnion would be penultimate version, iota, gitpi, and so on, until you reach gitpa, the initial, raw version. So by default, you'd be presented with the final version, but you could "zoom in" to see the versions that were prior and less editorial, with more commentary.
