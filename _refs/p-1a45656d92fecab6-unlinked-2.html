Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music/Performance

  • Performer

    • In this garden, Performer pages model a real person who performs music for listeners: who plays an instrument, sings, improvises, DJs or live-codes. A Performer is a kind of Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music, and the rest follows Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker.

    • What counts as an instance

      • A person whose public performing is a significant part of their identity: soloists, ensemble and orchestra players, singers, bandleaders who play in their band, improvisers, DJs and live coders.
      • The hub's line saying what the person makes names the instrument or voice, as "violinist" or "pipa player".
      • A teacher of an instrument who does not perform in public stays a Logseq/Entity/Person.

    • Boundary cases

      • A person who leads an ensemble from the podium carries Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music/Performance/Conducting in place of Performer.
      • A live coder performs by writing code in front of an audience, and carries Performer: Person/Atsushi Tadokoro. A live coder who also builds the language or software that live coders perform with adds Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music/Tech.
      • A performer who writes the pieces they play adds Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music/Composition. One known mainly for songs in the popular, folk, blues or rock traditions adds Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music/Composition/Song, as Person/Adrianne Lenker does.
      • A sound artist whose work is performed live, such as a breath or object performance in a gallery, carries Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Art/Sound and Performer.

    • Works

      • A performance page links its performers with performer::, as Logseq/Entity/Music/Performance and Logseq/Entity/Music/Recording describe, and those links list the performances in the performer's linked references.

    • Examples in this garden

      • Person/Madonna, Person/Steve Reich, Person/Benjamin Sung
Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music/Performance/Conducting
  • In this garden, Conductor pages model a real person who leads an orchestra, choir or other ensemble as its conductor. A Conductor is a kind of Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music/Performance, so a page marked Conductor leaves Performer out.
Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music/Tech
  • A person who programs patches or builds systems only for their own pieces leaves Music Technologist out: Person/Huichun Yang carries Sound Artist and Performer, and Person/Wei Yang carries Sound Artist and Composer. Music Technologist is for technology other musicians use: a language, a synthesis method, or a plugin, device or instrument other people play, such as Person/Tobias Hunke's Max for Live devices.
Logseq/Entity/Person/Maker/Music/Theory
  • For scales, Music Theorist is for a person who names or classifies them in writing, as Heinrich Glarean named the Ionian and Aeolian modes. A composer who builds their music on a scale or tuning carries the entities for their music: Yatsuhashi Kengyō, who is credited with the hirajōshi koto tuning and played the koto, carries Composer and Performer, and Claude Debussy, who built pieces on the whole-tone scale, carries Composer.
Logseq/Frontmatter/created-by-identities
  • A name that is only a person's ordinary biographical name can use created-by:: or performer:: directly without a separate identity page.
  • created-by:: records known people who made the particular work or recording; performer:: records known performers. Neither property is inferred from identity membership alone.

Person/Matthew Wright
  • Composer and performer
  • Dr. Matthew Wright is a media systems designer, improvising composer/performer, computer music researcher, father of an energetic 8-year-old, and the Executive Director and acting Technical Director of Stanford University’s Center for Computer Research in Music and Acoustics (Uni/Stanford/CCRMA). His computer music career began in 1990 as an undergraduate in a class from David Wessel at UC Berkeley’s Center for New Music and Audio Technologies (CNMAT), where he joined the staff as a researcher from 1993-2008, before and during his CCRMA PhD. He later worked at the University of Victoria and UC Santa Barbara. His research has included real-time mapping of musical gestures to sound synthesis, helping develop and promote the Sound Description Interchange Format (SDIF) and Open Sound Control (OSC) standards, computer modeling of the perception of musical rhythm, and musical creation with technology in a live performance context. As a musician, he plays a variety of Middle Eastern and Afghan plucked lutes, Afro-Brazilian percussion, and computer-based instruments of his own design, in both traditional music contexts and experimental new works.

Synth/Aesthetic/Coast/East
  • In the 1960s-1990s and beyond, the US/Coast/East synth community traditionally prioritized more of a division between the engineer and the performer; many synths made on the East Coast had stored Presets that were similar to pipe organ stops, and the process for using these synths usually focused on a division between creating a Preset and performing a Preset.
