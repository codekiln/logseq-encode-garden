33.1 Max for Live Instruments
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/01 Max for Live Instruments/01 DS Clang

    • 33.1.1 DS Clang

      • Cowbell, clave and noise percussion from two tones, white noise and a filter.
      • Tone A and Tone B — level of each cowbell tone
      • Filter — high-pass and band-pass cutoff; higher is brighter
      • Noise — amount of white noise
      • Clave — switches to clave; Repeat then adds repeats
      • Pitch, Decay (length) and Volume
33.1 Max for Live Instruments
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/01 Max for Live Instruments/02 DS Clap

    • 33.1.2 DS Clap

      • Claps from filtered noise and an impulse sent through two panned delay lines, from tight electronic claps to looser hand claps.
      • Sloppy — time between the two delay lines, so the claps line up tightly or loosely
      • Tail — adds filtered noise after the impulse
      • Spread — stereo width, from mono at 0% to wide at 100%
      • Tone — brightness
      • Tune, Decay (length) and Volume
33.1 Max for Live Instruments
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/01 Max for Live Instruments/03 DS Cymbal

    • 33.1.3 DS Cymbal

      • Cymbals from sine and pulse waves plus high-passed noise, from a thin ride to a heavy crash.
      • Tone — high-pass cutoff; higher is brighter
      • Pitch, Decay (length) and Volume
33.1 Max for Live Instruments
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/01 Max for Live Instruments/04 DS FM

    • 33.1.4 DS FM

      • FM percussion modeled on a classic Japanese FM synthesizer, from noise bursts to metallic zaps.
      • Tone — low-pass cutoff; higher is brighter
      • Feedb. — FM feedback; more is noisier
      • Amnt — FM amount; Mod blends between modulation types
      • Pitch, Decay (length) and Volume
33.1 Max for Live Instruments
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/01 Max for Live Instruments/05 DS HH

    • 33.1.5 DS HH

      • Hi-hats from noise and sine waves, from closed to open.
      • Noise — white or pink
      • Tone — high-pass cutoff on the noise; higher is brighter
      • The pitched part runs through a resonant high-pass filter switchable between 12 and 24 dB slopes; Attack sets its attack.
      • Pitch, Decay (length) and Volume
33.1 Max for Live Instruments
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/01 Max for Live Instruments/06 DS Kick

    • 33.1.6 DS Kick

      • Kick drum from a modulated sine wave.
      • Pitch — tuning in Hz
      • Drive — distortion; OT — added harmonics
      • Attack — smooths the start of the sine
      • Click — adds a click for a sharper attack
      • Env — pitch modulation amount
      • Decay (length) and Volume

    • 33.1.7 DS Snare

      • Snare from a pitched oscillator and noise, from acoustic-style snares to gated noise snares.
      • Color — tone of the pitched part
      • Tone — amount of noise
      • Noise filter: low-pass, high-pass or band-pass
      • Tune, Decay (length) and Volume

    • 33.1.8 DS Tom

      • Toms from an impulse and pitched oscillator waves, from deep to sharp.
      • Pitch — tuning in Hz
      • Color — filter gain and cutoff
      • Tone — level of resonant band-pass filters that imitate a drum head's tuning
      • Bend — pitch envelope
      • Decay (length) and Volume

33.2 Max for Live Audio Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/01 Align Delay

    • 33.2.1 Align Delay

      • A utility that delays a signal by time, samples or distance.
      • Delay Mode:
        • Time — milliseconds; for lining up with A/V gear or slight stereo widening
        • Samples — for compensating latency from other devices
        • Distance — meters or feet; for timing offsets between speakers in a PA. A Temperature setting (°C or °F) matches the room, since sound travels at different speeds in warm and cold air.
      • Link L/R — the left channel's delay drives both channels and Delay Right is disabled
33.2 Max for Live Audio Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/02 Envelope Follower

    • 33.2.2 Envelope Follower

      • A modulator that follows the level of the incoming audio and turns it into a smooth control curve. Auto-wah is the classic use.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as described in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
      • Gain — input gain
      • Rise and Fall — smooth the attack and release of the curve
      • Delay — delays the curve, in time or synced divisions (Delay Mode buttons)
      • Sidechain: by default the curve comes from the track's own input. The triangle at the top left opens the Audio Routing panel.
        • turn on Sidechain, choose the source track in Audio From, and choose Pre FX, Post FX or Post Mixer (and a Rack channel if needed)
        • Direct and S.C. meters show the track and sidechain levels
        • Sidechain Mix — 0% uses only the track input, 100% only the sidechain; a Drum Rack as sidechain gives rhythmic ducking or gating
33.2 Max for Live Audio Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/03 LFO

    • 33.2.3 LFO

      • A modulator that drives mapped parameters with a low-frequency oscillator.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as described in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
      • Waveform — Sine, Up, Down, Triangle, Square, Random, Bin, Stray or Glider
      • Shape bends the wave; Steps quantizes it into up to 24 steps. Shape is disabled for Random, Bin, Stray and Glider; Steps for Random, Bin and Square.
      • Jitter adds randomness; Smooth softens sudden changes, including jitter.
      • Rate — in Hz or synced divisions (Time Mode toggles). In Hz, ×10 multiplies the rate.
      • Depth — modulation amount for all targets, 0% to 100%
      • Offset — moves the center of the output up or down; the horizontal line in the display marks the center
      • Phase — start position in the cycle, 0% to 100%
      • Hold — freezes the output; releasing Hold continues from the held value
      • R — retriggers from the Phase position
33.2 Max for Live Audio Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects/04 Shaper

    • 33.2.4 Shaper

      • A modulator whose output is a breakpoint envelope drawn in its display.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as described in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
      • Drawing:
        • click to add a breakpoint; Option-drag (Mac) or Alt-drag (Windows) between points to curve a segment; Shift-click a point to delete it
        • Clear erases the envelope; six presets next to it start a new one
        • Grid sets grid divisions; Snap snaps points to the grid
      • The small display at the top right shows the output like an oscilloscope.
      • Jitter adds randomness; Smooth softens sudden changes.
      • Phase — start position in the cycle; R retriggers from there (disabled in Sync time mode)
      • Offset — moves the center of the output up or down
      • Trigger modes:
        • Loop — repeats at Rate
        • 1-Shot — plays once each time the mappable T button is pressed
        • Manual — the Manual control scrubs through the envelope
      • Rate — in Hz or synced divisions
      • Depth — modulation amount for all targets

33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • MIDI-triggered modulators (Envelope MIDI, Expression Control, Shaper MIDI) and MIDI utilities (MIDI Monitor, MPE Control, Note Echo). The modulators map targets the same way as the audio-effect modulators in Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/02 Max for Live Audio Effects.
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/01 Envelope MIDI

    • 33.3.1 Envelope MIDI

      • A modulator that drives mapped parameters with an ADSR envelope triggered by notes.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as in the audio-effect modulators.
      • Loop Mode, with an LED that flashes on each trigger:
        • Free — triggers on every note
        • Sync — retriggers at the Sync Rate division
        • Loop — loops at the rate set by Global Time
        • Echo — repeats every Env Echo Time; Env Feedback sets how long the echoes last
      • Global Time scales the whole envelope: 1.00 matches the ADSR times, higher stretches, lower compresses.
      • Amount — modulation amount for all targets
      • Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release, each with a Slope slider for curved stages; the handles in the display can be dragged too.
      • Velocity — scales the envelope peak by note velocity
      • Sustain toggle — off makes the envelope one-shot: Attack, Decay, then Release however long the note is held
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/02 Expression Control

    • 33.3.2 Expression Control

      • A modulator driven by MIDI and MPE sources.
      • Five Mod Source tabs, each set by its MIDI Input Source menu to one of: Velocity, Modwheel, Pitchbend, Pressure, Keytrack, Expression, Random, Increment, Slide, Sustain. Map assigns a target to each source.
      • The Modulation toggle at the top right:
        • Modulate — the target stays adjustable and the two values merge. A polarity toggle chooses Bipolar or Unipolar, and Output Range Max sets the output at full input.
        • Remote Control — Expression Control alone sets the value, between Output Range Min and Max.
      • Curve Type — linear (two breakpoints, shaped by Curve A) or S-shaped (three breakpoints; Curve A shapes the upper part, Curve B the lower). Curve Link ties both parts to Curve A, inversely. Min/Max and the middle point's X-Y sliders, or dragging in the display, also shape the curve.
      • Increment Steps (1–32), active when Increment is a source, sets how many notes it takes to sweep the whole range. Stopping transport resets the count.
      • Random Amount, active when Random is a source, sets how far each note's value can deviate.
      • Smoothing Type — linear or logarithmic; Rise and Fall set how fast values move up or down, 0–1000 ms.
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/03 MIDI Monitor

    • 33.3.3 MIDI Monitor

      • A utility that shows incoming MIDI and MPE data.
      • Note display — incoming notes on a keyboard, with root, chord name and velocity
      • Flow diagram — a running list of notes and other messages such as pitch bend and aftertouch; Freeze pauses it and Clear empties it
      • MPE display — a running view of note, velocity, slide, pressure and per-note pitch
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/04 MPE Control

    • 33.3.4 MPE Control

      • MPE Control reshapes incoming MPE data with curves, usually ahead of a device that uses that data. It can also turn MPE into ordinary channel-wide MIDI so an MPE controller can play instruments without MPE.
      • MPE sends controller data per note, so each note can be shaped on its own; ordinary MIDI CCs affect every note. More on MPE in the Editing MPE chapter.
      • Example: with Expression Control mapping Slide to an instrument's filter cutoff, MPE Control can shape the slide so only high slide values open the filter fully.
      • Three sources, each with its own curve and on/off toggle: Press (pressure), Slide, and NotePB (per-note pitch bend). The selected source's curve is drawn in front.
      • Curve types, chosen at the bottom left of the display:
        • Linear — two breakpoints. Curve bends it to compress (easier to stay low) or expand (easier to reach high values). Min and Max, or dragging the end points, set the range.
        • S-shaped — a third, movable breakpoint in the middle splits the curve in two. The halves are linked; Curve Link off gives each its own Curve dial. The X-Y controls also move the middle point.
      • The triangle at the bottom right opens the advanced settings. Each source has a Smooth toggle with its own Rise and Fall, plus source-specific controls.

      • 33.3.4.1 Press

        • MPE pressure: per-note pressure on a key or pad after the initial strike, like polyphonic aftertouch.
        • Default — a value to use for notes that carry no MPE data
        • Swap to Slide — sends pressure to the Slide source instead, for controllers that only have polyphonic aftertouch
        • Press to AT — converts pressure to channel aftertouch for instruments without MPE

      • 33.3.4.2 Slide

        • MPE slide: the finger's vertical position on a key or pad, sent as CC 74.
        • Default — a value for notes without MPE data
        • Centered — for pad controllers: the middle of the pad gives zero, rising as the finger moves away vertically
        • Mode:
          • Abs (default) — values are absolute
          • Rel — every note starts in the middle of the range and moves only as the finger slides
          • Ons — the value updates only on Note On; this turns off smoothing
        • Slide to Mod — converts slide to Mod Wheel (CC 1) for instruments without MPE

      • 33.3.4.3 NotePB

        • MPE per-note pitch bend: horizontal movement on a key or pad, bending only that note.
        • Pitch Range — matches a controller and an instrument with different bend ranges. At 2x, a controller with ±48 semitones drives an instrument set to ±24. When per-note bend is mapped to something other than pitch, higher values cover more range with less movement.
        • NotePB to PB — converts per-note bend to ordinary pitch bend for instruments without MPE
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/05 Note Echo

    • 33.3.5 Note Echo

      • A MIDI echo: repeats each note at set intervals with falling velocity.
      • Sync on — the delay is chosen in 16th notes from numbered switches; 4 is one beat. The Delay Time percentage nudges the repeats off the grid for a drum-machine swing.
      • Sync off — the delay is in milliseconds; drag the Delay Time slider or type a value.
      • Input — Thru plays the original note and the echoes; Mute plays only the echoes.
      • Pitch — transposition added on each repeat
      • Delay — velocity of the echoes; Fback — how much output feeds back into the delay
      • MPE — echoes MPE data with the notes (otherwise it is filtered out). Press, Slide and Note PB then set feedback for each; below 100% the echoed MPE data fades with each repeat.
33.3 Max for Live MIDI Effects
  • Ableton/Live/UG/33 Max for Live Devices/03 Max for Live MIDI Effects/06 Shaper MIDI

    • 33.3.6 Shaper MIDI

      • A modulator with a breakpoint envelope that restarts on every MIDI note and can respond to velocity — unlike the audio-effect Shaper.
      • Mapping, Mod, polarity and Min/Max work as in the audio-effect modulators.
      • Drawing:
        • click to add a breakpoint; Cmd-click (Mac) or Ctrl-click (Windows) adds the single sustain breakpoint, drawn larger. The envelope holds there while the note is held.
        • Option-drag (Mac) or Alt-drag (Windows) between points to curve a segment; Shift-click a point to delete it
        • Clear erases the envelope; six presets next to it start a new one
        • Grid and Snap as in Shaper
      • The small display shows the output; its LED flashes on each trigger.
      • Jitter adds randomness; Smooth softens sudden changes.
      • Velocity — how much note velocity scales the envelope; 0% ignores velocity
      • Offset — moves the center of the output up or down
      • Loop — repeats the envelope at Rate while a note is held, ignoring the sustain breakpoint
      • Rate — in Hz or synced divisions
      • Depth — modulation amount for all targets
      • Echo — how much each echo of the envelope feeds the next; Time — the echo interval
