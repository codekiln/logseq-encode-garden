Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes

Page reference

  • 9.3 Warp Modes

    • Warp Modes stretch time with granular techniques, repeating or skipping small segments called grains. Each mode chooses, overlaps and crossfades grains differently, so each suits different material, and each can be used creatively.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/01 Beats Mode

      • 9.3.1 Beats Mode

        • For material with a strong rhythm, such as drum loops and most dance music; it keeps transients intact.
        • Preserve: Transients for percussive material, or a grid division to keep fixed beat divisions. Large divisions with transposition give rhythmic artifacts.
        • Transient Loop Mode, for the end of each segment between transients:
          • Loop Off: plays to its end, then silence until the next transient.
          • Loop Forward: jumps back to a zero-crossing near the segment's middle and loops until the next transient.
          • Loop Back-and-Forth: plays back and forth from a zero-crossing near the middle until the next transient; with preserved transients this often sounds best, especially at slow tempos.
        • Transient Envelope: the fade on each segment. 100 means no fade; lower values decay faster, from smoothing clicks to gating effects.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/02 Tones Mode

      • 9.3.2 Tones Mode

        • For audio with a clear pitch: vocals, monophonic instruments, basslines.
        • Grain Size sets the average grain size, adjusted by how clear the pitch changes are. Small grains suit distinct pitch movement; large ones reduce noise but can add artifacts.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/03 Texture Mode

      • 9.3.3 Texture Mode

        • For sounds without a clear pitch: polyphonic orchestral music, pads, noise, drones, and sound design.
        • Grain Size ignores pitch here. Fluctuation adds randomness; higher values vary more.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/04 Re-Pitch Mode

      • 9.3.4 Re-Pitch Mode

        • Changes playback speed so pitch moves with tempo, like a turntable or an old sampler: double speed is an octave up. Transposition controls are disabled.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping/03 Warp Modes/05 Complex and Complex Pro Mode

      • 9.3.5 Complex and Complex Pro Mode

        • For mixed material such as whole songs. Complex Pro is a variant that can sound better on polyphonic textures and full mixes.
        • Formants: at 100%, transposition keeps the original formants, the tone's resonances. No effect without transposition.
        • Envelope, default 128: lower for high-pitched material, higher for low-pitched.
        • Both can use more CPU; freeze or resample tracks to save it.

Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI

  • 13. Converting Audio to MIDI

    • Commands that turn audio into MIDI: slicing, and converting harmony, melody or drums into a new MIDI track.
    • Besides warping, Live can pull musical information out of an audio clip and write it into a MIDI clip.
    • With an audio clip selected, the Create menu and the clip's context menu offer four commands: Slice to New MIDI Track, and Convert Harmony, Melody or Drums to New MIDI Track.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/01 Slice to New MIDI Track

      • 13.1 Slice to New MIDI Track

        • Slicing cuts the audio into pieces of time, one per MIDI note, without analysing pitch or rhythm. The pieces keep playing the original sound.
        • The dialog slices by beat division, by transients or by Warp Markers, and offers a Slicing Preset. A Rack holds at most 128 chains, so Live refuses settings that would make more than 128 slices.
          • Your own slicing presets go in the Defaults folder of the User Library.
          • Preserve warped timing keeps timing changes from warping; with it off, slices sound like the raw audio.
        • The result:
          • a new MIDI track with a clip holding one note per slice, in a rising chromatic sequence;
          • a Ableton/Instrument/Drum Rack on that track with one chain per slice, each a Ableton/Instrument/Simpler loaded with its slice;
          • the Drum Rack's Ableton/Macro controls mapped to envelope, loop and crossfade settings across every Simpler, as set by the slicing preset.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/01 Slice to New MIDI Track/01 Resequencing Slices

          • 13.1.1 Resequencing Slices

            • The sliced clip forms a rising "staircase" of notes. Editing the notes, or dragging Drum Rack pads onto each other to swap their notes, plays the slices in a new order.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/01 Slice to New MIDI Track/02 Using Effects on Slices

          • 13.1.2 Using Effects on Slices

            • Each slice has its own Drum Rack chain, so it can take its own audio effects. To share effects across several slices, select their chains and press Cmd+G (Mac) / Ctrl+G (Windows) to group them into a nested Rack, then add effects after it.
            • MIDI effects placed before the Drum Rack, such as Arpeggiator or Random, rearrange the slices as they play.
            • Slicing suits drum loops but also works on voices and ambient textures.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/02 Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track

      • 13.2 Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track

        • Detects the pitches in polyphonic audio and writes them to a clip on a new MIDI track, which comes with an Instrument Rack playing a piano sound.
        • Unlike slicing, the Convert commands don't play the original audio; the notes drive a different instrument.
        • Works on recordings from your collection, or on your own playing of guitar, piano or other harmonic instruments.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/03 Convert Melody to New MIDI Track

      • 13.3 Convert Melody to New MIDI Track

        • Detects the pitches in monophonic audio and writes them to a new MIDI track with a synth Instrument Rack. Its "Synth to Piano" Macro moves the sound from an analog-style synth to an electric piano.
        • Singing, whistling or a solo instrument can all become MIDI notes this way.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/04 Convert Drums to New MIDI Track

      • 13.4 Convert Drums to New MIDI Track

        • Extracts rhythms from unpitched, percussive audio onto a new MIDI track with a Drum Rack, trying to sort hits into kick, snare and hi-hat.
        • Adjusting the audio clip's transient markers before converting changes where notes land.
        • Suits breakbeats, beatboxing or tapping on a surface.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/05 Optimizing for Better Conversion Quality

      • 13.5 Optimizing for Better Conversion Quality

        • For the most accurate conversions:
          • use material with clear attacks — notes that swell in may be missed;
          • use isolated instruments, since every instrument present gets detected;
          • use uncompressed audio (.wav, .aiff) or high-bitrate lossy files.
        • Live places note boundaries at the clip's transient markers, so adding, moving or deleting markers before converting tunes the result.
        • The "wrong" command can be useful: Convert Harmony on a monophonic vocal tends to add notes that weren't there.

Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO
  • Track inputs and outputs: monitoring, external audio and MIDI, resampling and routing between tracks.
  • Audio/MIDI From — the input. Return tracks take their input from their sends.
  • Audio/MIDI To — the output. Every track outputs audio except MIDI tracks with no instrument.
  • In each chooser pair, the upper Type chooser picks a category (such as Ext. for an audio or MIDI interface) and the lower Channel chooser picks a specific input, output or channel within it.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/02 External Audio In-Out

    • 17.2 External Audio In/Out

      • Choose Ext. In as an audio track's Input Type to use the interface's inputs; the Input Channel chooser lists them with level meters that flash red on overload. Outputs work the same way. Changes apply to all selected tracks.
      • The Configure… entry opens the Audio Settings, whose Channel Configuration dialogs choose which interface inputs and outputs Live uses and whether they are mono or stereo pairs.
      • Channels can be renamed in those dialogs (Tab moves between them); the names show in the choosers and belong to that audio device.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/02 External Audio In-Out/01 Mono-Stereo Conversions

        • 17.2.1 Mono/Stereo Conversions

          • A mono input records mono samples; anything else records stereo. Inside the device chain the signal is always stereo.
          • Mono becomes stereo by copying the signal to both sides. Routing to a mono output sums left and right and lowers the result 6 dB to avoid clipping.
          • See also Ableton/Track/Q/How can I turn a mono signal into stereo in a channel?
Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/01 Monitoring

Page reference

  • Each audio and MIDI track has three monitor modes:
    • Auto (Ableton/Track/IO/Monitor/Auto) — monitors while the track is armed, but not while it plays clips. Suits most recording.
    • In — always monitors the input and silences the track's clips, turning it into an aux-style input. The track's Activator switch turns blue to show it.
    • Off (Ableton/Track/IO/Monitor/Off) — never monitors. For acoustic instruments heard in the room, an external mixer, or an interface's direct monitoring. Set Overall Latency in the Audio Settings when recording this way.
  • With In or Auto, Keep Monitoring Latency in Recorded Audio is on by default, lining the recording up with what you heard. Leave it on for software instruments and effects; turn it off for acoustic instruments or external monitoring. Right-click the In or Auto button to switch it.
  • Clicking a Monitor button applies to all selected tracks. With In/Out shown, Delete or Edit → Return to Default resets it: Off for audio tracks, Auto for MIDI tracks.

Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/05 Internal Routings/02 Making Use of Internal Routing

  • 17.5.2.2 Recording MIDI as Audio

    • When a complex instrument turns one note into something closer to a passage, record its audio: one MIDI track hosts the instrument, and an audio track taking input from it records the result.
  • Route several drum tracks to one submix audio track that goes to Main, so one fader controls the whole kit.
  • Impulse offers each of its eight sample slots as a source for other tracks. An audio track whose input is the Impulse track can choose one slot, which takes that slot out of Impulse's own mix for separate effects. Most plug-ins don't remove the tapped output this way.
  • A multi-timbral instrument plays a different part on each MIDI channel. Extra MIDI tracks feed its parts by pointing Output Type at the instrument's track and Output Channel at each part's channel; audio tracks tap its individual outputs.
  • External Instrument devices on other MIDI tracks can do both at once: set MIDI To to the instrument's track and channel, and Audio From to one of its auxiliary outputs. Several can share one track inside a Rack. The instrument's main outputs stay on its own track.

Ableton/Live/UG/20 Bounce to Audio

  • 20. Bounce to Audio

    • Bouncing tracks, group tracks and time selections to new audio tracks.
    • Bouncing turns tracks, clips or time selections into audio on new audio tracks — to experiment with the result, to lock in sound design before mixing, or to lower CPU load.
    • It works in both views; a track created by a bounce appears in both.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/20 Bounce to Audio/01 Bouncing Individual Tracks

      • 20.1 Bouncing Individual Tracks

        • Bounce Track in Place replaces a track with an audio track of its output. It replaces Freeze and Flatten from earlier versions. Find it in the track title bar's or a clip's context menu.
        • Bounce to New Track renders selected clips or a time selection to a new audio track and mutes the originals. Context menu, or Cmd+B (Mac) / Ctrl+B (Windows).
        • Bounces are taken after the effects and before the mixer: device processing is printed, while volume, pan and sends carry over as settings on the new track.
        • A time selection across several Arrangement tracks makes one new audio track per track with a selected clip.
        • Bounce Track in Place affects both views and bounces both Session and Arrangement clips. Bounce to New Track bounces only the selection; the new track appears empty in the other view.
        • New tracks and clips are named after the source plus "(Bounce)" and take the source track's colour. Files go to the Project's Samples/Processed/Bounce folder.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/20 Bounce to Audio/02 Bouncing Group Tracks

      • 20.2 Bouncing Group Tracks

        • Bounce Group in Place replaces a whole Group Track with one audio track. Find it in the group's title bar, a group slot, or the group's main lane context menu.
        • Bounce Group to New Track renders selected clips or a time selection in the group to a new track and mutes the originals; also Cmd+B / Ctrl+B.
        • Groups bounce after the mixer, from what reaches the Main output — including effects and sends inside the group, excluding the Main track's own effects.
        • Tracks routed outside the group are left out and may leave silence; return tracks count only if their output reaches Main.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/20 Bounce to Audio/03 Pasting Bounced Audio

      • 20.3 Pasting Bounced Audio

        • Paste Bounced Audio bounces whatever you copied in its current state at the moment you paste, so you can change the source and paste again without recopying. Handy for variations with different effects, or for gathering bits of several tracks into one.
        • Copy clips in one Session track, or clips or a time selection in the Arrangement (a group's main lane works too). Then paste into an audio track, an empty MIDI track or a take lane from the main lane's context menu, or with Cmd+Option+V (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+V (Windows). An empty MIDI track becomes an audio track.
        • If the copied source is deleted, the command is unavailable until you copy again.
Ableton/Live/UG/20 Bounce to Audio/01 Bouncing Individual Tracks

Page reference

  • 20.1 Bouncing Individual Tracks

    • Bounce Track in Place replaces a track with an audio track of its output. It replaces Freeze and Flatten from earlier versions. Find it in the track title bar's or a clip's context menu.
    • Bounce to New Track renders selected clips or a time selection to a new audio track and mutes the originals. Context menu, or Cmd+B (Mac) / Ctrl+B (Windows).
    • Bounces are taken after the effects and before the mixer: device processing is printed, while volume, pan and sends carry over as settings on the new track.
    • A time selection across several Arrangement tracks makes one new audio track per track with a selected clip.
    • Bounce Track in Place affects both views and bounces both Session and Arrangement clips. Bounce to New Track bounces only the selection; the new track appears empty in the other view.
    • New tracks and clips are named after the source plus "(Bounce)" and take the source track's colour. Files go to the Project's Samples/Processed/Bounce folder.
Ableton/Live/UG/20 Bounce to Audio/02 Bouncing Group Tracks

Page reference

  • 20.2 Bouncing Group Tracks

    • Bounce Group in Place replaces a whole Group Track with one audio track. Find it in the group's title bar, a group slot, or the group's main lane context menu.
    • Bounce Group to New Track renders selected clips or a time selection in the group to a new track and mutes the originals; also Cmd+B / Ctrl+B.
    • Groups bounce after the mixer, from what reaches the Main output — including effects and sends inside the group, excluding the Main track's own effects.
    • Tracks routed outside the group are left out and may leave silence; return tracks count only if their output reaches Main.

Ableton/Live/UG/22 Stem Separation
  • Splitting recorded audio into separate stems such as vocals, drums and bass.
  • Stem separation splits recorded audio — a song, loop or sample — into parts you can warp, chop, rearrange and process like any audio clip.
  • Ableton/Live/UG/22 Stem Separation/02 Separating Audio Files and Clips

    • 22.2 Separating Audio Files and Clips

      • Choose Separate Stems to New Audio Tracks from the context menu or Create menu, on a file in the browser or a clip in either view.
      • In the dialog:
        • toggles pick which of the four stems to make;
        • Merge to Single Track combines two or three chosen stems into one;
        • the quality selector picks High Speed or High Quality;
        • Separate starts the process and stops playback.
      • Each stem gets its own track inside a new Group Track, coloured to match the dialog's toggles; the group takes the source track's colour. A single stem, or a merged one, goes on one track instead.
      • Only the clip's audible region is processed — between its start and end markers, or from the start marker through the loop of a looped warped clip.
      • In the Arrangement, Separate Stems for Time Selection on a clip splits it at the selection's edges and separates only that part.
      • Source track effects move to the Group Track; track automation is copied to the group, and clip envelopes and modulation to each stem clip.
      • The source clip is deactivated to avoid doubling; select it and press 0 to turn it back on.
      • From the browser, stems land in whichever view has focus. In an empty Set with warping on for samples, the Set tempo follows the source.
      • Files go to the Project's Samples/Processed/Stems folder (merged stems keep their individual files too), in the File Type from the Record, Warp & Launch Settings, at 44.1 kHz and the source's bit depth.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/22 Stem Separation/02 Separating Audio Files and Clips/01 Separation Speed vs. Quality

        • 22.2.1 Separation Speed vs. Quality

          • High Speed separates every stem in one pass. It can still take several minutes on older computers.
          • High Quality gives cleaner results (a higher signal-to-distortion ratio) by running separate passes for Vocals, Drums and Bass, then deriving Others from what remains.
          • Crop the clip first to save time: Crop Clip Sample to Time Selection in the Sample Editor, or Crop Clip in the Arrangement, both Cmd+Shift+J (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+J (Windows).
          • In High Quality, choosing fewer stems saves time unless Others is included, since Others needs all three other passes. Unused stems from those passes are still saved in the Stems folder.
          • Older Windows computers and Intel Macs separate on the CPU only, so they're slower than Apple silicon Macs, which also use the GPU.
          • Some sounds land in an unexpected stem, such as hi-hat highs in Others or chorused synths in Vocals. Trying both modes can show which suits the material.
