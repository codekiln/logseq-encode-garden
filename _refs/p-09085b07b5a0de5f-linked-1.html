My Thoughts

  • On the Impermanence of Design in the wake of AI

    • The art of Design is a creative seance that begins with a prelude of Questions. Its acolytes construct the liturgy of creation with a prelude of questions to consider before taking any action, making any decision or making anything at all. Each question is a Sample, a marble pulled from a bag, each with a different quality, valence and relevance, that we put up to our eye and that we look through before proceeding, begging us to "choose wisely."
      • What perspectives should we consider?
        • What relevant personas do we not understand enough yet, and what can we do to listen deeper to the people that have them?
      • What would be ideal?
        • In an ideal world with no constraints, what would be possible?
      • What problems exist?
        • Which problems have been hidden, overlooked?
      • What is our purpose?
        • What is our mission?
      • Which creative constraints might be practical or fruitful here?
      • What hypotheses do we have?
        • What tests could we run?
      • What precedents exist?
    • Physicists first articulated the Physics/Observer/Effect after bumping up against the impossibility of perfect objectivity. The way we observe impacts the things we observe. Design flips the script here, in a gesture of Paradox/ical Hubris, asserting that the way we ask questions impacts the things we later make.
    • Lately I can't seem to shake the feeling that the art of design is being challenged by the emergence of AI.
      • We can build something quickly, and then that thing may, or may not, or may to a certain degree result in killing our want for it.
        • Like many people, I'm pro-prototype. I love the idea of them, I love doing them, I love exploring the possibility space.
      • But, design is turning into photography. Photography is a different sort of art. It's about being in many different sorts of places, meeting different sorts of people, taking many shots, and removing many shots. It's very different than painting the Mona Lisa. It's got a bit more of the aspect of a Slot/Machine; it's subject to a Poisson process: yes, no, no, no, yes, no, no.
    • What chance does design have of surviving contact with AI? It's so much more exciting to do than to consider. As AI emerges, will a prelude of questions still be deemed to a necessary antecedent to the act of creation? Humans Satis/fice. Where will we get our recommended daily intake of Vitamin Struggle?

Claude/Code
  • An explanation of what Claude/Code is, framed as "the story of how we got here" rather than a definition — given to a colleague who asked whether Claude Code is an AI/LLM.
    • The history of LLMs is largely the history of giving the LLM new tools it can "choose" to use.
      • Nov 2022: ChatGPT was an LLM with a single tool, "respond" — take a chat history plus a new user message, return one AI message. This single tool defined the chatbot era.
      • Later: ChatGPT "gave" the LLM tools tied to the Canvas in the web UI, letting it choose when to create or update a document shown to the right of the chat.
      • Later: ChatGPT "gave" the LLM a Code Interpreter tool (later Advanced Data Analysis), letting it choose to run Python and inspect the output when that would help.
    • Claude/Code and Codex are the latest evolution: they "give" the LLM tools that act on your computer — read a file, create a file, modify a file.
      • This class of tools is an AI/Agent/Agentic harness: the LLM runs in a loop, calling tool after tool. Most of what it does is not replying to you but invoking tools that correspond to things the operating system already knows how to do.
      • Whether in Claude Desktop, the VS Code extension, or the terminal (or the equivalents from other companies), "Claude Code" refers to an application that lets an LLM ask the operating system what it can do, then use it to take actions.
