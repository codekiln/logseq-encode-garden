Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/09 Selecting Clips and Time

  • 6.9 Selecting Clips and Time

    • Editing works by selecting, then running a command such as Cut, Copy, Paste or Duplicate. See Ableton/Arrangement View/Time Selection and Ableton/Selection.
    • Click a clip to select it. Click the background to place the insert marker, then move it with the arrow keys; Option (Mac) / Ctrl (Win) with left or right snaps it to locators and clip edges. Drag to select a timespan.
    • To select time inside a clip, unfold its track with the button by its name or U; Option+U (Mac) / Alt+U (Win) unfolds all. Resize by dragging the split line, with Option +/- (Mac) / Alt +/- (Win), or Option with a pinch; holding Option (Mac) / Alt (Win) while resizing one track resizes all.
    • Drag in a clip's waveform or MIDI display to select time in it. Clicking the loop brace runs Select Loop.
    • Shift-click extends a selection within or across tracks; Shift with arrows grows or shrinks it.
    • 0 deactivates the selected material, or the selected track. R or Reverse Clip(s) reverses a selection of audio, across clips; MIDI can't be reversed this way.
    • Left and right arrows nudge the selection.
    • Related: Ableton/Arrangement View/Clip/How To/Select in a not quantized way.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/10 Using the Editing Grid

  • 6.10 Using the Editing Grid

    • The cursor snaps to grid lines for the meter's subdivisions, in zoom-adaptive or fixed mode. Set the width from the context menu of a main lane or the MIDI Note Editor.
    • Shortcuts, also in the Options menu, Cmd on Mac / Ctrl on Windows:
      • Cmd+1 narrows the grid (eighths to sixteenths); Cmd+2 widens it.
      • Cmd+3 toggles triplets; Cmd+4 turns snapping off and on; Cmd+5 switches fixed and adaptive.
    • The current spacing shows at the lower right, above the time ruler.
    • Hold Cmd (Mac) / Alt (Win) while acting to bypass the grid, or to enable it temporarily when it's off.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/11 Using the Time Commands

  • 6.11 Using the …Time Commands

    • Cut, Copy and Paste act on the selection; their "… Time" versions insert or delete time across every track, time signature markers included.
      • Cut Time and Delete Time remove the selected time and close the gap, shortening the Arrangement.
      • Paste Time and Duplicate Time insert time, lengthening it.
      • Insert Silence adds a chosen amount of empty time at the insert marker.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/12 Splitting Clips

  • 6.12 Splitting Clips

    • Split, Cmd+E (Mac) / Ctrl+E (Win) or the context menu, divides a clip where you clicked in its display. With a range selected, it cuts that range out into its own clip. The parts are ordinary clips.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/13 Consolidating Clips

  • 6.13 Consolidating Clips

    • Consolidate, Cmd+J (Mac) / Ctrl+J (Win), the Edit menu or the main lane's context menu, joins selected adjacent clips into one, per track, across several tracks if selected. Useful for turning a looped passage into a single loop clip.
    • For audio it writes a new sample per track, recorded from the warping engine before the track's effects and mixer: it includes clip gain, warping, pitch and clip envelopes but no effects. Use Export Audio/Video for a post-effects file.
    • Samples go to the Project's Samples/Processed/Consolidate folder, or the Temporary Folder for an unsaved Set.
Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/14 Linked-Track Editing

  • 6.14 Linked-Track Editing

    • Linked-track editing applies comping and other phase-locked edits to several tracks together, for example the microphones on a drum kit. See Ableton/Linked-Track Editing and Ableton/YouTube/21/04/Learn Live Linked-Track Editing.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/14 Linked-Track Editing/01 Linking and Unlinking Tracks

      • 6.14.1 Linking and Unlinking Tracks

        • Link selected tracks with Link Tracks in a track header's context menu, or link a Group Track's tracks from the group header. Linked tracks show an indicator button in their headers.
        • A Set can have several linked groups, but a track belongs to only one. Hover the indicator to highlight a group's tracks; click it to select them.
        • To add tracks to a group, select them and Cmd-click (Mac) / Ctrl-click (Win) Link Tracks in a linked track's header. Any mix of linked and unlinked tracks can be linked into a new group.
        • Unlink Track(s) in the header's context menu removes tracks from their group.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/14 Linked-Track Editing/02 Editing Linked Tracks

      • 6.14.2 Editing Linked Tracks

        • These apply to every track in the group at once:
          • Moving and resizing clips; selecting clips and time; the "… Time" commands; splitting and consolidating.
          • Clip fades, for fades that start at the same position.
          • Arming and disarming.
          • Renaming, inserting and deleting take lanes, and Audition Mode, even where take lanes are hidden.

Ableton/Live/UG/06 Arrangement View/15 The Mixer in Arrangement View

  • 6.15 The Mixer in Arrangement View

    • Arrangement View has two sets of mixer controls: the mixer, and the Arrangement track controls beside each track. They share values.
    • Open the mixer with View → Mixer, Cmd+Option+M (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+M (Win), or the mixer view control at the bottom right. View → Mixer Controls and the Arrangement track controls submenu choose what shows.
    • Performance Impact meters, track delays and the crossfader exist only in the mixer.
Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View

  • 7. Session View

    • The grid of clips, tracks and scenes for playing and improvising without a fixed timeline, and recording a session into the Arrangement.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/01 Session View Clips

      • 7.1 Session View Clips

        • Click a clip's triangular launch button to start it, or select the clip by its name and press Enter; arrow keys move to neighbouring clips. A square Clip Stop button in a slot or in the Track Status field below the grid stops the track.
        • 0 deactivates the selected clips. Clips can be triggered from the computer keyboard or a MIDI controller, even mapped across a note range to play chromatically.
        • The grid gives random access: clips play in any order regardless of layout.
        • Stopping clips doesn't stop song time: Play stays lit and the Arrangement Position keeps running. Press Stop twice to stop everything and return to 1.1.1.
        • Rename clips, several at once, with Rename; add info text with Edit Info Text; pick a color from the context menu. Drag to reorder; Shift-click selects a range, Ctrl-click separate clips.
        • Group Track slots are shaded, in the color of the leftmost clip, when any track in the group has a clip in that scene. Their launch buttons launch those clips together, empty ones hold stop buttons, and clicking one selects its clips.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/02 Tracks and Scenes

      • 7.2 Tracks and Scenes

        • A track plays one clip at a time, so put alternatives in the same column: song parts, variations of a drum loop.
        • Drag the edges of a track's title bar to resize it, narrow enough to show only launch buttons and key controls; Option (Mac) / Alt (Win) resizes all tracks. 0 on a selected track header deactivates the track.
        • Rows are scenes. A Scene Launch button in the Main track column launches the whole row. Cancel Scene Launch in the Main track's context menu cancels a pending one.
        • After a scene launches, the next one down is selected, unless Select Next Scene on Launch is off in Launch Settings, so you can step down the list. Keys and MIDI can launch and scroll scenes.
        • Rename scenes with Rename, using Tab to move to the next; add info text and colors from the context menu.
        • Drag scenes to reorder. Nonadjacent scenes collapse together when dragged; Ctrl+up/down moves them without collapsing. Scene numbers follow position.
        • See Ableton/Live/Mode/Session.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/02 Tracks and Scenes/01 Editing Scene Tempo and Time Signature Values

          • 7.2.1 Editing Scene Tempo and Time Signature Values

            • Drag the left edge of the Main track's header to show the hidden Scene Tempo and Scene Time Signature fields. Launching the scene switches the Set to those values.
            • Drag in a field, or type and press Enter; the Scene View edits them too. Tempo 20 to 999 BPM; time signature numerator 1 to 99 over 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16.
            • Return to Default, Delete, or a double-click disables a field again.
            • Left and right arrows move from a slot or scene to these fields; Tab and Shift+Tab step through name, tempo and signature, scene by scene. With a field selected, Enter selects its scene and a second Enter launches it.
            • Scenes with tempo or meter changes get a colored launch button. Sets from before Live 11 that set tempo or meter in scene names have those values moved into the fields.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/02 Tracks and Scenes/02 Scene View

          • 7.2.2 Scene View

            • The Scene View opens when you select scenes, click a Scene Tempo or Time Signature field, or click the Main track's title bar.
            • Its upper section has Tempo and Signature sliders; its lower section has the scenes' Follow Actions.
            • The title bar shows the scene's name, number and color, or the count of selected scenes. Its context menu renames scenes and sets their color.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/03 The Track Status Fields

      • 7.3 The Track Status Fields

        • The Track Status field above each track's mixer controls shows what the track is doing:
          • A pie chart: a looping Session clip, with the loop length in beats on the right and the number of repetitions on the left. On a Group Track, without numbers, it means a track inside is playing.
          • A progress bar: a one-shot clip, with time remaining in minutes:seconds.
          • A microphone (audio track) or keyboard (MIDI track): the track is monitoring its input.
          • A miniature of clips: the track is playing the Arrangement.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/04 Setting Up the Session View Grid

      • 7.4 Setting Up the Session View Grid

        • Clips reach the Session from the browser or by recording.
        • Several files dropped at once stack in one track, vertically in Session View; hold Cmd (Mac) / Ctrl (Win) while dropping to spread them across tracks. This works for raw audio and MIDI files, not Live Clips.
        • Drag clips around the grid; select several with Shift or Cmd/Ctrl, or rubber-band select from an empty slot.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/04 Setting Up the Session View Grid/01 Select on Launch

          • 7.4.1 Select on Launch

            • Launching a clip also selects it so the Clip View shows it. Turn off Select on Launch in Launch Settings to keep the current view, for example a return track's devices, when launching clips or scenes.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/04 Setting Up the Session View Grid/02 Removing Clip Stop Buttons

          • 7.4.2 Removing Clip Stop Buttons

            • Edit → Add/Remove Stop Button adds or removes a slot's Clip Stop button. A slot without one is left alone by its scene: remove the stop button at scene 3, track 4, and launching scene 3 won't affect track 4.
              • My Notes
                • This is Keyshort Cmd E to toggle
        • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/04 Setting Up the Session View Grid/03 Editing Scenes

          • 7.4.3 Editing Scenes

            • Besides Cut, Copy, Paste and Duplicate, the Create menu has:
              • Insert Scene: an empty scene below the selection.
              • Capture and Insert Scene: a new scene holding copies of the clips now playing, launched at once without a break. Useful for keeping a good combination while you keep experimenting.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/07 Session View/05 Recording Sessions into the Arrangement

      • 7.5 Recording Sessions into the Arrangement

        • With Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Button/Arrangement Record on, Live logs Session playing into the Arrangement: launched clips, changes to their properties, mixer and device automation, and tempo and meter changes from launched scenes. Press it again or stop to finish.
        • The Arrangement then holds copies of the launched clips on the right tracks and times. No new audio is recorded, only clips.
        • A track plays either its Session clip or its Arrangement, not both. Launching a Session clip, or pressing a Clip Stop button, takes the track off the Arrangement until you press Ableton/Live/UI/Arrangement View/Button/Back to Arrangement, which lights up while what you hear differs from the Arrangement.
        • Stop All Clips sits in the Main track's status field. Session and Arrangement clips are independent, so you can record improvisations into the Arrangement again and again.
        • Move clips between views with Copy and Paste, by dragging onto the view selectors, or between windows with a Second Window (Cmd+Shift+W on Mac / Ctrl+Shift+W on Windows).
        • Pasting Arrangement material into the Session lays clips out top to bottom in time order, so stepping down the scenes rebuilds the arrangement.
        • Create → Consolidate Time to New Scene turns a selected Arrangement range into one clip per track in a new scene, writing new samples for audio tracks.
        • Related: Ableton/Arrangement View/Q/Can I record in Arrangement View while playing Session View clips? and Launchpad/Custom/Mode/1 Arrangement.

Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View

  • 8. Clip View

    • Clip properties and the clip editor: the panels for audio, pitch and time, transform and generate, plus looping, cropping and replacing clips.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout

      • 8.1 Clip View Layout

        • The Clip View has a title bar, clip panels on the left, and an editor on the right: the Sample Editor for audio clips, the MIDI Note Editor for MIDI clips. See Ableton/Clip/View.
        • Open it by double-clicking a clip, with the Clip View Selector or its toggle, or with Cmd+Option+3 (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+3 (Win). Opened with the toggle while the Device View is showing, it stacks above it. Drag its top border to resize; dragging it to the bottom closes it.
        • It always shows the selected clip. With two windows it opens in the focused one; F12 moves it to the other window, as does Shift+Tab when "Use Tab Key to Navigate" is off. One window can stay on clip editing while the other shows Session or Arrangement.
        • Clicking a track's status display in Session View opens the clip playing on that track.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout/01 Clip Title Bar

          • 8.1.1 Clip Title Bar

            • Holds the Clip Activator toggle, the clip's name and color, and, for audio clips, the Save Default Clip button.

            • 8.1.1.1 Clip Activator Toggle

              • Turns a clip off so it doesn't play when launched or during Arrangement playback; also available from the clip's context menu or with 0. Applies to every selected clip.

            • 8.1.1.2 Clip Name

              • A clip takes its file's name by default. Rename it from the title bar's context menu, or with Edit → Rename; Edit Info Text adds a description (Ableton/Info Text, Ableton/Clip/Q/Can info text move with the clip).
              • Renaming an audio clip doesn't rename its sample; rename the file in the browser.
              • With several clips selected, the title bar shows how many, and a rename applies to all of them.

            • 8.1.1.3 Clip Color

              • New clips take their track's color. Pick another from the title bar or clip context menu.
              • Assign Track Color to Clips, in a track header's context menu, resets them; on a Group Track, Assign Track Color to Grouped Tracks and Clips colors everything inside. Each affects only the view you run it in, Session or Arrangement.
              • With several clips selected, the title bar shows stripes, but a chosen color still applies to all.
              • Related: Ableton/How To/Produce a single sample audio clip for color coding.

            • 8.1.1.4 Saving Default Audio Clip Settings with the Sample

              • Save Default Clip stores the clip's settings, Warp Markers above all, in the sample's analysis file (Ableton/File/.asd), for every selected clip.
              • Live restores them whenever the sample is dropped into a Set from then on; existing clips aren't changed. A Live Clip, unlike this, also saves devices.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout/02 Clip Panels

          • 8.1.2 Clip Panels

            • Main and Extended Clip Properties panels, shared by both clip types, hold start, end, loop, time signature, groove and scale, plus launch settings for Session clips.
            • Audio clips add the Audio Utilities and Audio Transformation panels; MIDI clips add Pitch and Time Utilities and the MIDI Transformation and Generative panels.
            • Panels can sit side by side or stacked. Drag the panels' right edge left to stack them, or right to go back, or choose Arrange Clip View Panels Vertically, Horizontally or Automatically in the View menu. Live remembers the choice.
            • Double-click the title bar, or choose Fold, to minimize the panels.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout/03 Editor View Modes

          • 8.1.3 Editor View Modes

            • Audio clips switch between the Sample Editor and the Envelope Editor; MIDI clips between the MIDI Note Editor, Envelope Editor and MPE Editor. Use the tabs, the View menu, or Option+Tab (Mac) / Alt+Tab (Win).
              • Sample Editor: the waveform and warping.
              • Envelope Editor: clip envelopes that automate or modulate effects, mixer, clip and MIDI controls.
              • MIDI Note Editor: notes and velocities.
              • MPE Editor: per-note MIDI Polyphonic Expression.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/02 Main Clip Properties Panel

      • 8.2 Main Clip Properties Panel

        • The leftmost panel: clip region and loop, time signature, groove and scale.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/02 Main Clip Properties Panel/01 Clip and Loop Region Settings

          • 8.2.1 Clip and Loop Region Settings

            • The region and loop decide what plays when a clip launches. An unlooped clip plays from start to end, or until stopped.
            • Type start and end values, or press Set Start and Set End during playback to set them at the playhead, quantized to global quantization. Warped audio shows bars-beats-sixteenths; unwarped audio shows minutes-seconds-milliseconds.
            • The Clip Loop toggle loops the clip over Loop Position and Length, which defaults to the whole clip. Audio clips must be warped to loop.
            • During playback, Set Loop Position moves the loop start to the playhead and turns looping on; Set Loop Length then ends the loop at the playhead, capturing a loop on the fly. With looping off, Set Loop Length ends the loop at the playhead keeping its length, and turns looping on.
            • All of these can be MIDI mapped, for example a rotary encoder that walks the loop through a sample by the quantization step.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/02 Main Clip Properties Panel/02 Clip Time Signature

          • 8.2.2 Clip Time Signature

            • Sets a clip's time signature for display only; playback isn't affected. Independent of the project's meter, it makes polymetric combinations easy to spot.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/02 Main Clip Properties Panel/03 Clip Groove

          • 8.2.3 Clip Groove

            • The Clip Groove chooser picks a groove from the Groove Pool. Hot-Swap Groove picks a new one from the browser, replacing it in the clip and the Groove Pool.
            • Commit writes the groove into the clip and turns the groove off. On audio clips, positive groove velocity becomes a volume clip envelope, overwriting any existing one. See Ableton/Live/UG/14 Grooves.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/02 Main Clip Properties Panel/04 Clip Scale

          • 8.2.4 Clip Scale

            • The Scale button turns Scale Mode on for the clip; Root Note and Scale Name set the scale.
            • In audio clips the scale doesn't change playback; it's passed to scale-aware devices. In MIDI clips, the scale's keys are highlighted in the piano ruler. See Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/03 Extended Clip Properties

      • 8.3 Extended Clip Properties

        • Launch controls and MIDI bank and program controls. Launch controls appear only for Session clips, so an Arrangement audio clip has no panel here and an Arrangement MIDI clip shows only bank and program.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/03 Extended Clip Properties/01 Follow Action and Launch Controls

          • 8.3.1 Follow Action and Launch Controls

            • Empty slots split a track's clips into groups; Follow Actions set how clips in a group trigger one another.
            • Launch controls set how clips respond to mouse, keys and MIDI notes, launch timing, scrubbing, and how note velocity affects volume. Both are covered in Ableton/Live/UG/16 Launching Clips.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/03 Extended Clip Properties/02 MIDI Clip Bank and Program Change Controls

          • 8.3.2 MIDI Clip Bank and Program Change Controls

            • A MIDI clip can send a bank and program change when it launches, so each clip can call up a different sound on an external synth or plug-in. Live offers 128 banks, each with 128 sub-banks of 128 programs; check how many your synth supports.
            • Leave the choosers at "—" to send nothing.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel

      • 8.4 Audio Utilities Panel

        • Audio clips' panel for warping, reversing and external editing, tempo, gain and transposition, plus toggles for edge fades, RAM Mode and high-quality interpolation.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/01 Warp Controls

          • 8.4.1 Warp Controls

            • With Warp off, a sample plays at its own tempo, right for hits, atmospheres, effects and speech. With Warp on, rhythmic material follows the Set's tempo as it changes.
            • Live works out tempo and length for well-prepared loops; other samples may need hints. See Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/02 Reversing Samples

          • 8.4.2 Reversing Samples

            • Reverse writes a reversed copy of the sample and swaps it into the clip; it's saved under the Project's Samples/Processed/Reverse, or the Temporary Folder until the Set is saved.
            • Warp Markers stay fixed to the sample, so they flip (a marker on bar 2's downbeat ends up on the second-to-last bar), and loop and region settings flip too. Clip envelopes stay fixed in time.
            • Long samples show progress in the Status Bar and briefly lock editing, though playback continues. Live remembers both versions until it quits, so reversing again is instant.
            • Session View reverses one clip at a time; in Arrangement View, select a range and use Reverse Clip(s) or R. Avoid reversing during a performance: reapplying settings can glitch.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/03 Destructive Sample Editing

          • 8.4.3 Destructive Sample Editing

            • Edit opens the sample in the external editor set in File & Folder Settings. Playback must stop while you edit.
            • Warp Markers survive only if the length doesn't change, and the edit affects every clip using that sample.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/04 Clip Start and End Fades

          • 8.4.4 Clip Start and End Fades

            • The Clip Fade toggle adds a 0 to 4 ms fade, depending on the signal, at the clip's start and end to prevent clicks. Session clips only; Arrangement clips use their fade handles.
            • It's on by default when Create Fades on Clip Edges is on in Record, Warp & Launch Settings.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/05 Clip RAM Mode

          • 8.4.5 Clip RAM Mode

            • RAM Mode loads the clip's audio into memory instead of streaming it from disk. It helps when the disk can't keep up with many tracks, or when Legato Mode causes dropouts.
            • Use it sparingly: if RAM runs out the system swaps to disk, and swapped audio arriving late causes both mutes and rhythmic hiccups, worse than a disk overload's mutes.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/06 High Quality Interpolation

          • 8.4.6 High Quality Interpolation

            • High Quality uses a better sample-rate conversion algorithm at higher CPU cost, with less distortion, especially in the highs, when transposing or converting sample rates. Transposition of about 19 semitones either way stays free of audible aliasing.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/07 Clip Gain and Pitch

          • 8.4.7 Clip Gain and Pitch

            • Gain, in dB, sets clip gain; split handles show the range when several clips differ.
            • Pitch transposes in semitones, with a second field for cents (100 cents per semitone).
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/05 Pitch and Time Utilities Panel

      • 8.5 Pitch and Time Utilities Panel

        • MIDI clips' tools for selected notes or a time range, or the whole clip when nothing is selected. Each tool has its own section in Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/05 Pitch and Time Utilities Panel/01 Pitch Tools

          • 8.5.1 Pitch Tools

            • Transpose: by semitones, or scale degrees when the clip has a scale.
            • Fit to Scale: moves notes into the clip's scale; inactive without one.
            • Invert: flips the selection so the highest note becomes the lowest.
            • Interval Size and Add Interval: add notes a set number of semitones or scale degrees from the selection.
        • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/05 Pitch and Time Utilities Panel/02 Time Tools

          • 8.5.2 Time Tools

            • Stretch compresses or stretches note durations; ×2 and /2 double or halve notes, time selection or loop.
            • Duration with Set Length gives notes a chosen length.
            • Humanize Amount, up to half a grid step, with Humanize randomizes note start times.
            • Reverse flips the order of the notes; Legato stretches or trims each note to reach the next one.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/06 Transform and Generate Panels

      • 8.6 Transform and Generate Panels

        • For warped audio clips, the Transform panel has Quantize: the grid or a chosen meter value, with Amount setting how far Warp Markers move. Audio clips have no Generate panel.
        • For MIDI clips, the panels hold MIDI Tools. Transformation tools replace the selected notes, the notes in a time selection, or all notes; generative tools create note patterns in the time selection or loop. With Scale Mode on, pitch controls use scale degrees and generated notes stay in the scale. See Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/07 Zooming and Scrolling in the Clip View's Editor

      • 8.7 Zooming and Scrolling in the Clip View’s Editor

        • Works like the Arrangement: drag the time ruler vertically to zoom and sideways to scroll, or use the mouse wheel.
        • Z, or View → Zoom to Clip Selection, zooms fully to the selection; X steps back once per Z.
        • The Clip View Selector shows the whole clip, with an outline for the visible part; drag inside it to scroll or zoom.
        • The Follow toggle, or Options → Follow, scrolls with playback. It pauses when you edit in the editor and resumes on stop, restart, or a click in a scrub area.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/08 Playing and Scrubbing Clips

      • 8.8 Playing and Scrubbing Clips

        • Start and end markers can be dragged in the editor or moved with the arrow keys; with a marker selected, Option+arrows (Mac) / Alt+arrows (Win) move the whole region.
        • With Permanent Scrub Areas on, clicking the lower half of the waveform or the scrub area below the ruler jumps playback there, quantized to global quantization. Cmd+6 to Cmd+0 (Mac) / Ctrl+6 to Ctrl+0 (Win) change it.
        • Holding the mouse button over the scrub area repeats a portion the length of the quantization; small values or None let you scrub. Chase MIDI Notes plays notes that started before the play position.
        • With Permanent Scrub Areas off, Shift-click the scrub area or beat-time ruler.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/09 Looping Clips

      • 8.9 Looping Clips

        • The Clip Loop toggle loops the clip over its loop region; audio clips need Warp on.
        • In the editor the region is a loop brace: drag its markers to change it, or drag the brace to move it.
        • Left and right arrows nudge the brace by the grid; up and down shift it by its length. With Cmd (Mac) / Ctrl (Win), left and right shorten or lengthen it by the grid, and up and down halve or double it.
        • Edit → Duplicate Loop doubles the loop and its contents; in MIDI clips, notes after the loop move to keep their distance from its end.
        • Playback always starts at the start marker, so a clip can run into its loop.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/10 Clip View Sample Details

      • 8.10 Clip View Sample Details

        • The Sample Editor header shows the sample's name, sample rate, bit depth and channel count. With several clips selected, it shows all of them, an asterisk where values differ, and the count.
        • Click the name, or use Show in Browser, to find the file in the browser.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/11 Cropping Clips

      • 8.11 Cropping Clips

        • Crop Clip Sample keeps only the part between the start and end markers of an unlooped audio clip; for looped clips, from the earlier of start and loop start to the loop end. Crop Clip Sample to Time Selection keeps a selection.
        • Cropping writes a new sample to the Project's Samples/Processed/Crop, or the Temporary Folder until the Set is saved. Shortcut: Cmd+Shift+J (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+J (Win).
        • MIDI clips have the same commands under the names Crop Clip and Crop Clip to Time Selection.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/12 Replacing and Editing the Sample

      • 8.12 Replacing and Editing the Sample

        • Drop a new sample from the browser into the Clip View, or double-click or press Enter on one, to replace the clip's sample. Pitch, volume and other settings stay; Warp Markers stay only if the length is identical.
        • Show Similar Files in the Sample Editor's context menu searches for similar samples.
        • Manage Sample File opens the File Manager with the sample selected, to replace or edit it for every clip and instrument that uses it.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/13 Editing Clip Properties for Multiple Clips

      • 8.13 Editing Clip Properties for Multiple Clips

        • Select several clips by dragging from an empty slot, or with Cmd/Ctrl or Shift, to edit them together. Only properties they share are shown.
        • When values differ, sliders and knobs show the range and move it together. Dragging to the maximum or minimum makes them all equal, to be adjusted as one.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/14 Clip Defaults and Update Rate

      • 8.14 Clip Defaults and Update Rate

        • Clip Update Rate, in Record, Warp & Launch Settings, quantizes how quickly Clip View changes reach a playing clip. The same tab sets defaults for new clips, such as Launch Mode and Warp Mode.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout

  • 8.1 Clip View Layout

    • The Clip View has a title bar, clip panels on the left, and an editor on the right: the Sample Editor for audio clips, the MIDI Note Editor for MIDI clips. See Ableton/Clip/View.
    • Open it by double-clicking a clip, with the Clip View Selector or its toggle, or with Cmd+Option+3 (Mac) / Ctrl+Alt+3 (Win). Opened with the toggle while the Device View is showing, it stacks above it. Drag its top border to resize; dragging it to the bottom closes it.
    • It always shows the selected clip. With two windows it opens in the focused one; F12 moves it to the other window, as does Shift+Tab when "Use Tab Key to Navigate" is off. One window can stay on clip editing while the other shows Session or Arrangement.
    • Clicking a track's status display in Session View opens the clip playing on that track.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout/01 Clip Title Bar

      • 8.1.1 Clip Title Bar

        • Holds the Clip Activator toggle, the clip's name and color, and, for audio clips, the Save Default Clip button.

        • 8.1.1.1 Clip Activator Toggle

          • Turns a clip off so it doesn't play when launched or during Arrangement playback; also available from the clip's context menu or with 0. Applies to every selected clip.

        • 8.1.1.2 Clip Name

          • A clip takes its file's name by default. Rename it from the title bar's context menu, or with Edit → Rename; Edit Info Text adds a description (Ableton/Info Text, Ableton/Clip/Q/Can info text move with the clip).
          • Renaming an audio clip doesn't rename its sample; rename the file in the browser.
          • With several clips selected, the title bar shows how many, and a rename applies to all of them.

        • 8.1.1.3 Clip Color

          • New clips take their track's color. Pick another from the title bar or clip context menu.
          • Assign Track Color to Clips, in a track header's context menu, resets them; on a Group Track, Assign Track Color to Grouped Tracks and Clips colors everything inside. Each affects only the view you run it in, Session or Arrangement.
          • With several clips selected, the title bar shows stripes, but a chosen color still applies to all.
          • Related: Ableton/How To/Produce a single sample audio clip for color coding.

        • 8.1.1.4 Saving Default Audio Clip Settings with the Sample

          • Save Default Clip stores the clip's settings, Warp Markers above all, in the sample's analysis file (Ableton/File/.asd), for every selected clip.
          • Live restores them whenever the sample is dropped into a Set from then on; existing clips aren't changed. A Live Clip, unlike this, also saves devices.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout/02 Clip Panels

      • 8.1.2 Clip Panels

        • Main and Extended Clip Properties panels, shared by both clip types, hold start, end, loop, time signature, groove and scale, plus launch settings for Session clips.
        • Audio clips add the Audio Utilities and Audio Transformation panels; MIDI clips add Pitch and Time Utilities and the MIDI Transformation and Generative panels.
        • Panels can sit side by side or stacked. Drag the panels' right edge left to stack them, or right to go back, or choose Arrange Clip View Panels Vertically, Horizontally or Automatically in the View menu. Live remembers the choice.
        • Double-click the title bar, or choose Fold, to minimize the panels.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/01 Clip View Layout/03 Editor View Modes

      • 8.1.3 Editor View Modes

        • Audio clips switch between the Sample Editor and the Envelope Editor; MIDI clips between the MIDI Note Editor, Envelope Editor and MPE Editor. Use the tabs, the View menu, or Option+Tab (Mac) / Alt+Tab (Win).
          • Sample Editor: the waveform and warping.
          • Envelope Editor: clip envelopes that automate or modulate effects, mixer, clip and MIDI controls.
          • MIDI Note Editor: notes and velocities.
          • MPE Editor: per-note MIDI Polyphonic Expression.

  • 8.4.1 Warp Controls

    • With Warp off, a sample plays at its own tempo, right for hits, atmospheres, effects and speech. With Warp on, rhythmic material follows the Set's tempo as it changes.
    • Live works out tempo and length for well-prepared loops; other samples may need hints. See Ableton/Live/UG/09 Audio Clips Tempo and Warping.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/02 Reversing Samples

  • 8.4.2 Reversing Samples

    • Reverse writes a reversed copy of the sample and swaps it into the clip; it's saved under the Project's Samples/Processed/Reverse, or the Temporary Folder until the Set is saved.
    • Warp Markers stay fixed to the sample, so they flip (a marker on bar 2's downbeat ends up on the second-to-last bar), and loop and region settings flip too. Clip envelopes stay fixed in time.
    • Long samples show progress in the Status Bar and briefly lock editing, though playback continues. Live remembers both versions until it quits, so reversing again is instant.
    • Session View reverses one clip at a time; in Arrangement View, select a range and use Reverse Clip(s) or R. Avoid reversing during a performance: reapplying settings can glitch.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/03 Destructive Sample Editing

  • 8.4.3 Destructive Sample Editing

    • Edit opens the sample in the external editor set in File & Folder Settings. Playback must stop while you edit.
    • Warp Markers survive only if the length doesn't change, and the edit affects every clip using that sample.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/04 Clip Start and End Fades

  • 8.4.4 Clip Start and End Fades

    • The Clip Fade toggle adds a 0 to 4 ms fade, depending on the signal, at the clip's start and end to prevent clicks. Session clips only; Arrangement clips use their fade handles.
    • It's on by default when Create Fades on Clip Edges is on in Record, Warp & Launch Settings.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/05 Clip RAM Mode

  • 8.4.5 Clip RAM Mode

    • RAM Mode loads the clip's audio into memory instead of streaming it from disk. It helps when the disk can't keep up with many tracks, or when Legato Mode causes dropouts.
    • Use it sparingly: if RAM runs out the system swaps to disk, and swapped audio arriving late causes both mutes and rhythmic hiccups, worse than a disk overload's mutes.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/06 High Quality Interpolation

  • 8.4.6 High Quality Interpolation

    • High Quality uses a better sample-rate conversion algorithm at higher CPU cost, with less distortion, especially in the highs, when transposing or converting sample rates. Transposition of about 19 semitones either way stays free of audible aliasing.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/04 Audio Utilities Panel/07 Clip Gain and Pitch

  • 8.4.7 Clip Gain and Pitch

    • Gain, in dB, sets clip gain; split handles show the range when several clips differ.
    • Pitch transposes in semitones, with a second field for cents (100 cents per semitone).
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/05 Pitch and Time Utilities Panel

  • 8.5 Pitch and Time Utilities Panel

    • MIDI clips' tools for selected notes or a time range, or the whole clip when nothing is selected. Each tool has its own section in Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/05 Pitch and Time Utilities Panel/01 Pitch Tools

      • 8.5.1 Pitch Tools

        • Transpose: by semitones, or scale degrees when the clip has a scale.
        • Fit to Scale: moves notes into the clip's scale; inactive without one.
        • Invert: flips the selection so the highest note becomes the lowest.
        • Interval Size and Add Interval: add notes a set number of semitones or scale degrees from the selection.
    • Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/05 Pitch and Time Utilities Panel/02 Time Tools

      • 8.5.2 Time Tools

        • Stretch compresses or stretches note durations; ×2 and /2 double or halve notes, time selection or loop.
        • Duration with Set Length gives notes a chosen length.
        • Humanize Amount, up to half a grid step, with Humanize randomizes note start times.
        • Reverse flips the order of the notes; Legato stretches or trims each note to reach the next one.

Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/06 Transform and Generate Panels

  • 8.6 Transform and Generate Panels

    • For warped audio clips, the Transform panel has Quantize: the grid or a chosen meter value, with Amount setting how far Warp Markers move. Audio clips have no Generate panel.
    • For MIDI clips, the panels hold MIDI Tools. Transformation tools replace the selected notes, the notes in a time selection, or all notes; generative tools create note patterns in the time selection or loop. With Scale Mode on, pitch controls use scale degrees and generated notes stay in the scale. See Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/07 Zooming and Scrolling in the Clip View's Editor

  • 8.7 Zooming and Scrolling in the Clip View’s Editor

    • Works like the Arrangement: drag the time ruler vertically to zoom and sideways to scroll, or use the mouse wheel.
    • Z, or View → Zoom to Clip Selection, zooms fully to the selection; X steps back once per Z.
    • The Clip View Selector shows the whole clip, with an outline for the visible part; drag inside it to scroll or zoom.
    • The Follow toggle, or Options → Follow, scrolls with playback. It pauses when you edit in the editor and resumes on stop, restart, or a click in a scrub area.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/08 Playing and Scrubbing Clips

  • 8.8 Playing and Scrubbing Clips

    • Start and end markers can be dragged in the editor or moved with the arrow keys; with a marker selected, Option+arrows (Mac) / Alt+arrows (Win) move the whole region.
    • With Permanent Scrub Areas on, clicking the lower half of the waveform or the scrub area below the ruler jumps playback there, quantized to global quantization. Cmd+6 to Cmd+0 (Mac) / Ctrl+6 to Ctrl+0 (Win) change it.
    • Holding the mouse button over the scrub area repeats a portion the length of the quantization; small values or None let you scrub. Chase MIDI Notes plays notes that started before the play position.
    • With Permanent Scrub Areas off, Shift-click the scrub area or beat-time ruler.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/09 Looping Clips

  • 8.9 Looping Clips

    • The Clip Loop toggle loops the clip over its loop region; audio clips need Warp on.
    • In the editor the region is a loop brace: drag its markers to change it, or drag the brace to move it.
    • Left and right arrows nudge the brace by the grid; up and down shift it by its length. With Cmd (Mac) / Ctrl (Win), left and right shorten or lengthen it by the grid, and up and down halve or double it.
    • Edit → Duplicate Loop doubles the loop and its contents; in MIDI clips, notes after the loop move to keep their distance from its end.
    • Playback always starts at the start marker, so a clip can run into its loop.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/10 Clip View Sample Details

  • 8.10 Clip View Sample Details

    • The Sample Editor header shows the sample's name, sample rate, bit depth and channel count. With several clips selected, it shows all of them, an asterisk where values differ, and the count.
    • Click the name, or use Show in Browser, to find the file in the browser.
Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View/11 Cropping Clips

  • 8.11 Cropping Clips

    • Crop Clip Sample keeps only the part between the start and end markers of an unlooped audio clip; for looped clips, from the earlier of start and loop start to the loop end. Crop Clip Sample to Time Selection keeps a selection.
    • Cropping writes a new sample to the Project's Samples/Processed/Crop, or the Temporary Folder until the Set is saved. Shortcut: Cmd+Shift+J (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+J (Win).
    • MIDI clips have the same commands under the names Crop Clip and Crop Clip to Time Selection.
