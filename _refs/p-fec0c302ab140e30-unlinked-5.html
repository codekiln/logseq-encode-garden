Dev/Barefoot
  • A term coined by Person/Maggie Appleton to describe a new kind of developer who creates Dev/Home-Cooked Software for local communities rather than building software for the masses at industrial scale
  • Barefoot developers build software for themselves, their family and friends, their neighborhood, and their community
  • They don't need the complexity of professional industrial software - most local community software needs can be solved with simple CRUD apps, basic user authentication, and a few API calls
  • Build software with care and love for people they know
  • Maintain control over their software and data
  • Need language model agents that act as central orchestrators to guide them through building software
  • Dev/Home-Cooked Software
  • Home-Cooked Software and Barefoot Developers - Talk by Person/Maggie Appleton at Local-first Conference, May 2024

Economics/Q/Are most internet businesses winner-take-most businesses with four or five major competitors?
  • Cross-country evidence points in the same direction. OECD researchers found rising concentration across many countries and sectors from 2002 to 2014, with a stronger relationship in globalized and digital-intensive industries. Investment in software, data, and other intangible assets appears to help large firms scale: Intangibles and industry concentration.
  • Assessment: Casey’s intuition fits markets with strong network effects, low marginal costs, high switching costs, limited multi-homing, and large data or scale advantages. “Internet business” also includes ordinary retailers, publishers, professional services, and software companies for which those conditions may be weak. The defensible claim is that digital platforms and high-digital-intensity industries have a higher tendency toward concentration than less digital industries. A fixed ceiling of four or five firms is not supported by the studies above.

Emulation
  • Emulation reproduces one system's hardware or instruction set behavior in software on another system.
  • It enables software built for one architecture or device model to run where native support does not exist.
  • Teams use emulation to run legacy software, test cross-architecture builds, and preserve historical systems.
  • CPU, memory maps, devices, and firmware expectations are represented in software.
  • Running x86 guest software on an ARM-only host.
  • Emulated software is always unusably slow -> False. Performance varies by workload, translator quality, and host resources.
