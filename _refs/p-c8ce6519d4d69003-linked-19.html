MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyVoices that talk
  • The Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/11 Speech oscillator comes from the speech research behind the Speak & Spell toy. It produces vowels, numbers, letters and whole words. A toy voice saying words nobody chose is the most direct Halloween sound the instrument makes.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyAtmosphere5. Microfreak/Preset/012 Darkstream
  • Why it fits: a detuned Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/02 SuperWave one octave down, with a slow attack and a filter that a held envelope sweeps open. It swells in like strings in a horror score. Paraphonic mode lets you hold dark chords.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyAtmosphere6. Microfreak/Preset/105 SlowBurn
  • Why it fits: a Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/03 Wavetable through a high-pass filter with high resonance, a slow attack and a release that runs nearly as long as the instrument allows. A slow triangle LFO moves the timbre. Notes ring on after you let go, like an echo in an empty house.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyAtmosphere8. Microfreak/Preset/141 Digital Sea
  • Why it fits: a Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/13 Noise oscillator through a nearly closed band-pass filter, with a slow attack and a slow random LFO adjusting the cycling envelope. Filtered noise that rises and falls sounds like wind through a broken window.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyAtmosphere10. Microfreak/Preset/154 Old Tapes
  • Why it fits: Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/08 Two Op.FM through a band-pass filter, with a slow attack and a filtered random LFO on the cutoff. The band-pass thins the sound like a small speaker, and the random movement wobbles it like warped tape. An old song on a broken machine is a staple of ghost stories.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyOrgan and bells12. Microfreak/Preset/195 Night Organ
  • Why it fits: the Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/10 Chords oscillator plays a full chord from each key, with paraphonic mode on and a sine LFO adding a slight vibrato. A pipe organ in a minor key is the haunted-house sound most people know, from Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D minor.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyOrgan and bells13. Microfreak/Preset/017 Tubular
  • Why it fits: the Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/12 Modal oscillator models a struck resonating body, and this patch is tuned as tubular bells with a medium decay. The opening of Mike Oldfield's "Tubular Bells" became the theme of The Exorcist. Twelve strokes on one bell is also the midnight chime.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyBass and lead lines16. Microfreak/Preset/239 70s Dracula
  • Why it fits: a Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/16 HARM lead with a resonant low-pass filter, a short decay with no sustain, and paraphonic voices. Pressure adds noise to the oscillator. The name points at 1970s horror-film scores, and plucked resonant chords with breath-like noise fit that era.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyBass and lead lines17. Microfreak/Preset/248 GrowLing
  • Why it fits: a Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/15 SAWX through a band-pass filter, with the cycling envelope pushing both wave and timbre fully. Each note opens with a sweep that sounds like a growl.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyBass and lead lines18. Microfreak/Preset/050 Death
  • Why it fits: a Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/07 Waveshaper whose timbre is driven by both the cycling envelope and a clock-synced Sample-and-Hold LFO. The tone folds over itself in stepped, random patterns in time with the music, which sounds harsh and mechanical. Pressure shortens the envelope's fall.
MicroFreak presets for a Halloween partyVocoder voices
  • These need the gooseneck microphone of the Vocoder Edition, or a TRRS headset microphone in the headphone jack. Set Mic Gain in the Utility menu before the party. The Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc/03 Types/22 Vocoder oscillator puts your voice onto the synth's pitch, so you can speak to the room as a monster or a machine. See Microfreak/UG/19 Appendix B Vocoder.
Music/Scale/Blues
Blues ScaleQuirks
  • The MicroFreak manual lists its "Blues" setting as nine notes: C D E♭ F G♭ G A♭ A B♭. That collection is the hexatonic blues scale plus 2, ♭6 and 6, larger than any standard blues scale. The same manual's introduction to scales says blues scales use six notes. See Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales/01 Scale Settings and Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales.
Music/Scale/Dorian
Dorian ModeQuirks
  • The MicroFreak manual spells its "Dorian" and "Minor" settings almost identically; the "Minor" spelling is this scale with an added B natural. See Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales/01 Scale Settings.
Music/Scale/Dorian/Bebop
Bebop Dorian ScaleQuirks
  • Sources disagree on which note to add. The major-seventh form, C D E♭ F G A B♭ B, matches the MicroFreak manual's spelling of its "Minor" setting in Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales/01 Scale Settings.
Music/Scale/Major
Major ScaleUse
  • Its association with brightness or happiness is learned. The MicroFreak manual's chapter on scales says as much in Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales.
Music/Scale/Minor/Harmonic
Harmonic Minor ScaleQuirks
  • The MicroFreak manual lists its "Harmonic Minor" setting as C D E F G A♭ B. With E natural, that collection is harmonic major, Person/Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov's name for a major scale with a lowered sixth. The manual page is Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales/01 Scale Settings; which collection the firmware plays is not documented there.
Music/Scale/Minor/Natural
Natural Minor ScaleQuirks
  • The MicroFreak's "Minor" setting is listed in its manual as C D E♭ F G A B♭ B, which lacks A♭, the note that separates natural minor from Dorian, and adds B natural. See Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales/01 Scale Settings and Music/Scale/Dorian/Bebop. The Launchpad's "Natural Minor" setting carries this scale's name; its manual lists no pitches.

  • One of the last times I made music with the Launchpad and the Microfreak, I used the Launchpad/UG/09 Sequencer along with Launchpad/UG/05 Session Mode. The result was confusing and complex. I need to review the Launchpad sequencer and think more about when I'd want to use it vs the Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer. Having two sequencers around as well as the ability to loop anything in the session mode is rich in terms of a feature set, almost to a point of fault: there's too much flexibility and it's hard to know what to use for what. If only the Launchpad Sequencer was better synchronized with the Ableton/Live tempo.
