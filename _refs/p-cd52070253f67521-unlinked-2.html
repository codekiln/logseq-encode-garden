cybernetics
  • In the Republic and the Alcibiades, Plato uses kybernētikē, the steersman's art, as a picture for governing people. The steersman stands for someone who holds a course by continuous correction (Cybernetics, Wikipedia).
  • Norbert Wiener coined cybernetics with a research group that included Arturo Rosenblueth in the summer of 1947, and his 1948 book Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine put the word in print. He wrote that every existing term leaned too far toward one field, and that the ship's steering engine was among the oldest and best-developed feedback mechanisms.
  • The Josiah Macy, Jr. Foundation funded a run of meetings chaired by Warren McCulloch. Ross Ashby, Gregory Bateson, Heinz von Foerster, Margaret Mead, Person/John von Neumann and Wiener were among the participants, and mathematicians, neurophysiologists, anthropologists and engineers worked out a shared vocabulary of Feedback, information and circular causality (Macy conferences, Wikipedia). In Britain the Ratio Club did similar work over dinner from 1949 to 1958.
  • What kept the name turned toward biology, society and the observer. Heinz von Foerster's Biological Computer Laboratory at the University of Illinois (1958 to the mid-1970s) incubated second-order cybernetics, the cybernetics of observing systems, which puts the scientist inside the loop being described. Maturana and Varela's autopoiesis, Bateson's double bind and its use in family therapy, Stafford Beer's management cybernetics and Viable System Model, and Gordon Pask's conversation theory belong to this wave. Beer's Project Cybersyn in Chile (1971 to 1973) tried to steer a national economy on these principles.

Horcrux

  • Horcrux

    • Originally from Person/Joanne Rowling/Book/Series/Harry Potter, a Horcrux

      • was an object in which a dark wizard or witch had hidden a detached fragment of his or her soul in order to become immortal. As long as the receptacle remained intact, so too did the soul fragment inside it, keeping the maker anchored to the world of the living, even if their body suffered fatal damage.

    • In Harry Potter, Horcruxes are very evil; they were considered terrible dark magic and could only be created through an act of murder.
    • My Note
      • Have you ever known someone who placed so much importance on a particular object that if it were to be damaged or lost, it be as though that person had become damaged or lost? I have. The first time I read about Horcruxes in Harry Potter, it struck me as eerily familiar of the relationship between these people and their objects of power.
      • People imbue particular objects with their essence and map their identities onto them. Whether it's a wedding ring, a key to a chest, or a memento from a relative or a time in one's life or from one's ancestors, people create objects that mean more to them than the material they are made of. Not all of these are examples of what I would call Horcruxes, though.
      • I personally use the term Horcrux to refer to a thing that has an order of magnitude more significance to one person than to most other people because that person has done something to make that object a projection to them of their own identity. This is an uncommon interpretation of the word Horcrux.
      • For example, while the symbol of The Cross is highly related to Jesus Christ, I wouldn't call the cross a Horcrux. Christ didn't crucify himself to put his essence into the cross for immortality.
      • In another example, I wouldn't call an Olmec figurine a Horcrux. In the way I think about Horcruxes, they are related to the identity of a particular individual. They are like Person/Philip Pullman/Book/Series/His Dark Materials/Concept/Daemon.

Judaism/Gemara

  • Gemara

    • The rabbinic discussion of the Judaism/Mishnah, accumulated over centuries and recorded together with the Mishnah as the Judaism/Talmud.
    • The Gemara asks why a rabbi holds his position, which verse supports him, whether another Mishnah contradicts him, who the anonymous speaker is, and what unstated assumption would reconcile two statements. It keeps the dead ends, objections, hypothetical cases, minority positions and unresolved questions along with the conclusions.
Judaism/Mishnah

  • Mishnah

    • The early systematic written formulation of the Judaism/Torah/Oral, compiled around 200 CE under Person/Judah ha-Nasi.
    • It organizes Jewish law by subject, such as agriculture, festivals, marriage, civil law, sacrifices and purity, in place of a verse-by-verse commentary, and it records disagreement between named rabbis alongside the rulings. With the Judaism/Gemara it forms the Judaism/Talmud.
Judaism/Rabbinic

  • Rabbinic Judaism

    • The mainstream form of Judaism since late antiquity, built on the teaching of the rabbis and on the Judaism/Talmud as the authoritative interpretation of the Judaism/Torah.
    • It understands revelation as both the Judaism/Torah/Written and the Judaism/Torah/Oral, and it develops as a layered tradition in which each generation's interpretation becomes the next generation's text. Judaism/Concept/Layered Interpretation explains that structure.
Judaism/Talmud

  • Talmud

    • The Judaism/Mishnah together with the Judaism/Gemara, the central text of Judaism/Rabbinic.
    • Two Talmuds exist. The Jerusalem Talmud, also called the Palestinian Talmud, dates from roughly the fourth and fifth centuries. The Babylonian Talmud reached its received form in roughly the fifth and sixth centuries and became far more influential.
    • A traditional printed page places the Mishnah and Gemara at the center and surrounds them with Person/Rashi's commentary, the Tosafists' discussions, cross-references and later notes, so that one page shows generations of readers talking around one text. Judaism/Concept/Layered Interpretation describes the structure the page embodies.
Judaism/Torah

  • Torah

    • In the narrow sense, the five books of Moses: Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy. In the broad sense, Jewish teaching as a whole, so that studying the Judaism/Talmud counts as studying Torah.
    • The text commands without specifying. "Remember the Sabbath day and keep it holy" leaves open what counts as work, when the day begins and what may be carried, so interpretation starts at the first layer of the tradition. Judaism/Rabbinic distinguishes the Judaism/Torah/Written from the Judaism/Torah/Oral that accompanies it.
Judaism/Torah/Oral

  • Oral Torah

    • The interpretive tradition that Judaism/Rabbinic holds was revealed alongside the Judaism/Torah/Written and is needed to understand and practice it.
    • The Judaism/Mishnah wrote down part of it around 200 CE, and the Judaism/Talmud records the arguments over that writing. Because interpretation is built into the transmission of revelation, the chain of interpretation itself carries religious authority.
Judaism/Torah/Written

  • Written Torah

    • The written Pentateuch, the Judaism/Torah in its narrow sense, as Judaism/Rabbinic distinguishes it from the Judaism/Torah/Oral that is held to have been revealed with it.

LazyVim/Q/In LazyVim, how can I traverse wiki links in an Obsidian knowledge garden?

  • In LazyVim, how can I traverse wiki links in an Obsidian knowledge garden?

    • AI Answer

      • Short answer: the current dotfiles already enable LazyVim/plugins/extras/lang/markdown, which configures the Marksman language server. Open the garden from its root with nvim ., place the cursor inside a wiki link, and press gd. Use <C-o> to return to the previous location and <C-i> to move forward again. Press gr on a link to list references to its destination.
      • Why it works: LazyVim maps gd to the attached language server's “go to definition” action and gr to “references.” Marksman supports those actions for ordinary Markdown links and wiki links, including links to headings. Answer/Official from LazyVim's LSP documentation and the Marksman README.
      • Setup and checks:
        • 1. Start Neovim at the vault root: cd /path/to/vault && nvim ..
        • 2. Run :LazyExtras and check that lang.markdown is enabled. The current dotfiles already import this extra, and the installed Marksman reports version 2026-02-08. Answer/Official from LazyVim's Markdown extra.
        • 3. If gd does nothing, use <leader>cl to open LazyVim's LSP information and check that Marksman is attached to the Markdown buffer. :Mason shows whether the server is installed.
        • 4. If links work only within one file, make the vault a Marksman project by putting an empty .marksman.toml at its root or keeping it as a Git repository. This garden is already a Git repository. Answer/Official from the Marksman FAQ.
      • Optional Obsidian-specific navigation: obsidian.nvim adds :Obsidian follow_link, a smart action that can follow the link under the cursor, a picker for all links in the current note, and actions for moving the cursor to the next or previous valid link. It is useful when Marksman's gd and gr are too limited for the vault workflow.
