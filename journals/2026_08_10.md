- # The Cheat Sheet Doesn't Know Your Config
	- Ran a [[LazyVim]] cheat sheet ([thesujit on Cheatography](https://cheatography.com/thesujit/cheat-sheets/lazyvim-neovim/)) against what is actually installed, and the gap was mostly not in the keys — it was in the assumptions. The sheet documents `:Neotree` and the fzf-lua git pickers; neither plugin is here, so a third of its entries describe someone else's editor. The picker is [[nvim/Plugin/snacks.nvim]] all the way down, which is why `<leader>ss` — the symbol search every LazyVim sheet lists — simply is not bound.
	- Two entries were wrong rather than merely inapplicable, and those are the ones worth having as cards.
		- `<leader>sd` and `<leader>sD` are **reversed** from the sheet: lowercase is every diagnostic, uppercase is the buffer's.
		- In [[Lazygit]] `<ctrl+z>` is not redo — it suspends the app. Redo is `Z`. Confirmed against the v0.64 keybindings doc.
	- The sharper catch is one no sheet could carry: `config/autocmds.lua` disables diagnostics on `VeryLazy`, so `]d`, `[d`, `<leader>sd` and `<leader>sD` all do nothing until `<leader>ud` turns them on. Four keys that look broken and are merely waiting.
	- Also: with `wrap` on, LazyVim remaps `j`/`k` to `gj`/`gk` **only when no count is given**. A bare `j` moves by display line, `10j` moves ten real lines — the gutter and the keyboard measuring different things on the same paragraph.
