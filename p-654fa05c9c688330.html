  • Day 2: Major Lower Body Muscles - YouTube

    • Speaker: Person/Andrew Huberman
    • Channel: Andrew Huberman · 13:35 · unlisted
    • Book: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols, Chapter Two: Exercise

    • About

      • Leg day of the Chapter Two resistance training program: quadriceps, hamstrings, glutes, and calves. Each muscle group gets compound movements first, then isolation movements.
      • Sets, reps, and weights are in the book; the video shows form. A work set goes to failure, meaning the point where the movement can no longer be done in good form.

    • Video

        • 00:27 Back squat

          • Compound movement for the quadriceps. Work out stance width with light weights during the break-in weeks, squat below parallel, pause, and stand.

        • 01:07 Front squat

          • A variation for people who lack the ankle or shoulder flexibility to squat deep with the bar on their back.

        • 01:40 Hack squat

          • Test the machine's angle with little or no weight first. The sled locks the torso, so only the knee and hip move. Pick an angle and foot position that cause no joint pain.

        • 02:52 Rear leg elevated split squat

          • Also called the Bulgarian split squat; works with dumbbells alone, in a hotel or home gym. Standing farther from the bench emphasizes the quadriceps; standing closer leans the torso forward and shifts work to the glutes. Grip strength can limit the load.

        • 04:54 Other compound options

          • Huberman likes the belt squat, though most gyms don't have one.

        • 05:12 Leg extension

          • Isolation movement for the quadriceps. Start each rep by lifting the weight a centimeter so the muscle is under tension, squeeze at the top, and lower without setting the weight down between reps.

        • 06:18 Deadlift

          • Compound movement for the hamstrings, glutes, and lower back that also trains grip and core. Overhand grip, bar close to the shins, back flat; think of pushing the feet into the floor and hinging rather than yanking the bar up.

        • 07:39 Lying leg curl

          • Isolation movement for the hamstrings. Set the roller pad on the lower Achilles near the ankle. Keep continuous tension by stopping just short of touching the stack. Toe position changes which part of the hamstring works; experiment.

        • 09:38 Other hamstring options

          • Glute ham raise, which combines a deadlift-like hinge with a leg curl and takes some skill; standing leg curl.

        • 10:32 Calf raises

          • Huberman trains calves twice a week, once on leg day. Standing or angled calf raises probably cover both calf muscles; add seated calf raises if available. Knees locked, feet about shoulder width, full stretch at the bottom with the calves still active, then rise as high as possible.

        • 12:26 Single-leg dumbbell calf raise

          • Done on a step that isn't slippery, holding something lightly for balance. Very strong calves may outlast grip strength.
