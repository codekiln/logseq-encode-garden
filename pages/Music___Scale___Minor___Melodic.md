- # Melodic Minor Scale
	- Also called jazz minor and, in its ascending form, the acoustic collection. On C, ascending: C D E♭ F G A B. Steps in semitones: 2 1 2 2 2 2 1. The classical descending form is [[Music/Scale/Minor/Natural]].
	- ## History
		- The classical form records voice leading. Raising both sixth and seventh on the way up avoids the augmented second of [[Music/Scale/Minor/Harmonic]] while keeping the leading tone. Lowering both on the way down lets the line fall toward the dominant.
		- Baroque lines follow the harmony more than the direction. Over a dominant chord, Bach's descending lines keep the raised sixth and seventh.
		- [Twentieth-century]([[19]]) jazz theory uses the ascending form in both directions and treats each of its modes as a chord-scale. George Russell's *Lydian Chromatic Concept of Tonal Organization* ([[1953]]) brought Lydian-based thinking into that theory.
	- ## Use
		- Its modes cover much of jazz harmony beyond the diatonic system:
			- Mode 1, melodic minor on its own tonic, fits a minor-major seventh chord.
			- Mode 3, Lydian augmented, fits a major seventh chord with a sharp fifth.
			- Mode 4, Lydian dominant, fits a dominant seventh chord with a sharp eleventh, such as a tritone substitute.
			- Mode 6, Locrian ♮2, fits a half-diminished chord.
			- Mode 7, the altered scale or super Locrian, fits a dominant seventh chord with altered ninths and fifths.
	- ## Quirks
		- The fourth mode, C D E F♯ G A B♭ on C, approximates harmonics 8 through 14 of a fundamental on C. Writers on Bartók call it the acoustic or overtone scale for that reason.
		- The collection is one note away from both the major scale (lowered third) and Dorian (raised seventh).
