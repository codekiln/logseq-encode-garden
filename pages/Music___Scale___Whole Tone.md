- # Whole Tone Scale
	- Also Messiaen's first mode of limited transposition. On C: C D E F♯ G♯ A♯. Steps in semitones: 2 2 2 2 2 2.
	- ## History
		- Glinka used a descending whole-tone scale for the sorcerer Chernomor in *Ruslan and Lyudmila* (1842), and Russian composers after him kept it for the supernatural.
		- Debussy made it a harmonic field. "Voiles" (*Préludes*, Book I, 1909–10) stays on one whole-tone collection for most of its length and turns pentatonic in the middle.
		- Messiaen listed it first among his modes of limited transposition in *Technique de mon langage musical* (1944), and said he avoided it because Debussy and Dukas had used it so thoroughly.
	- ## Use
		- Impressionist harmony, and later film and television, use it for dreams, flashbacks and suspended time.
		- Jazz uses it over a dominant seventh chord with a ♯5 and 9 (C7♯5, C9♯5), and Thelonious Monk made descending whole-tone runs a signature.
	- ## Quirks
		- There are only two distinct whole-tone collections, the even and the odd pitch classes. Every rotation of the scale is the same scale, so it has no modes. On a device with root selection, the twelve roots produce only two sets of pitches.
		- It contains no perfect fifth, no leading tone and no minor or major triad; every triad built from it is augmented. No note can function as a tonic without help from outside the scale.
		- The French augmented sixth chord (A♭ C D F♯ in C) is a four-note subset of the scale, a pivot between functional and whole-tone harmony.
