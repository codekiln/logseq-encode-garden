- # Staccato
	- A note played staccato is cut short, so a small silence separates it from the next note. The line sounds detached, crisp and bouncing, more like taps or plucks than a sung phrase. The word is Italian for "detached".
	- Staccato shortens the sound, not the beat. Each note still starts on time, and silence fills out the rest of its written length. A staccato note often sounds for about half its written length.
	- Written as a dot above or below the notehead. A wedge or stroke marks staccatissimo, which is shorter still. Dots under a slur mark portato, also called mezzo-staccato, where the notes are only slightly separated.
	- The opposite is legato, where each note connects to the next without a gap.
	- On a synthesizer or sequencer, staccato comes from a short gate or from an amplitude envelope with no sustain.
		- The Staccato setting of [[Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/09 Span]] sets every note to half the shortest gap between note starts.
		- [[Microfreak/Sequence/Write a sequence]] gives staccato steps a 25% gate length.
		- [[Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/06 Cycling Envelope/03 Legato Options]] describes staccato playing on the keys as lifting the fingers before the next note.
