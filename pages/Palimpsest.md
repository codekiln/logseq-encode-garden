- # Palimpsest
	- ## Overview
		- A palimpsest is a writing surface reused after an earlier text has been erased or scraped away, while traces of the earlier writing remain. By extension, it describes something whose present form carries marks of earlier forms beneath it.
		- The image fits a knowledge garden when a page brings an original source together with later commentary: the source remains legible, while annotations add another layer of meaning.
	- ## Context
		- Medieval scribes reused parchment because preparing it was costly. Erasing the surface did not always remove the earlier text completely, leaving the older writing partly legible beneath the new text.
		- A literal manuscript palimpsest reuses one physical surface. A digital garden can preserve distinct source and commentary pages, then present them together as a layered reading.
	- ## In a knowledge garden
		- A source page can remain the record of what another garden says, while a local discussion page holds interpretation and response.
		- A [[Database/View]] can present the source and commentary together without making either layer stand in for the other. The combined view is palimpsestic in its reading experience, though the underlying digital pages are separate.
	- ## Source
		- [Palimpsest · French Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/palafittes/en/glossary/palimpsest) defines the manuscript form as reused parchment where the erased original remains partly readable.
