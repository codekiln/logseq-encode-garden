- # [Episode #152 ... The Frankfurt School - Walter Benjamin pt. 1](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/episode-152-the-frankfurt-school-walter-benjamin-pt-1/id659155419?i=1000513671114)
	- [[Person/Stephen West/Pod]] on [[Person/Walter Benjamin]] of the [[Frankfurt School]]. West starts with Benjamin's method, by way of *The Task of the Translator*, before reaching [[Person/Walter Benjamin/Essay/35/The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction]] and its idea of [aura]([[Person/Walter Benjamin/Essay/35/The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction/Aura]]).
	- ## The task of the translator
		- [01:16 — Walter Benjamin Begins With The Translator's Task](https://share.snipd.com/snip/3bb3a1c4-dc74-446f-b617-ff3278fc269f)
		- West argues that Benjamin's method has to come before his famous critiques of modernity and mechanical reproduction, and starts by asking what a translator's job is.
		- [02:29 — The Task of the Translator](https://share.snipd.com/snip/f180d9e3-4a6d-46d7-95b5-41c2e92dc872)
		- A translator does more than make a text accessible to a new audience; the work requires understanding what the art is and what it means.
		- [05:06 — Art's Purpose](https://share.snipd.com/snip/d5da12ad-2548-4f7a-adbc-8b738933a0ad)
		- Art is primarily the artist's expression, not something made for an audience, so a translator should not put the reader's ease ahead of the original.
	- ## Reproduction misses meaning
		- [07:35 — 1812 Overture Example](https://share.snipd.com/snip/ad346ed0-af69-4dbe-99af-3aef2259f2d4)
		- A note-perfect recreation of Tchaikovsky's *1812 Overture* would still not carry its full meaning; the raw data of a work is not all of it.
		- [08:08 — Perfect Reproduction Still Misses Art's Meaning](https://share.snipd.com/snip/7246aecc-d27c-4b32-9266-658a7f433d6a) ([second snip](https://share.snipd.com/snip/b321419b-a56c-42dc-9104-5f50fafbfd0e))
		- Meaning also depends on when, where and how the original was made and conveyed, which no reproduction of notes or words recovers.
		- [09:37 — Literary Criticism and Symbols](https://share.snipd.com/snip/cadae7ae-59ae-4868-bfd5-a73dccbc1d3b)
		- For Benjamin, literary criticism is about how we read the world, which is a collection of symbols we interpret constantly.
	- ## Photography and aura
		- [11:39 — Photography's Impact](https://share.snipd.com/snip/fb65cf9d-4b22-499f-8487-010de5bc3794)
		- Paintings were expensive and filtered through an artist; photographs made likeness cheap and direct, and changed how people see the world.
		- [13:10 — The Camera Cannot Gaze Back](https://share.snipd.com/snip/61b0241f-9995-4e6c-9f68-f8341bcc1184)
		- A photograph reproduces a face but not the presence of seeing someone who sees you back, or of standing in front of a landmark.
		- [13:38 — The Aura of Art](https://share.snipd.com/snip/c0da0c26-8bf7-49b1-93c8-7eb56146346e)
		- The aura is the unique presence of a moment in space and time, which reproduction loses.
		- [15:39 — Moon Photo Anecdote](https://share.snipd.com/snip/7c6ef54a-1a12-4dc3-ac10-cf5cb726d17a)
		- A photo of a beautiful moon comes out looking like a tennis ball: the experience does not survive the copy.
		- [16:44 — Photography and Distance](https://share.snipd.com/snip/63bbbe54-b7d2-48c5-ac7e-bdbec8dba995)
		- A photograph closes the distance between subject and object, and with it the aura, which changes what art is.
	- ## Technology outruns criticism
		- [18:38 — Modern Technology Requires New Critical Tools](https://share.snipd.com/snip/7d6a3215-c56d-4b4a-9bbd-58da27bac2c7)
		- [19:48 — Technology Dissolves The Aura Of Art](https://share.snipd.com/snip/828a8d8f-bb7e-4a85-8c30-73a85fe350ad)
		- Mass reproduction changed everyday experience so much that older cultural criticism stopped working; Benjamin compares it to grinding steel with a mortar and pestle.
		- [21:13 — Art in the Age of Reproducibility](https://share.snipd.com/snip/bbd3abaf-ae63-4d89-a514-216e0288769f)
		- [22:18 — Mass Reproduction Erases Our Uniqueness](https://share.snipd.com/snip/1d268345-b87e-4e59-9866-de5287d24ff2)
		- West extends the argument to people: selfies make our own likenesses infinitely reproducible, and we trade uniqueness for copies.
