- # Mark each page with the most specific type in each lineage
	- In the [[2026-10-01 Thu]] journal codekiln proposed that a page may be an instance of several unrelated entity types, and that a page carrying two types with the same "entity ancestry" is an anti-pattern. I read every parent and child pair under [[Logseq/Entity]]. The evidence does not disprove the hypothesis, with two refinements: it applies only to children that are kinds of their parent, and the rule that holds up forbids a type next to its own ancestor while allowing two sibling types. Three definition pages currently tell instances to do what the hypothesis forbids.
	- ## A child namespace means one of two things
		- A child segment under `Logseq/Entity/` sometimes names a kind of the parent and sometimes names a part of it.
		- **Is-a.** Every instance of the child is also an instance of the parent: [[Logseq/Entity/Article/Blog]], [[Logseq/Entity/Repo/Awesome]], the [[Logseq/Entity/Book/Section]] levels, [[Logseq/Entity/Person/Musician]], [[Logseq/Entity/Person/Knowledge Gardener]], [[Logseq/Entity/Book/User Guide]].
		- **Part-of.** The child is a different thing that belongs to the parent: [[Logseq/Entity/Podcast/Episode]] in a show, [[Logseq/Entity/Forum/Post]] in a forum, [[Logseq/Entity/Trade-Off/Component]] in a trade-off. [[Logseq/Entity/YouTube/Playlist]] runs the other way: a playlist contains videos.
		- Some child segments are only a namespace, because the parent has no definition page. [[Logseq/Entity/AI/Model]] is one.
		- The anti-pattern codekiln named applies to is-a children. For a part-of child, marking both is a different error. A page carrying [[Logseq/Entity/Podcast]] and [[Logseq/Entity/Podcast/Episode]] would claim to be a show and one of its episodes, and no single thing is both. Instance paths already express part-of: [[Hard Fork/Pod/26/09/A.I. Safety Goes Mainstream + a ‘Hard Fork’ Exit AMA]] files under [[Hard Fork/Pod]].
		- Today a reader learns which relation a child has only from its prose. A property on each child definition page would make the relation explicit and queryable, for example `extends::` pointing at Article on the Blog page and `part-of::` pointing at Podcast on the Episode page.
	- ## A name for entity ancestry
		- Candidates: *lineage*, *ancestry*, *type family*, *branch*, and *clade*, the biologist's word for an ancestor together with all its descendants.
		- I recommend **lineage**: the chain from a type up through its is-a parents to the topmost defined type. It is an ordinary word, it reads well in a rule ("two types in one lineage"), and it leaves out part-of children by definition, since an episode does not descend from a show. *Clade* is more exact, and few readers know it.
	- ## Siblings say two different things
		- Taken literally, "more than one entity with the same entity ancestry" also forbids a person who is both a musician and a knowledge gardener, since [[Logseq/Entity/Person/Musician]] and [[Logseq/Entity/Person/Knowledge Gardener]] share the Person lineage. Neither type implies the other, so marking both carries information.
		- The redundancy is between a type and its own ancestor: Musician already implies Person. The rule I would write is that **a page never carries a type together with one of that type's ancestors**. Put the other way, each page carries the most specific type it has in each lineage.
	- ## The garden currently tells instances to carry both
		- [[Logseq/Entity/Person/Knowledge Gardener]] and [[Logseq/Entity/Person/Musician]] each say "Frontmatter lists both types, Person first". [[Logseq/Entity/Book/User Guide]] marks a guide with both User Guide and Book, child first.
		- Eight instance pages follow those instructions: six musicians ([[Person/Daniel Lopatin]], [[Person/Eduardo Ramos]], [[Person/John Adams]], [[Person/John Chowning]], [[Person/Madonna]], [[Person/Steve Reich]]), the knowledge gardener [[Person/Chris Aldrich]], and the guide [[Microfreak/UG]].
		- Other children already work the way the hypothesis wants. [[Logseq/Entity/Book/Section]] says "The instance declares its most specific level". Blog and Awesome pages mark the child alone and say their shape is the same as the parent's.
		- The likely reason for marking both is backlinks: the backlinks of [[Logseq/Entity/Person]] list a musician only when the musician's page links Person directly. Moving to child-only markers would mean:
			- rewriting the three definition pages to mark the child alone;
			- changing `logseq-entity::` on the eight instance pages;
			- adding the lineage rule to [[Logseq/Entity]], together with the statement that an instance of a child is an instance of every ancestor;
			- listing all people with a query over every type in the Person lineage, because backlinks alone would miss the musicians.
		- None of that is done yet.
	- ## Where the Episode and YouTube rule went
		- [[Logseq/Entity]] says combination rules live on the primary type's definition page. The garden already bends this. [[Logseq/Entity/Concept]] is the primary type of every trade-off page, yet [[Logseq/Entity/Trade-Off]] holds the combined shape, and Concept's page says so. [[Logseq/Entity/Series]] holds its own combination rules whatever type comes first. In practice the rule sits on the page whose shape the combination changes most.
		- For an episode that is also a YouTube video, the rule is on [[Logseq/Entity/Podcast/Episode]], listed first, with a pointer from [[Logseq/Entity/YouTube]]. The episode is what codekiln listens to, and the recording is one of its sources.
		- Much of that rule is not about the combination. Video seconds as the clock, chapters as timestamp headings, and a collapsed transcript per chapter also describe [[1Password/YouTube/25/11/How 1Password Environments makes secrets management simple for developers]], which is not a podcast. Two parts depend on both types: mapping Snipd's audio time onto video time, and the episode belonging to a show. [[My/Principle/Simplify/Not every distinction is worth recording]] argues for moving the general video rules up to the YouTube page and keeping a short combination section on the episode page.
	- ## CSS and class inheritance
		- Class inheritance fits a lineage. A child definition page is a subclass: it inherits the parent's page shape and states only its deviations, which is [[My/Principle/Simplify/Don't Repeat Yourself DRY]] applied to definitions. Several unrelated markers on one page are [[Software/Inheritance/Multiple]], and "primary entity first" plays the part of Python's method resolution order: the listed order decides which rule wins when two types disagree.
		- CSS fits combinations. A page's markers act like an element's classes, `class="episode youtube"`. Rules written for `.episode` and for `.youtube` both apply. A rule written for `.episode.youtube` applies only when both classes are present, and it wins over either alone because it is more specific ([MDN on specificity](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Cascade/Specificity)). The combination needs no hierarchy.
		- CSS also has a separate inheritance along the document tree, where a child element takes some property values from its parent element ([MDN on inheritance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Cascade/Inheritance)). That matches part-of. An episode can take its `created-by::` and its subject from the show it files under without being a kind of show.
		- Put together: lineages resolve like classes, combinations resolve like compound selectors, and part-of containment supplies defaults like inherited CSS properties. Each of the three is already present in the garden in some form, so the grammar would name what exists.
	- ## Where a combination rule can live
		- **On the primary type's page.** This is the current rule. There is one place to look, and [[Logseq/Entity]] already states it. The cost: which type is primary is arbitrary for Episode and YouTube, and the rule is invisible from the other type's page unless that page carries a pointer.
		- **On a page for the combination**, the compound selector as its own definition, named like the `X/vs/Y` trade-off pages: `Logseq/Entity/Podcast/Episode/and/YouTube`. Each type page points to it. The cost: one more page per combination, and a third meaning for a child namespace segment beside is-a and part-of.
		- **On trait pages.** Reusable behavior such as timestamped video notes or frame transcription gets its own definition that any type can include: a YouTube video, a recorded talk, a course lecture. The cost: a new kind of definition page, and one more page for a reader to open.
		- I recommend the primary type's page with a pointer for now, since one page in the garden is both an episode and a video. A combination page earns its place when the section outgrows the type page or a second combination needs the same rules. Trait pages are worth trying first on the video-notes rules, which already apply beyond podcasts.
	- ## Mismatches
		- [[GitP]] and [[GitP/A]] are marked as episodes and describe a show.
		- No show page is marked with [[Logseq/Entity/Podcast]] yet. [[Hard Fork/Pod]], [[Latent Space/Pod]], and [[Person/Nathaniel Whittemore/Pod]] already have the shape.
	- ## Open questions
		- Should child definition pages declare `extends::` or `part-of::` in frontmatter?
		- Should the three definition pages and eight instance pages that mark parent and child move to child-only markers? If so, how should the garden list all people?
		- Should the general video-notes rules move from the episode page to [[Logseq/Entity/YouTube]]?
		- Should [[Logseq/Entity]] say that a combination rule lives on the page whose shape the combination changes most, as Trade-Off and Series already do?
		- Should [[GitP]] and [[GitP/A]] become [[Logseq/Entity/Podcast]] instances?
