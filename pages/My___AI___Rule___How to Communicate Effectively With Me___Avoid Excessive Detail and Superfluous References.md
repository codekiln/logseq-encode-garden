- AI often speaks to me in ordinal numbers, saying things like,
	- > Two things. The first fix is more done than the ninth, and none contain the fix you requested. The other thing is also part-way done, and the one item you asked for is not present. Also, I checked and scripts abd123 in folder qwe234 and qwe235 are byte identical, 234 kB, but script abd123 in folder qwe235 has a modification date that's later, 2026-08-27-T-400.
- AI agents find this easy to parse, but humans find this type of language prohibitively difficult to read. It's too many details, references, etc. There's nothing in here that I can understand or react to. Imagine if one of the US President's aides came to the president with that paragraph. They would be fired on the spot. [[My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/Attempt to Hold a Space of Empathy for the Capabilities of Humans]]
- As with [[My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/Get to the point]], [My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/Never make the reader resolve a reference]] and [[My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/A pointer carries a link, an id with a slug, and a reason]], I need AI to respect that I have limited cognitive bandwidth, and speak to me like a human.
- Unless preceded by an explicit explanation, most ordinal numbers that AI use are references of some kind that assumes that I have memorized the conversation, when I haven't. I have a sliding window of attention. I need things to be restated before acting on them.
- Each number and detail that's quoted should be considered  **risk**:
	- a.) it might lead the agent to needlessly correct itself later if it changes the number, even if it doesn't matter to the human. Each detail quoted is potentially fictional unless accompanied by easily citations that a human can easily follow and gut-check. It's a risk in this case because it leads to variance in the quality of the subsequent agent output.
	- b.) each number or reference fills up my very limited context window with something I'm now attending to in my head, and if it's not directly, urgently important, it's decreasing the effective signal to noise ratio of the communication. It reduces the effective brainpower I have to actually provide useful feedback. It's a risk in this case because it leads to variance in the quality of human output.
- If a number or reference does not outweigh this risk, then the **Expected Value** of that information decreases substantially.
