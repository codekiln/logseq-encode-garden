- # [SAE J3016 Levels of Driving Automation](https://www.sae.org/standards/content/j3016_202104/)
	- The standard that sorts self-driving cars into six levels, from 0 (no automation) to 5 (drives anywhere with no human). Regulators, carmakers and the press use these levels to describe what a car can do on its own.
	- ## Scope
		- The levels describe the driving automation system, not the car. One car can run at Level 2 on city streets and Level 3 in a highway traffic jam.
		- The key line falls between Level 2 and Level 3. Through Level 2 the human is driving and must watch the road at all times. From Level 3 up the system is driving while it is engaged.
	- ## Maintainer
		- [[SAE International]], jointly with ISO as ISO/SAE PAS 22736. The current revision is April 2021.
	- ## The levels
		- **Level 0 — No driving automation.** The system can warn or briefly intervene, as with automatic emergency braking or blind-spot warnings. The human drives.
		- **Level 1 — Driver assistance.** The system steers *or* controls speed, as with adaptive cruise control or lane centering alone. The human drives.
		- **Level 2 — Partial automation.** The system steers *and* controls speed together. The human must supervise constantly and take over at once.
			- [[Tesla]] Autopilot and Full Self-Driving (Supervised)
			- [[GM]] Super Cruise and [[Ford]] BlueCruise, which allow hands-off driving on mapped highways while a camera checks that the driver watches the road
		- **Level 3 — Conditional automation.** The system drives within a limited domain, such as a highway below a set speed. The human may look away but must take over when the system asks.
			- [[Mercedes-Benz]] Drive Pilot, the first Level 3 system certified in Germany and in the US states of California and Nevada
			- [[BMW]] Personal Pilot L3 on the 7 Series in Germany
		- **Level 4 — High automation.** The system drives with no human in a limited area or set of conditions, such as a mapped city. If something goes wrong it pulls over on its own. Most robotaxis sit here.
			- [[Waymo]], from [[Alphabet]], runs driverless rides in Phoenix, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Austin and other cities
			- [[Zoox]], from [[Amazon]], runs a purpose-built robotaxi with no steering wheel in Las Vegas, with a smaller public service in San Francisco
			- [[Baidu]] Apollo Go, [[Pony.ai]] and [[WeRide]] run driverless robotaxis in China
			- [[Cruise]], from [[GM]], ran driverless rides in San Francisco until GM shut the robotaxi business down in December 2024
		- **Level 5 — Full automation.** The system drives anywhere a human could, in all conditions. No production system has reached Level 5.
