	- ## Upgrade Ableton Live or make a DAW?
		- I am reconsidering my [earlier decision to keep Ableton Live]([[Music/Making/Log/26/10/27 Tue]]) and avoid the distraction of replacing it. I currently use [[Ableton/Live]] 11. A sale on the Live 12 upgrade, which would include Live 13, runs through the end of the year; I understand the discount is about 20%.
		- Live 12 would make some of my work easier: it has a newer JavaScript engine, and some Model Context Protocol integrations require Live 12 or later. I am lukewarm on the browser improvements. Live 13's keyboard-friendly device tray, with easier access to devices and copying between tracks, sounds compelling, but I am not desperate for the new features.
		- AI integration is the larger question. As I understand it, Live 13 has no official MCP, command-line tool, or API for the kind of integration I want. If Ableton does not provide one, I am unsure whether I want to keep using Live. An upgrade could add fuel to my creative life for a while, but it would not settle that question.
	- ## Building and making music
		- I like the DIY aesthetic: getting closer to a problem by making something that fits my needs. That preference has limits. I use [[LazyVim]] rather than build my own Neovim distribution because a shared tool benefits from many people's attention, while my own attention is limited.
		- I also know my pattern: I gain creative momentum, then start an ambitious technical project. The technical project absorbs the time I meant to spend making music, and eventually I have to stop. Building a digital audio workstation could repeat that pattern.
		- Live also gives me a shared language. At yesterday's synth meetup, I could explain my set layout to other musicians who knew Ableton. A DAW of my own might feel like paddling away from that community; I would have to explain both the setup and why I made it.
	- ## A DAW that can grow with me
		- My experience with [Logseq Gardener]([[Person/codekiln/GitHub/logseq-gardener]]) makes the alternative attractive. With AI agents, I have gradually built a faster, more customizable Rust-based static site generator around files I can commit to Git. The Ghost in the Patch Alpha site has a custom player, illuminations, and pages that explain or illustrate Ableton sets. I can keep shaping the tools as I discover what I want, and that control feels like a tailwind for creation.
		- The work took months and is still in progress; I have just started on a language server and want to interact with Fluxy through Neovim. Starting a DAW later would also postpone the long period in which it could grow with me.
		- I want to think ambitiously as AI capabilities improve. A solution scoped too narrowly today might become inflexible as newer models make larger projects possible. Building software with AI also shows how I construct things with AI, and can become part of my portfolio.
		- My DAW might take inspiration from Live but reflect different priorities: version control, hackability, clean APIs, and integration with other open, hackable tools. I am interested in a text-file-based DAW integrated with Logseq, a contemporary alternative to Csound.
		- I want a self-hosted system that lets me work without tracking which hard drive contains each asset, and a seamless experience across desktop and phone so I can edit outside. A private cloud is a possibility, though I would need to decide where to draw the line on infrastructure.
		- I could offer the software for free and explore an AGPL license or a Linux-first design. If hosting were involved, I would only need enough paying users to cover hosting or hardware costs. The design problem itself appeals to me: why pay someone else to carry the burden of a problem I am interested in solving?
