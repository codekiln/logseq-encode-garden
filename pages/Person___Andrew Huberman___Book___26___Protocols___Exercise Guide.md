- # Exercise Guide
	- Movement reference for [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols]], with demonstrations from the book's exercise videos and supplementary instructions for machine, cable, and core variations.
	- Choose a load and range that allow controlled repetitions. Start a new movement with light practice sets, and finish a set when another repetition would require losing control or changing the intended movement. Adjust the machine to fit your body before adding load.
	- ## Lower body
		- ### Back squat
		  id:: b76b82df-9f87-4138-bbfd-690eb59fd5a0
			- Set the rack hooks just below shoulder height and the safety bars below the bottom of your squat. Center the bar across the upper back, grip it securely, stand to unrack it, and take a short step back.
			- Brace your trunk, keep the whole foot planted, and bend hips and knees together. Lower through the deepest comfortable range you can control, then push the floor away to stand. Let your knees track in the direction of your toes.
			- Reduce the load or depth if your heels lift or your trunk position changes abruptly. A light goblet squat is a way to practice the pattern; the leg press below is a machine alternative.
			- Demonstration: [Huberman's back squat, 0:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CbEFgDFQBvk&t=27s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles]].
		- ### Leg press
		  id:: faa7a9b8-2ece-4daf-a95e-40b4f6d2113e
			- Adjust the seat so your back and pelvis stay supported, your heels sit flat on the plate, and the starting knee bend is comfortable. Hold the machine's handles and brace your trunk.
			- Press the platform away smoothly, stopping before the knees lock. Lower under control while keeping the pelvis against the pad. End the descent before the thighs compress your torso or the lower back rounds off the support.
			- Use less weight or a shorter range if your pelvis lifts. Check the release and safety stops with a light load before beginning a sled-style machine; machine mechanisms differ.
			- Source: [ACE's seated leg press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/154/seated-leg-press/).
		- ### Romanian deadlift
		  id:: 2869870c-1599-457a-ae33-ff9f0e0d0291
			- Begin standing with the bar or dumbbells in front of the thighs, feet about hip width apart, and knees slightly bent. Keep the weights close to the legs.
			- Push your hips backward as your torso inclines forward. Keep the knee bend modest and the spine steady. Stop when you feel hamstring tension and before your back position changes, then drive the feet into the floor and bring the hips forward to stand tall.
			- The weights need not reach the floor. Turning the movement into a deep squat or reaching lower by rounding the back changes the exercise. Practice an unloaded hip hinge or use lighter dumbbells to learn it.
			- Source: [ACE's Romanian deadlift](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/317/romanian-deadlift/).
		- ### Rear-foot-elevated split squat
		  id:: 33ea88d9-9c3e-49b5-8e54-fa197fdc97a3
			- Place the top of one foot on a stable low bench behind you and the other foot firmly on the floor. Begin without dumbbells, using light hand support for balance if needed.
			- Bend the front knee and hip to lower under control, then push through the front foot to stand. Find a stance in which the front heel stays down and the knee follows the toes. Keep the pelvis facing forward.
			- Lower the support or use a regular split squat with the rear foot on the floor if balance or hip position limits the movement. Add dumbbells only after both sides feel controlled; stance changes should follow comfort and the intended movement rather than a fixed distance from the bench.
			- Demonstration: [Huberman's split squat, 2:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CbEFgDFQBvk&t=172s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles]].
		- ### Trap-bar deadlift
		  id:: 1620a9f4-0ea1-4166-956b-ec8a149b8ff0
			- Stand in the center of the hex bar with feet about shoulder width apart. Bend at hips and knees to grasp the middle of both handles, with straight arms and feet flat.
			- Brace before the lift. Push into the floor and extend hips and knees together to stand tall. Keep the load balanced and the back position steady. Lower both sides to the floor under control, then reset before the next repetition.
			- Keep the same handle height between sessions when tracking progress. High handles shorten the range; a lighter load is another way to learn the lift. Avoid jerking the bar, letting the hips shoot upward before the shoulders, or leaning back at the top.
			- Technique source: [U.S. Army Fitness Test deadlift instructions](https://www.army.mil/aft/). Handle-height context: [Barbell Medicine's trap-bar guidance](https://forum.barbellmedicine.com/t/general-s-c-template-question/11830).
		- ### Standing calf raise
		  id:: 526ffd43-e635-4e9e-9ba3-573e013d59a4
			- Use a calf-raise machine or stand on a stable, nonslip step with light hand support. Place the balls of the feet securely on the surface and begin with the knees straight but not forced backward.
			- Lower the heels through a comfortable stretch, pause, then rise as high as you can without bouncing. Keep the ankles controlled and the same foot position throughout.
			- Begin on both feet with bodyweight if needed. A single-leg version increases the challenge; a dumbbell can add resistance once balance and range are consistent. Shorten the range if the Achilles or ankle is uncomfortable.
			- Demonstrations: [Huberman's calf raise, 10:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CbEFgDFQBvk&t=632s) and [single-leg version, 12:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CbEFgDFQBvk&t=746s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles]].
	- ## Upper body
		- ### Seated cable row
		  id:: 18f9208e-93ec-4dfd-8e0f-fad68278b000
			- Sit tall with feet supported, knees slightly bent, and a secure grip on the handle. Begin with arms extended and the cable already under light tension.
			- Pull the elbows back close to the ribs until the handle reaches the abdomen. Pause briefly, then extend the arms slowly. Keep the torso steady throughout the repetition.
			- Reduce the weight if you must rock backward, shrug, or curl the wrists to finish. A chest-supported row is an alternative when keeping the torso steady is the limiting factor.
			- Source: [ACE's seated cable row](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/48/seated-row/).
		- ### Machine chest press
		  id:: 4e220e16-e3e9-44a1-a955-b366f4b71534
			- Adjust the seat so the handles are at or slightly below shoulder height. Plant the feet and keep your back supported.
			- Press forward smoothly and straighten the arms without forcing the elbows into lockout. Return until the upper arms are beside the torso, keeping the movement controlled.
			- Avoid arching away from the backrest or letting the elbows travel far behind the body. Reduce the load or adjust the starting position if the machine pulls the shoulders into an uncomfortable stretch.
			- Source and video: [Mayo Clinic's chest press with a weight machine](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/multimedia/chest-press/vid-20084687).
		- ### Barbell bench press
		  id:: eb3b3bb5-c841-4409-8872-c3c90b20eacd
			- Set the bench and rack so you can unrack without reaching excessively. Use rack safeties or a capable spotter. Lie with feet planted, shoulders supported, and hands slightly wider than shoulder width; wrap the thumbs around the bar.
			- Lower the bar slowly toward the chest while keeping wrists stacked over forearms, then press upward. Keep the hips on the bench and avoid bouncing the bar off the chest.
			- A machine chest press is a substitute when a suitable rack or spotter is unavailable. Start with a light bar to establish a repeatable grip and bar path.
			- Demonstration: [Huberman's bench press, 0:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UEU6NYLRi6o&t=7s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles]]. Additional instructions: [ACE's barbell chest press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/5/chest-press/).
		- ### Pull-up and lat pulldown
		  id:: 8059bf28-2c8e-44dd-b1dd-b19312b070b9
			- For a pull-up, use an overhand grip and begin with the arms extended in a comfortable hanging position. Pull the chest toward the bar by driving the elbows down and back, then lower under control. Keep the movement free of swinging or kicking.
			- For a pulldown, secure the thighs under the pad and begin with the arms extended. Pull the handle toward the upper chest, then let the arms extend slowly. Keep the torso mostly upright and pull in front of the head.
			- Choose assistance or a pulldown weight that permits the whole intended range. A close underhand pulldown is the variation Huberman demonstrates; a comfortable neutral or overhand grip is another option when the equipment permits it.
			- Demonstrations: [pull-up, 6:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UEU6NYLRi6o&t=373s) and [pulldown, 6:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UEU6NYLRi6o&t=416s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles]].
		- ### Overhead press
		  id:: 53f5cffa-ccca-45e3-9cac-48f1aa160d92
			- Begin with the bar at the front of the shoulders, or dumbbells at shoulder height. Brace your trunk and keep the ribs over the pelvis. A seated bench with back support is an alternative to standing.
			- Press overhead through a comfortable range, allowing the head to move just enough for the bar to pass. Lower slowly to the start. Keep the forearms underneath the load.
			- Reduce the load if you need to lean backward or turn the lift into a push with the legs. A machine press or supported dumbbell press can simplify balance while you practice.
			- Demonstrations: [barbell press, 0:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IzpGEk-2th4&t=11s) and [dumbbell press, 0:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IzpGEk-2th4&t=52s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms]].
		- ### Chest-supported row
		  id:: 049e38c3-a344-485c-b6d1-f3a7d8b99371
			- On a rowing machine, adjust the chest pad so you can reach the handles with extended arms while the chest remains supported. Set the seat so the handle path is comfortable and the feet stay supported.
			- Brace, row the handles toward your ribs, pause, and extend the arms slowly. Keep your chest in contact with the pad instead of lifting away to finish a repetition.
			- For dumbbells, use an incline bench that supports the chest while leaving space for the arms to hang and row. Begin light; a different bench angle changes both the clearance and the line of pull.
			- Source for the supported machine version: [ACE's seated row with chest pad](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/168/seated-row/).
		- ### Biceps curl
		  id:: 7fdbdd6d-56fe-4045-8b16-c6fdd3f5f8bc
			- Stand or sit with a dumbbell in each hand, arms down, and trunk steady. Curl by bending the elbows; turn the palms upward if comfortable, then lower slowly.
			- Keep the upper arms mostly still and the wrists aligned with the forearms. Reduce the load if you must swing the torso or move the elbows far forward to finish.
			- A seated curl or cable curl can make the starting position easier to repeat. An EZ-bar is another grip option.
			- Demonstration: [Huberman's curl, 4:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IzpGEk-2th4&t=284s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms]].
		- ### Triceps pressdown
		  id:: f8ac83b4-f3f3-42cf-a950-ee2851c435a3
			- Attach a rope or bar to a high cable pulley. Stand with feet planted and knees softly bent, elbows beside the torso, and hands holding the attachment securely.
			- Extend the elbows to press the attachment down, then bend them slowly to return. Keep the upper arms in place and the shoulders quiet.
			- Avoid using bodyweight to shove the handle downward. Reduce the load if the elbows drift forward or the torso rocks. This is an elbow-extension exercise; Huberman's overhead cable extension uses a different shoulder position.
			- Source: [Mayo Clinic's Strength Training for Adults, triceps pushdown](https://cancereducation.mayoclinic.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/mc2972.pdf#page=12).
	- ## Core
		- ### Pallof press
		  id:: e31d0a13-b81d-48c5-9264-ff9bcee381a7
			- Set a cable or securely anchored band at chest height and stand sideways to its pull. Hold the handle at the chest with both hands, feet about hip width apart, and knees slightly bent.
			- Keep the chest and hips facing forward while pressing the hands straight ahead. Hold briefly, then return slowly. Repeat facing the other direction; resisting rotation is the task.
			- A practical starting dose is two sets of 8–12 presses per side with a two-second pause. Use less resistance or keep the hands closer to the chest if the torso turns or leans.
			- Source: [ACE's standing anti-rotation press](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/332/standing-anti-rotation-press/).
		- ### Forearm plank
		  id:: 70f2f0e8-2e18-4d87-b44b-0816895211a2
			- Place the elbows beneath the shoulders and extend the legs with toes on the floor. Brace the abdomen and lift into a steady line from head to heels while continuing to breathe.
			- Start with two holds of 20–30 seconds, ending sooner if the hips sag, rise, or the shoulders shrug. Shorter holds or an elevated support can make a stable position easier to maintain.
			- Increase the hold only while position and breathing remain steady. A longer hold with a sagging lower back is a different task from a controlled plank.
			- Sources: [ACE's front plank technique](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/32/front-plank/) and [ACE's bodyweight programming examples](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/september-2026/9193/body-weight-training-by-design-programming-for-every-fitness-goal/).
	- ## Mobility
		- These movements use a comfortable range and relaxed breathing. Ease out of a position that produces joint pain, tingling, or a sense of instability. Add range gradually over repeated sessions.
		- ### Sumo squat with rotation
		  id:: ce5d2c87-e68d-4b49-8b51-b6b982fd0b49
			- Take a wide stance with toes turned slightly outward. Lower into a comfortable squat with the feet grounded, then place the elbows lightly against the inner knees.
			- Keep one elbow supported while raising the other arm and turning the upper body to follow the hand with the eyes. Return to the center and change sides. Point diagonally upward if reaching the ceiling is uncomfortable.
			- Start with three slow turns per side, pausing briefly and breathing. Use a shallower squat or hold a stable support when the bottom position is difficult.
			- Demonstration: [Huberman's sumo squat with rotation, 0:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SzufA9jqrPw&t=28s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Mobility and Flexibility]].
		- ### Hip-flexor stretch
		  id:: e1158293-76af-42ca-9623-31b644f0d66a
			- In a split stance or half-kneeling lunge, place one leg behind the body and keep the pelvis facing forward. Use support for balance or padding under the rear knee.
			- Gently move the pelvis forward while keeping the trunk tall and the low back comfortable. Aim for a stretch at the front of the rear hip. Raising the same-side arm can add to the movement, provided the back remains controlled.
			- Begin with 10–30 seconds per side and repeat as comfortable. A shorter stance and smaller movement are useful starting versions. Avoid creating the stretch only by arching the lower back.
			- Demonstration: [Huberman's hip-extension stretch, 2:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SzufA9jqrPw&t=135s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Mobility and Flexibility]].
		- ### Reverse tabletop
		  id:: 19b12018-cf0d-4e22-92f8-68e3d9f7c583
			- Sit with knees bent, feet flat, and hands behind the hips, fingers pointing outward. Press through hands and feet to raise the hips toward a tabletop position.
			- Keep the neck comfortable and breathe. Lift only as high as your shoulders and wrists comfortably allow; begin with short holds and lower under control.
			- Work toward a comfortable ten-second hold before trying the video's further shoulder-extension progressions. Keep a lower hip position or a shorter hold if full height is not comfortable.
			- Demonstration: [Huberman's reverse tabletop, 3:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SzufA9jqrPw&t=221s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Mobility and Flexibility]].
		- ### Founder pose
		  id:: 652f98d7-20d4-41f8-849d-75b5e2eb382e
			- Stand with feet a little wider than shoulder width and knees softly bent. Hinge at the hips so the hips move backward and the torso inclines forward.
			- Lengthen through the spine, lift the chest, and raise the arms only as far as you can while keeping the position comfortable. Breathe and exhale slowly, then return to standing.
			- Begin with a shallow hinge and a few slow breaths. Keep the arms lower if raising them makes the back arch or the shoulders uncomfortable.
			- Demonstration: [Huberman's Founder pose, 5:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SzufA9jqrPw&t=315s). Full notes: [[Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Mobility and Flexibility]].
