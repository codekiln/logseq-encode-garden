- # Cultural Anthropology
	- ## Overview
		- Cultural anthropology studies how people make meaning and organize social life across communities and historical settings. It examines practices, institutions, values, relationships, and the ways people describe their own worlds.
		- The American Anthropological Association describes cultural anthropology as one of the discipline's four fields and emphasizes the study of how people use language, make meaning, and organize social action.
	- ## Approach
		- Cultural anthropologists observe communities in context and attend to the accounts people give of their own practices. Methods vary by question and community; no single method represents the whole field.
		- Cultural explanations are strongest when they attend to differences within a community as well as differences between communities. Culture is learned and contested, not a fixed trait shared identically by every member of a group.
	- ## Related concepts
		- Questions about borrowing, ownership, power, and unequal exchange between communities connect cultural anthropology to [[Cultur/al/Appropriation]].
