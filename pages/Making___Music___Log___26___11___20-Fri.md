	- I practiced [[Piano]] for an upcoming event today for the first time in a long time. It was like coming home. There's something so special about the state of attention that comes from inventing practice techniques for 3+ hours. Sitting with the piano, there's nothing else. Everything else dissolves. It's a deeper channel of music for me. That part of my brain goes way back.
	- The connection to making sounds with synths and organs goes way back for me, too; some of my earliest memories are playing organ and synth as well as piano. Organ and synth has always let me explore the space of timbre. Piano lets me explore the space of structure and articulation.
	- I'm nervous that playing piano will take me away from my newly rediscovered practice of synth. It feels comparatively more nascent and in need of nurturing. The event is in just under two weeks. If I go for that long without making any synth music, it could knock my balance off.
	- Part of the problem is that I have more experience with the phases of piano practice. There's warmups, technique practice, sight reading practice, slow detail practice, memorization practice, performance practice. Most of these are piano-bound, though a few, like listening to performances, is not.
	- With synth, these forms aren't as well developed, at least, they aren't as mature in my practice. There's configuring [[Ableton/Live/Set]]s, there's tweaking [[Launchpad/Custom]] modes, there's exploration of how sounds work in [[Microfreak/Preset]]s, there's trying to get to know a workflow that uses the [[Launchpad/Sequencer]] more intuitively, there's asking and researching questions about my equipment, there's capturing samples, processing samples, there's writing software to facilitate various parts of the musical process. So many parts of these are screen-bound and provide me with many opportunities for distraction. If the piano is good at distracting me, it's because I want to make more different kinds of music with it. The synth's relationship to the computer is the opposite. The ways in which it's good at distracting me lead me farther from making music.
	- I think I might need a more organized sense of when to do instrument building vs when to do music improv vs when to do set building. I'm into the style of "composition" that inolves constructing the instrument for improvisation and a structure for the performance. That involves all of these styles of "practice," and more.
	- One thing that has been confusing me a bit lately is whether to continue using the four colors of the [[Launchpad/Sequencer]] to structure my [[Ableton/Live/Set]]s. I think I might want to make some [[Launchpad/Custom]] modes that send sysex messages to set a specific preset to be one that I know. I might also want to make a few custom modes broken into quadrants for certain colors, when have each quadrant represent pads, sfx, etc. I've also been thinking about making a few innstruments that use the built-in devices in Ableton, maybe a few drum racks I've customized.
