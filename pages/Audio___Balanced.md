- # Balanced Audio
	- A way of sending one audio signal down two conductors plus a shield, so that noise picked up along the cable cancels out at the receiving end.
	- The receiver amplifies the difference between hot and cold. Hum and interference reach both conductors equally, so they drop out in the subtraction, which is why balanced lines can run long distances.
	- **Fully balanced** — the output drives hot and cold with opposite-polarity copies of the signal.
	- **Impedance-balanced** — the output drives hot only and ties cold to ground through a matching impedance. The noise rejection is the same, and the level is 6 dB lower than a fully balanced output.
	- Connectors with three contacts carry it: [[Audio/Connector/XLR]] and [[Audio/Connector/Phone/TRS/6.35mm]]. A TS cable makes the run unbalanced.
