- # Where does the phrase "the bane of my existence" come from?
	- ## [[AI Answer]]
		- Short answer: **bane** is an Old English word for "killer"; the phrase is that word stretched from a literal slayer to a chronic annoyance. The first documented use of the full idiom is not settled.
		- **Old English `bana`** meant "killer, slayer, murderer, a worker of death", whether a person, an animal or a thing. It descends from Proto-Germanic `*banon`, and is cognate with Old Norse `bani` ("death") and Gothic `banja` ("stroke, wound"). ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/bane))
		- **Poison sense** — "bane" also came to mean deadly poison, which survives in plant names like wolfsbane and henbane. ([Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/bane), [Today I Found Out](https://www.todayifoundout.com/index.php/2014/02/bane/))
		- **Softening to "ruin"** — by the 1570s "bane" meant "that which causes woe or ruin", no longer requiring a death. ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/bane))
		- **"Of my existence"** — an intensifier that makes the annoyance the ruin of one's whole life, said with humor. Saying "the bane of my existence" about a printer is a mock-tragic use of a word that once named a murderer.
		- Related: **baneful** ("harmful") comes from the same root. ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/bane))
		- Open: the sources above trace the word, not the phrase. A dated first citation for "bane of my existence" would need the Oxford English Dictionary or a corpus search.
