- # [Frankfurt School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_School)
	- A school of social theory and philosophy associated with the Institute for Social Research, founded on 3 February 1923 at the University of Frankfurt am Main during the Weimar Republic. Its tradition is known as critical theory.
	- ## Shared commitments
		- A commitment to human emancipation.
		- An attempt to combine the Marxist tradition, psychoanalysis and empirical sociological research.
		- Dissatisfaction with existing social theory, which its members held could not explain fascism and reactionary politics in liberal capitalist societies, and criticism of Marxism–Leninism as an inflexible system.
	- ## People
		- First generation: Max Horkheimer, Theodor Adorno, [[Person/Walter Benjamin]], Erich Fromm, Wilhelm Reich and Herbert Marcuse.
		- Later: Jürgen Habermas.
