- # Why does [[iPhone Mirroring]] time out connecting?
	- ## [[AI Answer]]
		- [[Answer/Official]] from [iPhone Mirroring: Use your iPhone from your Mac – Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120421)
		- **Short answer:** the Mac found the iPhone but couldn't finish the wireless connection. Usually one of the requirements below isn't met, or something else is using Bluetooth or Wi-Fi.
		- **Requirements Apple lists:**
			- The iPhone is locked and near the Mac. It can be charging or in StandBy.
			- Both devices have Bluetooth and Wi-Fi on and are signed in to the same Apple Account with two-factor authentication.
			- The Mac isn't sharing its internet connection or using AirPlay or Sidecar.
			- Only one Mac and one iPhone can mirror at a time.
		- **Apple's steps when the connection is interrupted or times out:**
			- Follow any instruction shown in the iPhone Mirroring window.
			- Install software updates on both devices and restart both.
			- Stop any heavy Bluetooth or Wi-Fi use on either device, such as streaming audio or video to another device, and check for wireless interference.
			- Check for VPN and other third-party security software on both devices. The Mac firewall must not block all incoming connections.
			- Revoke access to the iPhone in iPhone Mirroring settings, then set it up again.
			- As a last resort, sign out of the Apple Account on each device, restart, then sign back in.
		- **Not from Apple's page:** the screen streams over a direct Wi-Fi link between the devices, the same kind AirDrop uses. Office, campus and hotel Wi-Fi that isolates devices from each other can stop that link. If it fails on only one network, suspect that network.
		- Apple also notes that mirroring pauses after a period of inactivity, and unlocking the iPhone ends the session.
