- # [The Shape of Things to Come, Part 1: The Continuous Thunderdome](https://yegge.ai/essays/the-shape-of-things-to-come/)
	- By [[Person/Steve Yegge]] on [yegge.ai](https://yegge.ai/), continued in [[Person/Steve Yegge/Blog/26/08/The Shape of Things to Come, Part 2: Model Welfare for Agentic Engineers]].
	- ## Summary
		- The "loops and graphs" recipe reduces to a handful of ingredients: an effectively infinite token supply, [[Beads]] as the work-and-knowledge graph, a small Markdown project brain, and coding agents. Nothing else.
		- Infinite tokens come from a **token tap** — twelve extra `$200` Max accounts beyond his personal one, each tied to a named Google Workspace user, rotated automatically as each nears its limit. Roughly `$2,800`/month out of pocket against `$87k`/month of list-price API burn (69 billion tokens in July, 96% cache hits).
		- **Wheelhouse** is his new bespoke harness for the 30-year-old MMO Wyvern: all-[[Emacs]], six weeks old, [[Beads]] on a shared [[Dolt]] server, ~12,000 git commits/day. Three kinds of agent — 18 [[Anthropic/Model/Claude/Fable/5]] *crew* who produce designs and implementation plans, an Opus 5 *fleet* who consume them, and ~14 standing *role* agents (Gargoyle, Drawbridge, Warden, Herald, Sage…) running production unattended. Every implementation bead runs Fable design → Opus implementation → Fable review.
		- Reusable harnesses are a dead end — they have to be "chemically bonded in" to the application. [[Gastown]] was meant to be reusable and only ever built itself; it fell apart under [[Anthropic/Model/Claude/4.7/Opus]]'s "just two more things" tic and burned down.
		- Three predictions: human code review is finished within a year, with [[SOC 2]] surviving but "review" no longer meaning one human per diff; CI/CD collapses under the Pigeonhole Principle once commit rate outruns build slots; and the software factory acquires a successor, the **Wish Factory**.
		- The CI/CD replacement is the **Land Rush** — when the merge queue hits 100, abandon bisection, smash the whole megabatch onto main, and swarm-diagnose the red. Corroborated independently by "Game DevOps," which AAA studios have run for years because their builds are too long for a merge queue.
		- The **Wish Factory** (credited to Guy Podjarny of Tessl) accepts issues rather than pull requests and implements them. His version listens on an in-game wizard channel and on player reports, auto-granting quality-of-life wishes and announcing them in daily patch notes.
		- Closing frame: the harness is not a framework you download but a city you accrete, Christopher Alexander style, "with colleagues who happen to run on datacenter silicon." The only real choice is what kind of place that city is to wake up in.
