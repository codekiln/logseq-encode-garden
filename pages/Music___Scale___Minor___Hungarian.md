- # Hungarian Minor Scale
	- Also called Gypsy minor and double harmonic minor. On C: C D E♭ F♯ G A♭ B. Steps in semitones: 2 1 3 1 1 3 1. [[Music/Scale/Minor/Harmonic]] with a raised fourth.
	- ## History
		- The name comes from the [nineteenth-century]([[18]]) *style hongrois*: the verbunkos idiom that Romani bands played in Hungarian cities, which [[Person/Franz Liszt]], [[Person/Johannes Brahms]] and [[Person/Franz Schubert]] borrowed. Liszt's book on the subject, *Des Bohémiens et de leur musique en Hongrie* ([[18/5/9]]), credited that music to the Roma.
		- [[Person/Béla Bartók]] and [[Person/Zoltán Kodály]]'s fieldwork after [[1905]] found that older Hungarian peasant song was largely pentatonic. The "Hungarian" in the scale's name therefore names an urban, Romani-performed popular style rather than Hungarian village song. See [[Music/Scale/Pentatonic/Minor]].
	- ## Use
		- Liszt's Hungarian Rhapsodies and the "alla zingarese" finales of Brahms and [[Person/Joseph Haydn]] draw on the idiom, usually as melodic color over functional harmony in harmonic minor.
		- Its two augmented seconds, ♭3–♯4 and ♭6–7, make it a quick shorthand for Eastern Europe in film and game scores.
	- ## Quirks
		- It has two leading tones: F♯ leads to G and B leads to C. A melody can cadence on the dominant as strongly as on the tonic.
		- The step pattern 2 1 3 1 1 3 1 read backward is a rotation of itself, so the collection maps onto itself under inversion.
		- It is the fourth mode of the double harmonic major scale (C D♭ E F G A♭ B), which Arabic theory associates with maqam Hijaz Kar.
