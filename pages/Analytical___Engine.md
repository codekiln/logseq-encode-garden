- # Analytical Engine
	- ## Overview
		- A general-purpose mechanical computer designed by [[Person/Charles Babbage]], first described in [[18/3/7]]. It was never built.
	- ## Mechanism
		- The *mill* did arithmetic and the *store* held numbers, the same split later computers make between processor and memory.
		- Programs and data went in on punched cards, borrowed from the Jacquard loom that wove patterns into cloth.
		- The design allowed loops and conditional branching, which is what makes it general-purpose rather than a calculator.
	- ## Context
		- It followed Babbage's Difference Engine, which could only compute tables of polynomial values.
		- [[Person/Ada Lovelace]]'s 1843 notes describe what it could do, including a program for Bernoulli numbers. She wrote that it "weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard-loom weaves flowers and leaves."
		- Babbage's son Henry built part of the mill around 1910. No complete engine has been built.
