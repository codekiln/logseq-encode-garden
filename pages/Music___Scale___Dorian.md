- # Dorian Mode
	- Also called the protus authentic mode or Mode 1. On C: C D E♭ F G A B♭. Steps in semitones: 2 1 2 2 2 1 2. The second mode of [[Music/Scale/Major]].
	- ## History
		- The name is Greek but the scale is not. The ancient Greek Dorian octave species ran from E to E in modern terms, the collection now called Phrygian. Carolingian theorists, most visibly the anonymous *Alia musica* around [[9/0/0]], attached the Greek names to the church modes in a different order.
		- In plainchant and Renaissance polyphony, the D mode was defined by its final, its range and its reciting tone A. B♭ was common, so much Dorian music sounds Aeolian to modern ears.
		- Baroque composers often wrote D minor with no key signature, a habit inherited from Dorian notation. [[Person/Johann Sebastian Bach]]'s Toccata and Fugue in D minor BWV 538 is called "Dorian" for that reason alone: its notation has no flat, and its music is in D minor.
	- ## Use
		- Modal jazz made Dorian a vamp scale. [[Person/Miles Davis]]'s "So What" (*Kind of Blue*, [[1959]]) sits on D Dorian with a bridge in E♭ Dorian, and [[Person/John Coltrane]]'s "Impressions" uses the same form.
		- The i–IV vamp, a minor tonic with a major subdominant, is Dorian's signature in rock and Latin music, as in [[Person/Tito Puente]]'s "Oye Como Va" and the rock band Santana's cover of it, with [[Person/Carlos Santana]] on guitar.
		- English and Celtic folk melodies, among them "Scarborough Fair", are often Dorian.
		- In jazz chord-scale theory it is the default scale for a minor seventh chord functioning as ii.
	- ## Quirks
		- Its step pattern is a palindrome, so Dorian is the only diatonic mode that inverts to itself about its tonic. D is the center of symmetry of the white-key collection.
		- The raised sixth makes IV a major triad and puts a tritone between ♭3 and 6. That raised sixth is the note a listener uses to tell Dorian from Aeolian.
		- [[Music/Scale/Dorian/Ukrainian]] and [[Music/Scale/Dorian/Bebop]] carry Dorian in their names. Ukrainian Dorian derives from harmonic minor, and Bebop Dorian adds a chromatic passing tone.
		- The MicroFreak manual spells its "Dorian" and "Minor" settings almost identically; the "Minor" spelling is this scale with an added B natural. See [[Microfreak/UG/15 Using Scales/01 Scale Settings]].
