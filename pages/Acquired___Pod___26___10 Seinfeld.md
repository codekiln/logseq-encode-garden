- # [Seinfeld](https://www.acquired.fm/episodes/seinfeld)
	- Acquired, Fall 2026, episode 3. Ben Gilbert and David Rosenthal trace how *Seinfeld* went from Jerry Seinfeld's stand-up career through NBC's doubts to a television asset worth billions. The episode runs nearly four hours.
	- ## Practice and feedback in stand-up
		- [08:03 — Stand-Up Gives Instant Brutal Feedback](https://share.snipd.com/snip/0ef99630-2d1a-4802-a688-46694d995041)
		- Rosenthal describes Seinfeld taping and analyzing his own sets night after night. Gilbert points out that stand-up gives a performer a real-time score from the audience on material entirely in their control.
		- [11:26 — Jerry Seinfeld Trained Like an Olympian for Carson](https://share.snipd.com/snip/c89d9dc0-3725-4db7-ac28-1da36e69fd60)
		- Before his 1981 *Tonight Show* debut, Seinfeld performed the five-minute set about 200 times in clubs.
		- [12:27 — Jerry Seinfeld Stayed Relevant By Writing Every Day](https://share.snipd.com/snip/d5449ca5-c23c-4f9d-9d73-3168de3d24a9)
		- Gilbert says Seinfeld watched comedians stop being invited back once they ran through their material, and took up writing for hours every day on legal pads.
	- ## The HBR interview
		- Rosenthal reads the McKinsey exchange from Seinfeld's 2017 *Harvard Business Review* interview, which ends "The right way is the hard way." Gilbert connects the line to what Seinfeld learned on Carson: without the daily grind of writing and rewriting, a comic runs out of material.
		- [[Person/Cal Newport/Pod/26/10 Do Hard Things]] picks up the same reading.
