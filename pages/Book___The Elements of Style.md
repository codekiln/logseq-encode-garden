- # The Elements of Style
	- ## About
		- [[Person/William Strunk Jr]] printed the book privately in 1918 for the English students he taught at Cornell; a trade edition followed in 1920.
		- [[Person/E. B. White]], who had taken Strunk's course as an undergraduate, revised and extended it in 1959. **That is the edition people mean when they say Strunk and White**, and it carries White's own closing chapter, "An Approach to Style."
		- The book is organised as lists: elementary rules of usage, principles of composition, matters of form, and words and expressions commonly misused.
	- ## Why it is here
		- [[My/AI/Rule/How to Communicate Effectively With Me/Talk to me like a friend, not in vague aphorisms]] names this book as one of two tests a phrase should pass before it is written.
		- [[Book/Clear and Simple as the Truth]] is written against books of this kind. Thomas and Turner argue that rules of grammar, sense and sentence structure do not by themselves produce effective prose, and that step-by-step programmes make the problem worse by ignoring that there are several styles with different standards. What they offer instead is a stance the writer takes toward truth, reader and occasion.
		- The disagreement is about what a book on writing can supply: a set of prescriptions to check against, or a position to write from. For anyone deciding what to give an AI agent under [[My/AI/Rule]], that difference is more useful than either book on its own.
