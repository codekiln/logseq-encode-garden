- # Risk vs Reward
	- ## Summary
		- Every choice that could pay off could also go wrong, and the two arrive together: the options with the largest upside carry the widest spread of outcomes, and the options with the narrowest spread pay the least. Weighing risk against reward means deciding how much uncertainty to accept in exchange for how much expected gain. The answer turns on how much a loss would cost, whether it could be recovered from, and how many more times the same choice will come around.
		- The tension appears in investing, where it is stated as a premium demanded for bearing volatility; in engineering, where a faster or cheaper design carries more ways to fail; in choosing tools and dependencies, where capability comes with surface area; and in personal decisions about what to publish, share or keep private.

	- ## How the balance is struck
		- Size the bet by the worst case and the ambition by the best case. A loss that would end the game is never worth its expected value; a loss that is small and recoverable can be taken many times over.
		- Prefer choices whose risk is legible. [[Risk/Profile]] argues that a small, well-understood set of risks beats a larger set that is individually mild and collectively opaque.
		- Repeated choices favor the higher expected value. A one-shot choice favors the narrower spread.
		- Separate the risk that is paid for from the risk that is not. Exposure that could be avoided or spread out adds variance with no added reward.
