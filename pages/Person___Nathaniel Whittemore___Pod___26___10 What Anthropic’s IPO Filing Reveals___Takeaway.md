- # Unexpected Model Behavior and Precaution
	- Source: [[Person/Nathaniel Whittemore/Pod/26/10 What Anthropic’s IPO Filing Reveals]].
	- ## Response
		- I heard this discussed on [The AI Daily Brief — snipped audio and transcript, 09:06](https://share.snipd.com/snip/79f5a393-c58d-4128-b303-33278bc704e1). The discussion included substantial skepticism about the interpretation: this particular incident may have been a rare anomaly or a training artifact, and the evidence does not establish a deliberate backdoor.
		- My takeaway is that the explanation for this incident and the broader possibility are separate questions. Ever since DeepSeek became a thing, I’ve been considering whether open-weight models could be tainted and become a vector for exploitation and influence campaigns.
		- We will eventually need to operate as though that risk is real.
		- I think it’s quite possible, perhaps likely, that the U.S. government will eventually require government agencies and recipients of government funding to establish the provenance of any open-weight models those institutions use, including whether their training sources are approved. I’m thinking of something analogous to how accessibility requirements apply through the ADA and standards such as WCAG. That’s a prediction about where policy could go. Satya Nadella’s push toward model sovereignty and Microsoft’s move into open-weight models for fine-tuning look to me like signals in that direction.
