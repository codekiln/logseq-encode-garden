- # [Pantalaimon](https://truth-secrets-and-prophecies.fandom.com/wiki/Pantalaimon)
	- ## Overview
		- **Pantalaimon** (Pan) is the dæmon of [[Person/Philip Pullman/Book/Series/His Dark Materials/Character/Lyra Belacqua]] — the animal form of her inner self, able to speak, advise, and change shape until she grows up.
		- It matters because [[Person/Philip Pullman/Book/Series/His Dark Materials]] treats a person as something that can walk beside you: identity made visible, answerable, and able to settle.
	- ## Context
		- In Lyra's world a dæmon is a person's nature made external as an animal, usually of the opposite sex. Children's dæmons shift form; at adulthood they *settle* into the animal that fits the person's character.
		- Pullman's pictures for the idea include portraits where a sitter and a small animal share a psychological link, and Socrates' *daimon* — an inner voice given a body.
		- This garden already reaches for Pan when talking about a [[Horcrux]]: both are ways a person's essence lives outside the body. A Horcrux hides a torn fragment in an object. Pantalaimon *is* the person, walking and talking beside her.
	- ## Key Principles
		- **Companion-soul** — Pan is Lyra's dearest companion and a cautious counterweight to her impulsiveness; they are one life in two bodies, not a pet and its owner.
		- **Settling is coming of age** — form becomes fixed as identity does. Pan settles as a red-gold pine marten with a cream-white throat, grown from the ermine he favored as a child.
		- **Distance is injury** — a human and dæmon must stay near each other; forced separation is agony, and far enough apart it kills. Witches, shamans, and (after the Land of the Dead) Lyra and Pan can travel farther because they have already survived that tearing.
	- ## Mechanism
		- The name is Greek *Panteleimon*, "all-compassionate." He first appears as a dark brown moth; favored forms include a snow-white ermine, a moth, a wildcat, and a mouse.
		- He finds his settled form at the end of the trilogy when Will Parry touches him. After Lyra leaves him to enter the Land of the Dead, they can move far apart — an ability most people in her world never have.
		- For a human to touch another person's dæmon is taboo; dæmons may touch one another, and those contacts show the relationship between the humans.
	- ## Examples
		- [[Person/Philip Pullman/Book/Series/His Dark Materials/Character/Lyra Belacqua]] and Pan through the three books, from moth and ermine to pine marten.
		- [[Horcrux]] — the garden's other picture of identity stored outside the body; murder-made fragment versus living companion.
	- ## Misconceptions
		- "A dæmon is a familiar or a pet" — **False**; it is the person externalized. Kill the dæmon and the human dies; when the human dies the dæmon fades.
		- "Pantalaimon is a separate character who happens to travel with Lyra" — **Oversimplified**; they argue and can be parted, but they are not two people who met.
