- # Law of Demeter
	- **Statement**: a method may call methods only on itself, its parameters, objects it creates, and its own direct component objects — never reach through one object to act on something that object merely holds. [Law of Demeter (General Formulation)](https://www2.ccs.neu.edu/research/demeter/demeter-method/LawOfDemeter/general-formulation.html): *"Only talk to your immediate friends"* / *"Each unit should only talk to its friends; Don't talk to strangers."*
	- **Origin**: discovered at Northeastern University in the fall of 1987 by Ian Holland, while working on the Demeter Project; first published in [Lieberherr & Holland, "Object-Oriented Programming: An Objective Sense of Style," OOPSLA '88](https://www2.ccs.neu.edu/research/demeter/papers/law-of-demeter/oopsla88-law-of-demeter.pdf). [Law of Demeter: Principle of Least Knowledge](http://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/lieber/LoD.html) (Karl Lieberherr's own page): *"The name 'Law of Demeter' was chosen because the style rule was discovered while working on The Demeter Project ... (Demeter = Greek Goddess of Agriculture; grow software in small steps.)"*
	- **Kind**: heuristic — a specific case of the general Low Coupling Principle, adopted as a style rule rather than proved as a theorem. [Law of Demeter (General Formulation)](https://www2.ccs.neu.edu/research/demeter/demeter-method/LawOfDemeter/general-formulation.html): *"In this general form, the LoD is a more specific case of the Low Coupling Principle well-known in software engineering."*
	- **Example**: flagged in [[My/Pref/Dev/Tool/Secrets/fnox]] against the 1Password CLI, whose finest-grained access boundary is the Vault — any script that needs one secret ends up holding standing access to every secret in that Vault, the law's warning made concrete.
