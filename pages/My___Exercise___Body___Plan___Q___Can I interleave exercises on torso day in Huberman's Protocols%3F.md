- # Can I interleave exercises on torso day in Huberman's Protocols?
	- ## [[AI Answer]]
		- **Yes, for exercises with different main muscle groups.** On Huberman Lab, Andy Galpin says to train another muscle group while the first lift's rest clock runs. Huberman says he could alternate push and pull sets and gives overhead press with weighted pull-ups as an example. He would accept a small drop in reps or load to save time. Galpin favors uninterrupted recovery when maximizing strength performance is the priority. [Huberman Lab, Strength Training and Supersets at 27:30](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/dr-andy-galpin-how-to-build-strength-muscle-size-and-endurance?timestamp=1650)
		- *Protocols* gives 1–2 minutes between warm-up sets, 3–5 minutes between compound work sets, and 90 seconds to 3 minutes between isolation work sets. Huberman uses the clock to calibrate whether he feels ready. The book passages describe recovery and warm-up targets. The podcast is the direct evidence for using part of that interval for another exercise. [Readwise, *Protocols* rest interval summary](https://readwise.io/open/1063860514) · [Readwise, *Protocols* on timing rest by feel](https://readwise.io/open/1071344445)
		- For [[My/Exercise/Body/Plan/D4-Torso]], try alternating [[Exercise/Body/Strength/Barbell Bench Press]] with [[Exercise/Body/Strength/Pull-Up]] or [[Exercise/Body/Strength/Lat Pulldown]]. Allow 3–5 minutes from the end of one work set to the start of the next work set of that lift. Wait about 90 seconds after each lift before starting the other, then extend the interval if reps, form, or readiness decline. The bench press and bench pull pairing preserved total volume load in about half the time in a small crossover study, and another study tested bench press with lat pulldown. [Robbins et al., bench press and bench pull](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20440121/) · [Paz et al., bench press and lat pulldown](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31113178/)
		- Warm up each lift near its work sets. An easy pull warm-up can fit into a bench ramp interval if the next bench warm-up still feels ready. After the main pair, [[Exercise/Body/Strength/Dumbbell Fly]] and [[Exercise/Body/Strength/Dumbbell Row]] are another pairing to try. Give the row its longer recovery and count both sides as one set. These are applications of the podcast advice to the torso plan. [Readwise, *Protocols* warm-up ramp](https://readwise.io/open/1069405896)
		- Keep dips out of bench or fly rest windows because they share chest and shoulder work. Do back extensions and hanging leg raises after the main lifts. Huberman puts hanging leg raises at the end of torso day and emphasizes that they train grip as well as abs. If time is tight, dips are optional and the book says two work sets per exercise is usual. [Day 4 demonstration, hanging leg raises at 11:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UEU6NYLRi6o&t=716s) · [Readwise, *Protocols* on time and work-set count](https://readwise.io/open/1072993564)
