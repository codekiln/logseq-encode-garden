Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/Exercise Guide

  • Exercise Guide

    • Exercise instructions and demonstrations for Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols. Each included page describes one repeatable practice technique.
    • Start unfamiliar movements with a light practice load and a comfortable range. Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest belong to the training plan.

    • Lower body

      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Back Squat

        • Back Squat

          • Practice a loaded squat that develops leg strength while maintaining a stable trunk.

          • Setup

            • Set the rack hooks just below shoulder height and the safety bars below the bottom of your squat. Center the bar across the upper back, grip it securely, stand to unrack it, and take a short step back.

          • Practice

            • Brace your trunk, keep the whole foot planted, and bend hips and knees together. Lower through the deepest comfortable range you can control, then push the floor away to stand. Let your knees track in the direction of your toes.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Reduce the load or depth if your heels lift or your trunk position changes abruptly. A light goblet squat is a way to practice the pattern; the leg press below is a machine alternative.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's back squat, 0:27. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Leg Press

        • Leg Press

          • Practice extending the hips and knees against resistance with the torso supported by a machine.

          • Setup

            • Adjust the seat so your back and pelvis stay supported, your heels sit flat on the plate, and the starting knee bend is comfortable. Hold the machine's handles and brace your trunk.

          • Practice

            • Press the platform away smoothly, stopping before the knees lock. Lower under control while keeping the pelvis against the pad. End the descent before the thighs compress your torso or the lower back rounds off the support.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Use less weight or a shorter range if your pelvis lifts. Check the release and safety stops with a light load before beginning a sled-style machine; machine mechanisms differ.

          • Sources

            • Source: ACE's seated leg press.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Romanian Deadlift

        • Romanian Deadlift

          • Practice a loaded hip hinge that develops the hamstrings and glutes through controlled lowering and standing.

          • Setup

            • Begin standing with the bar or dumbbells in front of the thighs, feet about hip width apart, and knees slightly bent. Keep the weights close to the legs.

          • Practice

            • Push your hips backward as your torso inclines forward. Keep the knee bend modest and the spine steady. Stop when you feel hamstring tension and before your back position changes, then drive the feet into the floor and bring the hips forward to stand tall.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • The weights need not reach the floor. Turning the movement into a deep squat or reaching lower by rounding the back changes the exercise. Practice an unloaded hip hinge or use lighter dumbbells to learn it.

          • Sources

            • Source: ACE's Romanian deadlift.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Rear-Foot-Elevated Split Squat

        • Rear-Foot-Elevated Split Squat

          • Practice a single-leg emphasis squat with the rear foot supported, developing leg strength and balance.

          • Setup

            • Place the top of one foot on a stable low bench behind you and the other foot firmly on the floor. Begin without dumbbells, using light hand support for balance if needed.

          • Practice

            • Bend the front knee and hip to lower under control, then push through the front foot to stand. Find a stance in which the front heel stays down and the knee follows the toes. Keep the pelvis facing forward.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Lower the support or use a regular split squat with the rear foot on the floor if balance or hip position limits the movement. Add dumbbells only after both sides feel controlled; stance changes should follow comfort and the intended movement rather than a fixed distance from the bench.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's split squat, 2:52. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Trap-Bar Deadlift

        • Trap-Bar Deadlift

          • Practice lifting a load from the floor with coordinated hip and knee extension.

          • Setup

            • Stand in the center of the hex bar with feet about shoulder width apart. Bend at hips and knees to grasp the middle of both handles, with straight arms and feet flat.

          • Practice

            • Brace before the lift. Push into the floor and extend hips and knees together to stand tall. Keep the load balanced and the back position steady. Lower both sides to the floor under control, then reset before the next repetition.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Keep the same handle height between sessions when tracking progress. High handles shorten the range; a lighter load is another way to learn the lift. Avoid jerking the bar, letting the hips shoot upward before the shoulders, or leaning back at the top.

          • Sources

            • Technique source: U.S. Army Fitness Test deadlift instructions. Handle-height context: Barbell Medicine's trap-bar guidance.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Standing Calf Raise

        • Standing Calf Raise

          • Practice raising and lowering the heels against resistance through a controlled ankle range.

          • Setup

            • Use a calf-raise machine or stand on a stable, nonslip step with light hand support. Place the balls of the feet securely on the surface and begin with the knees straight but not forced backward.

          • Practice

            • Lower the heels through a comfortable stretch, pause, then rise as high as you can without bouncing. Keep the ankles controlled and the same foot position throughout.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Begin on both feet with bodyweight if needed. A single-leg version increases the challenge; a dumbbell can add resistance once balance and range are consistent. Shorten the range if the Achilles or ankle is uncomfortable.

          • Sources

            • Demonstrations: Huberman's calf raise, 10:32 and single-leg version, 12:26. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Front Squat

        • Front Squat

          • Practice a squat with the load held in front of the shoulders, developing the quadriceps with a more upright torso than the back squat.

          • Setup

            • Set the rack hooks just below shoulder height and the safety bars below the bottom of your squat. Step under the bar so it rests on the front of the shoulders against the neck, with the elbows raised in front and the fingers or crossed arms keeping the bar in place. Stand to unrack it and take a short step back.

          • Practice

            • Brace your trunk, keep the elbows high and the chest up, and bend hips and knees together. Lower as deep as you can while keeping the whole foot planted, then drive up to stand. Let your knees track in the direction of your toes.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Huberman offers it for people who lack the ankle or shoulder flexibility to squat deep with the bar on their back. If the elbows drop, the bar rolls forward; use less weight. Holding a dumbbell at each shoulder is a way to learn the pattern; the Exercise/Body/Strength/Back Squat is the back-loaded version.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's front squat, 1:07. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles. Source: ACE's front squat, a dumbbell version.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Hack Squat

        • Hack Squat

          • Practice a machine squat in which an angled sled holds the torso in place so that only the knees and hips move.

          • Setup

            • Stand on the platform with your back and shoulders against the pads. Test the machine's angle with little or no weight first, and choose a foot position that lets you bend deeply without joint pain.

          • Practice

            • Release the safety handles, then lower the sled under control by bending the knees and hips. Stop at the deepest range you can control with the back still against the pad, then push through the whole foot to return. Re-engage the safeties before stepping off.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. A work set ends at failure, the point where the next repetition can no longer be done in good form. Add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Moving the feet higher on the platform shifts work toward the hips and glutes; lower and narrower emphasizes the quadriceps. Change the angle or foot position before adding weight if any joint hurts. The Exercise/Body/Strength/Leg Press is a similar machine alternative.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's hack squat, 1:40. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Belt Squat

        • Belt Squat

          • Practice a squat with the load hung from a hip belt, training the legs without loading the spine or shoulders.

          • Setup

            • Use a belt-squat machine, or a dip belt with a plate hanging between the feet while you stand on two raised boxes. Fasten the belt around the hips and hold the machine's handles or a support for balance.

          • Practice

            • Stand tall, then bend hips and knees together to lower the load under control. Keep the torso upright and the whole foot planted. Push the floor away to stand.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Huberman likes this compound option, though most gyms don't have the machine. It suits days when the back or shoulders limit the Exercise/Body/Strength/Back Squat.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's other compound options, 4:54. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Leg Extension

        • Leg Extension

          • Practice isolating the quadriceps by straightening the knees against a machine pad.

          • Setup

            • Adjust the seat so the knee lines up with the machine's pivot and the roller pad rests on the front of the lower shin. Hold the handles and sit with your back against the pad.

          • Practice

            • Lift the weight about a centimeter off the stack so the muscle is under tension before the first repetition. Straighten the knees, squeeze the quadriceps at the top, then lower slowly. Stop each descent just before the weight touches the stack.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. A work set ends at failure, the point where the next repetition can no longer be done in good form. Add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Setting the weight down between repetitions releases tension; keep the stack off its rest for the whole set. Use less weight if the hips lift off the seat or the movement turns into a kick.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's leg extension, 5:12. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Deadlift

        • Deadlift

          • Practice lifting a barbell from the floor with a hip hinge, training the hamstrings, glutes, and lower back along with grip and trunk strength.

          • Setup

            • Stand with feet about hip to shoulder width apart and the bar over the middle of the feet, close to the shins. Hinge and bend the knees to grip the bar just outside the legs with an overhand grip. Flatten the back and lift the chest.

          • Practice

            • Brace, then push the feet into the floor and drive the hips forward to stand tall, keeping the bar close to the legs. Think of hinging instead of yanking the bar upward. Lower it by pushing the hips back and bending the knees once the bar passes them, and keep the back flat until the bar touches the floor.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Grip often gives out before the legs; an over-under grip, with one palm up, is a common way to hold heavier loads. The glutes and hamstrings should do the work, not the lower back. The Exercise/Body/Strength/Trap-Bar Deadlift uses neutral handles beside the body; the Exercise/Body/Strength/Romanian Deadlift starts standing and keeps the knees nearly straight.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's deadlift, 6:18. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles. Source: ACE's deadlift.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Lying Leg Curl

        • Lying Leg Curl

          • Practice isolating the hamstrings by curling the heels toward the hips while lying face down on a machine.

          • Setup

            • Lie face down with the knees just past the bench and lined up with the machine's pivot. Set the roller pad on the lower Achilles, near the ankle. Hold the handles and brace the trunk.

          • Practice

            • Curl the pad toward the buttocks without lifting the hips or arching the back. Pause briefly at the top, then lower slowly. Stop just short of the stack touching so the hamstrings stay under tension through the set.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. A work set ends at failure, the point where the next repetition can no longer be done in good form. Add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Toe position changes which part of the hamstring works; Huberman suggests experimenting. If the lower back takes over, use less weight. Curling one leg at a time is a variation.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's lying leg curl, 7:39. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles. Source: ACE's lying hamstrings curl.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Standing Leg Curl

        • Standing Leg Curl

          • Practice isolating the hamstrings one leg at a time by curling the heel toward the hip from a standing position.

          • Setup

            • Adjust the machine so the working knee lines up with its pivot and the roller pad rests on the back of the lower leg, above the heel. Stand on the other leg, hold the handles, and press the front of the thigh against its pad.

          • Practice

            • Curl the heel toward the buttocks without moving the hips or arching the back. Pause briefly, then lower slowly, stopping before the stack touches. Finish the set, then switch legs.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. A work set ends at failure, the point where the next repetition can no longer be done in good form. Add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Use less weight if the hips swing or the thigh leaves its pad. The Exercise/Body/Strength/Lying Leg Curl trains both legs at once.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's other hamstring options, 9:38. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Glute-Ham Raise

        • Glute-Ham Raise

          • Practice lowering and raising the torso on a glute-ham bench, combining a hip hinge with a hamstring curl.

          • Setup

            • Adjust the bench so the knees rest just behind the thigh pad and the ankles are locked under the rollers with the feet against the plate. Start upright on the knees with the hips extended and arms crossed over the chest.

          • Practice

            • Lower the torso by letting the knees straighten while the hips stay in line with the torso. As the body nears parallel to the floor, the hips can bend to continue downward. Return by curling the heels into the rollers and driving the hips forward until upright.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • The movement takes some skill. Push off the floor or a band with the hands to assist the ascent while learning it, or lower slowly and use the hands to return.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's other hamstring options, 9:38. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 2 - Major Lower Body Muscles. Source: NSCA's glute ham raise.

    • Upper body

      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Seated Cable Row

        • Seated Cable Row

          • Practice pulling a cable handle toward the torso while keeping the trunk steady.

          • Setup

            • Sit tall with feet supported, knees slightly bent, and a secure grip on the handle. Begin with arms extended and the cable already under light tension.

          • Practice

            • Pull the elbows back close to the ribs until the handle reaches the abdomen. Pause briefly, then extend the arms slowly. Keep the torso steady throughout the repetition.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Reduce the weight if you must rock backward, shrug, or curl the wrists to finish. A chest-supported row is an alternative when keeping the torso steady is the limiting factor.

          • Sources

            • Source: ACE's seated cable row.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Machine Chest Press

        • Machine Chest Press

          • Practice a supported horizontal press for the chest, shoulders, and triceps.

          • Setup

            • Adjust the seat so the handles are at or slightly below shoulder height. Plant the feet and keep your back supported.

          • Practice

            • Press forward smoothly and straighten the arms without forcing the elbows into lockout. Return until the upper arms are beside the torso, keeping the movement controlled.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Avoid arching away from the backrest or letting the elbows travel far behind the body. Reduce the load or adjust the starting position if the machine pulls the shoulders into an uncomfortable stretch.

          • Sources

            • Source and video: Mayo Clinic's chest press with a weight machine.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Barbell Bench Press

        • Barbell Bench Press

          • Practice a horizontal barbell press with a repeatable grip, stable support, and controlled bar path.

          • Setup

            • Set the bench and rack so you can unrack without reaching excessively. Use rack safeties or a capable spotter. Lie with feet planted, shoulders supported, and hands slightly wider than shoulder width; wrap the thumbs around the bar.

          • Practice

            • Lower the bar slowly toward the chest while keeping wrists stacked over forearms, then press upward. Keep the hips on the bench and avoid bouncing the bar off the chest.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • A machine chest press is a substitute when a suitable rack or spotter is unavailable. Start with a light bar to establish a repeatable grip and bar path.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's bench press, 0:07. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles. Additional instructions: ACE's barbell chest press.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Pull-Up

        • Pull-Up

          • Practice pulling the body toward an overhead bar through a controlled hanging range.

          • Setup

            • Use an overhand grip on a secure bar and begin with the arms extended in a comfortable hanging position.

          • Practice

            • Pull the chest toward the bar by driving the elbows down and back, then lower under control. Keep the movement free of swinging or kicking.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Use an assisted pull-up machine or another controlled assistance method when needed for the intended range. Reduce the added load or increase assistance if the movement becomes a swing.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's pull-up, 6:13. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Lat Pulldown

        • Lat Pulldown

          • Practice pulling an overhead cable handle toward the chest with a steady supported torso.

          • Setup

            • Secure the thighs under the machine pad and begin with the arms extended and the handle held securely.

          • Practice

            • Pull the handle toward the upper chest, then let the arms extend slowly. Keep the torso mostly upright and pull in front of the head.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Choose a load that permits the whole intended range without swinging backward. A close underhand grip is the variation Huberman demonstrates; a comfortable neutral or overhand grip is another option when the equipment permits it.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's pulldown, 6:56. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Overhead Press

        • Overhead Press

          • Practice pressing a load from shoulder height overhead while controlling the trunk.

          • Setup

            • Begin with the bar at the front of the shoulders, or dumbbells at shoulder height. Brace your trunk and keep the ribs over the pelvis. A seated bench with back support is an alternative to standing.

          • Practice

            • Press overhead through a comfortable range, allowing the head to move just enough for the bar to pass. Lower slowly to the start. Keep the forearms underneath the load.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Reduce the load if you need to lean backward or turn the lift into a push with the legs. A machine press or supported dumbbell press can simplify balance while you practice.

          • Sources

            • Demonstrations: barbell press, 0:11 and dumbbell press, 0:52. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Chest-Supported Row

        • Chest-Supported Row

          • Practice a horizontal pull while a pad or bench supports the torso.

          • Setup

            • On a rowing machine, adjust the chest pad so you can reach the handles with extended arms while the chest remains supported. Set the seat so the handle path is comfortable and the feet stay supported.

          • Practice

            • Brace, row the handles toward your ribs, pause, and extend the arms slowly. Keep your chest in contact with the pad instead of lifting away to finish a repetition.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • For dumbbells, use an incline bench that supports the chest while leaving space for the arms to hang and row. Begin light; a different bench angle changes both the clearance and the line of pull.

          • Sources

            • Source for the supported machine version: ACE's seated row with chest pad.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Biceps Curl

        • Biceps Curl

          • Practice elbow flexion against resistance with a steady upper arm and controlled wrist.

          • Setup

            • Stand or sit with a dumbbell in each hand, arms down, and trunk steady. Curl by bending the elbows; turn the palms upward if comfortable, then lower slowly.

          • Practice

            • Keep the upper arms mostly still and the wrists aligned with the forearms. Reduce the load if you must swing the torso or move the elbows far forward to finish.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • A seated curl or cable curl can make the starting position easier to repeat. An EZ-bar is another grip option.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's curl, 4:44. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Triceps Pressdown

        • Triceps Pressdown

          • Practice elbow extension against resistance from a high cable pulley.

          • Setup

            • Attach a rope or bar to a high cable pulley. Stand with feet planted and knees softly bent, elbows beside the torso, and hands holding the attachment securely.

          • Practice

            • Extend the elbows to press the attachment down, then bend them slowly to return. Keep the upper arms in place and the shoulders quiet.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Avoid using bodyweight to shove the handle downward. Reduce the load if the elbows drift forward or the torso rocks. This is an elbow-extension exercise; Huberman's overhead cable extension uses a different shoulder position.

          • Sources

            • Source: Mayo Clinic's Strength Training for Adults, triceps pushdown.
            • Demonstration: Huberman's triceps isolation, 8:43. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 7 - Shoulders and Arms.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Dumbbell Bench Press

        • Dumbbell Bench Press

          • Practice a horizontal press with a dumbbell in each hand, which lets each arm work on its own path and allows the weights to be set aside if a repetition fails.

          • Setup

            • Sit on the end of a flat bench with the dumbbells standing on your knees. Lean back and kick them up one knee at a time so they arrive at the shoulders as your back reaches the bench. Plant the feet, pull the shoulder blades down and back, and keep head, shoulders, and hips on the bench.

          • Practice

            • Press the dumbbells up until the elbows are straight, with wrists stacked over the elbows. Lower slowly toward the middle of the chest, slightly wide toward the armpits, then press back up without arching the lower back. At the end of the set, bring the weights back to the chest and sit up with them on the knees, or hand them to a spotter.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • If a repetition fails, lower the dumbbells to the sides and drop them clear of the body rather than straining to finish. Getting into and out of position safely limits the usable load more than the press itself; practice the kick-up with a light pair. Keeping the elbows closer to the sides shifts work toward the triceps; flaring them shifts it toward the chest.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's dumbbell bench press, 1:08. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles. Additional instructions: ACE's dumbbell chest press.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Incline Bench Press

        • Incline Bench Press

          • Practice pressing from an inclined bench to shift work toward the upper chest and front deltoids.

          • Setup

            • Set an adjustable bench to an incline; ACE gives 45 to 60 degrees for the dumbbell version, and a lower incline keeps more of the work on the chest. With a barbell, use a rack with safeties and a grip slightly wider than the shoulders. With dumbbells, bring them to the shoulders as in the flat dumbbell bench press. Plant the feet and keep head, shoulders, and hips on the bench.

          • Practice

            • Lower the weight slowly toward the upper chest, keeping wrists stacked over the elbows, then press up until the elbows are straight. Do not bounce the weight off the chest.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. Establish a repeatable movement with a light load first; add resistance gradually after completing the planned repetitions with consistent control.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Barbell and dumbbells both work. Dumbbells allow a deeper stretch at the bottom and let each arm find its own path; the barbell allows heavier, more even loading with rack safeties. Reduce the load if the lower back arches off the bench to finish a repetition.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Huberman's incline bench press, 2:05. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/26/09/Day 4 - Major Torso Muscles. Additional instructions: ACE's incline chest press.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Dip

        • Dip

          • Practice lowering and pressing the body between parallel bars to train the chest, front and side shoulders, and triceps.

          • Setup

            • Grip parallel bars, jump or step up, and support yourself with straight arms and the shoulders pulled down away from the ears.

          • Practice

            • Lean the torso forward somewhat and bend the elbows to lower the body under control until the upper arms are about parallel to the floor or to the deepest point you control without shoulder pain.             • Sets, time, and resistance follow the training plan. In the video it runs as a ladder with the Exercise/Body/Strength/Reverse Curl: one second of rolls and one reverse curl, then two and two, then three and three, climbing until failure.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Keep the elbows still and let only the wrists move. Wrist extensions done over the edge of a bench lose tension near the top, which is why this version is done standing.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Jeff Cavaliere's reverse wrist roll ladder, 5:16. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/18/12/The Perfect Forearm Workout.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Wrist Deviation

        • Wrist Deviation

          • Practice side-to-side wrist bending against a cable rope, training radial deviation (toward the thumb) and ulnar deviation (toward the little finger).

          • Setup

            • Attach a rope to a cable pulley and take one end in one hand. Stand close to the stack with the arm down at your side.

          • Practice

            • For ulnar deviation, start from neutral or slight radial deviation and bend the wrist down toward the little-finger side, slowly and under control. For radial deviation, turn around, take the grip on top, and push down through the little-finger side of the hand so the wrist bends up toward the thumb side, ending with the rope end facing forward.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. The video works each direction on each arm, with a Exercise/Body/Strength/Farmer's Carry between sets.

          • Cues and Variations

            • The range is small; keep the forearm still so only the wrist moves. A sledgehammer held at the side and lifted forward or backward trains the same motions.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Jeff Cavaliere's radial and ulnar deviation, 7:02. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/18/12/The Perfect Forearm Workout.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Forearm Rotation

        • Forearm Rotation

          • Practice turning the forearm palm-down (pronation) and palm-up (supination) against a cable rope, so each rotation meets resistance.

          • Setup

            • Attach a rope to a cable pulley and hold one end so the rope pulls across the hand.

          • Practice

            • For pronation, start with the palm up and turn the forearm over, pushing the fingers into the rope on every repetition. For supination, take the hand out to the side and turn it so the palm faces back toward the machine. Return slowly each time.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, resistance, and rest follow the training plan. The video works each direction to failure, with a Exercise/Body/Strength/Farmer's Carry between sets.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Twirling a dumbbell lets the weight spin the hand, so the forearm falls into each position without resisting it. The rope keeps tension through the whole turn.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Jeff Cavaliere's supination and pronation, 9:18. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/18/12/The Perfect Forearm Workout.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Farmer's Carry

        • Farmer's Carry

          • Practice walking while holding heavy weights at the sides to build grip endurance and trunk stability.

          • Setup

            • Pick up a pair of heavy dumbbells with a hip hinge, then stand tall with the weights at your sides and the shoulders pulled back.

          • Practice

            • Walk at a steady pace with short, even steps, keeping the trunk upright and the weights from swinging. Set the weights down with the same hinge you used to lift them.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Distance and load follow the training plan. In Jeff Cavaliere's forearm workout, one lap around the gym follows every set of every exercise. Beginners can do one carry per exercise.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Squeeze the handles throughout and keep the shoulders from rounding forward. Use lighter weights or a shorter walk if the grip fails before the posture does.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Jeff Cavaliere's farmer's carry, 4:26. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/18/12/The Perfect Forearm Workout.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Dead Hang

        • Dead Hang

          • Practice hanging from a bar with straight arms for as long as possible to build grip endurance.

          • Setup

            • Grip a pull-up bar overhand about shoulder width apart and lift the feet so the body hangs with the arms straight.

          • Practice

            • Hold the hang with a firm grip, keeping the bar in the palms. End the set when the grip fails, then step down.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Duration follows the training plan. Jeff Cavaliere gives 1:40 as an average fresh hang; at the end of his forearm workout he aims for one more minute.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Squeeze hard and keep the bar from sliding into the fingers, which loads the inner elbow. Feet on a box for partial support is an easier version.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Jeff Cavaliere's dead arm hang, 12:44. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/18/12/The Perfect Forearm Workout.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Collar Squeeze

        • Collar Squeeze

          • Practice hand-grip strength by squeezing a spring barbell collar, then flexing and extending the wrist while holding partial tension.

          • Setup

            • Hold a spring barbell collar in one hand as you would a hand gripper.

          • Practice

            • Squeeze the collar closed and release it until failure. Then hold it with a little tension and move the wrist back into extension and down into flexion, keeping the grip, until you can no longer control it.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets and repetitions follow the training plan. The video runs it as a drop-set burnout on each hand, with a Exercise/Body/Strength/Farmer's Carry between sets.

          • Cues and Variations

            • Holding tension with the wrist flexed is hard because the finger flexors are already shortened; that is the part being trained. A hand gripper can replace the collar.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Jeff Cavaliere's collar grip combo, 11:10. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/18/12/The Perfect Forearm Workout.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Plate Neck Raise

        • Plate Neck Raise

          • Practice neck flexion, extension, and side bending against a towel-wrapped weight plate while lying on a bench.

          • Setup

            • Wrap a weight plate in a towel. For flexion, lie face up with the head over the end of the bench and the plate on the forehead. For extension, lie face down with the plate on the back of the head. For side bending, lie on your side with the plate on the upper side of the head.

          • Practice

            • Set a Exercise/Body/Strength/Chin Tuck first. Lower the head toward the floor, then raise it against the plate through a controlled range, holding the plate with one hand. Repeat in each of the four directions.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Sets, repetitions, and load follow the training plan. Progress by adding plate weight; drop repetitions or weight before giving up the chin tuck.

          • Cues and Variations

            • The neck moves while the spine is unloaded. Neck bridges on the floor build size but compress the cervical spine while it moves, which can lead to bone spurs and nerve-root impingement.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Jeff Cavaliere's plate series, 3:50. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/16/11/Neck Exercises that Kill Your Neck.
      • Exercise/Body/Strength/Chin Tuck

        • Chin Tuck

          • Practice pulling the chin straight back to activate the deep neck flexors that forward head posture weakens.

          • Setup

            • Sit, stand, or lie with the head level and the eyes looking straight ahead.

          • Practice

            • Draw the chin straight back without tipping the head up or down. If needed, place an index finger on the point of the chin and push it straight in. Hold, then release.

          • Dose and Progression

            • Repetitions and holds follow the training plan. Once the tuck is strong, hold it through each direction of the Exercise/Body/Strength/Plate Neck Raise.

          • Cues and Variations

            • The movement is straight back, not down toward the chest. Rounded shoulders pull the head forward and up, stretching and weakening the front of the neck; the tuck counters that.

          • Sources

            • Demonstration: Jeff Cavaliere's chin tuck, 5:07. Full notes: Person/Andrew Huberman/Book/26/Protocols/YouTube/16/11/Neck Exercises that Kill Your Neck.
