  • Episode #153 ... The Frankfurt School - Walter Benjamin pt. 2 - Distraction

    • Person/Stephen West/Pod continues on Person/Walter Benjamin of the Frankfurt School, following Person/Stephen West/Pod/21/03 Episode 152 The Frankfurt School - Walter Benjamin Pt. 1. This part covers distraction, the commodity status of art, and the politics of mass-reproducible art in Person/Walter Benjamin/Essay/35/The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction.

    • Technology changes the person

      • 00:24 — Benjamin's Relevance (second snip)
      • 01:24 — Technology Changes People By Changing Their Reality
      • Benjamin still matters because changing the technology around someone changes their sensory experience and subjectivity. The internet, smartphones and self-driving cars now play the part film, radio and television played for him.

    • From storytelling to the novel

      • 02:06 — Evolution of Storytelling
      • Oral storytelling was collective and could not be reproduced; the printing press made the novel, which is read alone and reproduced at will.
      • 03:25 — Novels Trained Modern Political Concentration
      • Solitary reading matched the individualism of the industrial middle class, and the concentration it required helped readers draw out wisdom and form political views.

    • Art becomes a commodity

      • 04:51 — Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction
      • 05:10 — Mass Reproduction Makes Profit Part of Art
      • 05:28 — Mass Reproduction Turns Art Into A Commodity
      • Once art can be reproduced at scale, artists have to think about profit, volume, distribution and cost alongside artistic merit, and the relation between artist and audience passes through the work's status as a commodity.

    • Distraction

      • 06:49 — Film Trains Us To Consume Art Distractedly
      • 07:17 — Film and Distraction
      • 07:30 — Film Trains Us To Experience Art In Distraction
      • A painting allows sustained reflection; film's changing images do not, so distraction becomes the default way of taking in art. Benjamin sets the concentrated viewer absorbed by a work against the distracted mass that absorbs the work, as people absorb architecture.
      • 09:44 — Modern Cities Train Us To Live Distracted
      • 10:40 — Distraction as a Necessity
      • In a city, concentration stops being useful: ads, car horns, music, conversations, smells and the need to check over your shoulder all arrive at once, and distraction is how a pedestrian copes.
      • 11:10 — Modern Life Communicates Beyond Sight
      • The overload comes through every sense, not only sight. Benjamin argues writing cannot carry what a storyteller conveys through several senses.
      • 11:45 — Modern Life Trains Us Through Distraction
      • 12:49 — Modern Aesthetic Experience Happens Through Distraction
      • 13:17 — Cinema as Training
      • Film trains people for a distracted life. We experience buildings through use and passing notice, not by stopping to admire every tile in an airport bathroom, and we take in the world through habit more than deliberation.

    • Politics

      • 15:03 — Film's Potential and Peril (second snip)
      • Benjamin saw emancipatory potential in film, and also a way to deliver ideology to passive spectators: capital shapes what films get made, which can pacify discontent, and mass-reproducible stories can turn audiences into foot soldiers for murderous regimes.
      • 16:49 — Art's Political Turn (second snip)
      • Art shifts from a sacred cult object to something made for display. That makes the masses its critics, and also makes art politically usable, a weapon fascist leaders could use to distribute ideology.
      • 18:34 — The Crisis of Deliberative Politics
      • Mass communication lets leaders speak to the masses directly, which weakens representative democracy and strengthens political theater.
      • 21:17 — Platforms Filter Out Nuance By Design
      • West applies this to social media: character limits and engagement systems favor fortune-cookie messages that a distracted audience can absorb.
      • 23:10 — Technology Can Atrophy Our Ability To Share Experience
      • Technology can mediate life so heavily that people struggle to communicate experience, as World War I soldiers came home with much to say and no words for it.
